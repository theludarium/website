# Journalist requests: starter

Play 2 in `docs/marketing/LINK-STRATEGY.md`. Writers post requests for sources every day. A short, quotable answer that gets used usually comes with a link, and links from news and lifestyle sites are the most valuable kind for a young site. November and December are peak gift guide season, and the site already has the articles to draw from.

Prices checked 2026-11-04 on each service's own site.

## Which service

**Start free with HARO and Source of Sources. Add Featured's free tier for roundup quotes.** Pay for nothing until one of these lands a placement.

| Service | Cost today | What the free plan gets you | Fit for gift guides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **HARO** (helpareporter.com) | Free | Unlimited replies. Three emails a day with every topic mixed in; no keyword filter, so you filter in Gmail | Good. Lots of consumer and gift queries, also lots of competition. Featured.com bought it from Cision in 2025 and relaunched it |
| **Source of Sources** (sourceofsources.com, Peter Shankman) | Free (he asks for a charity donation or a link instead) | Unlimited replies, up to three emails a day | Good. Consumer, lifestyle, and travel outlets. Strict: off-topic pitches get you removed |
| **Featured** (featured.com; Terkel now redirects here) | Free; Lite $39 a month ($29 a month billed yearly); Pro $99 ($79); Pro+ $299 ($239) | A daily allowance of answers; the exact count isn't published | Good. Mostly listicle and roundup questions, which is what gift guides are |
| **Qwoted** | Basic free; Pro $1,188 a year | 2 pitches a month, and alerts arrive two hours late | Medium. Strongest for business and finance writers |
| **SourceBottle** | Free plan can't pitch; $25 a pitch or $65 a month | Profile and alerts only | Lifestyle-friendly, but mostly Australian and New Zealand outlets |
| **MentionMatch** (was Help a B2B Writer) | Free | Unlimited | Poor fit: business software writers only |
| **ProfNet** (Cision) | Paid, no public price | None for experts | Too expensive for this |

Also free, and good for the togethering and loneliness angle: **Radio Guest List** (radioguestlist.com) for podcast and radio guest requests, and the #journorequest tag on X and Bluesky.

**Paid upgrade, only if the free ones produce placements:** Featured Lite at $29 to $39 a month. Skip Qwoted Pro at this stage.

## How to answer

- Answer within a few hours. Most requests close fast, and early answers get read.
- Lead with the answer. The first two sentences should work as the quote.
- Under 200 words. Name specific games and prices; writers love a concrete pick.
- Link the one article it came from, plus the press kit for photos and bio.
- Only answer what you know. Skip requests that want a doctor, a therapist, or a research credential, even on loneliness.
- Two or three answers a week is the goal. Log each one at the bottom of this file.

Claude can turn any request into a first draft from the articles. Paste the request into a session and ask for a draft answer.

## Sample answers

Each answer follows the same shape: the direct answer in the first two sentences, one or two specifics a writer can quote, and the link to the page it came from. Journalists cut answers down, so the best line goes first. Edit to fit the actual request; never send one unchanged.

Sign-off for every answer:

> Levi JA Wilson, founder of The Ludarium (theludarium.com), Indianapolis. The Ludarium publishes free board game guides and free courses that teach specific games. Press kit and photos: https://theludarium.com/press/

### 1. "Best board game gifts for people who don't play board games" (or "for beginners", "for families")

Source: [The Best Board Game Gifts for New Players, Holiday 2026](https://theludarium.com/articles/board-game-gifts-new-players-2026/)

> The best board game gift for a new player is not the cleverest game on the shelf. It is the one that gets opened on the day, taught that week, and played again. Most gifted games stall in the middle: the box sits in a closet because nobody wants to read the rulebook.
>
> So pick a game that teaches in ten minutes. Ticket to Ride (about $40) is my pick for a family with kids eight and up. Azul (about $32) is the one for a couple; at two players, taking the tile your partner needs is half the game. Codenames (about $20 to $25) is the safe pick for a group that fills a living room.
>
> One tip that costs nothing: open the box and punch out the cardboard before you wrap it, and write "I'll teach you" on the tag. A game you play together on the day is a game that gets played again.
>
> Full list with player counts and prices: https://theludarium.com/articles/board-game-gifts-new-players-2026/

### 2. "Affordable gifts under $30" (or "stocking stuffers", "budget gift ideas")

Source: [Best Board Game Gifts Under $30](https://theludarium.com/articles/best-board-game-gifts-under-30/)

> A $15 card game that gets played forty times is a better gift than a $70 box that gets played twice. Price tracks how many components are in the box, not how good the game is, and many of the best-designed games are card games that cost under $20.
>
> If you know nothing about the person, buy Codenames (about $25). It scales from four players to a full party. For a couple, Patchwork or Jaipur (both under $30) are true two-player games, not compromises. For a stocking, Flip 7 is about $8 and plays a whole table in twenty minutes.
>
> What to skip: licensed versions of classic games. Monopoly with a movie on the box is still Monopoly.
>
> Full list: https://theludarium.com/articles/best-board-game-gifts-under-30/

### 3. "Gifts for grandparents" (or "gifts for older parents", "games for the whole family at the holidays", "keeping seniors connected")

Source: [Board Games for Grandparents: 10 Gifts They'll Play](https://theludarium.com/articles/board-games-for-grandparents-gift/)

> The best game for a grandparent is one that comes out of the box after you leave. That means pieces they can see and hold, rules that feel like something they already know, and rounds under thirty minutes.
>
> Rummikub (about $13 to $18) is the safest pick for anyone who already plays cards: it is rummy with chunky tiles that are easy to grip and read. If small print is a problem, Qwirkle has no text at all and puts a grandparent and a seven-year-old on even ground. For a full holiday table, Hues and Cues seats ten and needs no reading.
>
> The real gift is the second visit. Teach it once, lose gracefully, and leave it set up on the table instead of back in the box. A game is a reason for the grandkids to sit down, and that time together is the point.
>
> Full list, with notes on large print games: https://theludarium.com/articles/board-games-for-grandparents-gift/
