# Building The Ludarium

We are building a game lounge business in Indianapolis, and we are doing it in public. This category is the record: the decisions, the numbers we plan around, the things we got wrong, and what we changed. If you are thinking about opening a board game cafe, a lounge, or any third place, it is the notebook we wish someone had left open for us.

The first pieces explain the shape of the thing. Why a lounge and not a store, which is mostly a question about what you sell: belonging or boxes. Why we will teach and recommend games but never stock them, and what that costs us. And the membership math, worked out with real assumptions: how many members cover rent, why churn matters more than price, and where the model breaks.

Then the practical steps. What we want in a first space, which turns out to be a checklist for saying no. How we are building the library, a hundred games chosen by ratio and job rather than by rating. Why we run corporate game nights before we have a door, because revenue without rent and a community before a lease are both worth having.

One piece is about the word. Togethering is on everything we make, and the explainer says why we needed a new word for people coming together in person to share life, with a game as the medium.

One piece is a list of mistakes. Seven things we believed in planning that were wrong, and what replaced them. It will get longer.

And one piece is for you. How to help The Ludarium open before it opens, ranked by impact, from free to founding member.

We will keep this category current as the plan meets reality. The long plan, one membership that works in every city, is the last piece here, and it is the one most likely to change.
