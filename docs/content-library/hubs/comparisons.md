# Comparisons

Most board game comparisons end with "it depends." Ours end with a verdict. Each piece in this category takes two or three games that people hesitate between, compares them on the things that matter at a table, and says which to buy for which group. If you are standing in a store or looking at two tabs, these are written for that moment.

The comparison points are the same every time: player count, play time, teach time, weight, interaction, and feel. We put them in a table so you can scan, then explain the parts a table cannot show. Azul and Sagrada both have pretty tiles and reward entirely different kinds of thinking. Codenames and Decrypto are both word games and produce very different rooms.

The gateway pair is where most people start. Catan versus Ticket to Ride is the first decision many new hosts face, and the answer depends on how your group handles trading and confrontation. Pandemic versus Forbidden Island is the same designer, the same idea, and a different weight, which makes it a clean choice once you know your group.

The engine builders and deck builders get their own pieces. Wingspan versus Everdell. Dominion versus Clank!, which is really a question of whether you want a board at all. For campaigns, the Gloomhaven family is compared on price, scenario count, and commitment, with a clear place to start.

Two comparisons reach beyond board games. D&D versus Pathfinder for beginners, judged on learning curve, starter sets, and finding a group. Cube versus Commander, the two Magic formats most groups argue about, and the one that fits a regular table.

And one comparison is about where to play. Board game cafe versus home game night, on cost, library, hosting work, noise, and who shows up. We have an opinion, and it is less one-sided than you would expect from people building a lounge.

If you want the deeper reasoning behind any pick, the strategy guides and What to Play lists go further.
