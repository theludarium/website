# Glossary

Board game terms get in the way more than people admit. Someone says a game is a "mid-weight euro with a tableau and a bit of take-that," and a new player hears nothing. The words are useful, which is why hobbyists use them, but nobody hands you a dictionary at the door.

This category is that dictionary, one term at a time. Each explainer says what the word means, where it came from, which well-known games show it clearly, and why it matters when you are choosing what to play. We use games most people have seen: 7 Wonders for drafting, Wingspan and Splendor for engines, El Grande and Risk for the majority-versus-control distinction, Munchkin and Exploding Kittens for take-that.

Some entries settle arguments. Eurogame versus Ameritrash is a label fight that has run for twenty years, and the explainer says what each camp means and why most new games borrow from both. Kingmaking is a word people use when they are annoyed, and the piece explains why it happens and how to handle it at the table without a fight.

Some entries are practical tools. Board game weight, the complexity number on BoardGameGeek, is the single most useful figure for picking a game for a mixed group once you know what a 1.5 or a 3.5 feels like. Card drafting and engine building describe the two mechanics behind a huge share of the games made since 2010. Understand them and dozens of rulebooks get shorter.

And one entry is just a good story. The meeple is a small wooden person that became the symbol of a whole hobby, and the word is younger than most of the people using it.

Read these in any order. If you are new, start with weight, then engine building, then drafting. Those three cover most of what you will hear at a game store counter. If you design games, or want to, the whole set is a vocabulary lesson, and pairs with our game design category.

Each entry is short on purpose. The goal is that you can read one in five minutes and recognise the thing at your next game night.
