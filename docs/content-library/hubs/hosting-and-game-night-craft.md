# Hosting and Game-Night Craft

Hosting a game night is a craft. The game matters less than the dozen small decisions around it: who is coming, how many, what you play first, where the snacks go, how the room is lit, when it ends. Get those right and people come back. Get them wrong and the best game in the world sits in its box while everyone checks their phone.

This category is the practical core of The Ludarium. The question it answers is how to host a game night that people want to return to, week after week, without the host burning out. Every piece here comes from running tables, not theory.

Start with the complete checklist, which runs week by week from invites to the first ten minutes. Then the lineup piece, which treats a game night like a show in three acts: a short opener, one main event, a quick closer. Then the headcount guide, because the single most common hosting mistake is choosing a game built for four when six people show up.

From there the pages handle the problems that actually come up. The player who takes forever on every turn. The rules argument that is about to turn personal. The snacks that are about to ruin a card. The friend who is always on their phone. Each gets a short piece with a fix you can use this week.

There is also the question of the room. Lighting, seating, and sound decide whether a table feels easy to play at, and almost nobody plans them. Storage matters too; a hundred games in an apartment is possible with the right shelves and a willingness to cull.

Two pieces come from our earlier work on standing game nights and belong here as well: how to start a group that still meets a year from now, and the phone basket rule, which changes a table more than any game does.

If you are an introvert who likes hosting but hates the drain, there is a piece written for you. If your nights run until one in the morning and attendance is slipping, read the one on ending on time.

The game is the reason. The people are the point. A good host remembers that in the fifth hour, when it is hardest.
