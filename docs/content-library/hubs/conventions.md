# Conventions

Gen Con happens in our city every August, so a Gen Con guide was always going to be part of The Ludarium. This category covers the convention from the first badge purchase to the week after, plus the smaller Midwest shows that many people end up preferring. It is written for attendees, for the people who bring games to show, and for anyone who finds four days of crowds a lot.

Start with logistics, because they decide the trip. The hotel piece explains the housing lottery, the downtown four-night rule, and what to do if you land in the suburbs. The budget guide covers real badge prices, cheaper beds, and how to leave the Exhibit Hall solvent. Event registration is its own small science: the catalog, the wish list, the noon rush, and generic tickets. The packing list is built from what people actually run out of.

Then the trip itself. Gen Con with kids covers the wristband rules, the kids' area, and how long a six-year-old lasts in the hall. Conventions for introverts is a pacing plan: where the quiet is, how to meet people at the table rather than in the aisle, and when to go back to the room without guilt.

Two guides are for the other side of the table. How to demo a board game at a convention, including the two-minute pitch and the ten-minute teach. How to pitch a game to publishers at Gen Con, from getting meetings to what gets decided in the first minute.

The Midwest piece lists eleven conventions in Ohio, Wisconsin, Michigan, Missouri and Minnesota, with dates checked on each show's site. Many are smaller, cheaper, and easier to play at.

And one guide is about afterward. What to do after Gen Con is how to turn four days into a year of game nights, which is the whole point of going.

Three Gen Con pieces live in our Indianapolis category: for first-timers, for survival, and for locals.
