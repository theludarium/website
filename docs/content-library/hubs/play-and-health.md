# Play and Health

The benefits of playing board games are mostly not about the games. They are about what a weekly table does to the people around it: regular contact, shared laughter, a reason to leave the house, a group that notices when you are missing. The research on loneliness, friendship, and long life keeps pointing at those things, and a game is the easiest way we know to produce them on schedule.

This category makes that case carefully. The core question is whether play is good for adult health, and in what ways, and the answer is yes with specifics. The Harvard Study of Adult Development, the U.S. Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on loneliness, and the Blue Zones work all show up here, named in prose, with the figures hedged where they should be. We do not invent studies, and we flag every statistic for the editor to source before it publishes.

The pages fall into three groups. The first is the health evidence itself: board games and dementia, games as stress relief, play and ADHD, social prescribing, the social side of laughter. The second is the idea we built The Ludarium around: togethering, social fitness, and why a standing game night is a longevity practice rather than a hobby. The third is the social batch, ten shorter pieces on what in-person play does for men who have lost the habit of friendship, teenagers and parents, people who just moved to a new city, and groups that last.

If you are skeptical that games deserve a place in a health conversation, start with the loneliness piece. If you are past skeptical and want the framework, read togethering and social fitness back to back. If you are over sixty, or love someone who is, the retirement piece and the dementia piece are the two to read first.

The Ludarium's connection to the LEV Institute, which works on longevity ethics, shows up most clearly here. We think the social side of long life is the least-copied and most important part, and a table with a game on it is where we start.

The game is the reason. The people are the point. In this category, that line is a health claim.
