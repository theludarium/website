# Gift Guides

The best board game gift is the one that gets played, and that depends on who opens it. A game that delights a couple who play every Friday will sit in a closet at a grandparent's house. A clever party game is a strange thing to hand a coworker you barely know. Most gift lists ignore this and rank games by how much reviewers liked them.

These guides start with the person. Each one names a recipient, a budget, and the practical constraints: how many people they usually have around, how much reading they will tolerate, whether they already own a shelf of games. Then it picks. Every pick has a current price, checked at major retailers at the time of writing, and a sentence on why it fits that person and not just anyone.

The budget guides come first because that is how most people shop. Under $30 covers the sweet spot where good games live. Stocking stuffers covers small boxes under $20 that still get played after December. Black Friday explains which games drop 40 to 50 percent every year and which never go on sale, so you know whether to wait.

Then the people. Couples who play together, sorted by how they play. Grandparents, where big pieces and short games matter more than depth. Teenagers, who will mock anything earnest. The coworker you know by first name only, where safe matters more than clever. The person who already owns everything, where the answer is usually not another game.

Two guides go deeper. The D&D starter set piece compares the two current boxes and tells you what to add so the gift becomes an actual campaign. The accessories guide covers sleeves, inserts, mats and trays, the things gamers want but rarely buy themselves.

Prices move. Each guide notes when it was checked. If you are buying for a game night host rather than a player, the hosting category has more ideas, and our Indianapolis pieces list local stores where you can see the box before you buy.
