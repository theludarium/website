# Cooperative and Campaign Games

Cooperative board games put everyone on the same side against the box. Campaign games stretch that over weeks or months, with a story that carries from one session to the next. Together they are the fastest-growing corner of the hobby, and the one most likely to pull in people who say they do not like competing with friends.

This category covers both. The core question is simple: which co-op should my group play, and how do we keep it going? The answers depend on who is at the table. A couple wants something different from a group of six. A table with one loud planner needs a game that keeps information hidden, or that planner will play everyone's turn for them. A group that can only meet twice a month should think hard before opening Gloomhaven.

We start with the fundamentals. Pandemic is still the clearest teaching case for how a co-op builds tension, so it gets its own explainer. The Crew does the same for cooperation under a talking ban, and it is the best game we know for teaching a new group how to read each other. From there the list of fifteen best co-ops sorts the field by player count, length, and difficulty.

Then the harder questions. Legacy games ask you to tear up cards and sticker the board, which thrills some groups and horrifies others. Campaign games break when someone misses a session, so there is a piece on playing without them and catching them up. Semi-cooperative games put a traitor inside the team, which can be the best night of the year or the end of a friendship, depending on how you run it.

If you are new, read the best-of list and the Pandemic explainer, then try The Crew. If you are deciding on a big box, read the Gloomhaven guide before you buy. If your co-op nights keep ending with one person running the table, the quarterback-problem picks are for you.

One thing worth saying plainly: co-ops are not easier or softer than competitive games. They are a different kind of hard. Losing together, then talking about why, teaches a group more about itself than winning alone ever will.
