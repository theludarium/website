# What to Play

The best board games are the best for a specific table. Six coworkers after work need a different game from a couple on a Tuesday, who need a different game from twelve people at a party. Ask what to play without saying who is playing and you will get a list of famous titles, half of which will fall flat.

This category answers the question properly. Every list here is built around a situation: a group size, a time limit, a type of player, a mood. Each pick comes with the player count, the play time, and a sentence about who it suits and why. We would rather you buy one game that fits than three that are well reviewed.

If you are hosting people who say they do not like board games, start with the list written for exactly them. It fixes the real complaints, too long, too much reading, too much waiting, with twelve games that avoid all three. If you are turning a casual friend into a regular, the gateway games piece names ten games and what each one leads to next.

Then the lists by constraint. Party games for eight or more people, with notes on running a big table without chaos. Two-player games for date night, from calm puzzles to friendly rivalries. Games under thirty minutes, for openers, closers, and nights that run short. Card games that stay funny after the third round and are not Cards Against Humanity.

Two comparison pieces round it out. Wingspan, Everdell, and Cascadia look alike on the shelf and play very differently; that piece tells you which one to buy first. And the best-of-2026 list is a running one, updated as new releases clear a bar we explain in the article.

Finally, the Ludarium Library: the fifty games we would put on every lounge table, sorted by job. It is the closest thing we have to a canon, and it changes slowly on purpose.

Start with the list that matches your next game night. Read the Library when you want to understand how we think about all of them at once.
