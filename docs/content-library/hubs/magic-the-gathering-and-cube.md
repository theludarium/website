# Magic: The Gathering and Cube

An MTG cube is a hand-picked set of Magic: The Gathering cards, usually 360 to 540 of them, that a group drafts again and again. Nobody buys packs. Nobody's deck is better because they spent more. The cube belongs to the table, and every draft is a new puzzle built from the same stack.

This category covers Magic through that lens. The core question is how to play Magic with friends in a way that costs little, teaches well, and keeps a group together for years. Cube is our answer, and Cube Commonwealth, the founder's own drafting community, is where most of these lessons were learned.

Start with the explainer if you have never heard the word cube used this way. Then the build guide, which walks through a 360-card first cube for under $200, from card list to sleeves to box. The pauper-versus-vintage piece helps you decide what kind of cube fits your group: one costs less than a hundred dollars, the other has a Black Lotus in it, and they play very differently.

Once you have a cube, you need to draft it well and host it well. The draft strategy piece is for players who keep ending up with forty-six playables in five colors. The hosting piece covers timing, seating, pairings, lands, and the small rituals that get eight people through three hours and back next month.

Then the arguments. Proxies: cheating, theft, or common sense? Commander: why four-player politics breeds grudges and how to keep your friends. And a longer piece on what thirty years of Magic taught a generation about deckbuilding, bluffing, and patience.

If you are teaching someone Magic, skip the starter decks. A cube draft teaches the game faster, and there is a piece here on why.

Magic is a competitive game with a social problem: it is easy to spend a lot and play alone. Cube fixes both. One box of cards, owned by nobody in particular, is enough to hold a group together for a decade.
