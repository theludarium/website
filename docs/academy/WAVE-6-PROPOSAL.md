**The pattern:** heavy strategy games with factions, characters, or many moving parts (Root, Spirit Island, Dune, Terraforming Mars, Ark Nova) earn 3 to 8 times more impressions per page than light gateway games (Azul, Splendor, Carcassonne, King of Tokyo). Their players search specific questions: a faction, a map, a setup rule, a strategy. Each lesson answers one, and that is the long tail Google is showing.

**Codenames is the exception** among light games, mostly on player-count and rules questions.

**Caveat:** this is two weeks of data and small numbers. It is a strong hint, not proof.

## The first wave: 15 games

Chosen for (1) a top BoardGameGeek ranking, (2) the Root and Spirit Island pattern of factions, characters, or deep rules that people search one question at a time, (3) being actively played and in print, and (4) no existing course.

**Asymmetric and faction games** (the Root pattern)
1. **Arcs.** From Root's designer and publisher, rules-heavy, with lots of card and campaign questions. Root is the top course.
2. **Scythe.** Factions and player mats; long-running searches for faction strategy.
3. **Twilight Imperium (4th edition).** 25 factions and a famously dense rulebook.
4. **Terra Mystica.** 14 factions, each with its own strategy.
5. **War of the Ring (2nd edition).** Asymmetric two-player; many rules questions.

**Character and campaign co-ops** (the Spirit Island pattern)
6. **Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion.** How most people start Gloomhaven. Links to the two existing Gloomhaven articles.
7. **Marvel Champions.** Hero-by-hero questions and an active community.
8. **Arkham Horror: The Card Game.** Investigator and deckbuilding questions.

**Heavy euros** (the Ark Nova and Terraforming Mars pattern)
9. **SETI: Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence.** The newest game near the top of BGG; search interest is rising now.
10. **Great Western Trail (2nd edition).**
11. **Concordia.**
12. **Agricola.**

**Where demand is already showing up**
13. **7 Wonders Duel.** Queries like "7 wonders duel progress tokens explained" already find the 7 Wonders course, at position 67. A course of its own should rank far better. Move the two existing Duel lessons into it, with redirects.
14. **Star Wars: Unlimited.** TCG courses earn steady impressions, and an article already exists to link from.

**Rising social game**
15. **Blood on the Clocktower.** Character-by-character questions from a large, active community, and it fits togethering well.

**On the bench** for wave 6, or swaps if one above falls through: Gaia Project, Brass: Lancashire, Viticulture, Mage Knight, Frosthaven, Flesh and Blood, Star Wars: Rebellion.

## How to ship it

- **Size:** launch each course with about 12 core lessons (setup, a turn, scoring, factions or characters, first-game mistakes, strategy), not 20. That makes about 180 new pages instead of 300. Add lessons later where Search Console shows demand.
- **Pace:** 5 courses a week over three weeks, so Google sees a steady stream, not a spike.
- **Indexing:** add each course page and its how-to-play lesson to the daily indexing runs.
- **Rules accuracy:** heavy games take longer to check. Follow the review checklist (`docs/academy/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md`), and use no publisher art or rulebook text.
- **Measure:** after 4 to 6 weeks, compare impressions per page with the current heavy courses (about 13 to 19 per page in their first two weeks). If the wave matches, plan wave 6 from the bench. If it doesn't, put the effort into deepening the courses that work.
