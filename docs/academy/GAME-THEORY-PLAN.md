# Read the Table: the Game Theory course plan

Levi asked (2026-11-27) for an Academy course on game theory. Ludarium players already learn game theory by trying to win; this course gives them a structured path, shows how to use it at the table, and carries it to work (their role, their team, their company's strategy). It is also the foundation of a corporate service, Game Theory workshops (`/game-theory-workshops/`).

Course file: `src/content/courses/game-theory.yaml` (`group: "Skills"`, which switches the course pages to "cheat sheet" wording and drops the game facts). Lessons: `src/content/course-lessons/game-theory/`.

## Every lesson

- One idea, 600 to 900 words, frontmatter like every other course lesson (`oneIdea`, `tryThis`, `nextTeaser`, `emailSubject`, sources).
- Teach the idea with a board game first, then a tiny table or tree a reader could sketch on a score pad, then **At the table** (how it changes your play) and **At work** (one short section applying it to a job, a team, or a business). The At work sections are the seed of the corporate workshops, so keep them concrete and ethical: better decisions and better incentives, never manipulation.
- Examples prefer games with Ludarium courses (Catan, Codenames, Ticket to Ride, Wingspan, Splendor, Power Grid, Root, Twilight Imperium, Dune: Imperium, 7 Wonders Duel, Sushi Go!, The Crew, Sky Team, chess, Cube draft, Ludus Latrunculorum), plus well-known others (poker, Skull, Love Letter, The Resistance, Diplomacy, Cosmic Encounter, Modern Art). Every rule claim about a game must be right.
- Every claim about the theory carries a source: the original paper or book where possible, or a solid textbook or encyclopedia (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Nobel Prize press releases).
