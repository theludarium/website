Levi asked (2026-11-27) for a second corporate service next to the team and leadership workshops: game theory workshops for companies, built on the free Academy course Read the Table (`/academy/game-theory/`). The sales page is `/game-theory-workshops/`; its inquiry form posts to `/api/inquiry` with `service=game-theory`, so the email to Levi is titled "Game theory workshop inquiry" and GA4 still counts the thanks page as `corporate_inquiry`.

Prices are not set (see `revenue-ops/drafts/corporate-option-sheet.md` for the team-workshop price levels, which can carry over). Nothing here is promised on the site except "we bring the games, run the session, and lead the debrief."

## Who buys it and why

- **Buyer:** heads of L&D and HR, sales and partnerships leaders, founders and leadership teams.
- **The problem they name:** teams that negotiate badly, departments that work against each other, goals and bonuses that reward the wrong behavior, leaders who react instead of thinking ahead.
- **The promise:** your people practice strategy, negotiation, and incentive design by making real decisions at a table, then use the same map on the decisions in front of them at work.
- **Why games:** people remember what they did, not what they heard. A short game creates a shared experience the whole room can analyze in the debrief.

## Formats

The same three formats as the team workshops, so one price sheet covers both.

| Format | Length | Group | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy Session | 90 minutes | up to 16 | One module (usually "Map the game" or "Cooperate or defect"), one debrief, the cheat sheet |
| Half-Day Strategy Lab | 3.5 hours | up to 24 | Three modules and debriefs, plus the "Map the game" worksheet on a real decision the team brings |
| Full-Day Program | 6 hours | up to 30 | Five or six modules in a full arc, team worksheets on real decisions, a written summary for the sponsor, and a 30-minute follow-up call |

## Modules

Each module runs about 45 to 60 minutes: teach a short game (5 minutes), play (15 to 25 minutes), debrief (15 to 20 minutes). Every game can be taught in five minutes to people who do not play games. The course lessons behind each module give Levi the theory and the talking points.

| Module | Game or exercise | The idea | Course lessons | Work debrief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Map the game | A ten-minute cooperate-or-defect card round | Players, choices, payoffs, information | 1 and 3 | Map a current project: who are the players and what does each actually want? |
| 2. Cooperate or defect | Repeated rounds of the same card game, then a surprise "last round" | Prisoner's dilemma, repeated games, reputation | 9 | Where does our team defect on each other, and what makes cooperation pay? |
| 3. Read the signals | Codenames (two teams; one copy per 8 to 10 people) | Signals, cheap talk, shared context | 10 | Which of our messages are believed, and which are noise? |
| 4. The winner's curse | A sealed-envelope auction of a jar of coins nobody has counted (the classic winner's curse demo, lesson 11), then For Sale (3 to 6 players a table) for bidding under a budget | Auctions, bidding, overpaying | 11 | Where do we overpay to win: hiring, vendors, deals? |
| 5. The negotiation table | A trading exercise with private goals (each player wants different sets of cards) | BATNA, growing the pie, commitment | 12 and 13 | Know your walk-away point before every negotiation; plan the deal that grows the pie |
| 6. Coalitions | A three-player split-the-prize vote, then debrief | Coalitions, fair shares, the kingmaker | 14 | Who holds the swing vote on our big decisions, and why? |
| 7. Fix the rules | Teams redesign the payoffs of an earlier game so cooperation wins | Mechanism design, incentives | 15 | Find one goal or bonus that rewards the wrong move, and redesign it |
| 8. Two moves ahead | The "guess two thirds of the average" game (course lesson 2), then a short game tree | Thinking about others' thinking, backward induction | 2, 6, and 18 | Before the next big decision, ask: what will they do once they see our move? |

Suggested arcs:
- **Strategy Session:** module 1, or module 2 for a team-trust focus.
- **Half-Day Lab:** 1, 2, 5 (negotiation focus) or 1, 2, 7 (incentive focus) or 1, 4, 5 (sales and partnerships focus).
- **Full-Day Program:** 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8.

## The debrief, every time

1. **What happened?** Each table says what they chose and why, in one sentence.
2. **Name it.** Levi names the idea (one slide or one card) and draws the small table or tree.
3. **Where is this at work?** Pairs find one real example from their own job.
4. **What will you do differently?** Each person writes one move for next week on their worksheet.

Keep it ethical and say so out loud: game theory is for better decisions and fairer incentives, never for manipulating colleagues or customers.

## Take-home materials

- The Read the Table cheat sheet (the course teach sheet PDF at `/academy/game-theory/game-theory-teach-sheet.pdf`), printed for every person.
- A one-page "Map the game" worksheet: players, choices, payoffs, information, the likely equilibrium, the move you want, and the incentive you could change. (Course lesson 16 explains the method; turning it into a printed worksheet is a next step.)
- For the Full-Day Program: a short written summary for the sponsor: the themes the team surfaced and the incentive changes they proposed.
- A link to the free course so people can keep going.
