You picked the perfect game on Tuesday. On Saturday, your sister brings her kids, two friends arrive an hour late, and everyone is on their second glass of wine by the time the box is open. The game is still perfect. The room it was perfect for never showed up.

This lesson is about the gap between the game you planned and the night you got. The fix is not a better pick. It is three picks, chosen in advance and decided on the night.

## What changes on the night

Here are the things that most often move the right pick, and which way they push.

- **Energy.** A tired room wants short and simple. A buzzing room wants something loud and social. Neither wants a long silent think.
- **Drinks.** A drink or two makes laughing easier and remembering rules harder. Lean toward party games and away from anything with a scoring table.
- **Kids.** Children nearby mean interruptions, and interruptions break long games. Pick something with short turns that survives a parent leaving the table.
- **Late arrivals.** Do not start the long game while waiting. Start something short that people can join between rounds.
- **A late start.** Count back from when people will want to leave, not forward from when you planned to start. If it is already late, the long game is already gone.
- **Headcount.** One cancellation or one surprise plus-one can push a game out of its best player count.

None of these is a failure. They are just facts about the night, and you cannot know them until people are in the room.

## Three plans

**Plan A** is the game you prepared, from your shortlist and your ladder. You have done the solo run, you have a script, and it fits the person you most want to teach.

**Plan B** is a fifteen to twenty minute game that plays across a wide range of player counts and teaches in under a minute. It is your answer to every change in the list above. It should be a game you can teach half asleep.

**Plan C** is no game. The evening goes on: dinner, talk, a walk. This is not giving up. A night where nobody was pushed into a game is a night that keeps the door open. A night where a tired newcomer was pushed into a long one closes it.

The skill is in the switching. Decide in advance what sign moves you from A to B, and from B to C. "If we have not sat down by eight, plan B." "If the baby is still up after the first round, plan C." Deciding the sign ahead of time means you do not have to argue with yourself in the moment, when you most want to play your game.

## A worked example: the ninety-minute game that should have been twenty

Picture this. Plan A is [Ticket to Ride](/academy/ticket-to-ride/) for four, a good newcomer pick that plays in about an hour. Two guests arrive late, and one brings a friend. By the time the board is set up, it is past nine and one of the newcomers has started yawning.

You start anyway, because you set it up. The teach runs long, as first teaches do. The first game with newcomers runs well past the box time, and with five at the table instead of four, the turns stretch further. Ninety minutes later, someone is still placing trains while the newcomer counts down the cards in her hand to see how long she has left. The game ends. Everyone says thank you. Nobody mentions a next time.

Now the same night with three plans. At nine you see the signs: late start, tired faces, five people instead of four. You put Ticket to Ride back on the shelf and pull out plan B. [Codenames](/academy/codenames/) takes two teams and about fifteen minutes a game, and turns are talk instead of rules. Or [Flip 7](/academy/flip-7/), where the whole teach is "take another card or stop, and a repeat number busts you." Two quick games, lots of laughing, everyone home by ten. On the way out, the newcomer asks what the train game was.

That question is the whole point. Plan A did not die. It became the next game night, and now someone is asking for it.

## Set it up so switching is easy

Plans only help if switching costs nothing. Keep plan B in reach, not buried on the shelf, and do not set up plan A so fully that putting it away feels like a loss. If you find yourself thinking "but I already set it up," that is the sunk cost talking, not the room.
