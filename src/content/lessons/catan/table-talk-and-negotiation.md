Two players sit at the same table with nearly the same cards. One gets every trade they ask for, and the robber never seems to visit. The other gets told "not this time" all night, and the robber lives on their best hex. The board did not decide that. The talking did.

Catan is a game of open conversation. Every trade, every robber move, and every "who's winning?" happens out loud. This lesson is about using that talk on purpose, while staying the kind of player people are glad to sit with.

## What is table talk actually for?

It does three jobs.

1. **Get deals.** Turn "maybe" into "yes" on the trades you need.
2. **Point the table at the real leader.** You cannot stop the leader alone. Three players together can.
3. **Keep yourself off the radar.** The player nobody worries about gets trades and avoids the robber.

You already have the tools for the first job. [Lesson 17](/academy/catan/art-of-the-trade/) taught specific offers: name the cards, the count, and the person. This lesson adds the reasons that get those offers accepted, and the talk that happens between trades.

## How do you persuade someone in Catan?

Give them a true reason that serves their game. People say yes to deals that help them, not to deals that help you.

- **Speak to their need.** "You've been stuck on brick for three turns. My brick for your ore?" That reason is about them.
- **Use numbers, not feelings.** "Red's at 8" lands better than "Red's doing really well."
- **Make yes easy.** One clear offer. No long haggle.
- **Then stop talking.** The longer you sell, the more the table wonders what you are building.

And keep it honest. You will play these people again. A player who twists the facts gets doubted forever. A player who is straight about public facts, like the score, gets believed when it counts.

## What is an alliance of convenience?

A short, open understanding between players who share one problem. Usually that problem is the leader.

Say Red is at 8. You and Green are both at 6. A simple line does it: "Green, Red's at 8. Want to hold off trading with Red until one of us catches up?" Now two of the three other players have closed their doors to the leader, and the robber has two players pointing it at Red.

Good alliances share a few traits:

- **They are said out loud.** No side deals in whispers. Everyone at the table can hear it.
- **They are short.** One goal, like slowing the leader, and they end when it is done.
- **They help both sides.** Each player in the alliance is making their own best move. You are not giving anything away.
- **Nobody holds anyone to them.** Agreements in Catan are not binding. If Green breaks it, that is the game, not a betrayal.

That last point matters most. The moment Red falls behind and you take the lead, the alliance is over. Expect Green to turn the same tools on you. Smile about it. That is exactly how it should work.

## How do you talk the robber off your hex?

Give the roller a better target, with a reason that helps them. From [Lesson 22](/academy/catan/robber-tactics/), you know the best robber move hits the leader's biggest hex. Your job is to make that obvious, fast.

- **Speak early.** Say it as the 7 lands, before the pawn is in their hand.
- **Use pips and points.** "Red's city on the 6 is 10 pips. My settlement on the 5 is 4. And Red's at 8."
- **Name the steal, too.** "Red has 7 cards. I have 2."
- **Say it once.** One calm sentence. Then let them decide.

What does not work: pleading. "Not me, I've been robbed all game" gives no reason, and it tells everyone your hex matters. It also wears thin by the second game.

And never offer a card to steer the robber. At home it turns a clean game into a bribe market. At official events it is out: once the robber is in play, nobody may talk about trading resources until the robber move is done.

## How do you stay likable without becoming the target?

The goal is to be the player everyone is happy to deal with, and nobody is worried about.

- **Do not announce your own strength.** Nobody needs to hear that your engine is humming.
- **Do not gloat.** A big roll for you is a quiet "nice." A big roll for someone else is a sincere "nice."
- **Make fair offers, and take "no" well.** "No worries" keeps the door open for next turn.
- **Credit the dice.** "I got lucky on the 8s" is true, and it lowers the temperature.
- **Bluff lightly about hidden cards.** "Who knows what's under there?" is part of the game. Lying about the visible score is not.
- **When someone says you are leading, agree.** "Yep, fair, I'd rob me too." Arguing sounds like hiding something.

Here is the hard truth: if you are actually winning, good talk will not keep the robber away forever. It just keeps you from being the target before you are the real leader. That is often enough.

## What about players who cannot win?

Late in a game, a player far behind may still decide who wins by whom they trade with and where they put the robber. That is kingmaking, and it is part of every negotiation game. Our article on [kingmaking in board games](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) covers it in depth.

Treat that player the way you would want to be treated. Give them good reasons, not pressure. A player who feels respected is far more likely to listen when you point out who is really at 9.

## What changes at an official tournament?

Table talk is welcome at official events, with a few firm lines. Check the current [CATAN tournament rules](https://catanevents.com/hosts/tournament-rules/) before any event, since they change each season. As of this writing:

| Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Making offers to the active player on their turn | A trade between two players when neither is the active player |
| Agreements and promises, as long as everyone knows none of them are binding | Giving or getting a card for free, in one trade or across several |
| Bluffing about your face-down development cards | Helping another player win with no benefit to yourself |
| Pointing out who is leading | Talking trades while the robber is in play |

The free-card rule is the one that catches friendly players. No gifts, ever, even to help someone who is behind. Every trade has to be a real trade. Level 4 covers the rest of the official rules.

## Try it this week

Your drill: talk the robber off your best hex once per game.

1. **Know your best hex.** After setup, name the hex that would hurt most to lose.
2. **Keep your pitch ready.** Each round, find the leader's biggest hex in pips, and note the leader's score and hand size.
3. **When a 7 lands or a Knight comes out, speak first.** One sentence: the leader's hex, its pips, the leader's score, and what you would lose by comparison.
4. **No cards, no pleading.** Just the reason.
5. **Track the result.** Did the robber go where you suggested? Mark Y or N.
6. **After the week, review.** Which pitches worked? Most players find that the shorter the pitch, the better it lands.
