Picture two players swapping a wheat for a brick. It looks even. One card each, everyone smiles. But one of them builds a settlement this turn, and the other holds a wheat with nothing to spend it on. That trade had a winner.

Level 1 taught you the rules of trading and the cost of the bank. This lesson is about winning trades with other players on purpose. Not by tricking anyone. By knowing what a card is worth to each side before you say yes.

## Who wins a trade?

Every trade helps both players. Nobody agrees to a deal that leaves them worse off. So "is this trade good for me?" is the wrong question. The useful one is **"who does this help more?"**

Here is a quick test. After the trade, what can each of you build, and how soon?

| After the trade | Who won |
| --- | --- |
| You build this turn, they build nothing yet | You, clearly |
| You both build this turn | Usually whoever builds the bigger thing |
| They build this turn, you are still a card short | Them |
| They score a point, and they are the leader | Them, and maybe the game |

A card is worth what it finishes. A brick that completes your settlement is worth a point and new income. The same brick in a hand that is three cards short is worth very little today.

## When should you offer 2 for 1?

A lopsided offer sounds like losing. Often it is the smartest trade at the table.

Look at the net cost. Giving 2 cards for 1 drops your hand by 1 card. Compare that with the supply rates from [Lesson 9](/academy/catan/bank-and-port-trades/):

| Way to get 1 card | Cards out of your hand, net |
| --- | --- |
| 1-for-1 with a player | 0 |
| 2-for-1 with a player | 1 |
| 2:1 port | 1 |
| 3:1 port | 2 |
| Bank, 4:1 | 3 |

So a 2-for-1 with a player costs you the same as a 2:1 port would, and you do not need to own the port. Offer one when:

- **It finishes a build this turn.** A point now is worth a spare card.
- **You are over 7 cards.** As [Lesson 10](/academy/catan/hand-management/) showed, a 2-for-1 shrinks your hand and makes the offer easy to accept.
- **You are paying in cards you have too many of.** Wool piles up for most players. Two wool you cannot use are worth less than one brick you can.
- **Nobody bites at 1-for-1.** A sweeter offer is still far cheaper than the bank.

## When should you ask for 2 for 1?

Flip it around. Sometimes you hold the card everyone wants. Then you can ask for more.

Ask for 2 cards when:

- **You hold the scarce resource.** Early on, that is often brick. Later, it is often ore. [Lesson 8](/academy/catan/resource-value-over-time/) covers why.
- **They are one card short of a build.** That card is worth a point to them. Paying 2 for it is still a good deal for them, and a better one for you.
- **You are the only source.** If nobody else will trade it, the bank is their only other option. Your 2-for-1 costs them 1 card net instead of the bank's 3. They will usually take it.

Keep the ask fair enough to land. A 3-for-1 ask saves them only 1 card over the bank, so it usually gets a no. A 2-for-1 is the sweet spot: clearly good for you, still clearly better than the bank for them.

## Can you trade when it is not your turn?

Yes and no. Only the active player trades with other players. If it is Red's turn, Red can trade with anyone, but you cannot trade with Green.

What you can do is **make offers to the active player**. This is one of the most underused tools in the game. Red has just rolled and is looking at a fresh hand. If you watch what Red needs, you can speak first.

"Red, I'll give you an ore for your brick."

Now Red has a ready-made deal, and you get your brick without waiting a whole round. Three habits make off-turn offers work:

- **Watch what the roll just paid.** Red's hand just grew. You saw what came in.
- **Offer before Red asks.** The first clear offer often wins.
- **Stay quiet when Red is the leader.** An off-turn offer to the leader is still feeding the leader.

## Why should you never feed the leader?

You met the rule in [Lesson 12](/academy/catan/beginner-mistakes/). Here is the sharper version.

The leader turns cards into points faster than anyone. So the question is not the price. It is **what the card finishes**. Before any trade with a player at 7 or more points, ask: "What does this card let them build?"

- **If it finishes a city, a settlement, or a development card,** the answer is no. Even 3-for-1 in your favor can hand the leader the last piece of a win.
- **If it finishes nothing yet,** and the deal is clearly lopsided your way, it may be fine.
- **If you are unsure who is leading,** count visible points and make a guess at hidden Victory Point cards. Level 3 has a full lesson on reading the real score.

Saying no is not rude. It is the game. A warm "not this time" keeps the table friendly. Our guide on [how to win at Catan](/articles/how-to-win-at-catan/) has more on trading without feeding the leader.

## Why make every offer specific?

Compare two offers.

- "Anyone have brick?"
- "Sam, my wool and my wheat for your brick?"

The first invites the whole table to name a price. It also tells the leader exactly what you need. The second does three useful things:

1. **You set the price.** Sam only has to say yes or no.
2. **You pick the partner.** Naming Sam keeps the leader out of it.
3. **It is fast.** A clear offer gets a clear answer. Long haggles give everyone time to work out what you are building.

A good offer names three things: **the cards you give, the cards you want, and the person.** If you want a 1-for-1 to land, add a reason that helps them: "You've been short on wheat all game."

If the answer is no, say thanks and move on. You can make a new offer, try someone else, or check your ports. A friendly no today keeps the door open for a yes next turn.

## Try it this week

Your drill: make every offer specific. No "anyone have...?" all week.

1. **Use the three-part shape.** Every offer names what you give, what you want, and who. "Jo, two wool for your ore."
2. **Check the price against the supply.** If your offer costs more cards net than your best port or the bank, use the supply instead.
3. **Make one off-turn offer per game.** Watch a roll, spot what the active player needs, and offer first.
4. **Ask "what does this finish?"** before every trade with a player at 7 or more points. If it finishes a build, decline with a smile.
5. **Score your trades.** On a scrap of paper, mark each trade W or L: did it help you more, or them? After the game, count. Aim for more Ws than Ls by the end of the week.
