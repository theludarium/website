Ninety seconds is plenty for a simple turn. Roll, collect, build a road, pass. It is not plenty if you start thinking when the dice land. Then you look at your hand, look at the board, wonder about a trade, change your mind, and the timer beeps while your hand still hovers over the bank.

The fix is not to think faster. It is to think earlier. This lesson shows what to plan on other players' turns, how to run your own turn in under a minute, and when to spend the time bank.

## What timer will you face?

The current [CATAN tournament rules](https://catanevents.com/hosts/tournament-rules/), as of October 2026, give hosts two options for keeping games moving:

| Option | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Tournament timer | 90 seconds of turn time plus a 10-minute time bank. The rules link a ready-made setup on the Shared Game Timer site, and hosts can adjust the settings. |
| Judge-enforced turns | After an hour of play, a judge may put a slow table on timed turns, about 2 minutes each, and shorter if the event is running late. Any player can also ask a judge for timed turns once opening placements are done. |

Your 90 seconds are your normal turn. The bank is a reserve you draw on when a turn runs long. The rules page does not spell out how the bank refills or what happens when it runs dry, and hosts can change the settings. So at the start of the day, ask the judge two things: how the bank works, and what happens at zero.

## How long is a 10-minute bank, really?

Ten minutes is 600 seconds. Say you take 15 turns in a game. Spread evenly, that is 600 / 15 = 40 extra seconds a turn, so about 90 + 40 = 130 seconds each.

But turns are not even. Most are simple. A few decide the game. A better way to think of the bank is as three big turns. Give each one about 3 minutes, and 3 x 3 = 9 minutes are spoken for, with 1 minute left as a cushion.

Good turns to spend it on:

- **A closing turn,** like the two-point turns from [Lesson 25](/academy/catan/endgame/)
- **A big trade** with several players making offers
- **A tricky robber move** when the leader is not obvious

Bad turns to spend it on: rethinking a plan you already made, counting cards you could have counted on someone else's turn, and asking the table for brick three different ways.

## What should you plan on other players' turns?

Everything you can. Each time a turn ends, update a short plan for your next one. It has four parts.

1. **Your build.** What will you build if the cards come? Name it: "City on the 6 wheat corner."
2. **Your gap.** Which cards are you missing for it, and who probably has them? [Lesson 21](/academy/catan/card-counting/) is your tool here.
3. **Your offer.** One specific trade, ready to say: "Two wool for one ore, anyone?"
4. **Your 7.** If you roll a 7, which hex gets the robber, and whom do you rob? [Lesson 22](/academy/catan/robber-tactics/) has the reasoning.

Add one more check before your turn: do you want to play a Knight before you roll? Decide on their turn, not yours.

The plan changes as the dice change. That is fine. Each roll only nudges it. Updating a plan takes a few seconds. Building one from nothing takes a minute.

Green's plan: build a city on the 8 ore corner. Green holds 2 ore and 1 wheat and needs 1 more ore and 1 more wheat. Blue has been collecting ore. Green has spare wool to offer. On a 7, the robber goes on Red's 6 wheat field, and Green robs Red, the leader.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Green rolls a 9. Green's 9 wheat field pays 1 wheat. |
| 0:10 | "Blue, two wool for one ore?" |
| 0:25 | Blue says yes. Cards swap. |
| 0:40 | Green builds the city and says "done." |

Forty seconds, and 50 left on the clock. Nothing about that turn was rushed. Green just did the thinking earlier.

## Where do turns lose time?

Most slow turns lose it in the same few places.

| Time sink | The fix |
| --- | --- |
| Deciding what to build after the roll | Name your build on the turn before |
| Open-ended trade talk | One specific offer, then take the first fair yes |
| Picking a robber target from scratch | Choose your 7 plan before you roll |
| Recounting your hand | Sort your cards by type and keep them sorted |
| Building, then rethinking the road | Decide the road's direction when you decide to build |

Trading is the big one. Open with your one ready offer, let the table answer or counter, pick the best deal, and move on. Your turn is not the place to talk through every possible trade. If nobody bites in about 20 seconds, use the bank rate or your port, or pass and try again next turn.

## How do you stay calm with a clock running?

A few habits help.

- **Say "done" out loud.** It ends your turn cleanly and starts the next player's clock.
- **Do not watch the timer.** Watch the board. Your routine is the clock.
- **Accept a decent turn.** A good move made with time to spare beats a perfect one made at the buzzer.
- **Let the timer handle slow players.** You never need to rush anyone. The clock does it for you, and the table stays friendly.

Turn timers also help your home games. Our guide to [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) explains why long turns drain a table, and how to help a slow player without making them feel bad.

## Try it this week

Your drill: play five games with a turn timer.

1. **Pick a timer.** A shared timer app or website works, or a phone timer that one player resets each turn. Set 90 seconds a turn and a 10-minute bank for each player.
2. **Tell your group the rules.** Explain what happens when time runs out at your table. A friendly version: you finish your current action, then pass.
3. **Plan out loud, once.** In your first game, say your four-part plan (build, gap, offer, 7) under your breath before each of your turns.
4. **Track your bank.** After each game, write how much bank time you had left and which turns used it.
5. **Find your time sink.** Look at the table above. Which one cost you the most? Fix that one in the next game.
6. **Finish with an untimed game.** Notice how much faster you play now, even with no clock.
