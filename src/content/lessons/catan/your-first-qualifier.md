Signing up for your first tournament feels bigger than it is. You picture a silent room of experts who have memorized every board. Then you walk in and find a game store on a Saturday, a few dozen people who like Catan, and a host who is glad you came.

This lesson starts with my own first qualifier, at Moonshot Games in Noblesville, Indiana, on October 24, 2026. The rest is the guide I wish I had read first: how to register, what the day looks like, what to bring, how to think about it, and how to be a good opponent.

## How do you sign up?

Official CATAN events in the U.S. are listed on [Best Coast Pairings](https://catan.bestcoastpairings.com/play/events), often called BCP. You make an account, find an event near you, and register there. [Lesson 29](/academy/catan/how-catan-tournaments-work/) explains where a local qualifier sits on the ladder.

Then read the event listing closely. It tells you the things the rules page does not:

| Detail | What to look for | At Moonshot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry fee | Set by each host | $10 |
| Payment | How and when you pay | Cash or Venmo at the door, after registering on BCP |
| Check-in | When to arrive | 10 to 10:15 a.m. |
| Length | When it should end | About 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. |
| Field size | The player cap | 32 players |
| Eligibility | Who may play | U.S. residents, 18 or older by the date of Nationals |

Two more things from that listing are worth knowing. Only the winner of a local advances. And once you win a local this season, you cannot enter another local in the same region.

If your plans change, unregister. Events have caps, and your seat could go to someone on the waiting list.

## What does the day look like?

Arrive in the check-in window. The host collects fees and gets everyone into the system. Then a judge explains the tournament rules and the code of conduct. Listen closely: this is when to ask how the turn timer and time bank work.

The format depends on how many people show up. Under the current [CATAN tournament rules](https://catanevents.com/hosts/tournament-rules/), an event needs at least 12 players for official results. With 12 to 28 players, everyone plays three preliminary games, and the top four meet at a final table. With 29 to 79, the top 16 after three games play semifinals, and the four semifinal winners play the final.

Each game runs the way [Lessons 31 to 34](/academy/catan/map-study/) described: five minutes of map study, timed placements, then timed turns. Setup alone can take 20 minutes. That is why a three or four game day runs about eight hours, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. at Moonshot.

## What should you bring?

- **The entry fee,** in the form the host asked for
- **A charged phone,** for BCP and for keeping in touch between games
- **Water and food,** since eight hours is a long time on store snacks
- **A pen and a small notebook,** for a review between games
- **A layer,** because game rooms run hot or cold
- **A copy of CATAN, only if the host asks.** Some events ask players to bring one. If the listing does not say, ask the host

Leave your phone in your pocket during games. The official code of conduct keeps personal devices off the table while you play, though a photo of the board before or after a game is fine. AI help of any kind is banned.

## What mindset helps most?

Go to learn. Your first qualifier is a scouting trip. You will see how strong players read a board, trade, and finish, and you will find out which parts of your game hold up under a clock.

A few habits help:

- **Set goals you control.** Finish every placement inside the window. Plan on other players' turns. Score as many points as you can in every game.
- **Play every game for points.** As [Lesson 33](/academy/catan/play-for-points/) showed, a 7-point loss beats a 4-point loss in the standings.
- **Reset between games.** Write the three-line review from [Lesson 28](/academy/catan/reviewing-your-games/), then let the game go. The next board is new.
- **Expect some bad dice.** Three games is a small sample. Judge your day by your choices, not your record.

## What is good tournament etiquette?

Most of it is just being a good table guest. A few points are specific to official events.

- **Count the pieces before you start.** The rules ask players to check the components at the start of each game. Tell a judge if anything is missing.
- **Give your card count when asked.** Keep the cards themselves hidden.
- **Roll into the tray or cup** if the table has one.
- **Make real trades only.** No free cards, ever, not even to slow the leader.
- **Claim your win on your own turn.** Say it when you reach 10. Forget, and you wait a full round.
- **Call a judge right away,** calmly, if something seems wrong. Problems raised later are treated as normal play. Judges settle rules questions, but they do not give strategy advice.
- **Check the score sheet before you sign it.** Once it is signed, the result stands.
- **Be kind about the clock.** Never rush a slow player yourself. The timer does that job.
- **Thank your table.** Win or lose, shake hands or say "good game." These are the people you will see at the next event.

## Try it this week

Your drill: register for a qualifier.

1. **Find your events.** Search [Best Coast Pairings](https://catan.bestcoastpairings.com/play/events) for CATAN events within driving distance. If you did the Lesson 29 drill, start with your list.
2. **Read each listing.** Note the date, check-in time, fee, payment method, player cap, and eligibility.
3. **Pick one and register.** Pay the way the host asks.
4. **Put it on your calendar,** with the check-in window and your travel time.
5. **Play one home game under tournament conditions** before the event: timed placements, a turn timer, and the official rules from [Lesson 30](/academy/catan/tournament-rules/).
6. **Pack the night before.** Fee, phone, water, food, pen and notebook, a layer.
7. **After the event, write your own recap.** Three lines per game, plus the one thing that surprised you most.
