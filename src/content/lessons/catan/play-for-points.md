At home, a loss is a loss. Somebody wins, everybody else shrugs, and you set up again. Whether you lost with 9 points or 4 does not matter to anyone.

At a tournament, it matters a lot. Most players who reach a final table get there with some losses on their sheet, and those losses were not all equal. This lesson shows how the standings work, walks through the math, and gives you a plan for the games you cannot win.

## How are tournament standings ranked?

Here is the order in the current [CATAN tournament rules](https://catanevents.com/hosts/tournament-rules/), as of October 2026, in plain words:

| Order | What counts | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wins | Number of preliminary games you won |
| 2 | Total victory points | Your points from every preliminary game, added up, at most 10 per game |
| 3 | VP percentage | In each game, your points divided by the total points at your table; then add those shares together |
| 4 | Places | More second-place finishes, then more third-place finishes |
| 5 | Lots | A random draw, if all else ties |

Wins come first, always. But look at what follows. Two tiebreakers in a row are about points.

## Why do tiebreakers decide so much?

Because ties on wins are everywhere. Picture a qualifier with 24 players. Under the current rules, a field of 12 to 28 plays three preliminary games, and the top four meet at a final table.

Each round seats six tables of four, so each round has six winners. Over three rounds, that is 6 x 3 = 18 wins. Say two players win twice. That uses 2 x 2 = 4 wins, leaving 18 - 4 = 14 wins for 14 different players with one win each.

So two final seats are settled by wins, and the other two go to the best 2 out of 14 one-win players. That race is decided on points. Your losses are your tiebreakers.

## How much is a good loss worth?

Take two players with one win each. Both won a game with 10.

| | Game 1 | Game 2 | Game 3 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Riley | 10 (win) | 4 | 5 | 19 |
| Jordan | 10 (win) | 8 | 7 | 25 |

Jordan's losses were worth 8 + 7 = 15 points. Riley's were worth 4 + 5 = 9. Jordan finishes 6 points ahead on the first tiebreaker, without a single extra win.

Every point counts once, in any game. Turning a 5-point loss into a 7-point loss is two points of standings, the same two points a winner earns between 8 and 10. Note the cap, though: points past 10 in a game do not count, so a 12-point blowout scores the same as a clean 10.

## How does VP percentage work?

If total points also tie, VP percentage decides. In each game, divide your points by everything your table scored. Then add your three shares. The rules ask for four decimal places when it is done by hand.

Here are Maya and Sam. Both have one win and 24 total points.

| | Game 1 | Game 2 | Game 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maya's table | **10**, 8, 6, 5 = 29 | 10, **7**, 6, 4 = 27 | 10, 8, **7**, 6 = 31 |
| Maya's share | 10 / 29 = 0.3448 | 7 / 27 = 0.2593 | 7 / 31 = 0.2258 |
| Sam's table | 10, **7**, 5, 4 = 26 | **10**, 9, 8, 7 = 34 | 10, **7**, 7, 5 = 29 |
| Sam's share | 7 / 26 = 0.2692 | 10 / 34 = 0.2941 | 7 / 29 = 0.2414 |

Add them up. Maya: 0.3448 + 0.2593 + 0.2258 = 0.8299. Sam: 0.2692 + 0.2941 + 0.2414 = 0.8047. Maya ranks higher.

Look at why. Sam's win came at a table where everyone scored big, 34 points in all, so his 10 was a smaller share. A point is worth more at a low-scoring table. You cannot choose your table, but it is one more reason to slow the leader instead of letting them run away.

Three-player tables get a fix so they are not unfairly easy. The rules add a dummy score equal to the average of the three scores, rounded, to the table total. If a three-player table scores 10, 6, and 5, the average is 21 / 3 = 7, so the table total is 21 + 7 = 28. A 6 there is worth 6 / 28 = 0.2143.

## When should you switch from winning to scoring?

Play to win first. A win is worth more than any number of points, and players come back in Catan more often than you would think. [Lesson 27](/academy/catan/recovering-from-a-bad-start/) showed how.

But some games end without you. Look for these signs:

- **The leader can win next turn,** and you cannot stop them.
- **You are three or more points behind** and the leader has a clear path, like the ones from [Lesson 25](/academy/catan/endgame/).
- **Your best plan needs two more rounds** that the game will not give you.

When you see them, change the question. Stop asking "how do I win?" and ask "what is the cheapest point I can score before this ends?"

## What are the cheapest late points?

| Build | Cost | Points | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City | 5 cards | 1 | You hold ore and wheat and have a settlement |
| Settlement | 4 cards, plus 2 per road | 1 | An open spot is one road away or less |
| Longest Route | 2 cards per road | 2 | You are one or two roads from passing the holder |
| Largest Army | 3 cards per development card | 2 | You already have Knights in hand |

A sure point now beats a better point later, because the game ends the moment someone claims 10 on their turn. If you can build a city this turn, build it.

Two bonus tiles deserve a second look. Taking Longest Route or Largest Army gives you 2 points and takes 2 from the holder, so your share of the table total rises too.

One more thing. Ask the judge at the start of the day how hidden Victory Point cards are recorded when the game ends, so you know whether a late development card can help your score.

## Is playing for points fair to the table?

Yes, as long as you play for your own points. The official rules are clear that moves serving your own goals are fine. What is not fine is helping another player for no gain of your own, and giving away free cards is never allowed, even to slow the leader.

So do not hand the game to your friend. Do not sulk and stop trading, either. Make real trades that help you score, and keep blocking the leader when it also buys you another turn. Our guide on [how to be a good loser](/articles/how-to-be-a-good-loser/) covers the mood side. And if you have seen a player who cannot win pick the winner, [kingmaking](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) explains why tournaments draw the line where they do.

## Try it this week

Your drill: in a game you cannot win, push for the highest score you can.

1. **Keep score for everyone.** In each game this week, write every player's final score, not just the winner's.
2. **Name the moment.** When the signs show you cannot win, say "points" to yourself and write down the round.
3. **List your cheapest points.** Write every point you could still reach and what each costs, like the table above.
4. **Score before it ends.** Build the cheapest sure point each turn. Do not save for a bigger turn that may never come.
5. **Compute your share.** After each game, divide your score by the table total. Write it to four decimal places.
6. **Compare with your old losses.** After a week, look at your scores in games you lost. Did they go up?
