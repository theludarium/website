At 7 points, Catan changes. The early game was about income. The middle was about growth. Now the table starts counting your points, and every card you hold matters. Plenty of games are lost right here, by players who reach 7 first and then drift.

This lesson is about the last three points. How to plan them, which builds get you there fastest, and how to finish before the table can stop you.

## Why plan at 7 and not at 9?

Because at 9 it is usually too late to plan. By then everyone can see you are close. The robber finds your best hex, trades dry up, and your last point can take five rounds instead of one.

At 7, you still have room. You can choose which points to take, in what order, and how much of that the table sees. Think of 7 as the moment the game turns from building an engine to closing it out.

## What are your paths from 7 to 10?

Start by listing every point you could still reach. There are only a few kinds ([Lesson 5](/academy/catan/how-to-win/) has the full list):

- A **city** on one of your settlements
- A **settlement** on an open spot you can reach
- **Longest Route** or **Largest Army**, 2 points each
- A **Victory Point card**, hidden in your hand or still in the deck

Then write two or three paths that add up to 10. Take Blue. She shows 7 visible points: 2 cities and 3 settlements. She has played 2 Knights, and nobody holds Largest Army yet. She also holds one unplayed development card, and it is a Victory Point. So her real total is 8. She needs only 2 more.

| Path | What it needs | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cities | 2 cities: 4 wheat and 6 ore | 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 |
| Army | One more Knight played, to reach 3 | 8 + 2 = 10 |
| Mixed | A settlement on the open 9-pip spot, then a city | 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 |

A path is only real if you can name the cards it needs. Pick the one your income feeds fastest, and keep a second one as a backup in case the robber or a rival blocks the first.

## Which builds close a game fastest?

Compare what each point costs late in the game.

| Build | Cost | Points | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City | 5 cards | 1 | Needs a settlement to upgrade, and ore |
| Settlement | 4 cards, plus 2 per road | 1 | Late in the game, open spots are rare and contested |
| Take Longest Route | 2 cards per road | 2 | You must beat the holder, not tie |
| Take Largest Army | 3 cards per development card | 2 | You need to draw the Knights first |
| Development card | 3 cards | 1, only if it is a point | About 1 in 5 from a fresh deck |

Two things stand out. First, **the bonus tiles are the only 2-point moves in the game.** If you are one road or one Knight away, that is often your fastest finish. Taking one from another player also costs them 2 points, as [Lesson 19](/academy/catan/longest-route-and-largest-army/) showed.

Second, **cities are the most reliable closer.** Nobody can take the spot from you, and they need no wood or brick. A player at 7 with settlements on good wheat and ore often has the cleanest path at the table.

Development cards are the wild card. A card is worth less than a sure point, but it is safe from the robber, safe from discards, and hidden. Late in the game, after many Knights have been played, the odds of drawing a point go up. [Lesson 26](/academy/catan/development-card-math/) does that math.

## How do you avoid tipping off the table?

The table reacts to what it sees. So manage what it sees.

- **Keep Victory Point cards face down.** A hidden point is a point nobody plays against. Reveal it only when it wins the game.
- **Watch your visible score.** At 9 visible, you are everyone's target. At 8 visible with a hidden point, you might not be.
- **Do not beg for your last card.** "Anyone have ore? I really need ore" tells everyone what you are building. Make quieter offers, or use the bank and your ports.
- **Let someone else look like the leader.** If another player shows more points or holds a bonus tile, the robber and the trade freeze tend to go their way. [Lesson 23](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/) shows how a table decides who is really winning.

Hiding has a limit. A player holding four development cards and a full hand looks dangerous no matter what. The goal is not to look weak. It is to avoid sitting one point from victory, in plain sight, for a whole round.

## How do you set up a two-point turn?

The strongest finish is to jump from 8 to 10 in a single turn, so the table never sees you at 9.

A few ways to get there:

- **A bonus tile.** Taking Longest Route or Largest Army is 2 points at once. Road Building can do it with two free roads.
- **A build plus a hidden point.** At 8 visible with a Victory Point card in hand, any single build wins. Reveal the card as you build.
- **Two builds in one turn.** A city and a settlement together cost 9 cards. That is hard to hold. But Invention can supply the last 2, and a port can turn spare cards into the missing ones.

Saving up has a price. If you end your turn with 8 or more cards, you lose half on a 7. In a four-player game there are four rolls before you can build again: three opponents' rolls, then your own. The chance that none of them is a 7 is 5/6 x 5/6 x 5/6 x 5/6 = 625/1296, about 48%. So about **52%** of the time, a 7 lands first, just as [Lesson 10](/academy/catan/hand-management/) warned.

That is a coin flip you rarely need to take. Pass the dice with 7 or fewer cards, or park some of them in a development card, which never counts toward your hand.

## When exactly do you win?

You win the moment you have 10 or more points during your own turn. Not at the end of the turn. Not when someone else counts. The moment.

That has a few practical edges:

- **You can win before you roll.** A Knight played before the dice can take Largest Army and win on the spot.
- **A Victory Point card you built this turn counts.** It is the one development card you may use on the turn you build it.
- **Points gained on another player's turn wait.** If a rival's settlement breaks a route and the tile comes to you, you win when your own turn comes, if you still have 10.
- **Count before you pass the dice.** Players miss their own win more often than you would think. From 7 on, add your visible points and hidden cards every turn.

## Try it this week

Your drill: from 7 points, plan your path to 10 before each roll.

1. **Mark the moment.** When you reach 7 points, counting hidden Victory Point cards, say "planning" to yourself.
2. **Write two paths.** On a scrap of paper, list two ways to reach 10 and the exact cards each one needs.
3. **Check before every roll.** Which path is closest now? Did the last round change it?
4. **Look for a two-point turn.** A bonus tile, a hidden point plus a build, or an Invention that finishes two builds.
5. **Pass the dice with 7 cards or fewer,** unless your build is one card away and you have decided the risk is worth it.
6. **After the game, count the rounds** from your seventh point to the end. If you did not win, which path stalled, and why?
