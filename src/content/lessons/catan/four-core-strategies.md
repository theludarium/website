Ask a few strong Catan players how they won their last game, and you will hear a few different stories. One rushed cities. One built roads everywhere and took Longest Route. One sat on a wheat port and traded with the supply all game. One took a little of everything and adapted.

They are not making it up as they go. These are the four core strategies, and nearly every winning game follows one of them. This lesson lays out all four, what each one needs, and how to choose yours. The short version: pick one early, and stick with it unless the board forces a change.

## Why pick a strategy at all?

Your cards are limited, and each strategy wants different cards. Roads want brick and wood. Cities want ore and wheat. If you half-chase both, you end up holding a little of everything, building nothing, and handing half your hand to the next 7, as [Lesson 10](/academy/catan/hand-management/) warned.

A lane fixes that. It tells you what to build next, what to trade for, and what you can happily trade away.

## What are the four core strategies?

Here they are side by side.

| Strategy | Core resources | Most points come from | The opening looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ore-wheat-wool | Ore, wheat, wool | Cities, development cards, Largest Army | Strong mountains and fields, plus a pasture |
| Wood-brick | Wood, brick, then wheat and wool | Settlements, Longest Route | Strong forest and hills, with open space nearby |
| Port engine | One resource in bulk | Whatever the port buys | Two big hexes of one resource, a 2:1 port in reach |
| Balanced | All five | A mix of everything | All five resources on decent pips |

Now each one up close.

## Strategy 1: How does the ore-wheat-wool plan win?

**What it is:** you settle on mountains and fields, with a pasture or two. You turn settlements into cities early, and you build development cards with what is left.

**Why it works:** a city adds a point without needing a new spot, and it doubles that corner's income. Nobody can take a city spot from you. Development cards bring Knights toward Largest Army, which needs 3 or more Knights and is worth 2 points, plus hidden Victory Point cards.

**A path to 10:** 3 cities (6 points), 1 settlement (1), Largest Army (2), and 1 Victory Point card (1). That is 6 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 10.

**The weak spot:** a slow start. With little brick and wood, you build few roads, so you can get boxed in at two or three settlements. Your early growth depends on trades. And ore is usually scarce, so other players will want it too.

**Best when:** ore and wheat are strong on this board, and you can get both on good numbers. Lesson 18 does the math on when a city beats a new settlement.

## Strategy 2: How does the wood-brick plan win?

**What it is:** you settle on forest and hills, spread out fast, and claim open spots before anyone else. Many wood-brick players chase Longest Route along the way.

**Why it works:** early in the game, brick and wood are the cards everyone wants, as [Lesson 8](/academy/catan/resource-value-over-time/) showed. Each new settlement is a point plus new income, on new numbers. A wide network also reaches more ports and more open corners.

**A path to 10:** 4 settlements (4 points), 2 cities (4), and Longest Route (2). That is 4 + 4 + 2 = 10.

**The weak spot:** every settlement also needs wheat and wool, and cities need ore. By the midgame the board fills up, and brick and wood lose value. Longest Route can change hands, too. A challenger only needs a longer route than yours to take it.

**Best when:** brick and wood are plentiful, and there is open space near your starting spots.

## Strategy 3: How does a port engine win?

**What it is:** a strong supply of one resource, fed into the 2:1 port for that resource. Say your two settlements touch an 8 forest and a 5 forest. That is 5 + 4 = 9 wood pips, or about 9 wood every 36 rolls. With the 2:1 wood port, those 9 wood become about 4 or 5 cards of whatever you need.

**Why it works:** you become your own trading partner. You do not need the table's goodwill, and you never have to feed the leader a card to get one back.

**The weak spot:** the robber. Everyone can see where your income comes from, and one robber on your big hex stalls the whole engine. Ports also sit on the coast, so port corners touch fewer hexes, and you usually need your third settlement to reach the port at all.

**Best when:** one resource is plentiful, you can grab its best hexes, and its 2:1 port is within a road or two. Lesson 20 builds one of these step by step.

## Strategy 4: How does a balanced build win?

**What it is:** you touch all five resources on decent pips and build whatever the dice hand you.

**Why it works:** you are rarely stuck. You trade less, which means you feed other players less. It shines on a flat board with no clear edge, or when the players ahead of you in the draft took every lane.

**A path to 10:** 2 settlements (2 points), 3 cities (6), and one of the two bonuses (2). That is 2 + 6 + 2 = 10.

**The weak spot:** no edge. You are average at everything. Most balanced players still need to lean into one lane by the midgame, often cities.

## Why does mixing lanes often fail?

Balanced is not the same as mixing. Balanced is a plan: spread out early, then commit. Mixing is changing your mind every few turns.

Here is what mixing costs. Say you build 3 roads toward Longest Route, then decide to rush cities instead. Those roads cost 3 brick and 3 wood. That is 6 cards that scored nothing, one more card than a whole city costs. Do that once and you have handed the table a free turn.

You can still switch when the board forces you to. If your expansion spot is taken and you are walled in, cities may be your only way forward. Just switch once, on purpose, not a little every turn.

## How do you pick your lane?

Start with your board read from [Lesson 13](/academy/catan/read-the-board/).

| If the board shows... | Lean toward... |
| --- | --- |
| Strong ore and wheat you can reach | Ore-wheat-wool |
| Plenty of brick and wood, plus open space | Wood-brick |
| One plentiful resource with its 2:1 port nearby | Port engine |
| Flat pips and no clear edge | Balanced |

Then look at the table. If two players before you settled on the big mountains, ore just got expensive, and a third ore player will fight for scraps. An empty lane is often the best lane. If nobody took the brick, the player who does becomes the one everyone has to trade with for early roads.

Your seat matters too, because the players ahead of you choose first. That is the next lesson. For more on reading the table during a game, see our guide on [how to win at Catan](/articles/how-to-win-at-catan/).

## Try it this week

Your drill: play four games, one strategy each.

1. **Assign the lane before setup.** Game one is ore-wheat-wool, game two is wood-brick, game three is a port engine, and game four is balanced. Stick to the assignment even if the board fights you. The point is to feel each lane.
2. **Place for the lane.** Use the "opening looks like" column above to choose your two spots.
3. **Write a one-line plan.** For example: "City on my 8 ore corner, then development cards."
4. **Track where your points come from.** At the end, write down how many points came from settlements, cities, bonuses, and Victory Point cards.
5. **Note one temptation.** Write down the moment you most wanted to switch lanes, and what pulled you.
6. **Compare after four games.** Which lane felt natural? Which board would have suited each lane better? Next time, let the board pick.
