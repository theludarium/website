[Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) takes the island you know and puts it in a much bigger ocean. Your home island still works the way it always has. The difference is everything past the coast: small islands, gold, open water, and a pirate. You need the [base game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) to play it, and if you need a refresher on that, the [Catan course](/academy/catan/) starts with [how a turn works](/academy/catan/how-a-turn-works/).

This lesson teaches the Seafarers rules from the official 6th edition rulebook (2025). Older boxes use some different names, but the ideas below match.

## What is in the box?

Thirty hexes: 19 sea, 2 gold fields, 2 deserts, and 7 more land hexes. Six sea frame pieces, 10 number discs, and 10 ports. Sixty ships, 15 in each player color. One pirate ship. Then a pile of scenario pieces: VP tokens, cloth tokens, pirate lair tokens, wonder tiles, and markers. Your first game needs only the ships, the pirate, the gold fields, and the VP tokens.

## How do you set up?

![A corner of a Seafarers board with land, sea, a gold field, a settlement with a road and a ship, the robber, the pirate ship, the sea frame, and the ship supply](/academy/catan-seafarers/setup.svg)

Every game of Seafarers is a scenario, and each scenario has its own map for 3 players and for 4.

1. **Build the frame.** Turn the base game's frame pieces over to their plain side, so no coastline or ports show. Combine them with the Seafarers frame pieces in the shape the scenario map shows. The rulebook suggests pressing the base pieces down onto the Seafarers pieces, not the other way around.
2. **Lay the hexes, number discs, and ports** as the map shows. Some maps mark random ports with a question mark.
3. **Place the robber and the pirate** where the map says.
4. **Place starting pieces** the way base Catan does it: one settlement each in turn order, then a second in reverse order, with your second settlement paying your starting cards. There is one new option. If a starting settlement sits on the coast, you may place a ship on a neighboring sea edge instead of a road.

The scenario also tells you where starting settlements may go. In the first scenario, for example, they must be on the main island.

## How do ships work?

A ship is a road for the water. It costs **1 wood and 1 wool**.

- Ships go on edges of sea hexes. A coastal edge, between land and sea, can hold a road or a ship, but not both.
- A new ship must connect to one of **your ships or your buildings**. It can never attach straight to a road. To turn a road network into a sea route, you need a settlement or city at the shore where they meet.
- Like roads, ships cannot pass through another player's settlement or city.
- No new ship may go on an edge of the hex the pirate sits on.

## Can you move a ship?

Yes, one ship per turn, during your Action phase. The rules are strict:

- Not a ship you built this turn.
- Only a ship with an **open end**, meaning one end touches none of your ships or buildings.
- Never a ship that is part of an unbroken line of ships between two of your buildings, even if a rival has built on that line since.
- Never to or from an edge of the pirate's hex.

Pick it up and place it anywhere a new ship could legally go.

## What do gold fields produce?

When a gold field's number is rolled, each settlement on it takes **1 resource card of its owner's choice**: brick, wood, wool, wheat, or ore. Each city on it takes 2, in any mix. In most scenarios, gold sits on islands you have to sail to.

## What does the pirate do?

When you roll a 7, the base rules still apply: big hands discard half. Then you choose: move the robber as usual, **or** move the pirate. The pirate goes to a new sea hex, or off to the frame. If it lands on a sea hex, you steal 1 random resource card from one player who has a ship on that hex.

Playing a Knight works the same way. You must move the robber or the pirate, your choice. Lesson 6 covers [when to pick which](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-and-the-robber/).

## What changes about development cards and Longest Route?

**Road Building** now builds 2 roads, 2 ships, or 1 of each, free.

**Longest Route** replaces Longest Road. It works the same way: the first player with 5 continuous roads and ships takes the tile, worth 2 VP, and a longer route takes it away. The catch: roads and ships only count as one route where they meet at **one of your settlements or cities**. A road that ends at the shore and a ship that starts beside it are two separate routes.

## How do you win?

Each scenario sets its own goal, usually a victory point total above base Catan's 10, with bonus points for settling new islands. The first scenario, [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/), asks for 14. As in base Catan, you only win during your own turn.
