In base Catan, a 7 gives you one tool. In Seafarers it gives you two, and only one can move at a time. The robber works the land. The pirate works the water. Choosing well between them is a real Seafarers skill.

## What triggers the choice?

Two things:

- **Rolling a 7.** First, as in base Catan, every player with 8 or more resource cards discards half, rounded down. Then you choose: move the robber or move the pirate.
- **Playing a Knight.** You must move the robber or the pirate, your choice. Either way, the Knight counts toward Largest Army as usual.

## What does the robber do?

Nothing new. You move it to a new land hex, steal 1 random resource card from a player with a settlement or city on that hex, and the hex produces nothing while the robber stays. Everything in the base course's [robber tactics lesson](/academy/catan/robber-tactics/) still applies on land.

## What does the pirate do?

You move the pirate to a new sea hex, or off to the frame. If it lands on a sea hex, you choose one player with a ship on that hex and steal 1 random resource card from them.

Then it stays, and while it stays it is a **blockade**:

- No one may build a new ship on any edge of its hex.
- No one may move a ship to or from any edge of its hex.

Sea hexes produce nothing, so the pirate never cuts anyone's income. Its whole power is the steal and the blockade.

## Robber or pirate: the one question

Ask: **how will the leader score their next points?**

**From production.** They are building cities on the main island, or they need one more good roll for a development card. Use the robber on their best hex. You cut income that would turn into points.

**From a voyage.** They are a ship or two from an island, and landing there scores a settlement plus any bonus the scenario offers. In [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/), that is 3 points from one settlement. Put the pirate on the hex their lead ship needs. They cannot build the next ship onto its edges, and they cannot move a ship there either. If the pirate's hex already holds one of their ships, you also get a card from them.

Leaders who are about to land are easy to spot. Count the edges between every lead ship and the nearest open settlement corner. One or two edges is a threat. The base course's [threat assessment lesson](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/) has the habit for counting points; add ships to it.

## What is each one worth?

Some simple math helps.

**The robber's value** depends on the number. A 6 or 8 comes up 5 times in 36 rolls. Put the robber on an 8 that touches two of the leader's settlements, and you deny them 2 cards each time it hits, about 0.28 cards per roll on average (5 in 36, times 2). Over a round of 4 players, that is about 1.1 cards. Their cities would make it more.

**The pirate's value** is time. A blockade lasts until someone moves the pirate again, with a 7 or a Knight. A 7 comes up 6 times in 36 rolls, so on average a 7 arrives about every 6 rolls: roughly a round and a half with 4 players. Knights shorten that. If the blocked player rolls the 7 or plays the Knight, they will move the pirate straight away, so the blockade only buys turns while it is someone else holding the next 7.

A round and a half of delay sounds small. But a blocked voyage may have to reroute, and every extra ship costs a wood and a wool.

## When to send the pirate to the frame

The rules let you move the pirate off the sea onto the frame. That lifts the blockade for everyone. Use it when the pirate is blocking your own voyage and there is no sea hex where it would hurt a rival more than it hurts you. Otherwise, a sea hex that blocks or robs someone is almost always better.

## Defending against the pirate

You cannot stop a 7, but you can make the pirate less attractive:

- **Do not leave a voyage one ship short for long.** If you can build two ships in one turn and land, do it rather than one ship a turn.
- **Keep a second route in mind.** If the pirate lands in front of you, know where your lead ship can be moved instead. [Ships or roads](/academy/catan-seafarers/ships-or-roads/) covers how that move works.
- **Hold a Knight.** A Knight lets you move the pirate on your own turn, no 7 needed.
- **Stay under 8 cards.** The discard rule still applies, and saving up for a voyage is exactly how players end up holding 9.

## Later scenarios change the pirate

Some scenarios change how these pieces work. In The Wonders of Catan, the pirate goes back in the box. In The Pirate Islands, the robber goes back in the box and the pirate gets its own movement rules. The scenario playbooks later in this course cover each one.
