Longest Route is worth 2 points in Seafarers, the same as in the base game. Ships count toward it alongside roads, which sounds simple. It is not, because of one rule about how the two join and one rule about moving ships. Together they make the tile more fluid at sea than on land. For when to chase it at all, see [Longest Route and Largest Army: chase, contest, or ignore](/academy/catan/longest-route-and-largest-army/) in the base course.

## The rules that matter

- The first player with 5 continuous roads and/or ships takes the Longest Route tile. Anyone who later has more takes it immediately.
- **Roads and ships count as one route only where they meet at one of your buildings.** A road that touches a ship at an empty intersection does not connect to it.
- Once during your turn you may move 1 ship, if one of its ends is open (not touching your other ships or your buildings). You cannot move a ship you built this turn, a ship in a continuous line of ships connecting two of your buildings, or a ship to or from an edge of the pirate's hex.

Ships cost wood and wool, roads cost brick and wood. So a sea route never asks for brick, which matters on a map where brick is scarce.

## The coastal settlement is the hinge

Because roads and ships join only at your buildings, every mixed route needs a settlement or city on the coast where land meets sea. Picture 4 roads running down to the shore, a settlement on the coast, then 3 ships heading out. With the settlement, that is one route of 7. Without it, it is a route of 4 and a separate route of 3, and neither may be enough.

This gives you a plan and a weakness.

**The plan:** if you have a road line and a ship line that almost meet, a settlement at the right coastal corner can merge them in one build. That settlement may jump you from nothing to the tile.

**The weakness:** an opponent's building on your route breaks it, at sea as on land. A rival who settles a coastal corner your ship line passes can split it in two. Lines that run along a coast past open corners are easier to cut than lines through open water.

## The free segment

Moving a ship does not change how many ships you have. It can change how many count.

Your longest route is the longest continuous path, not the total of all your pieces. A ship sitting on a short side branch adds nothing. If that ship has an open end, you can lift it and place it at the tip of your main line, as long as the new spot follows the normal placement rules. Your route grows by 1, and it cost you no cards.

The same trick works in reverse for reach. A ship that is no longer useful, say one left behind after you landed on an island, can leapfrog to the front of a new voyage. A single loose ship can keep stepping forward turn after turn, one edge per turn.

Two limits keep this honest. The ship you move must have an open end, and it cannot be part of a closed line of ships between two of your buildings. Once you land a settlement at the far end of a voyage, that whole line of ships is locked in place. Plan your moves before you land.

## When the tile is worth chasing

Run the same math as in [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/). The tile is 2 points. If your voyages to islands already build a long line, the tile comes almost free, and you should claim it. If you would need to build 3 or 4 ships just for length, that is 6 to 8 cards for 2 points that can be taken away. A bonus settlement is 3 points that cannot.

A good rule: **let your exploration build your route.** Sail toward points, and the length comes along. Build ships only for length when you are one or two segments short and the tile would decide the game.

## Contesting a sea route

You cannot remove an opponent's ships. You can only outbuild them or break their route with a building on a corner it passes. Two other levers exist:

- **The pirate.** A pirate on a hex next to a rival's line does not break the line, but it stops them from building or moving ships on that hex's edges. Parked at the tip of a growing route, it stalls the route for as long as it stays.
- **The coast.** If their route runs along a shore with legal settlement corners, a settlement of yours there cuts it.

Watch who is close to the tile, and remember that a moved ship can add 1 at no cost. A rival tied with you who has an open ship on a side branch is not tied. They are one move from taking the tile.

## When the tile is gone

Not every scenario uses it. In Cloth for Catan, the Longest Route tile goes back in the box. Check your scenario's setup before you plan around those 2 points.
