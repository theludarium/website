Most people who sit down to Seafarers already know base Catan. That is a gift for the host. You are not teaching a game. You are teaching five changes, and the table can start playing almost as soon as you finish.

The script below runs about ten minutes with the board already set up. Say it in this order. Print the [Seafarers teach sheet](/academy/catan-seafarers/teach-sheet/) so everyone has the rules in front of them.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/), the first scenario, using the fixed map for your player count. Put the robber and pirate where the map shows. Give everyone their 15 ships along with their usual pieces. Keep the VP tokens in a pile where everyone can reach them.

If anyone at the table has never played base Catan, teach that first with the [Catan setup lesson](/academy/catan/catan-setup/) and play a regular game. Seafarers on top of a brand new game is a lot at once.

## Minute 1: the goal

"This is Catan with an ocean. Everything you know still works. Today we play to 14 points instead of 10."

Point at the small islands. "Every time you build your first settlement on one of these small islands, you get 2 bonus points. Everyone can earn them, on every small island, even if someone got there first."

That second sentence matters. Players assume the bonus is a race for first, and it is not.

## Minutes 2 to 4: ships

Hold up a ship. "Ships are roads for the water. A ship costs one wood and one wool."

Then the one rule people get wrong: "A new ship has to touch one of your ships, or one of your settlements or cities. Never a road. If you want to go from a road out to sea, build a settlement on the coast first."

Show a coastal edge with your finger. "This edge, between land and sea, can take a road or a ship. Not both."

Finish with setup: "When you place your starting settlements, if you are on the coast, you can place a ship instead of a road."

## Minute 5: moving ships

"Once per turn you may move one ship. It has to be a ship with an open end, sticking out into the water. You cannot move a ship you just built, and once a line of ships connects two of your buildings, it is locked."

Keep it that short. The pirate restriction comes in a minute.

## Minute 6: gold fields

Point at a gold field. "Gold. When this number comes up, a settlement here takes any one resource you want. A city takes two, any mix."

Then point out where the gold sits. In Heading for New Shores, both gold fields are on small islands, so you have to sail to them.

## Minutes 7 and 8: the pirate

Hold up the pirate. "When you roll a 7, the robber works the same as always. But you can choose to move the pirate instead. The pirate goes on a sea hex, and you steal one card from someone with a ship touching that hex."

"Knights work the same: you choose robber or pirate."

"While the pirate sits on a hex, nobody can build ships on its edges or move ships to or from them. So it is a blockade."

That last line is what makes the pirate interesting, so say it clearly.

## Minute 9: the small changes

Two quick ones:

- "Road Building gives you two roads, two ships, or one of each."
- "Longest Road is now Longest Route. Roads and ships both count, but they only join into one route at one of your settlements or cities."

## Minute 10: what to watch for

Give the table one piece of advice, no more: "Settle on the coast if you can. Ships can only start from the shore at one of your buildings, so an inland start leaves you a long way from the sea."

Then start setup placement. If you want to go deeper on placement later, the [base Catan opening checklist](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/) still applies on the main island.

## Questions you will hear mid-game

- **"Can I build a ship off my road?"** No. Off a ship or a building only.
- **"Can I move the ship I just built?"** Not this turn.
- **"Can I steal with the pirate if nobody has a ship there?"** You can move it there, but there is no one to steal from.
- **"Did I win?"** Only on your own turn, at 14 or more.

Keep the rulebook closed unless a question stumps the sheet. The table learns faster by playing.
