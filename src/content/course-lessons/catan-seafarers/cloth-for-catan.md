Cloth for Catan changes what a point looks like. Much of your score can come from small islands you may not settle. You reach them with ships, open trade, and collect cloth when their number rolls. The catch: the cloth runs out, and when enough runs out, the game stops.

## Setup and special rules, in short

The map has two large islands and four small ones. The small islands carry eight number discs, and each disc is a **village** that produces cloth. Each village starts with 5 cloth tokens beside it, and 10 more form a general supply. The Longest Route tile goes back in the box.

You start with **three settlements**, not two. The first two go down as in base Catan but pay nothing. Then everyone places a third settlement with a road or ship, clockwise from the first player, and takes starting resources from it only. Your starts can sit on one large island or both.

The rest of the rules:

- **You may not build settlements on the small islands.** They are for trade only.
- **Opening trade:** when your route of roads and ships connects one of your buildings to a village, you take 1 cloth from that village at once.
- **Production:** when a village's number rolls, every player trading with it takes 1 cloth from its stack. If the stack is short, the extra comes from the general supply. A village with no cloth left produces nothing.
- **Scoring:** each pair of cloth is worth 1 point. A single leftover cloth is worth nothing.
- **Closed routes:** ships on a route that links your building to a village cannot be moved. You can still extend or branch from that route.
- **Robber and pirate:** the robber may not go to the small islands. You may not move the pirate until you have at least one route to a village. When you do move it, you steal either a resource or a cloth from a player with a ship on that hex.

## How the game ends

There are two endings. Reach **14 points** on your turn and you win. Or, if **5 or more villages have no cloth** at the end of a turn, the player with the most points wins, and a tie goes to the player with more cloth.

Eight villages, five empty, game over. Every cloth you take is also a tick of the clock.

## What the map rewards

A village pays everyone connected to it. That makes the numbers matter twice: a high number pays often, and it drains fast.

Count it out. A village on an 8 rolls on 5 of 36 rolls, about once every 7. The first player to connect takes 1 cloth, leaving 4. With one trader, four more 8s empty it, which takes about 29 rolls, or roughly 7 rounds at four players. Add a second trader and each 8 removes 2 cloth, so it empties in about half the time. A village on a 3 or 11 rolls on 2 of 36, once every 18 rolls, and can sit nearly full for most of the game.

So a crowded 6 or 8 village pays well but briefly, and a quiet 4 or 10 village that only you trade with can be the better long-term income. See [the dots lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/).

## Opening plan

1. **Spend your first two picks on production, your third on reach.** The third settlement sets your starting hand and goes in clockwise order, so the first player gets two picks in a row (the last of round two and the first of round three). Use that pair to take a strong spot and a coastal spot facing the best villages.
2. **Produce wood and wool.** Every ship costs one of each, and every village is across water. See [ships or roads](/academy/catan-seafarers/ships-or-roads/).
3. **Aim at two villages.** A route near both lets a short branch open the second.

## Midgame plan

A closed route's ships are fixed, so the pirate can park beside them. Keep your cloth count even: an odd cloth is worth nothing to you.

That cuts both ways. Taking 1 cloth from a player with an even count costs them a full point, and if your own count is odd, it completes a pair for you. Look for that two point swing.

Meanwhile, build cities. Cloth tops out quickly, and 14 points is a lot.

## Endgame: run the clock

When four villages are empty, the next one ends the game, so ask before every turn: am I ahead right now?

If you lead, you want the clock to finish. Opening trade with a village that has 1 cloth left takes that cloth at once and can empty the fifth village on your own turn, ending the game at the end of that turn. If you trail, avoid draining villages, push for points that do not touch the cloth stacks, and remember that ties go to the player with more cloth. <!-- VERIFY: confirm with the rulebook that taking a village's last cloth by opening trade counts toward the five empty villages at the end of that same turn; the rulebook says the check happens "at the end of the turn" and does not single out this case. -->

## The trap to avoid

Chasing cloth and forgetting the clock. The cloth leader often helps empty the villages, then loses the timed ending to someone with two more cities. Cloth is half a point per token; a city is a full point for five cards and keeps paying. Use cloth to pull ahead, and make the clock end when you lead.
