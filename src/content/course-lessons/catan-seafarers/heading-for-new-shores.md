The rulebook calls Seafarers a campaign, and Heading for New Shores is chapter one. It is the gentlest map in the box: a familiar island in the middle, three small islands out in the water, and one new reason to sail. If you have played base Catan, you already know how to play most of it.

This lesson follows the [6th edition rulebook](/academy/catan-seafarers/how-to-play-seafarers/). Play the fixed map first; the rulebook recommends it because it is balanced.

## What does the board look like?

There are two fixed maps, one for 3 players and one for 4. Both have a main island and three small islands, outlined on the rulebook map.

- **4 players:** the main island is the classic 19-hex Catan island, desert in the middle, with all 9 ports around it. Three small islands sit across the water: one of 2 hexes, one of 5, and one of 2. Both gold fields are on the 5-hex island.
- **3 players:** the main island is smaller, 14 hexes with no desert, and all 8 ports. The three small islands hold the other 8 land hexes, with one gold field on a 2-hex island and the other on a 4-hex island.

The robber starts on the desert with 4 players. With 3, there is no desert, so it starts on the hex with the 12. The pirate starts out at sea, where the map shows it.

## What are the special rules?

Only two.

**Start on the main island.** Both starting settlements, with their road or ship, go on the main island.

**Settle the small islands for bonus points.** Each time you build your first settlement on a small island, take 2 VP tokens and tuck them under that settlement. You can earn this bonus once on each of the three small islands. It does not matter if someone else got there first. Everyone who settles an island gets its 2 points.

## How do you win?

Reach 14 or more victory points during your own turn.

Fourteen is 4 more than base Catan. The island bonus is worth up to 6: three islands at 2 points each. So the scenario is built around a simple trade. You can try to squeeze 14 points out of the main island like a long game of base Catan, or you can sail. Two islands give you 4 bonus points, which is exactly the gap between 10 and 14.

## What does the map reward?

**A settlement on a new island scores 3 points.** One for the settlement and 2 for the bonus. On the main island, the same settlement scores 1. That is the whole reason to build ships. The cost is the ships it takes to get there, at 1 wood and 1 wool each.

**Gold sits offshore.** On both fixed maps the gold fields are on small islands. A settlement on gold turns its rolls into whatever you are short of. With 4 players, both gold numbers are 3-dot numbers (a 4 and a 10); with 3 players, one is a 5, a 4-dot number. If dots are new to you, the [Catan number probability lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/) explains them.

**Wood matters twice.** Roads cost brick and wood. Ships cost wood and wool. Every voyage leans on your forests. In the 3-player fixed map, all three forests are on the main island, so they are worth fighting for during setup.

**The main island is still crowded.** With 4 players, 19 hexes and 8 starting settlements fill up fast. The islands are where the room is.

## A simple plan for your first game

**Setup.** Put one settlement on a strong spot on the main island, using the usual [opening checklist](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/). Put the other on the coast facing the island you want, with a ship instead of a road. Try to touch a forest and a pasture between the two, since those make ships.

**Early game.** Build toward the closest island first. Count the edges: every one is a ship. A settlement 3 ships away costs 3 wood and 3 wool for the ships, plus the settlement itself.

**Middle game.** Once you land, take the 2 points and look for the next island. Cities on your main island production will carry you the rest of the way.

**Late game.** Count everyone's points, bonus tokens included. A player one settlement away from 14 may only need to reach one more island. That is where the [pirate](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-and-the-robber/) earns its keep.

## The variable setup

After a game or two, try the variable setup. The main island's hexes, ports, and number discs are placed at random, with no two red numbers (6 and 8) next to each other. The small islands' hexes and numbers are shuffled into their outlined areas. The robber starts on the 12 with 3 players and on the desert with 4. The islands stay where they are, so the plan above still works.
