Everything you know about base Catan openings still holds. Pips are production, production is tempo, and a bad number costs you every turn of the game. If you need a refresher, start with [the five-point opening checklist](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/) and [number probability in plain English](/academy/catan/number-probability/).

Seafarers adds one question that the base game never asks: where does your fleet start? The answer shapes the whole game, so it belongs in your opening, not in turn ten.

## The launch rule that changes placement

A new ship must connect to one of your ships or to one of your buildings. It cannot connect to a road. So a road that runs down to the shore does nothing for your fleet. Only a settlement or city on the coast can launch ships.

That has a hard consequence. If both of your starting settlements are inland, your first ship waits until you have built a road to the coast and a new coastal settlement. That is two roads and a settlement, eight cards, before the first wood and wool go into a hull. Players who drift inland for the best numbers often find the islands claimed before they can sail.

The rulebook gives you a shortcut: if you place a starting settlement on the coast, you may place a ship on an adjacent empty sea edge instead of a road. A starting ship is a free first step toward an island. See [ships or roads](/academy/catan-seafarers/ships-or-roads/) for the general case.

## Wood and wool go up in value

Every ship costs one wood and one wool. Every settlement still costs brick, wood, wool, and wheat. So a player who plans to explore spends wood and wool at almost double the base game rate.

Two habits follow:

- **Count wood and wool pips separately.** A start with 7 or more pips across forest and pasture can feed a fleet. A start with 3 is planning to trade for every ship.
- **A wool port is no longer a weak port.** In the base game, wool often piles up. In Seafarers, wool has a job, and a 2:1 wool port turns spare wool into everything else.

Brick matters less than it does in base Catan once your fleet is moving, because ships do not use it. But you still need brick for every settlement you land, so do not leave yourself with none.

## Count the sea before you place

Here is the habit that separates Seafarers players from base game players with boats. For each coastal spot you are considering, count:

1. The sea edges from that spot to the nearest legal settlement spot on another island. Remember the distance rule still applies on the far shore.
2. Whether the pirate sits on that path. You cannot place a ship on an edge of the pirate's hex, so a pirate in the channel adds a turn or two of waiting or a detour.
3. Who else is pointed the same way. Two fleets aimed at the same small island means a race for its best corner.

Then turn the count into cards:

| Ships to the far shore | Ship cards | Plus the settlement | Total cards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | 6 | 4 | 10 |
| 4 | 8 | 4 | 12 |
| 5 | 10 | 4 | 14 |

A settlement on the main island usually costs two roads plus the settlement: 8 cards. So a two-ship island costs the same as a home expansion, and every extra ship adds 2 cards. Whether the extra cards are worth it depends on what the island pays: its numbers, any gold, and in many scenarios a bonus for landing there. That is the subject of [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/).

## Two shapes of a good start

Most strong Seafarers openings fall into one of two shapes.

**The anchor and the harbor.** Your first settlement goes on the best production you can find, coast or not. Your second goes on the coast facing your target island, with a starting ship pointing at it. The anchor pays for the voyage; the harbor launches it. This is the default, and it suits scenarios where you must start on a main island and the bonuses lie offshore.

**The double harbor.** Both settlements touch the sea, ideally facing different islands. You give up a few pips for two launch points and more choices later. This shape shines when islands are close and many, and when the main island is crowded at 4 players.

What rarely works is the inland fortress: two rich settlements in the middle of the main island and no coast. It produces well for five turns, then watches the rest of the table sail away.

## Road or ship for your starting piece?

If your settlement is on the coast, you choose. A starting road claims a land expansion spot and blocks a neighbor. A starting ship claims a sea lane and saves you 2 cards on the voyage. Ask which race is tighter. On a crowded main island with 4 players, the road may matter more. When the nearest island is two edges away and nobody else is close, the ship is a head start you will feel by turn five.
