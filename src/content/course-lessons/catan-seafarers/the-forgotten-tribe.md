The Forgotten Tribe opens the second half of the Seafarers book, where each scenario adds rules of its own. It turns the usual plan around. You still sail to the islands, but you cannot settle them. What the tribe offers sits on the water instead: victory points, development cards, and ports, waiting for the first ship to reach them.

## Setup and special rules

- **The map.** One large island in the center holds every number disc. Around it, small islands of the forgotten tribe carry no numbers. The same map serves 3 and 4 players.
- **Prizes on the edges.** 8 victory point tokens go on marked sea edges near the outer islands. The top 4 cards of the development card deck go face down on other marked edges, unseen. The ports, 5 of them 2:1 and one 3:1, go face up on marked edges at random. The large island has no ports of its own at the start.
- **Where you start.** Both starting settlements, with their road or ship, go on the large island.
- **Settling.** You may build settlements only on hexes with a number disc, which means only on the large island.
- **The robber** starts on a desert, which has no number disc. After that it may only move to a hex with a number disc, by a 7 or a Knight.
- **Collecting.** When you build or move a ship onto an edge with a prize, you take it:
  - A **victory point token** is worth 1 point.
  - A **development card** is yours as if you bought it this turn. You can play it on a later turn, or reveal a victory point card at once to win.
  - A **port** goes face up next to one of your coastal settlements, with at least one empty edge between it and any other port. If you have no legal place, you keep it in front of you until you do. You can use it as soon as it is placed.

## How you win

The first player to have 13 or more victory points during their turn wins.

## What the map rewards

Every settlement in the game must go on the 18 numbered hexes of the large island. At 4 players, that land fills quickly. So the sea is where the extra points live:

| Source | Points available | How you get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Victory point tokens | 8 in total | Ships |
| Development cards on edges | 4 cards; a few may be points | Ships |
| Longest Route | 2 | Roads and ships |
| Better trades | Indirect | Ports from the sea |

In the base development deck, 5 of the 25 cards are victory points, so each card on an edge has about a 1 in 5 chance of being a point. Knights are far more likely.

The prizes reward a fleet that moves well. As covered in [the longest route at sea](/academy/catan-seafarers/longest-route-at-sea/), you may move one ship with an open end each turn. Moving onto a prize edge collects it. So a single free ship at the end of your line can step forward one edge per turn, collecting as it goes, without costing a card.

## Opening plan

1. **Settle strong numbers on the large island first.** The land is scarce. Two good settlements now are worth more than they are in most scenarios, because you may not get many more.
2. **Make one of them coastal, facing the nearest prizes,** with a ship as its starting piece. Count the sea edges to the nearest token and the nearest port.
3. **Favor wood and wool.** Every prize is reached by ship. A start with no wood or no wool will trade for every hull.
4. **Know your port target.** Five of the six ports are 2:1. Look at which 2:1 port matches your best hex, and make it your first voyage. A port grabbed early pays for every turn after it.

## Midgame plan

- **Build a short line, then hop.** Sail just far enough to reach the ring of prizes, then use your free ship move every turn to reach the next one. A ship move is free; a ship built is 2 cards.
- **Keep the hopping ship loose.** Do not settle at both ends of a line you still want to move. A line of ships between two of your buildings is locked.
- **Take the ports that fit your production.** Plan a coastal spot with room for a port before you collect one, or it will wait in front of you.
- **Count the tokens.** There are only 8. When a rival has 3 or 4 and you have none, they are well ahead of what their buildings show. See [threat assessment](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/).
- **Use the pirate.** Parking the pirate beside the next prize a rival is reaching for stops their ships from moving or building onto those edges.

## The trap

**Sailing for land out of habit.** Earlier scenarios teach you that a voyage ends with a settlement and a bonus. Here the outer islands cannot be settled at all. A long fleet aimed at a big outer island earns whatever prizes lie on the way, and nothing more.

The quieter trap is building for length instead of reach. A line of ships locked between two of your buildings can no longer move to collect anything. Build lines that end in open water, keep a ship free, and let the prizes, not the length, decide where you sail.
