Your first games of Seafarers are where the table builds its habits. Some mistakes are rules slips, carried over from base Catan without anyone noticing. Others are legal plays that quietly lose the game. This lesson covers both, checked against the 6th edition rulebook.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Building a ship off a road.** The most common one. A new ship must connect to one of your ships or one of your settlements or cities. A road that runs down to the beach does not count. Build a settlement on the coast, then sail from it.

**Putting a road and a ship on the same edge.** A coastal edge, between land and sea, holds one or the other. Not both.

**Moving a ship you just built.** A ship cannot move on the turn it was built. Build it this turn, move it next turn if you still want to.

**Moving a locked ship.** Only a ship with an open end can move. And once a line of ships connects two of your buildings, every ship in it is fixed for good, even if a rival later builds a settlement on that line.

**Moving more than one ship.** One ship per turn, total.

**Ignoring the pirate's blockade.** Nobody can build a new ship on an edge of the pirate's hex, or move a ship to or from one. Players remember the steal and forget the blockade.

**Short-changing gold.** A settlement on a gold field takes 1 resource of the owner's choice. A city takes 2, and they do not have to match.

**Thinking the island bonus is a race.** In [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/), your first settlement on each small island earns 2 VP even if another player settled there first. Nobody is shut out of the bonus by arriving second.

**Joining roads and ships through nothing.** For Longest Route, roads and ships only connect at one of your settlements or cities. A road and a ship that touch at an empty corner are two routes.

**Forgetting Road Building can make ships.** It builds 2 roads, 2 ships, or 1 of each. Late in a voyage, two free ships can be a whole island.

**Winning on someone else's turn.** The scenario goal only counts during your own turn, just as in base Catan.

## The big strategic mistake: sailing too late

New players treat the sea like an afterthought. They build the main island first, the way they would in base Catan, and only start ships once the land runs out. By then the best island spots are gone, and the bonus points they needed are many turns away.

Do the math in Heading for New Shores. Fourteen points is 4 more than base Catan, and two island bonuses are worth exactly 4. A player who never leaves the main island has to find those points somewhere else, usually by building far more than everyone else.

The fix: decide during setup which island you are heading for, and build your first ship in your first few turns. Even one ship on the water is a claim that tells the table where you are going.

## Ship chains to nowhere

The opposite mistake is building ships because they are fun. A long line of ships that does not reach a settlement spot scores nothing, except possibly Longest Route. Before each ship, ask where it ends: which corner of which island will hold your settlement, and how many more ships until you get there.

## Running out of wood

Roads and ships both need wood. A player with one forest who wants to build a road network and a fleet will stall. Wool matters more than in base Catan too, because every ship needs one. Look at your wood and wool numbers during setup, the same way you would check brick and wood for an early road game. The [base Catan lesson on resource value](/academy/catan/resource-value-over-time/) explains why early building resources matter so much.

## Landing without a plan

Reaching an island is not the finish line. Where you land matters. Land on a corner that touches good numbers, or a gold field, and the settlement keeps paying for the rest of the game. Land on the first empty corner your ship reaches, and you get the bonus but little production.

## Forgetting the pirate

New players often keep moving the robber because it is familiar. The pirate is the only way to stop a rival's voyage. When someone is two ships from an island, a 7 or a Knight on the pirate can hold them up until the next 7 or Knight moves it. [Lesson 6](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-and-the-robber/) covers when to choose it.

## A checklist for game three

At the start of each of your turns, ask:

1. Which island am I heading for, and how many ships away is it?
2. Do I have the wood and wool to keep sailing?
3. Who is closest to landing on a new island, and can the pirate stop them?
