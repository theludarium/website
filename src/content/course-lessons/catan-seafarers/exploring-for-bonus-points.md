In most Seafarers scenarios, the first settlement you build on each new island earns a bonus of 2 victory points, marked by tokens you slide under the settlement. That bonus, more than gold or ships, is what pulls the game out to sea. This lesson does the math on it and fixes the most common misreading.

## The bonus, scenario by scenario

The idea is the same each time, but the details change:

| Scenario | Where the bonus is | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heading for New Shores | Each small island | 14 points |
| The Four Islands | Each island that is not one of your home islands | 13 points |
| Through the Desert | Each unexplored region, including the land strip past the desert | 14 points |
| The Fog Islands | No island bonus; discovery pays a resource card instead | 12 points |
| The Forgotten Tribe | No island bonus; tokens sit on sea edges for your ships | 13 points |

The rules for each live in the scenario playbooks in Level 3. For now, the bonus itself.

## It is not a race to be first

Read the rule closely: you earn the 2 points for your own first settlement on each island, and it does not matter if other players have already built there. So the bonus is not a prize for the first explorer. Every player can earn it on every island that counts as new for them.

That changes how you should feel when a rival lands on "your" island first. You have not lost the bonus. You have lost a corner. The real race is for the best intersection on the island, and on a small island with a single good number, that race is tight. On an island with room for two or three settlements, there is no hurry at all.

## The math: points per card

A settlement on a new island scores 1 point for the settlement and 2 for the bonus: 3 points. Here is what it costs, using the ship counts from [Seafarers openings](/academy/catan-seafarers/seafarers-openings/):

| Ships needed | Total cards | Cards per point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 8 | 2.7 |
| 3 | 10 | 3.3 |
| 4 | 12 | 4.0 |
| 5 | 14 | 4.7 |

Compare the base game ways to score:

| Build | Cards | Points | Cards per point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Settlement with two roads | 8 | 1 | 8.0 |
| City | 5 | 1 | 5.0 |
| Development card (if it is a point) | 3 | 0 to 1 | 3.0 at best, usually far worse |

Even a five-ship voyage scores more per card than a city, and a new settlement also produces resources for the rest of the game. That is why strong Seafarers players treat each reachable bonus as the first item on their shopping list. It is also why 14 points in Heading for New Shores is not as far away as it sounds: two bonuses are 4 points on top of normal building.

## The settlement cap

You have 5 settlements. Two are on the board from the start, so you can place only three more before you must upgrade one to a city to free a piece. If you plan to land on three new islands, you need every settlement you own for them. Building a fourth settlement on the main island early can leave you with no piece when your ship arrives. Plan the islands first, and fill home spots only with what is left.

## When to sail

Exploring too early starves your production. Exploring too late leaves you landing on the worst corner. A rough timing guide:

- **Sail as soon as one voyage is fully funded** from your production in a few turns, usually after your first or second build at home.
- **Do not leave a half-built line of ships sitting in the sea** for many turns. Ships earn nothing until they land, and a pirate placed beside your line stops you from extending it.
- **Land on production, not on scenery.** A bonus settlement on a 2 and a 12 still scores 3 points, but it gives you almost no cards. If the island has a better corner and you can reach it with one more ship, that ship is usually worth it.
- **Count the end.** When you are 3 or fewer points from winning, one bonus settlement can end the game on your turn. Opponents who can see that will put the pirate in your path. Keep a second target ready.

## What your opponents are counting

Every bonus is public: the tokens sit under the settlement. So the table can see who has collected what, and who still has easy points offshore. If one player has two islands left within three ships each, they may be closer to winning than their score shows. Use the [threat assessment](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/) habit from the base course, and add a line for "bonuses in reach."
