In base Catan, a road is the only way to reach new land. Seafarers adds a second way, and a choice you will face every few turns. The answer comes from where you are going and what you produce.

## What does each one cost?

| | Road | Ship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | 1 brick, 1 wood | 1 wood, 1 wool |
| Goes on | Land edges and coastal edges | Sea edges and coastal edges |
| Connects to | Your roads and buildings | Your ships and buildings |
| Can move? | Never | One per turn, with limits |
| Pieces | Your usual roads | 15 ships |

Both cost wood. So the real choice is between spending a brick and spending a wool. A player rich in pastures and short on hills should lean toward ships where the map allows it. A player with two hills and one pasture may want to reach the coast by road and save wool for the water.

## Where you are going decides most of it

Roads cannot cross water and ships cannot cross land. So for most builds, the map makes the choice for you.

**The target is on your island.** Build roads. Nothing else gets there.

**The target is across water.** Build ships. You will need a settlement or city on the coast to sail from, because a ship can never attach to a road.

**The edge is coastal.** Here you have a real choice, because a coastal edge, between land and sea, holds either one. Ask what comes next. If the line continues along the land, a road keeps it connected to your road network. If it heads out to sea, a ship is the start of a voyage, as long as it touches one of your buildings or ships.

## The switch point is a building

A road network and a fleet only join at one of your settlements or cities. That matters twice.

First, for building: to turn a road into a voyage, you need a coastal settlement at the end of the road. Plan that corner before you build toward it.

Second, for [Longest Route](/academy/catan-seafarers/how-to-play-seafarers/): your roads and ships only count as one route through that same building. A coastal settlement where a 3-road line meets a 3-ship line gives you a 6-segment route. Without the settlement, you have two routes of 3, and neither one qualifies for the tile, which needs 5.

## What moving a ship really does

You may move one ship per turn. It must have an open end, it cannot be a ship you built this turn, and it must be placed somewhere a new ship could legally go. A legal spot means it connects to one of your ships or buildings. So if you take the lead ship off a line and put it back at the front, the line is the same length it was. Moving does not add distance.

What moving does add is **direction**. Use it to:

- **Change course.** Your lead ship points at an island a rival just settled. Swing it toward a different one, for free.
- **Steer around a blockade.** If the pirate sits on the hex ahead, you cannot build on its edges. A lead ship that is not on one of those edges can be moved to head another way. A ship already on the pirate's hex is stuck until the pirate leaves.
- **Shift a ship to another line.** A spare ship can join a voyage that needs it, as long as its new spot connects to your pieces.

Roads offer none of this.

## When a ship line freezes

Once a line of ships connects two of your buildings, none of its ships can move again. That happens the moment you settle at the far end of a voyage. From then on the line is as permanent as a road, so use its flexibility before you land.

## The costs of going to sea

Ships carry two risks roads do not.

**The pirate.** A player who moves the pirate onto a sea hex can steal from anyone with a ship on that hex, and while it sits there, no one can build or move ships on its edges. A long voyage through open water gives rivals several hexes to block. Roads on land only ever face the robber, which steals from players with buildings on its hex.

**Distance.** Count the edges before you start. An island 4 ships away costs 4 wood and 4 wool before the settlement, which itself costs a brick, a wood, a wool, and a wheat. That is 5 wood and 5 wool in total. If you produce wood on one 3-dot number, you collect about 1 wood every 12 rolls on average (3 chances in 36). The [Catan number probability lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/) shows the math.

## A quick rule of thumb

1. Build toward a settlement spot you can name.
2. If the spot is on land, use roads. If it is across water, use ships, launched from a coastal building.
3. On a coastal edge, choose by what comes after it and by whether you have brick or wool to spare.
4. Use your one ship move to fix direction, not to gain distance.

The [base Catan lesson on expansion planning](/academy/catan/expansion-planning/) has more on claiming a spot before you need it, and all of it applies at sea.
