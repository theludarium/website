Every scenario so far came with a map. The New World variant hands you the hexes and lets the table build its own. No one has seen the board before, no one has a favorite opening, and every game asks the same question fresh: where is the value, and how far away is it? This is the Admiral level because it tests everything at once.

## Setup and special rules, in short

For 3 or 4 players, the New World uses 42 hexes: 19 sea and 23 land (4 hills, 5 forests, 5 pastures, 5 fields, 4 mountains). The listed setup has no gold fields and no deserts. You lay all 42 face up at random inside the frame, and the table may agree to shift hexes to change how many islands there are or how big they get.

Then the number discs go on the land at random. If two red numbers (6 and 8) land side by side, swap one with a black number next to it until every red is separated.

Then **players take turns placing a random port** face up, on an edge between land and sea or between land and the frame, at least one edge away from every other port. The robber and pirate go where the rulebook map shows.

Your starting settlements may go on one island or two. Those are your **home islands**; every other island is unexplored for you. The first time you settle each unexplored island, you earn **1 bonus point**. To win, reach **12 points** on your turn.

The rulebook also lets you change the number and type of hexes freely, with one note: gold fields should not get red numbers.

## Step 1: count the islands

Random placement makes islands of every size, from a lone hex to a long chain. Count them. Each island beyond your home islands is a possible bonus point, and 12 points is two more than base Catan, so the bonuses are part of the plan, not a side quest. See [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/) for the timing.

## Step 2: total the pips per island

The 23 number discs carry 74 pips in all (the dots under each number; see [the dots lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/)). That is about 3.2 per land hex. Add them up island by island. A big island with average numbers is a home. A small island with a 6 or 8 on it is a prize worth a ship trip, because you will collect both the bonus point and a strong number.

Then total pips by resource, as you would in [reading any Catan board](/academy/catan/read-the-board/). With only 4 hills and 4 mountains, brick and ore are likely to be scarce, and one unlucky draw can make either one very scarce. Name the scarcest resource before you place.

## Step 3: measure distance in ships

Water is the real terrain of Seafarers. From each strong corner, count the ships it would take to reach the nearest other island. Two ships away is a near neighbor. Five ships is ten resources before you can even build the settlement. The best home corner is one with good numbers that also sits close to two different islands, so your first ships open two options. The [ships or roads lesson](/academy/catan-seafarers/ships-or-roads/) has the cost math.

## Step 4: place ports with purpose

Port placement is a draft, and most players waste it. Each turn you place a random port token, so you cannot pick its type, but you can pick its edge. Two good uses:

- **A 2:1 port beside a corner you plan to settle, when the token matches a resource you will produce a lot of.** Your surplus becomes whatever you lack.
- **A 3:1 port where your second or third settlement will go,** so a lopsided hand always has an outlet.

And one defensive use: if you draw a 2:1 port that would be perfect for the player to your left, put it somewhere quiet.

## Step 5: choose your home islands

This is the decision unique to the New World. Splitting your starts across two islands makes both of them home, which removes one island from your possible bonuses. Starting both settlements on one island keeps every other island worth a point to you, but may cost you production.

Do the math simply. If the second island's corner is clearly better, by two or more pips or a scarce resource, split. If the corners are close, stay on one island and keep the point open. Remember the bonus is per island, not per first player there, so a crowded island is still worth a point to you.

## The trap to avoid

Playing the New World like a base game map. A strong landlocked start in the middle of a big island can be the worst seat at the table if every ship trip is five edges long. On a random map, the corner that reaches the most islands quickly often beats the corner with the most pips.
