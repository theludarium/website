Gold fields are the hex everyone points at during setup. They glitter on the board, they sit on far islands, and they produce something no other hex does: a card of your choice. This lesson puts a number on that glitter so you can tell a good gold field from a pretty one.

## The rule

When a gold field's number is rolled, each settlement on it pays its owner 1 resource card of their choice: brick, wood, wool, wheat, or ore. Each city on it pays 2 resource cards in any combination. So a city on gold can take an ore and a wheat in one roll, or two of the same.

The robber works on gold the way it works on any land hex: a gold field under the robber pays nothing.

## What a card of your choice is worth

A normal card is worth exactly one of its type. A gold card is worth one of whatever you need most. The gap between those two depends on how you would otherwise get that card.

| How you would get the missing card | Cards it costs you | What a gold card saves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You already produce it | 1 | Nothing extra |
| A 2:1 port of the right type | 2 | 1 card |
| A 3:1 port | 3 | 2 cards |
| The bank at 4:1 | 4 | 3 cards |
| A trade with a player | 1 plus a concession | The concession, and no help to a rival |

So a gold card is worth between 1 and 4 ordinary cards. For a player with no port and a hole in their production, each gold card is close to the top of that range. For a player who already makes every resource, gold is just production.

That is the first rule of gold: **gold is worth most to the player whose production has a gap.** If your start has no ore, a gold field is your ore field, plus a backup for everything else.

## Count the pips

Gold produces on its number like any hex. Out of every 36 rolls, on average:

| Number | Rolls that pay | Gold cards per 36 rolls, settlement | City |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 or 12 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 3 or 11 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| 4 or 10 | 3 | 3 | 6 |
| 5 or 9 | 4 | 4 | 8 |

Scenario designers know gold is strong, so they tend to keep it off the best numbers. In the 4-player fixed map of Heading for New Shores, both gold fields sit on small islands, on a 4 and a 10. The variable setup for Through the Desert says outright: no red numbers (6 or 8) on gold fields.

Run the comparison. A settlement on a 4 gold earns about 3 cards per 36 rolls. A settlement on a 6 wheat earns about 5. If each gold card saves you two cards of trading, the gold settlement is worth about 3 plus 6, or 9 cards of buying power, against the wheat's 5. If you already make everything, the wheat wins. That is why the same gold field can be the best spot for one player and a trap for another.

## A city on gold

A city doubles any hex, but on gold it does something extra: it lets you pick two different cards at once. A city on a 5 gold makes about 8 cards of your choice per 36 rolls. Those cards round out whatever your other hexes give you, so your builds stop waiting on one missing card.

Cities cost 2 wheat and 3 ore. A gold field can pay part of its own upgrade, which makes the gold city one of the cleanest snowballs in Seafarers. When you have one settlement on gold and the ore and wheat for a city, upgrading the gold settlement first is usually right. The production math behind city timing is in [cities first?](/academy/catan/cities-first/)

## How to take a gold card

You pick your gold card when it is produced, before you see what you will draw in trades. Pick for the next build, not for the rarest card on the table. Rare cards help only if you can use them or trade them, and a card you hold through a 7 can be lost.

A good order of questions:

1. What one card would complete a build this turn? Take it.
2. If none, what card would complete a build next turn? Take it.
3. If none, take the card no one at the table will trade you.

## Gold draws fire

Everyone can see who sits on gold. Expect the robber there, especially if you are leading. If your whole plan is one gold field, one robber parked on it can stall you for several turns. A second source of the resources you need is insurance. See [the pirate and the robber](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-and-the-robber/) for both thieves.

## Not every scenario has working gold

The Four Islands has no gold fields at all. In The Fog Islands, the gold fields are hidden in the fog stack, so nobody knows where they will appear. In The Forgotten Tribe, the gold fields sit on the outer islands, which carry no number discs and cannot be settled, so they never produce. Read the component list for your scenario before you plan around gold.
