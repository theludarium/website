Through the Desert is the fourth scenario in the Seafarers book and the last of the simpler ones. A big island is split by a desert. On one side lies the main island, where everyone starts. On the other side lies a thin strip of land you can reach on foot, if you are willing to build roads across the sand. Out to sea, small islands wait for your ships.

## Setup and special rules

- **The map.** One large island with a band of 3 desert hexes cutting across it. The larger part is the main island. The smaller part, past the desert, is the land strip. Small islands sit offshore. The robber starts in the desert in the fixed maps.
- **Where you start.** Both starting settlements, with their road or ship, go on the main island.
- **Unexplored regions.** The land strip and each small island are unexplored regions. The first time you build a settlement in each one, you take 2 bonus points. It does not matter if other players already built there.
- **Variable setup** keeps the deserts where the map shows them, shuffles the main island separately from the unexplored regions, and keeps red numbers (6 and 8) apart and off the gold fields.

## How you win

The first player to have 14 or more victory points during their turn wins.

## What the map rewards

In both the 3- and 4-player fixed maps, there are four unexplored regions: the land strip and three small islands. That is up to 8 bonus points per player, more than half of the 14 you need. The scenario's own supply backs this up: it provides exactly 2 tokens for every player in every region.

The gold fields sit out in the unexplored regions in the fixed maps, not on the main island, so they reward whoever travels.

The big idea is the **two kinds of travel**:

| Route | Pieces | Cost per step | Reaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overland, across the desert | Roads | Brick and wood | The land strip |
| By sea | Ships | Wood and wool | The small islands, and the strip's coast |

A desert hex produces nothing, but its edges are land, so roads can run along them. The desert is a road you pay for with no hexes to farm along the way. It is still a short, safe route: the pirate cannot block a road, and the robber only stops production.

So a player rich in brick should look hard at the strip. A player rich in wool should look at the islands. Both earn the same 2 bonus points per region.

## Opening plan

1. **Take strong production on the main island first.** It is the only land you can start on, and in a 4-player game it fills fast. Use [the five-point opening checklist](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/).
2. **Choose your direction with your second settlement.** A spot at the edge of the desert points you at the strip. A spot on the east coast, facing the small islands, points you out to sea. A spot that does both is rare and worth a few pips.
3. **Match the route to your resources.** Brick and wood in your start: plan the desert crossing. Wood and wool: take a ship as your starting piece and count sea edges to the nearest island.
4. **Note where the robber starts.** It sits in the desert, so it is not costing anyone production on turn one. Expect the first 7 to send it to a strong number on the main island.

## Midgame plan

- **Collect regions one at a time, and never leave a route half-built.** Each region is 3 points for a settlement. Four regions, plus your two starting settlements, is 14 points worth of settlements alone, but you only have 5 settlement pieces. Expect to need a city or two to free pieces and add points.
- **Count the end.** At 14 points, a typical winning line is two starting settlements, three or four bonus settlements, and a city or two. Every bonus you collect brings the end closer by 3.
- **Take the strip early if others are heading there.** The bonus is not a race, but the strip is small and its best corners go quickly. See [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/).
- **Use length.** A road line across the desert is a long, cheap Longest Route candidate, and it can join a ship line at a coastal settlement. See [the longest route at sea](/academy/catan-seafarers/longest-route-at-sea/).
- **Land on the gold.** A settlement on an island gold field earns the bonus and a card of your choice every time it rolls. That double payoff is often the best single landing on the map. See [gold fields](/academy/catan-seafarers/gold-fields/).

## The trap

**Crossing the desert because it is there.** The desert route feels free because it needs no ships, but every road costs brick and wood, and the desert hexes beside it pay nothing. If the main island is crowded and your brick is thin, a three-road desert crossing can take as long as a voyage while giving you nothing to farm on the way. Worse, you may arrive to find a rival already on the strip's best corner.

Treat the desert as one route among several. Count both distances before you commit, pick the one your production pays for, and keep a second target in mind in case someone gets there first.
