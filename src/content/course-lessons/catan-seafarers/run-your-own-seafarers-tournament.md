Organized Seafarers play is rare today, and you may never find an event near you. That might grow as more players learn the scenarios. Until then, the way to play competitive Seafarers is to host it yourself. This lesson gives you a format for one evening or one Saturday, built for a group of 8 at two tables of 4. It works for 4 or 12 just as well.

## Decision 1: the scenarios

Not every scenario makes a good tournament round. You want rules everyone can learn in a few minutes, little hidden information, and a clear score at the end.

**Good choices:**

- **Heading for New Shores** (14 points to win). The starting scenario; everyone can play it.
- **The Four Islands** (13 points). No single main island, so the opening matters more.
- **Through the Desert** (14 points). Rewards reading which unexplored region to chase.

**Leave out, at least the first time:**

- **The Fog Islands,** because face down hexes add luck that no one controls.
- **The Pirate Islands,** because you cannot win without your fortress, so the final score says little about how close a player came.
- **New World,** because a random map can favor one seat. Save it for a fun final round once the standings are settled.

For a three round event, play the three good choices in that order, so the simplest comes first while people warm up.

## Decision 2: fixed setups

The Seafarers rulebook recommends playing each scenario's fixed setup first because it provides a balanced board. That is exactly what a tournament needs. Every table plays the same printed map each round, so no one can blame a bad shuffle. Set up both tables from the rulebook before players sit down, and have someone else check them.

Borrow one idea from official base game events: a few minutes of map study, on a shared timer, before anyone places. The [tournament rules lesson](/academy/catan/tournament-rules/) in our base Catan course describes how official events handle it, and the [map study checklist](/academy/catan/map-study/) works on a Seafarers board too, with one extra line: count ship distances to each island.

## Decision 3: seating

Seat order matters in Catan; see [seat position](/academy/catan/seat-position/). Make it fair by rotation, not luck.

Over four rounds with four players a table, each player can sit in each seat exactly once. Over three rounds, make sure no one is first player twice, and write the seat on a sign up sheet so there is no debate. Mix the tables each round so players meet new opponents. For round 1, draw seats at random. After that, seat by standings: the top four at one table, the next four at the other.

## Decision 4: scoring across scenarios

Different targets break simple point totals. Eleven points is close to a win in The Four Islands (13) and further from one in Heading for New Shores (14). So score each game as a share of the target:

**Round score = 10 × your points ÷ the scenario's target, capped at 10, plus 2 for the table winner.**

Two examples:

- 11 points in The Four Islands: 10 × 11 ÷ 13 = 8.5.
- 11 points in Heading for New Shores: 10 × 11 ÷ 14 = 7.9.

The winner scores 10 plus 2, so 12. Bonus point tokens from exploring count, since they are part of your score. Add the rounds up. Break ties by most table wins, then by the head to head result if the tied players met.

## Timing

Seafarers games run longer than base Catan, since the targets are 13 or 14 points instead of 10. Set a round length, such as 90 minutes, and announce it before the first roll.

When time is called, finish the current round so that every player has had the same number of turns, ending with the player to the right of the first player. If no one has won, score the table as it stands. That rule matters: without it, the last player to roll gets a free extra turn.

Keep turns brisk. The base course's [turn timer lesson](/academy/catan/beating-the-turn-timer/) works here, and a phone timer in the middle of each table is enough. You do not need judges, just one host who settles questions with the rulebook open.

## Running the day

- **Before:** print the rulebook pages for each scenario, set up the tables, and prepare the sheet.
- **Between rounds:** ten minutes to post scores, reseat, and reset the map.
- **At the end:** announce the winner, then ask each player to name one decision that cost them a game. Our [review lesson](/academy/catan/reviewing-your-games/) turns that into a habit.

Then plan the next one. A second event with the same group shows who improved, and a third makes it a season.
