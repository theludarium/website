The Four Islands is the second scenario in the Seafarers book and the first with no main island. Four islands of land sit in the sea, and every player starts on one or two of them. It plays fast, rewards a clear plan, and punishes players who forget that islands are points.

## Setup and special rules

- **The map.** Four islands, no deserts, no gold fields. In the 3-player fixed map, the four islands are the same size. In the 4-player fixed map, two islands are large and two are small. The robber starts on the hex with the 12.
- **Home islands.** You may place your two starting settlements, with a road or ship each, on one island or on two different islands. The island or islands you start on are your home islands. Every other island is unexplored for you.
- **The bonus.** The first time you build a settlement on each of your unexplored islands, you take 2 bonus points. It does not matter if other players already built there. Home islands differ from player to player, so one player's home is another player's bonus.
- **The variable setup** shuffles land and numbers into the four island areas, with one guard: forests and pastures never get a 2, 3, 11, or 12. Ports may be shuffled too.

## How you win

The first player to have 13 or more victory points during their turn wins.

## What the map rewards

There is no main island to fight over and no gold to chase, so this scenario is the cleanest test of [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/). The number of bonuses you can earn depends on your start:

| Your start | Unexplored islands for you | Bonus points available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both settlements on one island | 3 | 6 |
| One settlement on each of two islands | 2 | 4 |

Now count a path to 13 with one home island. Two starting settlements are 2 points. Three bonus settlements are 3 more, plus 6 in bonuses: 11. One city makes 12, and Longest Route or a second city makes 13 or 14. Those three bonus settlements also use up your last three settlement pieces, which is exactly the five you own. The scenario is built so that a player who lands on every island is most of the way home.

With two home islands, you start with more variety in production but 2 fewer bonus points to collect. You must find those 2 points elsewhere, usually a second city or the Longest Route tile.

## Opening plan

1. **Read the islands, not just the hexes.** For each island, count its pips and note which resources it lacks. An island with no ore or no wheat cannot build cities on its own.
2. **Default to one home island when it has good numbers and at least four resource types.** You keep all 6 bonus points in reach, and every other island is a target.
3. **Split across two islands when no single island gives you both wood and wool** (your fleet fuel) along with a decent number. Production you cannot ship with is not production at all in this scenario.
4. **Put at least one settlement on the coast facing the nearest other island,** and take a ship as its starting piece. Count the sea edges to the nearest legal corner over there. In the 4-player map the islands are close, so a two- or three-ship voyage is common.
5. **Look at who else is on your island.** If three players crowd one island, its land will fill in five turns. That pushes everyone to sea early. Plan for it.

## Midgame plan

- **Land on one new island every few turns.** At 3 points a landing, each voyage is the best value on the board. Do not let a ship line sit half-built.
- **Pick the corner, not just the island.** The bonus does not depend on being first, but good corners do. Sail first to the island where a rival is about to take the only strong intersection.
- **Upgrade once your pieces run out.** After your fifth settlement lands, cities are your points. Choose the settlements on your best numbers first.
- **Use your ports.** With nine ports spread across four small islands, many coastal corners touch one. A 2:1 port that matches a hex you already farm is often worth a voyage of its own.
- **Watch the pirate.** It starts out at sea, and a pirate on your channel stops new ships there, so keep a second target island in mind.

## The trap

**Building at home.** It feels safe to fill your home island first: short roads, no sailing, steady production. But each home settlement is 1 point for about 8 cards, while each new island is 3 points for about 10. Worse, every settlement you place at home is a piece you no longer have for an island. A player with five settlements on home islands has no way to earn another bonus without first building a city. Sail early, and let home building fill the gaps.
