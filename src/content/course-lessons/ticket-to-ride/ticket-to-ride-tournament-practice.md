You know the rules, the map, the odds, and the Mega Game. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: four players at a table, a deck of 69 tickets, a time limit, and opponents who count trains as carefully as you do. This lesson gives you a six-week plan to get there.

The plan assumes about three to four hours a week. Scale it up or down, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-tournaments/) lists the main options:

- **The World Boardgaming Championships,** held each summer in Pennsylvania. Its Ticket to Ride event welcomes beginners, plays four-player tables, and uses the USA 1910 Mega Game by default.
- **The Train Gamers Association at Gen Con** in Indianapolis, with its base-game USA tournament and its multi-map Game System Tournament. The association also runs events at Origins and at its own Railcon.
- **Online play** on Board Game Arena, if a trip is not possible this year. It is also where most of your practice will happen.

Check the event's current rules page when it is posted. Formats change from year to year.

If you are near Indianapolis, the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) gets first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play four-player games. That is the standard tournament table, and it plays differently from two or three. [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=tickettoride) is the easiest place to get many in. Aim for four to eight games a week, and play the variant your event uses.

**2. Format.** At least one session a week, play the event's format exactly. For WBC, that means the Mega Game: 5 tickets dealt, keep 3; draw 4, keep 1; both bonus cards. If you can, play it across a real table with a timer set for the round length, so you learn how the pace feels.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Map drill.** Name the routes into each of the [dead-end cities](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-usa-map/), with their lengths. Then name the 6-train routes from memory.
- **Ticket drill.** Draw a random ticket from your box and estimate its shortest path in trains before you trace it. The [ticket value lesson](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-destination-tickets/) gives you the answer key: the value on the card is close to the true number.
- **Scoring drill.** At the end of an online game, find each player's longest path on the board by eye before the result appears, using the method from the [scoring lesson](/academy/ticket-to-ride/how-to-score-ticket-to-ride/).

**4. Review.** After every loss, ask three questions, and write the answers down:

1. Which ticket or route cost me the most, and when could I have seen it coming?
2. Who ended the game, and was I ready for it?
3. Did I lose a bonus, the longest path or the Globetrotter, by a small margin?

Patterns appear fast. A mistake you see three times is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: foundations.** Volume and review, plus the map drill every session. Focus on the [midgame](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-midgame-strategy/) habit of naming what each rival is building before your turn.

**Weeks 3 and 4: the format.** Switch most games to your event's variant. Practice choosing 3 of 5 tickets quickly and well. Track your completed ticket count every game, since in the Mega Game it decides the Globetrotter.

**Week 5: event rules.** Play under your event's exact procedures: count your 45 trains before each game, treat train, card, and ticket counts as public, and use the event's tie-breakers when you score. Run the [endgame](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-endgame-scoring/) checklist in every game.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Play a handful in the first half of the week, then ease off. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers from week to week:

- **Win rate** in four-player games.
- **Ticket score:** completed minus failed. It should never be negative.
- **Tickets completed per game,** the Globetrotter number.
- **Bonuses won:** longest path and Globetrotter, out of games played.

If your ticket score is rising and your failed tickets are falling, the plan is working, whatever your results.

## The people across the table

Tournament play can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view. Every game in this plan is a game against other people, often people who love Ticket to Ride as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the close endings afterward. The players you meet at a WBC heat or a Gen Con session are a big part of why it is worth going.

That is the end of the course. You can play, teach, and score; open with a plan and draw with purpose; read the map and every rival on it; weigh tickets, odds, and long routes; and walk into a tournament knowing its format. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
