Ticket to Ride's final scoring is where games flip. A player who led the scoring track all game reveals two failed tickets and drops to third. Another flips over a pile of finished tickets and takes the longest path bonus too. None of it has to be a surprise. You can count most of it before the last turn.

## How the end works

The end is triggered at the end of a turn. When a player finishes their turn with 0, 1, or 2 trains left, every player gets one final turn, including the player who triggered it. Then the game ends.

So once anyone reaches 2 trains, you have exactly one turn left. The trouble is that you often get much less warning than that. A player with 8 trains and a big hand can claim a 6-train route and trigger the end in a single turn.

## Count the trains

Glance at every supply each time around the table. A useful rule: once any player is near 12 trains, think of the game as having only a few turns left for everyone.

Two numbers matter:

1. **The fewest trains anyone has.** That player controls when the game ends.
2. **The trains your unfinished tickets still need.** If the shortest path to finish them is longer than the turns you probably have, fix your plan now.

Remember you need a train for every space of a route. With 3 trains left, a 4-train route is out of reach, whatever cards you hold.

## What a failed ticket really costs

A ticket you finish adds its value. A ticket you fail subtracts the same value. So the gap between finishing and failing is double the number on the card.

Take the rulebook's own example of a 4-point ticket from Atlanta to Washington. Finished, it is +4. Failed, it is -4. Spending a turn and a few trains to finish it is worth 8 points to you, more than the 7 points of a 4-train route.

That makes late ticket draws dangerous. If you draw 3 tickets with only a few turns left, you must keep at least 1, and if you cannot finish it, it is pure loss. Draw late only when your railroad already connects most of the map's big cities, or skip the draw.

## The longest path bonus

The longest continuous path of your own trains is worth 10 points, the same as a 5-train route. Tied players all score it.

How to count yours:

1. Find the single longest line of your trains. It may loop and pass through a city twice, but no train counts twice.
2. Count trains, not routes. A 6-train route adds 6.
3. Ignore branches. Only the longer side of a fork counts.

Compare with your nearest rival late in the game. If you are close, a short route on the end of your line can win 10 points for 1 or 2 trains. If you are far behind, do not chase it. Spend those trains on routes and tickets.

## Your last trains and cards

When the game ends, leftover cards and trains score nothing. In your final turns:

- **Finish tickets first.** Each one is worth double its value compared to failing it.
- **Then spend your hand.** Claim the longest route you can afford with your remaining trains and cards.
- **Then grab the bonus.** If a short route extends your longest path past a rival's, take it.

## Should you trigger the end?

Ending the game is a weapon. If your tickets are finished and other players are clearly still working on theirs, dropping to 2 trains gives them exactly one turn to catch up. That often is not enough.

If your own tickets are not finished, triggering the end is usually a mistake. You still get one final turn, but one turn rarely fixes a broken ticket.

## A worked example

Two players at the end of a game:

| | Player A | Player B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Route points | 68 | 80 |
| Tickets finished | +27 | +25 |
| Tickets failed | 0 | -7 |
| Longest path | +10 | 0 |
| Total | 105 | 98 |

Player B built more and longer routes and led the scoring track all game. But one ticket drawn late cost 7 points, and Player A's single connected railroad earned the bonus. A single connected railroad that finishes tickets often beats a scattered set of big routes.

If players tie on total points, the player who completed the most tickets wins. If they are still tied, the one holding the longest path card wins.

## A last-turn checklist

1. Which of my tickets are unfinished, and can I finish one this turn?
2. Can I extend my longest path past my rival's?
3. What is the longest route my trains and cards can still claim?
4. Is anyone else one claim away from ending the game?
