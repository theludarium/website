Ticket to Ride looks like a family game, and it is one. It is also a serious tournament game, with a long-running event at the World Boardgaming Championships, a full weekend of events at Gen Con in Indianapolis, and a busy online scene. You do not need to win any of them to enjoy them. But knowing they exist changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament Ticket to Ride differs from home play

Rules vary by event. Here is how the World Boardgaming Championships runs it:

- **Four players per table,** with three-player tables when the numbers do not divide evenly.
- **A time limit.** Each round is scheduled for 2 hours.
- **Count your trains.** Every player confirms they have exactly 45 before the game starts.
- **Public counts.** Each player's number of trains, train cards, and tickets is public information.
- **More tickets.** Later rounds use the USA 1910 Mega Game from the [player counts and maps lesson](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-expansions/), with every ticket in play and both bonus cards.
- **Event tie-breakers.** Instead of the rulebook's order, the event breaks a tied game by the longest path award, then most tickets made, then ticket score, then route score, then later turn order.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention run by the Boardgame Players Association at Seven Springs Mountain Resort in Pennsylvania. WBC 2026 ran from July 24 to August 1. Many games crown a champion there, and Ticket to Ride is one of them, with more than 20 years of events behind it. Donte Saccenti won it in 2026, according to the [WBC winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

From the [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/ttr.html):

- **Who can play:** the event is marked "Beginning Players Welcome."
- **Heats:** four qualifying heats. The 1910 Mega Game is the default, and the base game is offered for players who prefer it.
- **Advancing:** you must win a game in a heat to qualify. Up to 64 winners advance to a quarterfinal, 16 to a semifinal, and 4 to the final.
- **Later rounds:** the quarterfinal, semifinal, and final all use the 1910 Mega Game.

If you have only played the base game, try the Mega Game at home a few times before you go.

## The Train Gamers Association at Gen Con

The [Train Gamers Association](https://traingamers.com/) is an all-volunteer nonprofit that runs tournaments for train games. Its events include [Gen Con](https://traingamers.com/gencon/), held in Indianapolis, where the 2026 convention ran July 30 to August 2.

Its 2026 Gen Con rules describe two Ticket to Ride competitions:

- **The TTR US tournament.** Win a game in any scheduled session of the base USA game, and you are eligible for its quarterfinals later in the weekend.
- **The TTR Game System Tournament.** The many maps are split into ten categories, from the USA and Europe to the Map Collection. A first place finish earns 3 points, second 2, and third 1, and your best five categories count. The top sixteen go on to a quarterfinal on the USA Mega Game, a semifinal on Europe, and a final on a map drawn from the finalists' choices.

The association also runs events at Origins in Columbus, Ohio, and at its own Railcon in Rosemont, Illinois.

## Online play

Online is the fastest way to get many games in.

- **[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=tickettoride)** runs Ticket to Ride in your browser, for 2 to 5 players, with options that include the 1910 and Mega variants.
- **The official digital game** from Marmalade Game Studio is on Steam, iOS, Android, PlayStation, Xbox, and Nintendo Switch, with online play against people around the world.

Online versions may deal the starting tickets the older way. Check the setup screen so you know which rule you are playing.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights. Ask the staff whether Ticket to Ride is on the schedule, or bring your own copy.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups, and a game that teaches in ten minutes is an easy one to bring.
- **Gen Con.** If you live near Indianapolis, the Train Gamers Association runs scheduled Ticket to Ride sessions there all weekend.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play four-player games.** That is the standard tournament table.
- **Play the Mega Game** if you plan to go to WBC. More tickets change which ones you keep.
- **Keep score sheets.** Write down route points, ticket points, and the longest path every game, not just the total.
- **Review your losses.** Which ticket failed, and when could you have seen it coming? Which route did you lose, and to whom?
