You can play, teach, and score 7 Wonders. You can open with a plan, read your neighbors, count the endgame, track hands, and price a Wonder board. The last step is practice that looks like the event. This lesson gives you six weeks.

The plan assumes three to four hours a week. Scale it, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-tournaments/) covers the main options:

- **The World Boardgaming Championships** 7 Wonders event, marked Beginning Players Welcome. Check the current [WBC site](https://www.boardgamers.org/) for dates and previews.
- **A local tournament** at a game store or convention near you.
- **A bracket you run yourself.** Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers the format.

Then read that event's rules. The [tournament rules lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-wonder-bidding/) covers the WBC details that change play.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play online, where games are fast. [Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwonders) has 7 Wonders. Aim for five to ten games a week, at four-player tables, since that is the WBC default.

**2. Format.** At least once a week, play across a real table under your event's rules: base game, the event's table size, and a timer for the round. Practice having full payment ready before you reveal.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week.

- **Science drill.** Write three random science sets, such as 2 compasses, 1 gear, 3 tablets. Score each, then say which next symbol adds most. The [scoring lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-scoring/) has the method.
- **Wonder pricing drill.** Draw four boards at random and write a bid for each, as in the rules lesson. Revisit after a week of games.
- **Card list drill.** Read the List of cards and chains for four players and name the chains in Age I that lead to free cards later.

**4. Review.** After every loss, write short answers to three questions:

1. Which neighbor beat me, and which row of the score pad did it?
2. Which hand did I pass that fed them most?
3. Where did my coins go?

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: volume.** Play and review. Record your score pad rows after every game.

**Weeks 3 and 4: format.** Add the event's rules to every game. Play two in-person games a week under a timer. Start the pricing drill.

**Week 5: the hard parts.** Play only under event rules. Run Wonder auctions in your practice games, even informal ones.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Read your notes for the pattern you see most, and fix that one thing. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep a few numbers from week to week:

- Your win rate at four players.
- Your average score.
- Which score pad row is your biggest, and whether that changes.

If the numbers move the right way, the plan is working, whatever the event result.

## Bring a copy

The WBC preview asks owners to bring a copy of the game. If you are buying, the base game is **[7 Wonders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F65MX4L?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** on Amazon.

## The people across the table

A tournament can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view. Every game in this plan is a game with other people, many of whom love 7 Wonders as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the close finishes afterward. If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.
