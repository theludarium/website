You can play, teach, and score both games. You can open with a plan, read your neighbors and the layout, count the endgame, track hands, control rows, and see a sudden win coming. The last step is practice that looks like the event. This lesson gives you six weeks for either track, or both.

The plan assumes three to four hours a week. Scale it, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-tournaments/) covers the main options:

- **The World Boardgaming Championships** events for 7 Wonders and Duel, both marked Beginning Players Welcome. Check the current [WBC site](https://www.boardgamers.org/) for dates and previews.
- **A local tournament** at a game store or convention near you.
- **A bracket you run yourself.** Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers the format.

Then read that event's rules. The [tournament rules lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-wonder-bidding/) covers the WBC details that change play.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play online, where games are fast. [Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwonders) has 7 Wonders, and its [Duel page](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwondersduel) has Duel. Aim for five to ten games a week on your track. For 7 Wonders, choose four-player tables, since that is the WBC default. For Duel, alternate first and second seat.

**2. Format.** At least once a week, play across a real table under your event's rules: base game, the event's table size, and a timer for the round. For 7 Wonders, practice having full payment ready before you reveal. For Duel, practice the balanced draft from the rules lesson.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week. Pick the ones for your track.

- **Science drill (7 Wonders).** Write three random science sets, such as 2 compasses, 1 gear, 3 tablets. Score each, then say which next symbol adds most. The [scoring lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-scoring/) has the method.
- **Wonder pricing drill (7 Wonders).** Draw four boards at random and write a bid for each, as in the rules lesson. Revisit after a week of games.
- **Card list drill (7 Wonders).** Read the List of cards and chains for four players and name the chains in Age I that lead to free cards later.
- **Layout drill (Duel).** Lay out Age II from your own copy, count the safe picks, and say who would take the last card with no play-again. The [layout lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-duel-layout-control/) explains the count.
- **Supremacy drill (Duel).** At random points in an online game, pause and say the shield gap and both science counts before you look. The [supremacy lesson](/academy/7-wonders/7-wonders-duel-supremacy/) explains why.

**4. Review.** After every loss, write short answers to three questions.

For 7 Wonders:

1. Which neighbor beat me, and which row of the score pad did it?
2. Which hand did I pass that fed them most?
3. Where did my coins go?

For Duel:

1. Did the game end by military, science, or points?
2. Which pick opened the card that beat me?
3. When did the shield gap or the science count first become a threat, and did I see it?

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: volume.** Play and review. On the 7 Wonders track, record your score pad rows after every game; on Duel, record your result by seat and by how it ended.

**Weeks 3 and 4: format.** Add the event's rules to every game. Play two in-person games a week under a timer. Start the pricing drill or the layout drill.

**Week 5: the hard parts.** Play only under event rules. On the 7 Wonders track, run Wonder auctions in your practice games, even informal ones. On Duel, play every elimination-style game knowing a tie loses for the first player.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Read your notes for the pattern you see most, and fix that one thing. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep a few numbers from week to week:

- **7 Wonders:** your win rate at four players, your average score, and which score pad row is your biggest.
- **Duel:** your win rate as first and as second player, and how many of your wins and losses came by supremacy.

If the numbers move the right way, the plan is working, whatever the event result.

## Bring a copy

Both WBC previews ask players to bring a copy of the game, and the Duel preview warns that players can be turned away if there are not enough. ## The people across the table

A tournament can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view. Every game in this plan is a game with other people, many of whom love 7 Wonders as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the close finishes afterward. If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

That is the end of the course. You can play, teach, and score both games; open with a plan; read Wonder boards and draft Duel Wonders; run a coin economy and choose Progress tokens; track every hand, control the layout, and count a sudden win; and walk into a tournament knowing its rules. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
