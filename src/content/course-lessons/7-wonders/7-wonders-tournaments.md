7 Wonders is easy to teach and quick to play, which makes it a good tournament game. You can play many rounds in a day, and skill shows up over many games even when the cards are random. You do not need to win anything to enjoy competing. But knowing how tournaments run changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament play differs from home

Competitive play tends to share a few habits:

- **Base game only,** at least by default.
- **Fixed table sizes.** At WBC, 7 Wonders uses four-player tables wherever possible.
- **Ways to balance the start.** At WBC, late rounds auction the Wonder boards.
- **A time limit per round,** so slow play has a cost.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention run by the Boardgame Players Association, held each summer at Seven Springs Mountain Resort in Pennsylvania. Many games crown a champion there, and 7 Wonders has its own event, marked **Class B, Beginning Players Welcome**, which makes it a friendly first tournament. (7 Wonders Duel has a separate event, covered in the [Duel tournaments lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-tournaments/).)

The details below come from the 2026 event preview.

### 7 Wonders at WBC

From the [2026 7 Wonders preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/7WS.html):

- **Three heats,** each with two games. Players stay for both games, and seating is shuffled between them.
- **Four-player tables** wherever possible.
- **Advancement** to a quarterfinal (64 players), semifinal (16), and final (4), ranked first by total heat wins, then by a points system for finishing places.
- **Wonder bidding** in the semifinal and final: four Wonders are drawn at random, and players bid victory points they will give up to choose one.
- **Rounds of 2 hours.**
- **Editions.** The second edition is preferred, but the preview notes that most games will still use first edition copies. Owners are asked to bring a copy.

The preview also clarifies one rule for the event: you must have every resource, coins included, when you reveal a card to build it.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwonders) has 7 Wonders for 3 to 7 players in your browser, with the Edifice expansion as an option. Online play is the fastest way to get many games in, and many games is how tournament skill grows.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights, and a game that seats seven is an easy one to get to the table. Ask the staff. <!-- VERIFY: check whether Repos Production or Asmodee has a store locator or organized-play program for 7 Wonders, and link it here if so. -->
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Run your own event.** A 30-minute game fits several rounds into an evening. Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers brackets and seeding.
- **Start a group.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play four-player games** with random Wonders, and write down your score by category after each one. Where your points come from tells you what you lean on.
- **Review your losses.** Which neighbor beat you, and with what?
