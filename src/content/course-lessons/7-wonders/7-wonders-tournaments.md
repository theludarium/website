7 Wonders and Duel are easy to teach and quick to play, which makes them good tournament games. You can play many rounds in a day, and skill shows up over many games even when the cards are random. You do not need to win anything to enjoy competing. But knowing how tournaments run changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament play differs from home

Competitive play for both games tends to share a few habits:

- **Base game only,** at least by default.
- **Fixed table sizes.** At WBC, 7 Wonders uses four-player tables wherever possible.
- **Ways to balance the start.** At WBC, late rounds of 7 Wonders auction the Wonder boards, and the Duel event adjusts who starts the Wonder draft.
- **A time limit per round,** so slow play has a cost.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention run by the Boardgame Players Association, held each summer at Seven Springs Mountain Resort in Pennsylvania. Many games crown a champion there, and both 7 Wonders and 7 Wonders Duel have their own events. Both are marked **Class B, Beginning Players Welcome**, which makes them friendly first tournaments.

The details below come from the 2026 event previews.

### 7 Wonders at WBC

From the [2026 7 Wonders preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/7WS.html):

- **Three heats,** each with two games. Players stay for both games, and seating is shuffled between them.
- **Four-player tables** wherever possible.
- **Advancement** to a quarterfinal (64 players), semifinal (16), and final (4), ranked first by total heat wins, then by a points system for finishing places.
- **Wonder bidding** in the semifinal and final: four Wonders are drawn at random, and players bid victory points they will give up to choose one.
- **Rounds of 2 hours.**
- **Editions.** The second edition is preferred, but the preview notes that most games will still use first edition copies. Owners are asked to bring a copy.

The preview also clarifies one rule for the event: you must have every resource, coins included, when you reveal a card to build it.

### 7 Wonders Duel at WBC

From the [2026 Duel preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/7WD.html):

- **Two heats.** In each, you play two games, one as first player and one as second. Win at least two heat games to qualify.
- **Single elimination** after that: quarterfinal (8 players), semifinal (4), and final (2).
- **No expansions by default.** In heats, you can ask to play with them and be paired with others who want the same.
- **A balanced Wonder draft.** In elimination rounds, after each set of four Wonders is dealt, the second player decides who starts the 1-2-1 draft. The preview says this is meant to offset the first-player advantage.
- **Ties** go to blue-card points, as in the rulebook. If still tied, the game is a draw: a win for both in the heats, and a loss for the first player in elimination rounds.
- **Bring a copy.** Players may be turned away if there are not enough games.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwonders) has 7 Wonders for 3 to 7 players in your browser, with the Edifice expansion as an option. Its [7 Wonders Duel](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwondersduel) page includes both the Pantheon and Agora expansions. Online play is the fastest way to get many games in, and many games is how tournament skill grows.

The publisher also has an official [7 Wonders Duel app](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/7-wonders-duel/app) for iOS and Android.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights, and a game that seats seven is an easy one to get to the table. Ask the staff. <!-- VERIFY: check whether Repos Production or Asmodee has a store locator or organized-play program for 7 Wonders, and link it here if so. -->
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Run your own Duel bracket.** Duel is short enough for an evening tournament. Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers brackets and seeding.
- **Start a group.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **7 Wonders: play four-player games** with random Wonders, and write down your score by category after each one. Where your points come from tells you what you lean on.
- **Duel: play both seats.** Track your results as first and second player separately.
- **Review your losses.** In 7 Wonders, which neighbor beat you, and with what? In Duel, which pick opened the card that beat you?

That is the end of the course. You can now play and teach both games, open with a plan, read your neighbors and the Duel layout, count the endgame, choose expansions, and find a tournament. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
