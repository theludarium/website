Your first three games are where habits form. Some of them are rules quietly played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that cost a lot of points. This lesson covers both, checked against the current second edition rulebook and the publisher's FAQ.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Buying from the wrong player.** You can buy only from your two neighbors. The player across the table is not a shop.

**Buying the wrong things.** You can buy what a neighbor's brown cards, grey cards, and starting resource produce. Resources from their yellow cards and Wonder stages are not for sale.

**Paying the bank.** Trading coins go to the neighbor who sells, not the reserve. Only coin costs printed on a card go to the reserve.

**Spending coins you just received.** Coins a neighbor pays you this turn cannot be spent until your next turn. The FAQ says the same for coins from your own cards.

**Buying from a card built this turn.** A card your neighbor builds this turn cannot sell you anything until next turn.

**Using one resource twice.** Resources are never spent, but each symbol pays only once per turn. One wood card gives you one wood a turn, not two.

**Building a duplicate.** You can never have two cards with the same name in your city, even though some cards come in more than one copy at higher player counts.

**Building Wonder stages out of order.** Stage 1, then 2, then 3, left to right.

**Selling the sixth-turn discard.** On the last turn of an Age you get 2 cards. You may sell the one you play for 3 coins, but the other card is discarded for nothing.

**Passing the wrong way.** Left in Ages I and III, right in Age II. Check the card backs.

**Fighting the whole table.** Military compares only with your two neighbors, and a tie gives no tokens to either player.

**Scoring science wrong.** Each symbol type scores its count squared, and each set of three different symbols adds 7. New tables often forget one of the two.

## Strategy traps

**No resources in Age I.** A player who skips brown and grey cards early spends Ages II and III paying neighbors or selling cards they cannot afford to build.

**Chasing science alone.** Green cards are visible. Once your neighbors see you collecting them, they stop passing them. Our [7 Wonders strategy guide](/articles/7-wonders-strategy/) explains why science is a trap more often than a plan.

**Forgetting your Wonder.** Wonder stages are the best home for a card you cannot use and your neighbor wants. Many first games end with a board half built.

**Selling too often.** Three coins is about one point. A card built is usually worth more.

**Overbuilding military.** Beating a neighbor by one shield scores the same as beating them by five.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before each pick, ask three things. Can I pay for it, with my own resources or a neighbor's? Does it help a Wonder stage or a chain I am building? And who gets the cards I pass, and what will they do with them?

That last question is the one most new players skip. Glance at the city of the neighbor you pass to before you choose. You do not need to read every card. Notice their colors: a row of green, a pile of red, a stage that needs ore. That habit is the start of everything in the next two levels.
