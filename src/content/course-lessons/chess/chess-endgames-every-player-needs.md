Endgames are where many games are really decided, and they are the part of chess most developing players skip. The good news is that a few positions cover a lot of ground. This lesson teaches them at a level you can use over the board: the ideas and the key steps, not every variation.

## The king becomes a fighter

In the middlegame, the king hides. In the endgame, with fewer pieces left to attack it, the king becomes a strong piece. The first rule of almost every endgame: bring your king toward the center and toward the pawns. A king that stays on the back rank is a piece you are not using.

## King and pawn against king: the opposition

This is the most important ending to understand, because many endgames trade down into it.

**The opposition** is when the two kings stand on the same file (or rank) with one square between them. The player who does not have to move is said to have the opposition. Since kings cannot move next to each other, the side to move must give way.

**Why it matters:** with king and pawn against king, the stronger side wins in many positions by getting its king in front of its pawn and taking the opposition. Then the defending king has to step aside, and the attacking king walks forward and escorts the pawn home.

**The key rules for the attacker:**

- **King in front of the pawn.** Lead with the king, not the pawn.
- **Reach the sixth rank.** For a pawn that is not on the a or h file, if your king reaches the sixth rank in front of its pawn, you win no matter whose move it is.
- **Rook pawns are often draws.** With a pawn on the a or h file, the defending king only needs to reach the corner in front of it.

**The key rule for the defender:** stand in front of the pawn and take the opposition whenever the attacker steps forward. If you keep it, the game is drawn, often by stalemate.

## The rule of the square

Can a lone king catch a passed pawn? You do not need to count moves. Imagine a square with one side running from the pawn to its promotion square. If the defending king can step inside that square on its move, it catches the pawn. If it cannot, the pawn promotes.

Two adjustments: a pawn still on its starting rank can move two squares, so draw the square from the square it would reach after a two-square move. And pieces in the king's way can change the answer, so check for blockers.

## Rook endings

Rook endings are the most common endings in chess, so they deserve the most study. Two positions are the base of all of them.

### The Lucena position: how to win

The attacker has king and rook plus a pawn on the seventh rank, with the king on the promotion square in front of it. The defending king is cut off on the side by the attacker's rook. The problem: the attacking king cannot step out from in front of the pawn without being checked again and again from behind.

**The method, called building a bridge:**

1. Move your rook to the fourth rank, the rank that will later block the checks.
2. Step your king out from in front of the pawn.
3. When the checks come, walk the king down toward your rook. When it reaches the right rank, block the next check by putting your rook between the king and the checking rook.
4. The checks are over, and the pawn promotes.

### The Philidor position: how to draw

The defender has king and rook, with the king on or near the promotion square of the enemy pawn. The attacker has king, rook, and one pawn that has not yet reached the defender's third rank (the sixth rank, if the defender is Black).

**The method, called the third-rank defense:**

1. Put your rook on your third rank (the sixth rank, if you are Black), keeping the enemy king from advancing to it.
2. Wait there with the rook, moving it along that rank.
3. When the pawn advances to your third rank, the enemy king loses its shelter in front of it. Now swing your rook to the far end of the board, behind the enemy king, and check it from there. The king has nowhere to hide, and the game is drawn.

If you know only these two, you will win and save more rook endings than most players at your level.

## Practice that sticks

Endgame skill comes from playing positions out, not reading about them. Set up each position in an app or on a board and play it against an engine or a friend, from both sides.
