Most chess teaching goes wrong in the same way. The teacher sets up all 32 pieces, explains six moves and three special rules, and then plays a full game that the learner loses in fifteen moves. Chess rewards a slower start. This lesson is a host script for teaching it in pieces, with notes for a child and for an adult. The printable [chess teach sheet](/academy/chess/teach-sheet/) covers the same ground on one page.

For general teaching habits that work for any game, see [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/).

## Before you start

- **Set up in front of them.** Let the learner place the pieces with you, using the checks from [lesson 1](/academy/chess/how-to-set-up-a-chess-board/): light square on the right, queen on her color.
- **Give them White.** Moving first is one less thing to think about.
- **Decide your goal.** For a child, the goal of the first session is fun and the pawn game. For an adult, it is one full game with the basic moves.

## The script, in order

### 1. The goal, in one sentence

"We each have a king. If you trap mine so it cannot escape, you win." Show a king, and say the king is never captured. The game just ends when it is trapped.

### 2. The pawn game

Set up only the pawns. Each player tries to get one pawn to the far side. Pawns move forward one square, or two on their first move, and capture one square diagonally forward. This one mini game teaches the trickiest piece, the idea of capturing, and the idea that the far side matters. It also takes five minutes.

### 3. Add one piece at a time

Add rooks to the pawn game, then bishops, then the queen. With each piece, show the move once, then let the learner find every square it can reach from the center of the board. Save the knight for its own moment: put a knight on an empty board and ask them to visit every corner. Kids often love the knight once it clicks.

### 4. Add the kings and check

Now add the kings and explain check: "When your king is attacked, you must fix it right away. Move it, block it, or capture the attacker." Play a few minutes with kings and a few pieces each, and call out every check as it happens.

### 5. A full game

Set up everything and play. Talk through your own moves out loud: "I am moving my knight here so it can reach the center." Point out threats against them before they make a mistake, at least for the first game.

### 6. Special rules, later

Castling, en passant, promotion, and stalemate can wait for the second or third game. Introduce each one when it comes up on the board. A rule seen in a real position is remembered. A rule explained in advance is not.

## Teaching a child

- **Keep sessions short.** Stop while they are still having fun. Two good mini games beat one long frustrating one.
- **Use the mini games for weeks.** Pawn wars and capture games are real chess training, not a lesser version.
- **Win and lose kindly.** Letting a child win every game teaches nothing, but crushing them teaches less. Give yourself a handicap instead, such as playing without your queen. Our article on [teaching kids to lose at games](/articles/teaching-kids-to-lose-at-games/) covers the feelings side.
- **Praise good thinking, not just captures.** "You saw that my bishop was attacking your knight" is the habit you want to grow.

## Teaching a new adult

- **Name the fear.** Many adults think chess is for geniuses. Say early that everyone starts by losing pieces, and that improving is mostly habits, not talent.
- **Show the why.** Adults like reasons. When you explain a move, give the idea behind it: center, safety, activity.
- **Offer takebacks in the first games.** If they blunder their queen, offer to undo the move and explain what happened. Stop offering once they find threats on their own.
- **Play, then review.** After the game, replay one moment that decided it. One lesson per game is plenty.

## Common teaching mistakes

- **Explaining every rule first.** They will forget most of it before move ten.
- **Silent games.** A learner gains far more from a teacher who thinks out loud.
- **Fixing every move.** Let small errors go. Point out the one that matters.
- **Skipping the why of check.** If they do not know they must escape check, the game will fall apart. Make sure that one is solid.

## After the first game

Send them home with the teach sheet, or point them to lesson 1 of this course so they can review the moves. A few short games in the first week build the moves into memory faster than one long game ever will.
