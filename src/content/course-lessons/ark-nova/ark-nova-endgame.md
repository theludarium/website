Ark Nova has no fixed number of rounds. The game ends when someone's appeal and conservation counters meet, and that moment can arrive sooner than the table expects. Players who plan for it finish with their best action loaded. Players who do not finish with a hand of animals they never played.

## The trigger, exactly

The end is checked at two moments: at the end of a player's turn, and during a break. If a player's counters sit in the same scoring area at either moment, or have passed each other, the end is triggered.

- **Triggered at the end of your turn:** every other player takes one more turn. You do not.
- **Triggered during a break:** everyone takes one more turn, you included.

Then comes final scoring. A counter that crosses on someone else's turn, say from a card effect that fires on a rival's turn, still waits for the end of your turn or the next break before it triggers anything.

## Who gets the last turn?

Read the first case again. When you trigger the end on your turn, you hand every rival one final turn and take none yourself. So the question is never only "Can I cross?" It is "If I cross now, can anyone pass me with one more turn?"

If your lead is bigger than any rival's best possible turn, cross. If it is not, you may do better growing your lead for a turn or two while staying just short of the line. Every turn you wait also gives rivals a turn, so this is a judgment, not a rule.

## How big can one turn be?

To judge a rival's final turn, look at what is public:

- **The card in their slot 5,** and how many X-tokens they hold. Animals at 5 means two animals.
- **Their money.** Big animals need big money.
- **Upgrades.** Association II can do several tasks and a donation in one action. Sponsors II can play more than one sponsor.
- **Open project levels** they qualify for, including releases. One project after 10 can be worth 15 appeal spaces.

Then add their Final Scoring card, which can be worth up to 4 conservation. You cannot see it, but you can often guess from their zoo.

## Line up your last turns

Once someone is close, start counting turns. Use the row planning from the [action cards lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-action-cards/) in reverse: decide which action you want for your final turn, then pick earlier actions that leave it in slot 5.

Three things almost never score at the end: money, X-tokens, and cards in hand. Plan to spend them. A last turn that uses three X-tokens to push Animals or Association to a higher strength is often the biggest turn of your game.

If a rival triggers the end, your next turn is your last, and you will know it. Keep a big turn ready so it is waiting whenever that happens.

## Count your end scoring first

Before you cross, count what final scoring adds:

- **Your Final Scoring card.** It pays 1 to 4 conservation, never more than 4. Check where you are on its scale and whether one more action reaches the next step.
- **End-game sponsors.** Cards with a brown end-game effect score now too.
- **The discard rule.** If nobody reached 10 conservation during the game, everyone discards one Final Scoring card before scoring.

These points come after the trigger, so they never end the game early. They can push you well past the crossing once it happens.

## Releases are a late-game tool

Releasing an animal costs its appeal and pays conservation. Early, at 2 appeal spaces per point, the trade is thin, and the lost appeal also lowers your income. After 10, at 3 per point, it gets much better. The rulebook's example releases a tortoise: 6 appeal lost, 4 conservation gained. Past 10, those 4 points cover 12 appeal spaces, a net gain of 6.

Keep a cheap animal or two in your zoo that fit a release project, on the table or in your hand. They are your emergency finish.

## Breaks near the end

A break near the end changes the math. It returns association workers, which helps a player planning a big Association finish. It pays income, which only helps if there are turns left to spend it. And if the end triggers during a break, the player who caused it also gets one more turn.

So before you push the Break token late in the game, ask who gains more from the break, you or the rival closest to crossing.

## A last-turn checklist

1. Can I cross this turn, and can anyone pass me with one more turn?
2. Which action will be in slot 5 on my final turn?
3. Am I spending all my money and X-tokens?
4. How much will my Final Scoring card and end-game sponsors add?
5. Is there a release that gains more than it costs?

One more thing about last turns: a player who cannot win may still decide who does, just by taking a card or a project level. Our piece on [kingmaking](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) has calm ways to handle that.
