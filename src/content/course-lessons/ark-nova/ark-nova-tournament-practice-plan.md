You know the rules, the row, the tracks, the projects, the economy, the maps, the break, and the endgame. The last step is practice that matches what you will face. This lesson gives you an eight-week plan built around how Ark Nova tournaments actually run.

The plan assumes four to five hours a week. Ark Nova games are long, so scale the number of games to your time, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your goal first

A plan needs a date. The main routes, from the [competitive play lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-competitive-play/) and the [online play lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-online-play/):

- **The World Boardgaming Championships** in Pennsylvania, with four-player heats, then a quarterfinal, semifinal, and final in 3-hour rounds.
- **The Ark Nova Digital Championship Series** qualifiers, monthly from October 2026 to January 2027.
- **Board Game Arena Season 27,** October 2026 to January 2027, where the top players of two regions are invited to Berlin.
- **A personal goal,** such as a target score or a solo win at 0 appeal, if no event fits.

## Know the format you are training for

The WBC event reports show what a big in-person tournament looks like. The [2024 report](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbook24/akn.html) counted 119 players across two heats. Each heat offered five designated advanced maps, chosen in reverse turn order. From the quarterfinal on, every table played the same map and players bid for their seats. Bowen Niu won the final with 116.5 points, 1.5 ahead of second place.

Three lessons for practice come out of that:

1. **Four-player tables.** Train at four.
2. **Advanced maps, drafted.** Practice choosing from a pool, not playing Map A.
3. **Close finishes.** The final was decided by a point and a half, so your last turns and your Final Scoring card matter.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play four-player games online, mostly real-time. Two to four games a week is a solid pace for a long game. Track your score, your placement, and your margin to the winner.

**2. The clock.** Once a week, play at a real table or a strict real-time online pace, and finish inside 3 hours at four players. Plan during others' turns. Note where your time went.

**3. Drills.** Fifteen minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Solo row drill.** A solo game at your hardest level, logging every action and strength, from the [solo practice lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-solo-practice/).
- **Map draft drill.** Lay out five random advanced maps and two random hands. Choose a map for each hand in under a minute, then explain why, using the [advanced zoo maps lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-advanced-zoo-maps/).
- **Last-turn drill.** Set up a late-game position from a replay and find the biggest final turn available.

**4. Review.** After every loss, answer three questions and write the answers down:

1. Where did my action row let me down, and when could I have fixed it?
2. Which break or project race decided the game?
3. How big was the winner's last turn, and did I count it before the end?

## The eight weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: foundations.** Volume and review, plus the solo row drill. Focus on gap per action: before each turn, name how much gap the action closes.

**Weeks 3 and 4: the table.** Add the map draft drill. In every game, track one rival's icons against the base projects and take at least one level they need.

**Weeks 5 and 6: the clock.** Add a clocked game each week. Your goal is to finish every game on time with your planned last turn. Practice the [break timing](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-break-timing/) habit: name who each break helps before you move the token.

**Week 7: event rules.** Play every game under your event's exact rules: its map pool, its seat order or bidding, and its scoring. Run the [endgame](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-endgame/) checklist and the [Final Scoring card](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-final-scoring-cards/) count in every game.

**Week 8: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Play a few in the first half of the week, then ease off. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers week to week:

- **Average score** in four-player games.
- **Average margin** to the winner, or your lead when you win.
- **Money and cards carried into the final turn.** The target is close to zero.
- **Projects supported** per game, which is also the official tiebreaker.

If your margin is shrinking and your final-turn waste is near zero, the plan is working, whatever your results.

## The people across the table

A long tournament game can feel like work. Keep one thing in view. Every game in this plan is with other people who love this zoo as much as you do. Learn their names, talk through the close finishes, and keep in touch. If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.
