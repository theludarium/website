The first game of Ark Nova is a learning game for everyone. The second and third are where habits form, good or bad. This lesson collects the rules people get wrong early and the strategy traps that keep scores low, with a fix for each.

## Rules slips

These are the ones that change results. Check each against your last game.

**1. Moving the wrong cards in the row.** After you use a card, only the cards to its left slide right. Cards to its right stay put. A table that slides everything ends up with very different strengths.

**2. Building away from your zoo.** Every building after the first must share an edge with a building you already have. The first one must cover at least one border space of your map. And no building may cover rock or water, or the spaces marked for an upgraded Build card.

**3. Kiosks too close together.** Two kiosks need at least 2 spaces between them, and those spaces can hold other buildings. Kiosk income also counts only buildings that share an edge with the kiosk, and an empty standard enclosure pays nothing.

**4. Forgetting reputation range.** On side I, the Cards action draws from the deck, and the only way to take a display card is to snap at strength 5. Taking cards "within reputation range" needs an upgraded Cards card.

**5. Going past 9 reputation.** Your reputation cannot reach 10 until your Cards action is on side II.

**6. Paying the partner zoo discount too broadly.** The discount is 3 money per continent icon on the animal, and only for the partner zoo's continent. An animal from another continent pays full price.

**7. Counting the wrong icons for projects.** Only icons along the top of your cards count, plus partner zoos, universities, and the rock and water icons in those top corners. The conditions on the left side of cards never count.

## Trap one: hoarding cards

New players love their hands. Every animal looks exciting, so they draw and keep, draw and keep. Then the break arrives and everyone must discard down to 3 cards, or 5 with one of the universities. Every card discarded at a break was an action spent for nothing.

**The fix:** before you take the Cards action, ask whether you will play what you draw before the next break. If not, take a different action. A hand of 3 cards you can play beats a hand of 8 you cannot.

## Trap two: building before you know the animal

An empty 5-space enclosure is a promise. If no 5-space animal arrives, you paid 10 money and a Build action for a hole in your zoo that earns nothing.

**The fix:** build for animals already in your hand. Check the size and any rock or water needs, then build the smallest enclosure that fits. Map A's free 3-space enclosure is a good home for your first mid-size animal.

## Trap three: appeal only, conservation never

Animals are fun, so first games often become animal shows. Appeal climbs to 50 or 60, conservation sits at 2 or 3, and the tracks never cross. Since every conservation point covers at least 2 appeal spaces, and 3 after 10, ignoring projects wastes the most efficient way to close the gap.

**The fix:** support a base project at its lowest level before the first break if you can. It moves you toward the 2 conservation bonus, which flips an action card or adds a worker. The [scoring lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-scoring/) shows the math, and the [conservation projects lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-conservation-projects/) shows which level to take.

## Two smaller habits

**Arriving at a break with money.** Income adds to whatever you hold, so cash sitting idle at a break did nothing for you. Spend down before the break, then let income refill you.

**Ignoring the clock.** New players rarely look at the Break token, and they get caught with a full hand. Glance at it every turn. Every Cards action moves it 2 spaces, and every Sponsors action that takes money moves it as far as the strength.

## Measuring your progress

Ark Nova scores are easy to compare once you know the scale. Meeting your two counters exactly scores about 100. A first game that never crosses scores less, sometimes much less. So a simple goal for game two or three: cross. Then try to cross a little earlier each time.

Keep a note after each game of your final appeal, your conservation, and how many projects you supported. Watching conservation climb from game to game is the clearest sign you are learning.
