A strong home group might play Ark Nova once or twice a month. A player chasing the top can play that many in a day online. Volume is not everything, but it is where patterns become instinct. And for the 2026 to 2027 season, online play is also a direct path to the world championship.

## The two main platforms

**[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=arknova)** runs Ark Nova in the browser for 1 to 4 players, with the Marine Worlds rules included on its game page. Board Game Arena runs ranked play with seasons, and the current season is part of the championship path below.

**[Ark Nova Digital](https://direwolfdigital.com/ark-nova/),** from Dire Wolf, offers solo play against an AI, online games, and pass and play. Its Championship Series is another path to the world championship.

- **The Ark Nova Digital Championship Series:** four monthly qualifiers, each over two days: October 29 to November 1, November 12 to 15, December 10 to 13, and January 14 to 17. The four qualifier winners meet in a streamed final, and its winner earns the invitation.
- **Board Game Arena Season 27,** October 2026 to January 2027: the best players from two regions are invited. The page defines a Western Hemisphere of the Americas, Africa, and Europe, and an Eastern Hemisphere covering the rest of the world.

The page says the four qualifiers receive a flight to Berlin and hotel accommodation for the main event.

## Real-time or turn-based?

Board Game Arena offers both real-time tables, where everyone plays in one sitting, and turn-based tables, where each player has a longer window per move.

- **Real-time** trains the tournament skill: deciding under a clock while others wait. WBC rounds run 3 hours at four-player tables, so practice finishing on time.
- **Turn-based** is good for deep thinking. Use it to work through hard positions slowly, then bring the habits to real-time.

A mix is healthy. If your goal is an event, lean toward real-time four-player games.

## Ranked play etiquette

Ranked play means real stakes for everyone at the table. A few habits keep it good:

- **Finish what you start.** Leaving a game ruins it for three people.
- **Keep your turns moving.** Plan during others' turns, as the [action cards lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-action-cards/) suggests.
- **Be kind in chat.** A short "good game" costs nothing, and the same players will be at your tables for months.

## Reviewing games

The fastest way to improve is to review your own losses. Online play makes this easy, because the game keeps a full log and, on Board Game Arena, a replay.

After each loss, answer four questions:

1. **Where did my row let me down?** Find a turn where the action you wanted sat at the wrong strength.
2. **Which break hurt me?** Who triggered it, and what did I carry into it?
3. **Which project level did I lose a race for?** Could I have taken it a turn earlier?
4. **How big was the winner's final turn,** and did I see it coming?

Write the answers in one line each. After ten games, the same answer will keep showing up. That is your next thing to fix.

## Watching strong players

Board Game Arena lets you watch games and replays of top players. Pick a strong player and watch one full game, pausing before each of their turns to guess their action. Count how often you match. It is the same reading skill the [competitive play lesson](/academy/ark-nova/ark-nova-competitive-play/) teaches, done at speed.
