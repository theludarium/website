Welcome to Level 5. You can run a great night of [Slugblaster](/academy/slugblaster/how-to-play-slugblaster/). Now let's run a great season. The book suggests about 5 to 10 episodes, each a run and a downtime, ending with the epilogue rules. That ending changes how you play the middle, so this lesson starts at the end and works back.

## The epilogue

On the last run of the season, skip the disaster roll. Then:

1. **Pick a time span.** The GM has the final say. A year works well, but it can follow the teens into adulthood.
2. **Roll the low point:** one die per 2 doom. The highest result picks how hard it is. A 1 to 3 is something small, like an amusing predicament or a loose end. A 4 or 5 is a setback or a long road. A 6 is a lasting injury, a lost friendship, a broken dream.
3. **Roll the high point:** one die per 2 legacy, read the same way, from a glimmer of hope or a memory you will always cherish, up to living the dream.
4. **Describe it as a montage,** low point and high point, and end on the high one. The book puts it best: this is Slugblaster, not Sadblaster.

Then the **crew epilogue**: as a group, answer what epic story everyone tells about the crew, its most perfect memory, what it gave the sport, whether it stays together, and whether any of you still hang out. Each personality has the final say on one question.

Now read the season backwards. Every doom you mark is a die in the low point. Every legacy is a die in the high point. A teen with 6 legacy and 2 doom rolls three dice for the high point and one for the low. That is the payoff for all those Perseverance and You Earned It beats.

## Doom and legacy all season

**Doom** comes from disasters, crew breakups, and the hard beats: a final warning at home, the low point of an arc, taking an L. **Legacy** comes from style beats like You Earned It, the payoff steps of most arcs, and fame perks that grant +1 legacy each. Doom can be cleared with certain beats, or with a run aimed at fixing the damage. Legacy can be lost to disaster or given up to mend a broken crew.

A good season is not one without doom. A teen with a little doom and a lot of legacy gets an epilogue with both a real scar and a real triumph, which is often the most satisfying kind.

## Factions

Factions are the people who are not you. The book sorts them into four kinds: rival **crews**, corporate **sponsors**, **authorities** like the portal cops of the Rescue division or a concerned parents' group, and **crowds**, the scenes of potential fans who decide whether you are cool.

Standing runs from +3 to -3 on the crew sheet: friendly, tight, and ally on one side; unfriendly, rivals, and enemy on the other. A faction can also be marked unstable, on the edge of a big swing. The book suggests that allies trigger good things like fans, collaborations, or free swag, and that enemies trigger events like being hunted or smeared.

Adjust standing at clean-up after each run, based on what the crew did. Standing also feeds the downtime tables: friends raise opportunity rolls, and enemies raise challenge rolls. Over a season, factions give runs their reason to exist. A sponsor wants a stunt. A crowd wants a party. Rescue wants your crew in a decontamination center.

## Fractures

The crew sheet has two fracture boxes. Fights, some arc steps, and some challenges add fractures, and Make Up and arc payoffs clear them. If the crew takes a fracture with both boxes full, the crew breaks up: everyone marks doom, and at least one teen has to leave. Getting back together takes a real sacrifice, like a teen clearing legacy or marking doom.

A breakup can be a great midseason twist, if the table wants it. If not, watch the fracture boxes and spend style on Make Up before the third one lands.

## Pacing the fame climb

Fame tiers cost 5, 7, 9, 9, and 11 crew style, 41 in all from Nobodies to Legends. A crew that hits its brands and wins its prizes can earn a large share of a tier in one good run, so Legends can be within reach inside a season if the crew focuses on it. Two things slow it down: style spent on beats, and runs that miss.

As GM, use the tiers as season acts. Up & Comers is when the scene notices. Well-Established is when it respects you. Major Players is when everyone has an opinion. Rising Stars brings more opportunities and more challenges. Legends is the finale.

## The last run

Make the final run the biggest of the season. If the crew rolled a pivotal opportunity along the way, a run with a huge prize and a lot of bite, this is its moment. Bring back the factions that mattered. End in a chase. And since there is no disaster roll, let everyone push their luck one last time.

## After Legends

If the table wants more, the book offers three paths: keep going with custom goals, jump a year ahead and restart the crew as nobodies in a bigger pond with new personality sheets, or play a new story, like the teens' younger siblings.
