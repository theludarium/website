Slugblasters love their gear, and most have one device they love most and never stop tinkering with. The book calls it your **signature**, or your setup. In [Slugblaster](/academy/slugblaster/how-to-play-slugblaster/) it is your go-to way of solving problems and a big part of who your teen is.

## The parts of a signature sheet

**Function.** What the device lets you do out of the box. A gravity blaster damages things at a distance with crushing force. A kinetic deck goes fast over solid ground and can turn super heavy. A robotic companion follows you like a loyal dog and does simple tasks.

**Look.** A line to describe it. The book offers a list of forms (a gravity blaster can be a gauntlet, a pistol, a wand, an orb) plus a table of descriptions like circuit-bent, sticker-covered, or borrowed. Is it a birthday present from a rich uncle, or something you built from shop-class scraps?

**Turbo.** The device's own boost and kick boxes. Describe how your device helps an action and mark them, just like attitude. Some boxes print grey: those open up with the universal mods below.

**Components and mods.** Mods are new powers. Each costs components, the abstract parts you collect.

## The twelve devices

The 1.33 sheets include twelve signatures. A quick map, by what each does best:

- **Moving fast:** Hardlight Board, Kinetic Deck, Skipmode Runners. These are the chase specialists.
- **Sneaking:** Riftninja Sneakers, which turn you ghostly.
- **Blasting things:** Gravity Blaster, Reality Cannon, Negafriction Sword.
- **Taking hits:** Powered Armour and Photonic Jacket let you nope certain kinds of slams for 1 trouble instead of 2.
- **Knowing things and carrying things:** Hyperoptic Visor, Voidwear Backpack.
- **A friend:** Robotic Companion.

Every teen also starts with a phone, a raygun, and a hoverboard, plus a personality item and two pieces of gear. So a teen whose signature is a sword can still shoot and still ride. The signature is where they shine.

## Components

The book uses four abstract component types:

- **Lenses:** prisms, diodes, optics.
- **Coils:** wiring, servos, drive trains.
- **Discs:** circuit boards, chips, firmware.
- **Gems:** power cells, alien minerals, reality-bending crystals.

You get them as run prizes, from the In the Lab beat, from opportunities like a sponsor's swag, and by trading with teammates.

## Mods

You start with one mod that costs 2 components. Each device has five of its own, and most fall into a few families:

- **Roll to do a new thing.** Phase through walls, teleport, lift a car, create illusions.
- **Mark turbo to avoid a slam.** Most devices have one: a force field, a blink out of existence, a skip a few seconds into the future. Each one is a nope that costs turbo instead of trouble.
- **A big top-end mod** costing four components that does something wild, like turning a target flat or splitting you into a crowd of yourselves.

Every device can also take three **universal mods**:

- **Power Cell** (2 gems): turbo gains 1 kick.
- **Stabilizer** (1 coil, 1 disc): turbo gains 1 boost.
- **Kitbashed:** buy a mod from another device's list.

## In the Lab

The **In the Lab** beat costs 1 style. Tinker, design, shop, and do any of these: install mods you have components for, uninstall mods to salvage their components, trade three matching components for one of your choice, and roll a die for one random component (1 coil, 2 disc, 3 lens, 4 gem, 5 your choice, 6 your choice and roll again).

Since you can buy it more than once per downtime, In the Lab is a steady drip of parts for 1 style each.

## Building a setup

A few defensible rules of thumb:

1. **Take a slam-dodging mod early.** Marking 1 turbo instead of 2 trouble is a great trade, and it protects your disaster roll (see [the odds lesson](/academy/slugblaster/slugblaster-trouble-disaster-odds/)).
2. **Then grow your turbo.** Stabilizer and Power Cell make every run better. A Stabilizer's extra boost cuts a failure chance in half when it matters.
3. **Then go weird.** Big mods create the moments people remember. Save components for one.
4. **Fit your personality.** The Smarts can carry a second signature and swap mods on the fly. The Chill can mash buttons to use a mod they do not own, at the risk of worse problems. Talk with your teammates before you borrow theirs.

## Gear is not damage

The book is clear: most gear does not add dice. It lets you do things you could not do otherwise. You cannot hack without hacking tools or shoot without a raygun. How much an attack does is the GM's call on impact, and kick is how you push it higher. A modded cannon is not stronger by the numbers. It opens options.
