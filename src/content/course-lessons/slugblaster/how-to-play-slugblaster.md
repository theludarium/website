[Slugblaster](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1736877518?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a tabletop roleplaying game about teenagers in a boring prairie town who sneak into other dimensions on hoverboards. They film tricks, blast giant bugs with homemade rayguns, try to get famous, and try to get home before their parents notice. One person runs the game as the game moderator, or GM. Everyone else plays one teenager in a crew of friends.

This lesson walks the core rules in the order you meet them at the table. The free reference sheets on [slugblaster.com](https://slugblaster.com/) cover the same ground in one page, and the [teach sheet](/academy/slugblaster/teach-sheet/) puts it all on one printable page.

![A Slugblaster table from above, with the GM sheet, player sheets, dice, and the shared crew sheet](/academy/slugblaster/setup.svg)

## What you need

A GM, 2 to 4 players, pencils, a handful of six-sided dice, and the printed playsheets. Each player has two sheets: a **personality** sheet (who your teen is) and a **signature** sheet (the one device they love most, like a hoverboard made of light or a gravity blaster). The crew shares a **crew sheet** with its fame, style, and a map of the multiverse where you draw the portals the crew knows.

## The shape of a night

Each session is an **episode** of about 2 to 4 hours, and a season runs about 5 to 10 episodes. Every episode has the same loop:

1. **Pick a run.** The crew decides what to do: a goal, a prize, and at least one obstacle in the way.
2. **Refresh.** Players refill their boost and kick. The GM gains 1 bite per player.
3. **The run.** The action part: portals, chases, monsters, parties.
4. **Disaster rolls.** Near the end of the run, usually on the way home, everyone checks whether their trouble catches up with them.
5. **Clean-up.** Collect style bonuses, clear slams, adjust how the factions feel about you.
6. **Downtime.** Back in town, players spend style and trouble on **beats**: short scenes about school, family, friends, and gear.

## The action roll

Any time you try something uncertain where failing would be interesting, you make an action roll. Say what you are trying, then roll one six-sided die.

- **6:** you succeed.
- **4 or 5:** you succeed, but there is a problem.
- **1 to 3:** you fail, and there is a problem.

You describe what your teen does. The GM describes how much it accomplishes, its **impact**, and what goes wrong. If you roll more than one die, you keep only the highest. There are no skills, no modifiers, and no target numbers. A huge task might sit on a **progress track** the GM draws. Each success marks it, and a more effective action marks more.

## Bending the roll

Your sheets hold small boxes you mark to help a roll, and they refill at the start of each run.

- **Boost** adds one die to the roll.
- **Kick** raises the impact: more damage, more progress, more style from a trick. Kick stays spent even if you fail.
- **Dare:** once per roll, take +1 die, +1 kick, or clear 1 trouble, in exchange for one cost: the GM gains 1 bite, the GM adds a problem you suggest, or you mark 1 trouble.

Your personality's boost and kick are called **attitude**, your device's are called **turbo**, and the crew's shared pool is called **hype**. You describe how each one helps, and you spend them before you roll. Teammates can spend theirs on your roll too, as long as they can say how they help.

## Problems, slams, and noping

A problem is either a **snag**, where the situation gets worse (an alarm, a second monster head, your ex at the party), or a **slam**, a lasting hurt like bruised, slimed, humiliated, or glitching gear. Write slams in your slam boxes. They give no penalties, and they clear at the end of the run. If you take a slam with no empty box, disaster strikes.

You decide whether your teen gets hurt. When the GM hands you a slam, you can say **nope**, describe the cool way you avoid it, and mark 2 trouble. You can nope slams, not snags.

## Trouble and disaster

**Trouble** is the homework piling up and the luck running out. You mark it for dares and nopes. Mark the eighth box and disaster strikes. Near the end of each run, every player makes a **disaster roll**: one die per empty trouble box, keep the highest. A 6 avoids disaster and marks 1 style. A 4 or 5 avoids it but marks 1 trouble. A 1 to 3 means something bad happens, and you mark **doom**, a long-term scar.

## Style and tricks

Say **"check it"** before any roll to add a trick or some show. Problems get worse, but success also marks 1 style, plus 1 more for each kick you spent. Style buys downtime beats and pushes your crew up the six fame tiers, from Nobodies toward Legends. Beats also earn **legacy**, the good memories and trophies that, with doom, decide each teen's epilogue when the season ends.

That is the whole engine. One die, a few boxes to mark, and a lot of slime.
