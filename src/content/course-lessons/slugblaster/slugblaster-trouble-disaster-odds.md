[Slugblaster](/academy/slugblaster/how-to-play-slugblaster/) gives you a lot of power over your own fate. You can nope any slam, take a dare whenever a roll matters, and push your luck again and again. The price is **trouble**, and the bill arrives at the end of every run in the **disaster roll**. This lesson puts numbers on that bill, so you can spend trouble on purpose.

## How the dice work, once more

Every roll in the game reads the same way: roll your dice, keep the highest. A 6 is a clean result, a 4 or 5 is a mixed one, and 1 to 3 is the bad one. Because you keep only the highest die, more dice push the result up fast.

| Dice | Best die is 6 | Best is 4 or 5 | Best is 1 to 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 17% | 33% | 50% |
| 2 | 31% | 44% | 25% |
| 3 | 42% | 45% | 13% |
| 4 | 52% | 42% | 6% |
| 5 | 60% | 37% | 3% |

The math is simple: the chance every die shows 1 to 3 is one half, multiplied once per die. The chance no die shows a 6 is five sixths, multiplied once per die.

Two lessons for action rolls. First, one boost cuts your failure chance in half, from 50% to 25%. That is a big swing for one box. Second, even with four or five dice, a 4 or 5 is common. The book says it plainly: you will roll 4s and 5s more than anything else, and that is still success.

## The disaster roll

Near the end of each run, usually as the crew portals home, every player rolls **one die per empty trouble box** and keeps the highest.

- **6:** you avoid disaster and mark 1 style.
- **4 or 5:** you avoid disaster but mark 1 trouble.
- **1 to 3:** disaster strikes. Something bad happens, and you mark doom.

The sheet has eight trouble boxes. So the table above, read by empty boxes, is your disaster chance:

| Trouble marked | Dice | Disaster chance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | 8 to 5 | 3% or less |
| 4 | 4 | 6% |
| 5 | 3 | 13% |
| 6 | 2 | 25% |
| 7 | 1 | 50% |
| 8 | none | Disaster strikes when you mark it |

Look at the shape. Each box you mark removes a die, and that doubles your disaster chance every time. Doubling 0.4% or 1.6% hardly matters, so the first four trouble barely move the needle. Doubling 6% to 13% to 25% to 50% does. That is the cliff.

## When noping is worth it

A nope costs 2 trouble. So the real question is: what does this slam cost me, compared with moving two rows down that table?

**Nope freely** while you sit at 0 to 2 trouble. Going from 2 to 4 moves your disaster chance from under 2% to about 6%.

**Think twice** from 3 trouble up. Going from 4 to 6 takes you from 6% to 25%.

**Take the slam** when it is mild and the run is nearly over. Slams clear at clean-up anyway. A "slimed" slam with ten minutes left costs you almost nothing.

**Always count your slam boxes.** If taking this slam fills your last box, a further slam means disaster. Then the nope is protecting you from a sure disaster, not a possible one.

Remember that teammates can take or nope slams for each other. A friend at 1 trouble noping your slam is far cheaper for the crew than you noping it at 5.

## Dares and other trouble

Marking 1 trouble is one of the three prices of a dare, and traits like The Grit's Skill let you mark 1 trouble to turn a 1 to 3 into a 4 or 5. These are often great deals early in a run. The Guts' Double Dare trait trades 2 trouble for 2 dice or 2 kick, which is strong when you are low.

The GM can also force an extra disaster roll by spending 2 bite, and disaster can strike on its own when you mark the eighth box or overflow your slams. Plan for a little slack.

## Clearing trouble

Trouble does not clear at clean-up. During a run, the usual way out is a dare that clears 1 trouble in exchange for bite or an extra problem (one rare device mod can clear some too). The rest you clear in **downtime**:

- **Trouble beats.** Many beats cost trouble rather than style, like the family and angst arcs. You clear the listed trouble and play out the scene, often taking a slam or doom.
- **Being Good** costs 1 style and clears 2 trouble: chores, homework, showing up for your shift.
- **Crew beats.** A Challenge or a Fight costs 5 trouble and brings the crew a problem or a fracture.

You can always buy a trouble-cost beat even if you lack the trouble. Teens in Slugblaster do not get to dodge their problems, only to trade them for different ones. That trade, played well, is the heart of [downtime](/academy/slugblaster/slugblaster-downtime-beats/).
