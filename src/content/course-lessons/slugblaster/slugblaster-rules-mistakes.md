Welcome to Level 1. You have played a run. Now let's make sure you played it right. Slugblaster's rules fit on a page, but players arrive with habits from other games, and a few rules are easy to read past. Each item below is checked against the version 1.31 rulebook and the 1.33 reference sheets. If you need the basics first, start with [how to play Slugblaster](/academy/slugblaster/how-to-play-slugblaster/).

## Rolling and bonuses

**Spending boost or kick after the roll.** Both are spent before you roll. Seeing a 2 and then reaching for a boost is not allowed by default. (The book offers this as an optional rule, called Afterburner, for a gentler game.)

**Adding the dice together.** Multiple dice never add up. You roll them all and keep the single highest. Three dice showing 2, 3, and 5 is a 5.

**Taking two dares on one roll.** One dare per action roll. Boost and kick have no limit, though. You can pour every box you have into one big moment.

**Thinking kick comes back on a failure.** It does not. Kick stays spent even when the roll fails. Boost is spent too. Both refill at the start of the next run.

**Giving the GM a roll.** Foes do not roll. Monsters and Rescue agents act when it makes sense or when a player rolls a problem, and their attacks land unless the player nopes the slam.

## Problems and slams

**Trying to nope a snag.** Noping only works on slams. A snag is new story, like an alarm or a second monster, and refusing it would subtract fun. (Solo play is the exception: the solo rules let you nope snags.)

**Giving slams penalties.** Slams have no built-in penalty. The GM may lower the impact of an action where it makes sense, like climbing a cliff while slimed, but there is no minus to the roll.

**Forgetting that a full slam track is a disaster.** If you take a slam with no empty box, disaster strikes. Most sheets have two slam boxes, so the third slam of a run is a big deal.

**Clearing super slams all at once.** A super slam, like "super bruised," only loses the "super" at clean-up. You start the next run still bruised. Noping one takes two nopes: the first removes the "super," the second the whole thing.

## Trouble and disaster

**Sharing trouble.** You can share boost, kick, dares, slams, components, and style with teammates, if you can say how. Trouble is the one thing you cannot share.

**Counting disaster dice wrong.** The disaster roll uses one die per *empty* trouble box, not per marked box. The more trouble you have, the fewer dice you roll. With all eight boxes marked you have already had your disaster.

**Saving trouble for later.** Trouble mostly clears through downtime beats, plus a dare that clears 1 and a rare trait or mod. It is meant to be spent. Players who hoard it roll a thin disaster pool at the worst time.

## Tricks

**Saying "check it" after the roll.** The trick is declared before you roll. Problems on that roll are worse, and success marks 1 style. You do not need to roll for small tricks in a safe driveway. The rule is for big, risky, look-at-me moments.

**Forgetting kick on tricks.** Each kick spent on a successful trick adds 1 more style. Kick is the main way to turn a landed trick into real fame.

## Downtime

**Thinking you need enough trouble to buy a trouble beat.** You can always buy a trouble-cost beat, even if you cannot afford it. You clear what you have, and you live with the story.

**Buying arc beats out of order.** Trait beats and arc beats are one-time buys. Arc beats go in order, and you can buy only one beat per arc each downtime. Other beats, like Being Good or In the Lab, have no such limit.

**Skipping clean-up.** After the run and before downtime: take the prize, award each player's style bonus if they earned it, add the crew brand bonus, clear slams, and adjust faction standing. Groups that jump straight to downtime lose a lot of style.

## Two edition notes

The rulebook says it changed one rule in this edition: The Smarts now refills 1 attitude whenever they or a nearby teammate fails an action. Players with older books may remember it differently. The 1.33 playsheets also list a few beat costs that differ from the 1.31 book. The book prices The Chill's Caught in a Plot at 1 style and Serendipity at 2; the sheets swap them. The book makes Final Warning in the family arc cost 3 trouble; every 1.33 personality sheet lists it as 3 style. When the two disagree, pick one at session zero and stick to it.
