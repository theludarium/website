Your first three races of Heat are where habits form. Some are rules slips, quietly played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that cost Spaces, and in a race, Spaces are everything. This lesson covers both, checked against the current Rules booklet.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Playing or discarding Heat cards.** Neither is allowed. A Heat card leaves your hand only through Cooldown, back to your Engine.

**Discarding Stress cards.** Also not allowed. The only way to clear a Stress card is to play it.

**Cooling down in 3rd or 4th gear.** Your gear gives Cooldown only in 1st gear (3) and 2nd gear (1). In the core game, the only other Cooldown is Adrenaline, for the last car.

**Counting slipstream toward a corner.** Slipstream's 2 Spaces never add to your Speed. Boost does, and so does the Adrenaline extra Space.

**Paying once for two corners.** If one move crosses two Corner Lines, check each corner on its own, starting with the first.

**Shifting two gears for free.** A two-gear jump costs 1 Heat, paid when you shift. If your Engine is empty, you can shift only one gear.

**Taking turns clockwise.** Movement goes in race order every round: front car first, last car last.

**Treating cars like walls.** You can always drive through other cars. Only the Space where you finish matters. If both of its Spots are taken, you stop in the first free Spot behind it.

**Picking the outside Spot.** You must take the Spot closest to the Race Line when it is free. When two cars sit side by side, the one nearer the Race Line counts as ahead.

**Looking through discard piles.** Nobody may browse a discard pile, not even their own. Only the top card is public.

**Shuffling your Play Area into a new deck.** If your deck runs out mid-turn, shuffle only your discard pile. The cards you played this round are not discarded until step 9.

**Slipstreaming across the finish line.** Slipstream cannot carry you over the line, or move you after it.

**Going easy on a spin out.** Spinning out means paying all your Heat, moving back before the corner, taking Stress cards into your hand (1 from 1st or 2nd gear, 2 from 3rd or 4th), and dropping to 1st gear. All four, every time.

## The big mistake: hoarding Heat

New drivers treat Heat like health. They brake for every corner and cross the finish line with four or five Heat still sitting in the Engine. Those cards were Spaces they chose not to drive.

Heat is a budget. Every point you go over a Speed Limit is a Space you gained by paying for it. The goal is not to save Heat. It is to spend it where it buys the most, and to cool down often enough that you never run dry at the wrong corner.

The opposite mistake shows up too: a driver who spends it all on the first lap and spends the second lap creeping through corners in 1st gear. [The openings lesson](/academy/heat/heat-opening-strategy/) gives you a simple budget for each lap.

## Not counting the Spaces

The board prints the number of Spaces to the next corner along the track. New drivers glance at their hand and play their biggest cards. Look at the board first instead.

Before you play cards, find two numbers: how far to the next Corner Line, and that corner's Speed Limit. Then pick a gear and cards that either stop you short of the line, or carry you over it at a Speed you can afford.

## Stuck in high gear with a hand of Heat

Heat cards fill your hand and do nothing. In 4th gear you need four playable cards every round. If your hand fills with Heat and you cannot play enough cards for your gear, the cluttered hand rule kicks in: you play what you can, fill the gap with Heat cards, and your car does not move this round. You also drop to 1st gear.

That is a lost round. Avoid it by shifting down before your hand gets clogged, ideally into a slow corner where low gear costs you nothing.

## Playing Stress into a corner

A Stress card adds a random 1 to 4. On a long straight, that is harmless. Right before a corner, it can turn a free crossing into a 3 Heat bill. Boost has the same risk, since it adds an unknown 1 to 4 and counts toward your Speed.

Play Stress where the variance does not matter. Our piece on [luck vs skill](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/) looks at the difference between randomness you can plan around and randomness that overrules your plan.

## Fearing last place

Last place is not a disaster in Heat. The last car each round gets Adrenaline: an extra Space and an extra Cooldown. A car just behind the pack also has slipstream targets everywhere. Sitting one Space behind two rivals going into a straight is often a good place to be.

## A quick checklist for race three

Before you play your cards, ask:

1. How many Spaces to the next corner, and what is its limit?
2. How much Heat can I spend this lap and still have some for the next?
3. Is a slow corner coming where I can drop gears and cool down?

That habit alone fixes most of what goes wrong in the first three races.
