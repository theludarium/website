Heat looks like a family racing game, and it is one. It also has a real competitive scene: an official Tournament Mode in the rulebook, an event at the World Boardgaming Championships, convention ring events, and online play. You do not need to enter any of them to enjoy the game. But knowing how serious drivers race changes how you look at your own races.

## Tournament Mode

The Advanced Play booklet ends with an official Tournament Mode for players who want a sharper game. It changes one thing: when you commit to your gear.

- In step 1, nobody shifts gears. Everyone still plays cards in step 2.
- In step 3, as you reveal, show how many cards you played, and only then move your Gear pawn to match.
- If that means shifting two gears, pay 1 Heat right then.

If you shifted two gears by mistake and cannot pay, the rules fix it: shifting up, discard one of your played cards at random; shifting down, add a random Basic card from your deck to your Play Area, the same way a Stress card does.

The effect is that you choose your gear and your cards as one decision, and nobody at the table sees your gear change before cards are committed.

## How tournament races differ from home races

Beyond Tournament Mode, tournament play, such as the WBC event below, tends to look like this:

- **Drafted upgrades** from the Garage module, instead of the Starting Upgrades.
- **Weather and Road Conditions** in play.
- **Full tables,** with Legends filling any empty seats.
- **A heats format:** several qualifying races, then a semifinal and a final for the best finishers.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. In 2026 it ran from July 24 to August 1 at Seven Springs Mountain Resort. Heat runs there as a Trial event.

The [2025 event report](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbook25/HET.html) shows how it worked that year:

- **72 players** entered.
- **Three heats** on the USA, France, and Great Britain tracks. The first used the basic upgrades and a standard Legend; the next two used a snake draft for upgrades and a Legend one step tougher.
- **A semifinal** on the Italy track, with 29 racers across 6 tables.
- **A single-lap final.** David Platnick won.

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/het.html) set up two heats on the USA and France tracks, with five-player tables, Legends at "pro" level filling short tables, drafted Garage upgrades, weather, and gear and cards declared together, as in Tournament Mode. Rounds were scheduled for two hours. The event was marked as welcoming beginning players. Jack Wolff is listed as the 2026 Heat winner on the [WBC results page](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

The [WBC page](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbcindex.html) lists WBC 2027 for July 24 to August 1, 2027. Trial events are chosen each year by a member vote, so check the 2027 event list before you plan a trip.

## Is there an official championship?

The word "Championship" in Heat usually means the season mode in the box, not a tournament circuit. We could not find a current official championship series for Heat from Days of Wonder or Asmodee.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=heat) has had Heat since September 2023. It is free to play in your browser, and its game page describes both the Championship system and solo play against Legends. Online play is the fastest way to get a lot of races in, and a lot of races is how your corner math gets quick.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good rivals.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights, and a six-car racing game is a natural fit. Ask the staff.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it. To run a small event, [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers formats, seeding, and tiebreaks.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Race with the Garage draft and weather on.** That is what tournaments use.
- **Use Tournament Mode at home,** so choosing gear and cards together feels normal.
- **Race tougher Legends.** Add 1 or 2 to their Top Speed, as the designers do.
- **Review every race.** Where did you pay Heat? Did you spin out? How much Heat was left at the finish?

That is the end of the course. You now know how to play, teach, open, manage the midgame, empty your engine on the final lap, pick modules and expansions, and find a race worth entering. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
