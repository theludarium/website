The final lap is where Heat races are won and lost. Drivers who were cautious all race suddenly have a full Engine and nothing to spend it on. Drivers who were reckless find a tight corner between them and the line with no Heat to pay for it. Both problems can be solved before the last lap begins, with a little counting.

## The finish line rules that matter

Four rules shape the last lap:

- **First across wins.** If several cars cross in the same round, the car furthest ahead wins. Side by side, the Spot nearer the Race Line wins.
- **Limits stop past the line.** Speed Limits on corners after the finish line do not count. Move as far as you can.
- **No slipstream finishes.** Slipstream cannot carry you across the line, or move you after it.
- **Adrenaline keeps going.** It is still based on how many cars started the race, not how many are left on the track.

The first rule matters more than it looks. If two cars can finish this round, the one that goes furthest past the line wins. Your last move is not about reaching the line. It is about going as deep past it as you can.

## Spend it all

Heat in your Engine at the finish line is worth nothing. The rulebook makes the point plainly in its opening pages: nobody hands out a prize for arriving in a pristine car.

Before the last lap starts, count:

1. Your Heat: what is in your Engine now, plus any you can still cool down.
2. The corners left before the finish.
3. What each corner will cost at the Speed you want to take it.

If the total cost is less than your Heat, you have spare Heat. Spend it: Boost on straights, take a corner faster, or jump two gears. If the total is more than your Heat, find the corner where braking costs you the fewest Spaces and plan to take that one slowly.

## Plan backward from the line

Work out the round you expect to cross the line, then the round before it, then the one before that.

- **The finishing round.** Use your highest gear, your best cards, and a Boost if you can pay for it. Speed limits past the line do not matter, so every point of Speed is distance.
- **The round before.** Set up so the finishing round has room to use a big hand. Ending just after the last corner is ideal.
- **Two rounds out.** Make sure the last corner is paid for. A spin out on the final corner sends you back before it, drops you to 1st gear, and hands you Stress cards. That usually ends your chance to win.

Cooldown still matters here. On the last lap it is not about the future. It is about having enough Heat in your Engine to pay for the final corner at full speed.

## Counting cards on the last lap

You may not look through your discard pile, but you can remember what you played. You know your deck started with three each of 1, 2, 3, and 4, plus a 0, a 5, a Heat card, and your Stress cards. If you have played most of your 4s since the last shuffle, your next draws are likely lower. Plan your finishing round around the cards you can expect, not the ones you hope for.

## Championship points math

In a Championship season, each race pays points by finishing place, as shown in the Hall of Fame on the board: 9 for first, 6 for second, 4 for third, 3 for fourth, and 2 for fifth.

That changes how you drive:

- **Consistency wins seasons.** Two second places (12 points) beat a win and a fifth (11).
- **A spin out is expensive.** Falling from second to fifth costs 4 points, more than the 3 between second and first.
- **The grid follows the standings.** After the first race, the leader starts on pole. But the driver last on the grid picks a new Upgrade first, and may swap an Upgrade with a leftover card. Trailing drivers get first pick of the upgrades.

Sponsorship cards add one more layer. You gain one at a Press Corner by crossing its Corner Line with a slipstream, or by going over its limit by 2 or more. You never gain a Sponsorship card on a turn when you spin out. They are one-time cards, and unused ones carry over to the next race of the season.

The season winner has the most Championship points. Ties go to whoever placed better in the final race.

## A last-lap checklist

1. Can I cross the line this round? If so, how far past it can I get?
2. If not, is the last corner before the line paid for?
3. Will any Heat be left in my Engine at the finish? If so, where can I spend it?
4. Could a rival finish in the same round, and how deep past the line do I need to be?
