You know the rules, the corners, the odds, and the reads. The last step is turning that into consistent results when a seat at a full table is on the line. This lesson is a six-week plan built around how tournament Heat is usually run. Shorten it or stretch it to fit your calendar.

## Know the event first

Read your event's rules before you plan anything. Formats vary. The World Boardgaming Championships event, covered in [the competitive Heat lesson](/academy/heat/heat-tournaments/), is a useful model. In 2025 it ran three qualifying heats on the base tracks, a semifinal, and a single-lap final. The 2026 preview set five-player tables, "pro" Legends filling short tables, drafted Garage upgrades, weather, gear and cards declared together, and two-hour rounds.

From your event's rules, write down five things:

1. Which tracks, and how many laps.
2. Garage draft or Starting Upgrades, and Regular or Advanced cards.
3. Weather and Road Conditions, yes or no.
4. Tournament Mode or the standard gear rule.
5. Table size, and how Legends fill empty seats.

Then practice that format, not the home game.

## The race log

Keep one line per race, on paper or in a phone note:

- Track, player count, and your finish.
- Spin outs.
- Heat in your Engine as you started the final lap, and at the finish.
- The one decision you would change.

The log is the whole point of the plan. Most improvement comes from seeing the same mistake three times in writing.

## Weeks 1 and 2: the format, at volume

- **Three or more races a week** in your event's format. A mix of table play and online play on [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=heat) works well; online races get you volume fast.
- **Use Tournament Mode** if your event does, every race, until you stop touching your Gear pawn in step 1. [The Tournament Mode lesson](/academy/heat/heat-tournament-mode-strategy/) covers how to drive it.
- **Short tables:** fill them with Legends at +2 Top Speed, the designers' own setting, as in [the Legends lesson](/academy/heat/heat-solo-legends/).

Goal for these weeks: no spin outs, and less than 2 Heat at the finish line, in most races.

## Weeks 3 and 4: drills

Keep racing, but add two short drills, ten minutes each.

**Corner drill.** Set up any track. Deal yourself a hand of 7 from a shuffled starting deck and put your car a few Spaces before a corner. Say the best play out loud: gear, cards, Speed, Heat owed. Then check it against the board. Repeat for every corner on the track. The [odds lesson](/academy/heat/heat-odds-boost-stress/) table should be in your head by the end: margin 0, a Boost costs 3.5 Heat on average.

**Lap plan drill.** Before each race, label every corner cool, spend, or slip, as in [the track planning lesson](/academy/heat/heat-track-planning/), and write a Heat budget for each lap. After the race, compare it with what happened.

If your event uses weather, draw tokens and redo the lap plan with them, as in [the weather lesson](/academy/heat/heat-weather-road-conditions/).

## Week 5: the draft and the full table

- **Draft every race,** in the event's card pool. After each draft, write your three picks and why, in one line.
- **Race full tables.** Five or six cars bring more slipstream, more blocked Spaces, and more Adrenaline swings than two players plus Legends. If you can only get two or three people, add Legends to fill the grid.
- **Practice the single-lap race.** Finals at some events are one lap. A one-lap race has no room for a slow start, and the final-lap rules from [the endgame lesson](/academy/heat/heat-endgame-strategy/) start almost at once.

## Week 6: taper and review

- **Read your log.** Find the mistake that appears most often, and race twice focused only on that.
- **Reread the event rules,** especially anything about clocks, round time, and how ties and advancement work.
- **Race lightly** in the last few days. You want the format fresh, not tired.

## On the day

- Count your Heat before every final lap. Spend it.
- Before each reveal in Tournament Mode, check your card count against your gear.
- After each heat, write one log line, then let the race go.

## Finding the races

For tables to practice at, start with your local game store, BoardGameGeek's local groups, and the ideas in our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/). If you run a practice event yourself, [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers formats and tiebreaks. Near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

That is the full course: from your first race on the USA track to a seat at a tournament table. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
