Heat is a simultaneous game: everyone picks their gear and cards at the same time. That makes it feel like guessing. But most of what you need to predict another car is on the table in plain sight. [The midgame lesson](/academy/heat/heat-midgame-strategy/) listed what is public. This lesson turns that list into a habit: before you commit your cards, give each nearby car a range of where it can land.

## What you can see

Under the standard rules:

- **Gear pawns.** Each rival's gear, give or take a shift of one (two for a Heat), tells you how many cards they will play, and so the rough Speed range of their move.
- **Engines.** Heat sits face up on each mat, so you can count how much each rival can pay this round.
- **Car positions.** How far each car is from the next Corner Line, and that corner's limit.
- **The top card of each discard pile,** which is public. The rest of the pile is not, and nobody may browse it.

What you cannot see: their hand, how many Heat cards are clogging it, and the cards they choose.

## Build a range for a rival

Take the car right ahead of you. In the core game, with Basic cards and the Starting Upgrades, a car playing N cards moves between about N and 4N Spaces, a little more with the 5, less with the 0. That is too wide to use. Narrow it:

**Corners narrow it.** If that car is 5 Spaces from a Corner Line with a limit of 4, and its Engine holds 1 Heat, it cannot afford to cross at a Speed above 5. A driver who wants to survive will either stop short of the line or cross slowly. Its likely landing zone shrinks to a few Spaces around the line.

**Empty Engines narrow it.** A rival with no Heat cannot Boost, cannot shift two gears, and cannot overshoot any corner without spinning out. Expect caution.

**A low gear with Heat on the mat says cooldown.** A rival who dropped to 1st or 2nd before a slow corner is probably cooling down. They will move little this round, and maybe a lot the next.

**A big Engine before a straight says speed.** A rival in 4th gear with plenty of Heat and open track ahead is likely to jump forward, and may Boost.

## Use race order

Cards are chosen together, but cars move one at a time, front car first. That order is information.

- **Further back,** you see where the cars ahead landed before you React and slipstream. You choose whether to Boost and whether to slip after seeing the field.
- **Up front,** you move first with no one to slipstream behind, and everyone behind you sees where you ended.

So when you are just behind a group, plan your cards to land within reach of where the group is likely to be, then let React and Slipstream make the final call. A Boost to reach a car you can then slipstream past is a classic sequence.

## Predict the blocked Spaces

When both Spots on a Space are full, a car that would end there stops in the first free Spot behind. Two rivals likely to stop side by side just before a corner create a wall. If your planned Speed ends on that Space, you get pushed back, sometimes out of slipstream range.

You cannot plan a block for certain, since everyone picks at the same time. But when two cars ahead both need to brake for the same corner, aim one Space shorter or a few Spaces longer than where they are likely to stop.

## Side by side counts

When two cars share a Space, the one on the Spot nearer the Race Line counts as ahead for all purposes, including race order and the finish. You always take that Spot if it is free. When you and a rival will likely land on the same Space, the car that moves first gets the inside, and that is the car further ahead. Plan for the outside Spot if you move second.

## Read the leader and the tail

**The leader** has no slipstream ahead and must pay for corners with cards and Heat alone. If they are low on Heat, expect them to brake at the next tight corner, and plan to arrive behind them.

**The last car** gets Adrenaline, as do the last two with 5 or more cars. That is 1 extra Space, counting toward Speed, and 1 extra Cooldown. A trailing car with a slipstream target ahead can gain a lot in one round. If you lead by only a few Spaces, count on the pack gaining.

## When the reads stop working

Tournament Mode changes one thing: nobody shifts until the reveal, so a gear change never shows before cards are committed. You still see last round's gear, every Engine, and every position. The next level covers how to race when the gear read disappears.
