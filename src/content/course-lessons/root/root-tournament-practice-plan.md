You know the rules, the factions, the odds, the endgame, the draft, the maps, and the tournament deck. The last step is practice that matches what an event will ask of you. This lesson gives you a six-week plan.

The plan assumes about three or four hours a week: one table game and a few digital games or drills. Scale it up or down, but keep every part.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/root/root-tournaments/) covers the main option, the World Boardgaming Championships Root event, and where to look for community events. Read that event's current rules before week one: its deck, its draft, its maps, its landmarks, and whether coalitions or hirelings are allowed. Every practice game should use them.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Faction reps.** Play at least two games a week, digital or at the table, and rotate factions. Keep a simple list of every faction you might be drafted into and tick each one off as you play it. In a drafted event, the faction you dread is the one you will be handed.

**2. One table game under event rules.** Once a week, play a full four-player game at a real table using the event's setup. Run the [Advanced Setup draft](/academy/root/root-advanced-setup-draft/), use the event's deck and a random map, and time the game. The 2026 WBC event used three-hour rounds, so practice finishing well inside your event's limit.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Draft drill.** Deal a draft pool, pick for every seat, and set up. Ask why each pick made sense. No need to play.
- **Map drill.** Lay out a map with random suits and name the three clearings you most want to rule as each faction in the pool.
- **Card drill.** Read ten Exiles and Partisans cards and say, for each, which faction wants it most.
- **Odds drill.** Name the cap, the expected hits, and the chance to clear for a few attacks, using the [battle odds lesson](/academy/root/root-battle-odds/).

**4. Review.** After every game, win or lose, answer three questions in writing:

1. On which turn did the winner become hard to stop, and who could have stopped them?
2. Which of my battles were worth it, and which were not?
3. What did I learn about the faction I played?

A mistake that shows up three times in your notes is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: breadth.** Faction reps and reviews. Play the factions you know least. Add the draft drill twice a week. Focus on reading each faction's next turn, as in the [endgame lesson](/academy/root/root-endgame-race-to-30/).

**Weeks 3 and 4: the event's world.** Switch every game to the event's deck and maps. Learn the landmarks your event uses from their cards. Run the map drill for each map in the rotation. Focus on card tracking: count ambushes in every game.

**Week 5: full rehearsal.** Two full table games under event rules, timed, with the draft. Treat them as real: no takebacks, decisions within a reasonable time, deals made in the open. Review both games the same day.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** A couple of games early in the week, then ease off. Reread your review notes and pick the two habits you most want to bring to the event. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers from week to week:

- **Factions played** out of the ones in your event's pool. The target is all of them.
- **Your score when the game ended,** win or lose. A rising average means you are racing better even when you do not win.
- **Game length** for timed table games.
- **Turns-to-30 calls.** How often your end-of-round prediction of who would win came true.

## At the event

- **Be early for the draft.** It shapes the whole game.
- **Ask rules questions out loud,** and let the GM settle them. Everyone plays the same game that way.
- **Talk about the board, not the person.** The [table talk lesson](/academy/root/root-table-talk-kingmaking/) applies doubly with strangers.
- **Thank the table.** You will see these players again.

## The people across the Woodland

Root is a game about four sides who need each other and fight each other at the same time. Tournament play makes that sharper, and it also puts you at tables with people who love this strange, generous game as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the turn that decided each game afterward. Those conversations are a big part of why the events are worth going to.

That is the end of the course. You can teach Root, play all four base factions with a plan, choose battles by the odds, read the race to 30, time a dominance card, draft under Advanced Setup, read any map, and walk into a tournament knowing its rules. The game is the reason. The people across the Woodland are the point.
