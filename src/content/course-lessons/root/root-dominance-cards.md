Welcome to Level 3. Dominance cards are the most misunderstood cards in Root. New players ignore them, and then lose to one. This lesson covers the exact rules, which factions can use them well, and how the rest of the table stops them.

## The rules, exactly

The base deck has four dominance cards, one per suit. They cannot be crafted. Under the Law:

- **Activating.** During your Daylight, if you have at least 10 victory points, you may activate a dominance card from your hand by placing it in your play area. Remove your score marker from the track. You can no longer score points.
- **Winning.** A fox, rabbit, or mouse dominance card wins the game if you rule three clearings of its suit at the start of your Birdsong. The bird dominance card wins if you rule two clearings in opposite corners at the start of your Birdsong.
- **Permanent.** An activated card cannot be removed or swapped for another.
- **Spent dominance.** A dominance card can be spent for its suit like any card. When it would go to the discard pile, it goes near the map instead. During their Daylight, any player can take it into hand by spending a matching card. To take the bird dominance, you must spend a bird.
- **Two players.** All four come out of the deck at setup.

The Vagabond plays by different rules: he cannot activate a dominance card for its normal victory. In games of four or more, he can instead activate one to form a coalition with the player in last place, and he wins if they win.

## Why the check happens at the start of Birdsong

This one detail shapes everything. You activate in Daylight, but you win only at the start of your next Birdsong. Between those moments, every other player takes a full turn. So a dominance card announces itself: everyone sees the card, everyone can count your clearings, and everyone gets one turn to break your rule in one of them.

That makes dominance a test of whether you can hold ground against the whole table for one round, not a sneak attack.

## Who can win with dominance?

Look at what each base faction is good at holding.

- **The Eyrie** rule ties as Lords of the Forest, so they need fewer warriors to hold a clearing than anyone else. A late-game Eyrie with roosts and armies across one suit is a natural dominance threat.
- **The Marquise** starts with a warrior almost everywhere and can recruit across the map. She has the bodies to rule three clearings of a suit, if she can keep them.
- **The Alliance** rules where its bases stand with warriors around them, and Guerrilla War makes those clearings hard to crack. A late Alliance with two bases can sometimes pull it off.
- **The Vagabond** cannot rule clearings at all, so for him dominance means a coalition, not a win of his own.

On the Autumn map, each suit has four clearings, so a suit dominance means ruling three of the four. The bird card asks for two opposite corners. In the standard setup, the Marquise's keep and the Eyrie's first roost sit in opposite corners, which makes the bird card a real long-term threat for whichever of them can push into the other's corner.

## When to switch

Switching gives up every point you have. That is a big price if you are close to 30. A rough guide:

- **Switch when your scoring has stalled** but your board presence has not. A Marquise who has run out of room to build, or an Eyrie whose Decree just collapsed, may hold more clearings than their score suggests.
- **Switch when you already rule the clearings,** or will after this turn. Activating and then trying to conquer gives the table two rounds to respond.
- **Do not switch if you are the clear leader on points.** If 30 is two turns away, a dominance card just tells everyone to attack you without slowing your rivals.

## How to stop a dominance win

You have one round. Use it.

- **Count.** Which clearings of the suit do they rule? Which one has the fewest defenders, and who can reach it?
- **Break rule, not pieces.** You do not need to remove everything. Moving enough warriors in to tie them, or removing one building, is often enough. Remember the Eyrie still rule ties.
- **Take available dominance cards** before a rival does. Spending a matching card in your Daylight to pull a dominance card from beside the map keeps it out of the hand of the player best placed to use it.
- **Coordinate.** Dominance is the one moment when every other player clearly shares a goal. Say so out loud, as the [table talk lesson](/academy/root/root-table-talk-kingmaking/) suggests.

## A note on tournaments

Tournaments often limit the Vagabond's side of this: the World Boardgaming Championships Root event in 2025 and 2026 allowed no coalitions. Their event previews list no other limits on dominance cards. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/root/root-tournaments/) at Level 5 covers the full format.
