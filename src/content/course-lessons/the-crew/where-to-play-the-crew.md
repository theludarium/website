The Crew does not need a tournament scene to be worth mastering. Its best stage is any table where a few people sit down together. The box is small, the teach is short, and the early missions finish in minutes. That makes it one of the easiest games to bring to strangers, and one of the best for turning strangers into a group.

## Where to find players

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights where you can bring your own game. Thames & Kosmos lists stores that carry its games on its [store locator](https://store.thamesandkosmos.com/pages/store-locator).
- **Libraries, cafes, and community centers.** Our guide to [finding a board game night near you](/articles/board-game-night-near-me/) covers where to look and what to expect, and it names The Crew as a great game to bring.
- **Game cafes.** Many cafe libraries stock The Crew next to the other quick co-ops. Our guide to [the board game cafe library](/articles/board-game-cafe-library/) explains how those shelves are built.
- **Work and school.** A lunch-hour mission or two fits a short break. See our guides to [starting a workplace game club](/articles/workplace-game-club/) and [board games for college students](/articles/board-games-for-college-students/).
- **Indianapolis.** Our [weekly guide to public game nights in Indianapolis](/articles/indianapolis-game-nights/) lists where to play now. And join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## Play online

Board Game Arena has The Quest for Planet Nine, free to play for 2 to 5 players, and Mission Deep Sea too. Online tables are a good place to practice counting and signals, and to keep a campaign going with friends who live far away.

## Bringing The Crew to a new group

The first session decides whether a new group sticks with it. A few habits help:

- **Teach in five minutes, then play.** Use the script from lesson 2 and the [teach sheet](/academy/the-crew/teach-sheet/). Save every later rule for the mission that brings it in.
- **Start at mission 1, even with experienced players.** The early missions are short, and they get everyone using the same habits.
- **Make the no-talk rule playful, not strict.** Laugh when someone slips and says "oh no." Then remind them gently. The rule is the heart of the game.
- **End on a passed mission.** People remember how a night ended.

## Keep a campaign going

A 50-mission campaign is a long trip. To keep a group together across many nights:

- **Keep one logbook for the group** and bring it every time.
- **Set a regular rhythm,** like a few missions after dinner once a week. Our article on [the crew card game](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) suggests one mission a week as a simple habit.
- **Rotate who reads the story.** Everyone gets a moment as the narrator.
- **Welcome guests.** A new player can join at any mission after a quick teach. Their first mission is a fine time to use the distress signal.

## Use The Crew to teach teamwork

The Crew is one of the best games for showing a team how it communicates. A failed mission is fast and cheap, and the lesson is clear. If you lead a team at work, our guides to [cooperative games and teamwork](/articles/cooperative-games-teamwork/) and [board games for team building](/articles/board-games-team-building/) show how to run it as a short, honest exercise.

## Practice like an expert

If you want to get better, not just play more:

- **Count every color you have a task in.** Do it until it runs on its own.
- **Name each trick's job** before you play: win, feed, or stay out.
- **Review each failure** with one question: which card first told us this was going wrong?
- **Try five players** for the full challenge of the later missions.
- **Fly a clean campaign** without the distress signal.

## The end of the course

You can now play The Crew, teach it in five minutes, avoid the first-session slips, send a signal that says more than one card, pick tasks your hand can win, spend rockets on purpose, count the colors, follow task tokens in order, read the table without a word, fly at every player count, run a campaign with or without the distress signal, choose what to play next, and find a crew. The rest is missions. The game is the reason. The people around the table are the point.
