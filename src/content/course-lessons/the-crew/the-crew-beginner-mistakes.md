Every new crew fails missions. That is part of the design: a fresh deal costs a minute, and you learn something every time. But some failures are not the deal's fault. They are the same few slips, made by almost every group in its first few sessions. Here they are, in the order they tend to show up, with the rule or the fix for each.

## Rules slips

**Playing a rocket when you could follow suit.** Rockets are trumps, but only when you cannot follow the color led. If pink is led and you have any pink card, you must play pink, even if a rocket would win the trick for your teammate. The rulebook is clear on this. Rockets have the same rule as any other suit when they are led: if someone leads a rocket, you must play a rocket if you have one.

**Thinking a high off-color card wins.** A green 9 played into a yellow trick wins nothing. Only the led color, or a rocket, can take the trick.

**Talking about your hand.** "I can't win that." "Do I have to?" "Oh no." Each one tells your crew something about your cards. The rulebook's rule of thumb: if you know it only because you see your own hand, do not share it. Even when you think the mission is already lost, keep quiet and play it out. The rulebook warns that crews often misjudge a lost cause, and the attempt only takes a minute.

**Signaling during a trick.** You may only communicate before a trick starts. Once the first card of a trick is down, the window is closed until that trick is over.

**Moving your token later.** Your signal was true when you made it. If your "highest" card later becomes your only card of that color, the token still stays where you put it. Your crew has to remember what it meant at the time.

**Signaling a rocket.** Not allowed, ever. Only color cards can be communicated.

**Forgetting who leads.** The commander leads only the first trick. After that, whoever won the last trick leads the next.

## Strategy traps

**Taking the task you hold.** It feels natural to grab the task for a card in your own hand. But a card you hold has to be won by you, which means you must play it into a trick you also win. That is hard with low cards. The rulebook's tips put it plainly: it is usually easier to win a card you do not have, especially a low one. If you hold the yellow 1 and take its task, your 1 has to win a trick, which only happens if nobody else plays a higher yellow or a rocket. If a teammate holding the yellow 9 takes that task instead, they just need to play the 9 into the trick where your 1 falls.

**Winning tricks for no reason.** You never have to win. A trick you win by accident might hold a card that belongs to a teammate's task, and the mission fails on the spot. When you are not chasing a task, play your lowest safe card.

**Leading your high cards early.** A 9 that is the top card of its color left is a tool for winning a task card later. If you spend it on an empty trick, you may not be able to win the trick that matters.

**Spending your signal right away.** Many beginners signal before the first trick every time. Sometimes that is right. But a signal sent after a few tricks, when everyone knows more, can say much more. The next lesson covers this.

**Ignoring the "no signal" message.** If nobody communicates, that tells you something too: no one sees a problem worth warning about. The rulebook's first tip says this directly. Go for your tasks.

## The habits that fix most of this

- **Before each trick, ask one silent question:** "Which task cards are still out, and who must win them?"
- **Know your role on this trick.** Are you trying to win it, trying to feed a card to a teammate, or trying to stay out of the way?
- **Play to the mission, not to the trick.** A trick-taking habit from other games, like taking every trick you can, works against you here.

## When the deal is just bad

Some deals are close to impossible. One player holds all the low cards of a color, or the commander has every rocket. The rulebook says plainly that the universe does not always cooperate. Play it out anyway, learn what you can, and reshuffle. Mission attempts are cheap. That is the gift of this game: you can fail ten times in an evening and still have a great night.
