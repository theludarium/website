The rules of The Crew barely change with the number of players. But the deal changes a lot. With 40 cards split among your crew, each extra player shrinks every hand, and smaller hands mean less control over who wins what. The rulebook says plainly that three players is the smallest challenge, and five the biggest.

## Three players

Each player gets 13 cards, and one player gets 14. After the last trick, one card is left in that player's hand and never played.

What it means for play:

- **Big hands, more control.** At 13 cards, the average hand holds about three cards per color. Being completely out of a color is rare: the chance a given hand has none of a color is under 2%, from the real deal of 13 cards out of 40.
- **More rockets per hand.** About 81% of three-player hands hold at least one rocket.
- **Fewer people to coordinate.** Each signal covers a bigger share of the deck.

### The Challenge for Three

If three players finds it too easy, the rulebook offers a harder variant. Before you deal, return all green playing cards, all green task cards, and the 1 rocket to the box. Then play as normal. All mission rules still apply. Only missions 13 and 44 are affected: you no longer have to win a trick with the 1 rocket.

With 30 cards in play, each hand drops to 10 cards, and with only three colors, every color matters more.

## Four players

Each player gets ten cards. Many players think this is the sweet spot: the hands are big enough to plan with, small enough that every signal matters. Our [The Crew article](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) agrees.

## Five players

Each player gets eight cards. The rulebook calls five players a special challenge, especially in the later missions.

- **Smaller hands, less control.** The chance a hand has none of a given color climbs to about 10%. Teammates run out of colors faster, so trumps and discards show up sooner.
- **Fewer rockets each.** About 39% of five-player hands have no rocket.
- **More tasks to share,** but more people to share them with.

### The five-player handoff

For five players, missions from 25 onward with a golden frame around the number get an extra rule. You can also use it in missions 27 and 37. After cards and tasks are dealt, and before the first trick, one player may hand one of their own task cards to another player. You decide together who gives and who takes, without revealing anything about your cards. The total number of tasks does not change, and using it does not add to your attempt count.

The trick is choosing the handoff without talking about your hand. Use what is public: the task picks, any signals made so far, and what the mission demands.

## Two players: JARVIS

The rulebook says The Crew is best with three or more, but it includes a two-player mode with a third crew member called JARVIS.

How to set it up:

1. Put the 4 rocket aside.
2. From the other 39 cards, deal seven face down in a row. Deal seven more face up, one on top of each. This double row of 14 is JARVIS.
3. Shuffle the 4 rocket into the remaining 25 cards and deal them evenly, 13 to each player.
4. Set out radio tokens, reminder cards, the distress signal token, and the task deck as usual.

How it plays:

- The player with the 4 rocket is commander, with the usual duties.
- The commander also runs JARVIS. They choose JARVIS's tasks and decide which face-up JARVIS card to play on JARVIS's turn, without discussion.
- Only JARVIS's face-up cards can be played. When one is played, the card beneath it is turned up after the trick ends, not before.
- JARVIS sits at the table like any crew member. You decide where.

Two-player play is more of a puzzle than a conversation. JARVIS's open cards give you information you would never have at a larger table, but the commander carries more of the planning.

## Picking a count for your crew

- **New crew:** three or four, for the gentlest start.
- **Experienced crew:** five, for the full challenge of the later missions.
- **Couples:** try JARVIS, or play on Board Game Arena with a group.
