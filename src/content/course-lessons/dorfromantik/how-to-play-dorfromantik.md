Dorfromantik: The Board Game asks you to build a countryside together, one hex tile at a time. Forests, grain fields, villages, a stream, and a railway grow outward from the first tile. The villagers want things: a forest of exactly five tiles, a stream exactly six long. Meet their wishes, close off your flagged areas, and stretch your longest track and stream to score points. Nobody loses. You play to beat your last score.

Designed by Michael Palm and Lukas Zach and published by [Pegasus Spiele](https://pegasus.de/en/Dorfromantik-The-Board-Game-Game-of-the-Year-2023/51240E), [Dorfromantik: The Board Game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BQ3W9GRL?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the Spiel des Jahres in 2023. It is based on the video game from Toukana Interactive, plays 1 to 6, and takes 30 to 60 minutes. This lesson follows the English rulebook, version 1.1, and it all fits on the one-page [Dorfromantik teach sheet](/academy/dorfromantik/teach-sheet/).

## What is in the box?

73 hex tiles: 48 Landscape tiles and 25 Task tiles, 5 for each of the five Task types (Forest, Grain, Village, Track, and Stream). There are 25 Task markers too: for each type, one 4, two 5s, and two 6s. Add a Score pad, a Campaign pad, and 5 small closed boxes in the insert. Leave the boxes shut until the Campaign sheet tells you to open one.

## How do you set up?

![Dorfromantik setup from above: an empty play area in the middle, Task tile stacks and Task marker piles on the left, Landscape tile stacks on the right with 3 tiles going back to the box, the closed boxes at the top, and the pads set aside](/academy/dorfromantik/setup.svg)

1. **Task tiles.** Mix all 25 facedown and make a few stacks at the edge of the table.
2. **Landscape tiles.** Mix them facedown, draw 3, and put them back in the box without looking. They sit out this game. Stack the rest facedown, apart from the Task tiles.
3. **Task markers.** Mix them facedown and sort them into piles by color, one pile per Task type.
4. **Pads.** Set the Score pad and the Campaign pad aside until the end.
5. **Start player.** Pick one. Turns go clockwise.

No tile goes on the table during setup. The first Task tile revealed on turn 1 starts the map.

## What happens on a turn?

On your turn you reveal one tile and place it. The rules decide which kind:

- **In the first 3 turns,** always reveal a Task tile.
- **After that,** count the Task tiles in play that still carry a Task marker. Fewer than 3? Reveal a Task tile. Exactly 3? Reveal a Landscape tile.

A revealed Task tile gets one random marker of its type, number up. Place them together.

Talk it over before you place. The player whose turn it is makes the final call.

## Where can a tile go?

Three rules cover placement:

1. **Touch the map.** Every tile must share at least 1 edge with a tile already in play, so the map stays one connected group.
2. **Tracks and Streams must match.** A Track edge may only touch a Track edge, and a Stream edge only a Stream edge. One facing an empty space is fine.
3. **Everything else is free.** Forest, Grain, Village, and the neutral Meadow edges can touch anything.

You can turn a tile any way you like before placing it.

## How do Task markers work?

A Task marker shows a 4, 5, or 6: exactly how many tiles its area needs. A **territory** is one or more tiles of one type (Forest, Grain, or Village) joined by matching edges. Track and Stream tasks count the tiles in that connected Track or Stream. The Task tile itself counts.

- **Exactly the number?** The task is complete. Move the marker to your Completed Task area. It is banked, even if the area keeps growing.
- **More than the number?** The marker goes back to the box.
- **Closed off short of it?** No open edge left and too few tiles: the marker goes back to the box.

Two limits: a Task tile may not make its area larger than its new marker, or close the area off below that number.

One tile can complete more than one task at once. Every time a marker leaves the map, completed or not, the next turn reveals a Task tile again.

## What are Flags?

Three Landscape tiles carry a Flag: green on a Forest, yellow on Grain, red on a Village. Place them like any other tile. A Flag scores only if its territory is closed off at the end, with no open edges.

## How does the game end?

The game ends the moment you must reveal a Landscape tile and none are left. One exception: if the last Landscape tile completes or cancels a task, keep revealing and placing Task tiles until one no longer completes or cancels a task.

## How do you score?

Fill in a Score sheet:

| Category | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Tasks | The total of every completed Task marker, by type |
| Flags | For each Flag in a closed territory, 1 point per tile in that territory |
| Longest Track | 1 point per tile in your longest unbroken Track |
| Longest Stream | 1 point per tile in your longest unbroken Stream |

The bottom section stays empty until the campaign gives you something to put there. Add it up, then turn to the Campaign sheet: your result decides how far the campaign moves. Lesson 9 explains that sheet, spoiler free.
