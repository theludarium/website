**This lesson is spoiler free,** like the campaign guide. It talks about how to approach the campaign, never about what any box, tile, or card holds. If you have not yet read [how the campaign sheet works](/academy/dorfromantik/dorfromantik-campaign-guide/), start there.

The rulebook closes with a question: how many games will it take to add every new component? There is no fixed answer. It depends on your scores, your choices at the forks, and the Achievement cards you manage. This lesson is about getting there in good spirits.

## Two ways content arrives

Remember the two engines. Your score crosses circles on the Advancement track, and green hexes along it open boxes or bring Achievement cards. Achievement cards set goals inside a game; meet one and you turn it over.

That means a campaign stalls in one of two ways: scores too low to cross many circles, or Achievement cards sitting faceup with no one trying for them. Each has its own fix.

## When scores stall

Your score decides how many circles you cross, and higher rows on the High Score List give more. If several games in a row land on the same row, go back to the review table in the [high score lesson](/academy/dorfromantik/dorfromantik-high-score-strategy/) and find the weak number. Often it is flow: too few Task tiles revealed.

Two small changes often help a stalled campaign:

- **Start every game by naming the waiting areas.** Say which types you will keep a 3-tile area ready for.
- **Call the endgame out loud** when the stack hits about a dozen tiles.

## When Achievement cards sit unclaimed

An Achievement card is only useful if someone is trying for it. At the start of each game, read every faceup card aloud. Pick one, maybe two, as the group's goal for this game. Then make placements that serve it when the cost is small.

Some goals pull against your score. That is fine. A slightly lower score that turns a card over can move the campaign more than a few extra points would. Talk it through before the game, so nobody feels the goal was forced on them mid-game.

## Choosing at the forks

After the second green hex, the Advancement track forks, and you choose which circles to cross. A few spoiler-free ways to choose:

- **Follow the table's taste.** If your group loves one part of the game, lean toward the branch that seems to build on it.
- **Finish a branch before starting another.** Spreading circles across branches can leave every branch a few short of its next green hex.
- **Count before you cross.** With a known number of circles to spend, check which green hex you can actually reach this time.

The rulebook's own example shows a group choosing between branches after a game, and notes they could have chosen differently. There is no wrong path, only a slower one.

## Folding in new content

Each time something new arrives, read its card in full before the next game, and do it as a group. Then ask the high score questions again: how does this change flow, waiting areas, the long lines, and the Flags? New content changes what a good placement looks like. A group that talks it through once plays it better every game after.

Keep the Score sheet honest too. The rulebook suggests checking off, before each game, the earned parts you will score at the end. It is easy to forget a scoring line you only just gained.

## Keeping a long campaign fun

- **Keep the same core group if you can.** The campaign sheet tracks one group's story. Guests are welcome; the sheet just keeps going.
- **Run a second campaign for a second group.** The rulebook lets you pause one and start another. Each has its own sheet, and each box's Components card tells you where every piece goes back.
- **Mark the moments.** The sheet already holds a game number for each result. A date and the players' names turn it into a keepsake.
- **Do not grind.** If a session feels like work, stop after one game. The campaign will wait.

## When the last box is open

Once every box is open, the High Score List is still there, and the top rows are the hardest goal the box has. Many groups keep playing for score. Others move on to the standalone sequel, covered in [player counts, expansions, and the video game](/academy/dorfromantik/dorfromantik-player-count-expansions/).
