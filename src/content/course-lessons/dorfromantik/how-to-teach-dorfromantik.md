Dorfromantik is gentle to learn and easy to over-teach. The rules fit on a page, but there are enough little cases (closed territories, cancelled markers, the ending) that a host can talk for twenty minutes before anyone touches a tile. Do not. Teach what the first turn needs, then teach the rest when the map asks for it.

Print the [Dorfromantik teach sheet](/academy/dorfromantik/teach-sheet/) and set it where everyone can read it. Need the rules yourself first? Start with [how to play Dorfromantik: The Board Game](/academy/dorfromantik/how-to-play-dorfromantik/). Our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) pairs well with this one.

## Before anyone sits down

**Set up from the sheet.** Mix and stack the Task tiles, mix the Landscape tiles, and return 3 of them to the box unseen. Sort the Task markers facedown into piles by color.

**Keep the boxes closed.** If this is a new copy, tell the table now that the 5 small boxes are a campaign and nobody peeks. That one sentence protects a lot of fun later.

**Clear a big space.** The map grows in every direction. A cramped table makes people place tiles to fit the table instead of the map.

**Prefer an app?** The rulebook points to an interactive tutorial on Dized as an alternative to reading it.

## The ten-minute teach

**Minute 1: the goal.** "We're building one countryside together. Nobody wins or loses. We score points at the end and try to beat our score next time."

**Minutes 2 and 3: the turn.** Hold up a Task tile. "The first three turns, we reveal one of these and give it a random marker of its type. After that, as long as three of these are sitting on the map with markers, we draw a Landscape tile instead. When a task leaves the map, we draw Task tiles again until we're back to three."

**Minutes 4 and 5: placing a tile.** "A tile has to touch the map. Railway and stream edges have to meet their own kind. Everything else, forest, fields, villages, meadows, can go next to anything. We all talk about where it goes, and whoever's turn it is decides."

**Minutes 6 and 7: Task markers.** Point at a marker. "This 5 means the forest this tile sits in needs exactly 5 forest tiles connected by forest edges. Hit 5 and we keep the marker. Go to 6 and we lose it. Close the forest off at 4 and we lose it too."

**Minute 8: Flags.** "Three Landscape tiles have a Flag. A flagged area scores a point for every tile in it, but only if it's fully closed in by the end."

**Minute 9: the end and the score.** "When we need a Landscape tile and the stack is empty, we stop. Points for completed tasks, closed flags, our longest track, and our longest stream."

**Minute 10:** "Questions?" Then reveal the first Task tile.

## Teach these when they happen

- **The first completed task.** Move the marker aside with a little ceremony. Point out that the next turn is a Task tile, because there are now fewer than 3 on the map.
- **The first marker lost.** When an area overshoots, return the marker to the box and say why. One lost marker teaches the exact-count rule better than any explanation.
- **The first illegal Task tile spot.** A Task tile cannot go where its area is already bigger than its new marker.
- **The first Flag.** Remind the table that it scores nothing until its territory has no open edges.
- **Ten Landscape tiles left.** Say it out loud. The ending changes how you place.

## Share the decisions

Dorfromantik's rule for decisions is simple: everyone talks, the active player decides. That makes it easy to share, and easy for one confident player to take over. A few habits help:

- **Ask before you suggest.** "Where do you see it going?" gives the active player the first word.
- **Offer two spots, not one.** It leaves a real choice.
- **Let small mistakes stand.** A slightly worse placement made by a newer player is worth more to the evening than a perfect one made for them.

Our piece on [the quarterback problem in cooperative games](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/) goes deeper on this.

## First-game slips to watch for

- Matching Forest, Grain, or Village edges as if they were required. They are not, though matching them is how territories grow.
- Leaving a Stream edge against a Meadow edge. Not allowed.
- Counting a territory through a mismatched edge. Only tiles joined by matching edges count.
- Forgetting that a Task tile counts toward its own task.
- Drawing a Landscape tile when only 2 markers are on the map.

## After the game

Fill in the Score sheet together, then let the newest player mark the Campaign sheet: find the row for your result and cross off that many circles. It is the moment that makes a group want to play again.
