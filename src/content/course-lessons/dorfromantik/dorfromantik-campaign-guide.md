**This lesson is spoiler free.** It explains only what the rulebook explains before anything is opened: how the Campaign sheet works, how new content is earned, and how to pause or restart. It says nothing about what is inside any box or on the back of any card. If you have not opened a box yet, you can read this safely.

[Dorfromantik](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BQ3W9GRL?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a campaign game. Each game stands on its own, but your score feeds a Campaign sheet that adds new content to future games. The rulebook asks two questions at the end: how far can you push your high score, and how many games will it take to open everything? This lesson helps with the second.

## After every game

Score the game on a Score sheet. Then turn to the Campaign sheet and do two things.

**1. Find your row on the High Score List.** Round your result down to the nearest value in the left column. The rulebook's example: 128 points rounds to 120. In the right column, marked with a crown, write the number of this game. You can land on the same row more than once.

**2. Cross off circles.** The middle of that row tells you how many small white circles to cross off on the Advancement track. Higher results cross off more. The lowest row covers 0 to 99 points. Above that, rows step up in tens and then in bigger steps toward the top of the list.

## The Advancement track

The Advancement track is a path of white circles with green hexes along it. Start at the very bottom left, at the hiker, and cross off circles one after another.

- **Skip the green hexes when counting.** They do not use up a cross. You pass through them.
- **Every crossed circle must connect** to the ones before it, directly or through a green hex.
- **After the second green hex, the track forks.** From then on you choose which adjacent circle to cross next, depending on what you want to reach.

That fork is the campaign's main decision. Each branch leads toward different green hexes. Look at what each branch points to before you spend your circles.

## What a green hex does

Reaching a green hex earns something. The rulebook spells out the first two: the first green hex opens Box 1, and the second opens Box 2. All the green hexes after that bring an Achievement card, taken from Box 3.

When you open a box, read its Components card first. It tells you what the box holds and how to add it to the game. What happens next is the surprise, and we will leave it there.

## Achievement cards

An Achievement card goes faceup beside your play area from the next game on, showing a closed lock. Its front names a goal. If you meet that goal during a game, turn the card over and follow the instructions on the back. The Campaign sheet has a list called Your Achievements where you cross off each one you complete.

So the campaign has two engines. Your score moves you along the Advancement track. Your Achievement cards reward specific goals within a game. Some content arrives one way, some the other.

## How new content is used

A few rules apply to everything you add:

- **Cross it off** in the list on the right of the Campaign sheet.
- **Use it in every later game** of that campaign. New content does not come and go.
- **Shuffle new tiles in by kind**, following the card's directions.
- **Some new tiles look like Landscape tiles from the back.** The rulebook asks you to return the 3 random Landscape tiles to the box first, during setup, and only then mix these in.
- **The Score sheet has room for it.** The bottom section, empty in your first games, is where new content scores. Before each game, the rulebook suggests checking off the parts you have already earned so you remember to score them.

## Pausing, restarting, and replaying

The campaign is replayable. The rulebook says you can pause a campaign at any time to start a new one, or to continue a different campaign with another group.

To do that cleanly:

- **Use a fresh Campaign sheet** for each campaign. The pad has 20 sheets, and the [Pegasus product page](https://pegasus.de/en/Dorfromantik-The-Board-Game-Game-of-the-Year-2023/51240E) offers the Campaign pad and Score pad as printable files.
- **Put components back in their boxes.** Each box's Components card lists what belongs in it, so you can sort everything back without guessing.
- **Check the list on the sheet** when you pick up an old campaign. The overview on its right edge shows what that campaign has already earned.

This also means a family can run one campaign with the kids and another with friends, each with its own sheet, from one box.

## A few spoiler-free tips

- **Write the date and the players** on every Score sheet. It turns the pad into a record of your table.
- **Talk about the fork before the game, not after.** Knowing what you are aiming for changes how you play.
- **Do not chase everything at once.** A campaign is many games long. A steady score that crosses a few circles each time gets there.
- **Store the pads in the box.** The insert has a place for them over the boxes.
