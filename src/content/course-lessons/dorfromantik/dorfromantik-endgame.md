Most of a game of Dorfromantik is about growing: bigger territories, longer lines, more tasks. The last stretch flips that. Every tile you still hold is a chance to finish something, and anything left open when the stack runs out scores less or nothing. This lesson is about that turn, and about the rule that can stretch the ending by several tiles.

## How the game ends, exactly

The game ends the moment you are required to reveal a Landscape tile and there are none left. Any Task tiles not yet revealed stay out of the game.

There is one exception, and it is worth points. If the last Landscape tile completes a task, or cancels one, the game continues: you reveal and place Task tiles until you can no longer complete or cancel any more tasks.

Two other cases to know:

- **Task tiles can run out first.** If all 25 are revealed, you simply keep placing Landscape tiles until those are gone.
- **Open tasks at the end score nothing.** Only completed markers count.

## When the endgame starts

Count the Landscape stack (Lesson 7 suggests stacks of 9). Somewhere around 12 tiles left, one stack and a third, most groups should change gear. That number is not in the rulebook; it is a rule of thumb. The reasoning is simple: closing a Flag territory or finishing a 6 can take several tiles, and you do not choose which tiles come.

## The endgame checklist

**1. Close the Flags.** A Flag territory with an open edge scores zero. Look at each one and count its open edges. Any Landscape tile that can close an edge without breaking a task should go there first. If a Flag territory has more open edges than you can realistically close, stop adding to it and close what you can; a lost Flag is lost either way, but its tasks may still pay.

**2. Sort the open tasks.** For each open marker, ask how many tiles it still needs and whether the right tiles are likely to come. A task needing 1 more Forest tile is a good bet. A Stream task needing 3 more Stream tiles with 5 Landscape tiles left is not.

**3. Cancel tasks that cannot finish.** This sounds wrong, but think about what a stuck task does: it holds one of the 3 slots, so you keep drawing Landscape tiles. If you deliberately push a hopeless area past its number, or close it off short, the marker goes back to the box and the next turn reveals a fresh Task tile. That new tile might complete on the spot if a waiting area of the right size is open. You give up points you were not going to get anyway, and you gain a turn that does not use the clock.

**4. Stop growing the longest lines into trouble.** Your Longest Track and Longest Stream still score per tile, so keep extending them, but only where a Track or Stream task is not still open on the same line.

## Setting up the last tile

The exception at the end rewards planning. If the last Landscape tile completes or cancels a task, the next Task tile comes out, and if that one completes or cancels a task too, another comes, and so on.

You cannot know what the last Landscape tile will be. But you can raise the odds that it finishes something:

- **Park tasks one tile short.** Several open tasks of different types, each needing exactly 1 more tile, give the last Landscape tile many ways to complete one.
- **Keep waiting areas open.** After the last Landscape tile, every following tile is a Task tile. A Task tile completes on the spot if it joins an area of the right size: 3 tiles for a 4, 4 tiles for a 5, 5 for a 6. Open areas at those sizes turn the run into a chain.
- **Remember cancelling counts.** The rulebook says completing or cancelling keeps the run going. A tile that pushes a hopeless task over its number still triggers the next Task tile.

The rulebook's own tip points the same way: keep the number of unrevealed Task tiles as low as possible. Every Task tile still in its stack at the end is a few points you never had the chance to score.

## A short worked example

Four Landscape tiles left. Open tasks: a Grain 5 at 4 tiles, a Village 6 at 4 tiles, and a Track 4 at 3 tiles. The red Flag's Village territory has two open edges.

- The next tile has Village edges. It joins the Village: now 5, one short of its 6, and it closes one Flag edge. Good.
- Next, a mostly Meadow tile. It closes the Flag territory's last open edge, but that would close the Village at 5 with its 6 still open. The group decides the Flag (5 points) is worth less than keeping the 6 alive, and places the Meadow elsewhere.
- The last two tiles each have a chance to complete the Grain 5, the Track 4, or the Village 6, and any of those triggers a Task tile.

The trade-off in the middle is the endgame in miniature. Talk it through; the count decides.
