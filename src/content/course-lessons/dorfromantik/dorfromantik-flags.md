Three tiles in a fresh copy of Dorfromantik carry a Flag: a green one on a Forest, a yellow one on Grain, and a red one on a Village. Each can be the biggest single score in your game, or worth nothing at all. The difference is whether its territory is closed when the game ends.

## How a Flag scores

At the end of the game, look at each Flag. If its territory is closed off, the Flag scores 1 point for every tile in that territory. If the territory still has any open edge, the Flag scores nothing.

A territory is all the tiles of that type joined by matching edges, so a green Flag scores the whole Forest it belongs to, not just its own tile. The rulebook's example shows both outcomes: a Grain territory of 5 tiles, closed, scores 5; a Forest with 3 open edges scores 0.

## What closed means

A territory is closed when none of its edges face an empty space. Every edge of that territory must touch a tile, and the touching edge must be something else, such as a Meadow or another terrain. (A Track or Stream edge can never touch it, since those only meet their own kind.) If a Forest edge faces an empty spot on the table, the Forest is open.

That is why closing takes work. Even a compact territory of 6 tiles can have more than a dozen outward edges. Many will be closed by tiles you place anyway. The last two or three are the hard ones, because they need tiles whose edges fit around everything else, including Track and Stream edges that must match.

## The tension: grow or close

Every tile you add to a Flag territory is worth a point, but it also adds open edges to close. Bigger is better only if you can still finish it.

A few habits help:

- **Pick a target.** When the Flag comes out, agree on a size. Something in the range of the Task numbers is a sensible first goal, because it lets the territory pay out for a task too.
- **Combine with tasks.** A Flag territory can also hold Task markers. A Forest that completes a 4, then a 5, then gets closed scores the 4, the 5, and the Flag. Three payouts from one area.
- **Close from the far side first.** Close the edges that are hardest to reach early, while you still have lots of tiles to choose from. Leave the easy edge for last.
- **Use Meadows and other terrain as walls.** Tiles that are mostly another terrain or Meadow are your closing tiles. When one comes up and fits, it is often the best place for it.

## When to stop growing

The stack is your deadline. You start with 45 Landscape tiles in play (48 minus the 3 returned at setup), and every Landscape turn uses one. Because the stacks sit facedown in plain view, you can count what is left at any time.

A rough rule: once about a third of the Landscape tiles remain, stop growing every Flag territory and start closing it. That leaves time for a bad draw or two. Lesson 8 covers the ending in more detail.

## When a Flag never appears

The 3 Landscape tiles returned to the box at setup are random, so a Flag can sit out a game. Lesson 7 does the math: in nearly 1 game in 5, at least one Flag never comes out. If a Flag is missing late in the game, it may be in the box. Do not hold space for it forever.

## Flags and task markers can clash

Closing a territory also ends its tasks. If a Forest still carries an open 6 and you close it at 5, that marker goes back to the box. Before you place the closing tile, check the territory's open markers. Finish them first, or accept the loss on purpose because the Flag is worth more.

This is the kind of trade a group should talk through out loud. One player sees the Flag, another sees the 6. Both are right; the count decides.

## A worked example

A yellow Flag lands on a Grain tile touching one other Grain tile. There is a Grain Task tile with a 5 nearby. The group decides: grow this field to 5, complete the task, then close it.

Two more Grain tiles and the 5-task Task tile join it: 5 tiles, task complete, 5 points banked. Now the field has open edges all around it. Over the next several turns, Meadow-heavy and Forest-heavy tiles go around it. When the last open edge closes, the Flag is worth 5 more. Ten points from five tiles.
