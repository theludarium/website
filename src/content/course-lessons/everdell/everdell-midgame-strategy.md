By summer, every city at the table has a shape. Some players have green cards humming. Others have a pile of resources and a plan. The opening was about investing. The midgame is about making each card you play do two jobs, and about watching what everyone else is about to do.

## What summer gives you

Preparing for summer brings all your workers home, adds 1 more (4 in all), and lets you draw up to 2 cards from the Meadow. Nothing produces.

Those 2 Meadow cards are a quiet gift. You choose them, they are face up, and they can be exactly the cards your city needs next. Two habits help:

- **Leave room in your hand.** With 8 cards already, you draw nothing. With 7, you draw 1.
- **Look before you prepare.** The Meadow changes every turn. Prepare for summer on a turn when it holds cards you want.

## Blue cards: rewards and discounts

Blue Governance cards come in two flavors.

**Rewards.** Some pay you each time you play a certain kind of card. The Courthouse gives a twig, resin, or pebble after you play a Construction. The Shopkeeper gives a berry after you play a Critter. The Historian draws you a card after you play either. In summer, when you play several cards a season, these add up fast.

**Discounts.** Others make cards cheaper. The Crane can be discarded from your city to take 3 resources off a Construction. The Innkeeper does the same for a Critter's berries. The Judge lets you swap one resource in a cost for another. Crane and Innkeeper can't be combined with other card-playing abilities, so use each one on its own big play.

A city with two or three reward cards turns every summer play into a small bonus. That is what an Everdell engine looks like.

## Destinations: spaces only you control

Red Destination cards are worker spaces in your own city. Nobody else can use them unless the card shows an Open sign. A few examples from the base game:

- **Inn:** play a Meadow card for 3 fewer resources.
- **Post Office:** give an opponent 2 cards, discard any number, then draw back up to your hand limit.
- **Lookout:** copy any basic or Forest location, even one where one of your own workers already sits.
- **University:** discard a card from your city, get its cost back, plus 1 resource and 1 point.

Cards with an Open sign let opponents visit too, and you gain a point token every time they do. If an opponent's Open card is the best space on the board this turn, use it. They gain a point; you gain the action.

## The Meadow is everyone's hand

Eight face-up cards, refilled the moment one leaves. Everyone at the table is shopping from the same shelf.

Watch what each opponent would want. A player with two Farms might want a Barge Toad or a General Store. A player one red card short of Grand Tour wants almost any Destination. Playing the card they need, when it also fits your city, is a normal and fair move.

One rule from the consolidated rulebook in the Complete Collection matters here: you cannot place a worker only to block a space. You must be able to use some or all of what it gives.

## Reading the table

Before each turn in summer, ask three questions:

1. **Which Events is each player close to?** Count colors in their cities. Only one player gets each Event.
2. **Who is about to prepare?** A player with every worker out is about to free up the spaces they hold.
3. **How many spaces does each city have left?** A player with three open spaces is choosing their final cards. One with eight is still building.

## Making room in a full city

Summer is when cities fill. If yours is crowded with cheap spring cards, two cards help:

- **Ruins:** discard a Construction from your city, get its cost back, draw 2 cards, and put the Ruins in its place.
- **University:** the Destination above, which removes a card and refunds it.

Trading a 1-point card for its cost back, then playing a better card in that space, is often worth a turn.

## Cards versus workers

You always have a choice between placing a worker and playing a card. In summer, both are strong, so ask which one can wait. A worker space you need that is about to be taken cannot wait. A card in your hand usually can. A card in the Meadow that someone else wants cannot.
