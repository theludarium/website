Everdell's final count surprises new players. A city that looked modest turns out to hold three purple cards, two Events, and a worker on the 5-point space, and it wins by ten. None of that is hidden. You can count it before autumn ends and make your last turns count.

## Where the points come from

At the end, every player adds five things:

| Category | What scores |
| --- | --- |
| Base points | The number printed on each card in your city |
| Point tokens | Every token you gathered during the game |
| Prosperity bonuses | The extra points on your purple cards |
| The autumn trail | 2, 3, 4, or 5 points for each worker you sent there |
| Events | 3 for each basic Event, plus whatever each special Event says |

The rulebook's own example shows how spread out this gets. Its 62-point city scores 22 from printed card values, 14 from point tokens, 10 from purple bonuses, 4 from a worker on the trail, and 12 from Events. Only about a third of the total came from the numbers printed on the cards.

## Autumn: the big season

Preparing for autumn brings your workers home, adds 2 more, and activates every green Production card for the last time. With 6 workers, autumn has more placements than winter and spring combined.

That Production is your last chance to turn resources into points with green cards. A Woodcarver pays up to 3 twigs for 1 point token each. A Doctor does the same with berries. A Monk gives up to 2 berries to an opponent for 2 point tokens each. If you have any of these, prepare for autumn with the resources they need already in hand.

## Purple Prosperity math

Purple cards score their printed points plus a bonus. The base game bonuses:

- **Castle:** 1 per Common Construction in your city.
- **Palace:** 1 per Unique Construction, counting itself.
- **School:** 1 per Common Critter.
- **Theater:** 1 per Unique Critter.
- **Ever Tree:** 1 per purple card, counting itself.
- **King:** 1 per basic Event and 2 per special Event you achieved.
- **Architect:** 1 per leftover resin and pebble, up to 6.
- **Gatherer:** 3 if paired with a Harvester.

Because each one counts something, a purple card is worth more the earlier you know you will play it. A player planning a Castle builds Common Constructions on purpose.

## The autumn trail

The trail at the bottom of the board opens in autumn. To place a worker there, discard cards from your hand equal to the space's number. The 5, 4, and 3 spaces are exclusive; the 2 space is shared. The worker stays and scores those points at the end.

Cards left in your hand at the end score nothing, so the trail is how you cash them in. A 5-card hand on the 5 space is 5 points for one worker. Players reach autumn at different times, and the first one there gets first pick of the 5 space. That is one reason to keep cards in hand late in the game rather than trade them at the Haven.

## Filling your last spaces

Your city holds 15 cards. When you have three or four spaces left, plan them as a set:

- **Biggest points last?** Not always. A purple card that counts other cards wants those cards in place first, but the purple card itself can come last.
- **Free spaces.** Events never use a space. The Wanderer takes no space. A Harvester and Gatherer share one.
- **Free cards.** The Queen, a Destination, lets you play a card worth up to 3 base points for free. The Postal Pigeon reveals 2 cards and lets you play one worth up to 3 points free.

A full city with nothing left to play is a fine way to end. An empty space at the end is points left on the table.

## Leftovers

Resources left at the end score nothing, except for the Architect and the tiebreaker. In your last few turns, spend them: on a card, on a green card's conversion, or on an Event's cost.

Passing also has a rule worth remembering. Once you pass, nobody can give you cards or resources. If an opponent's power would give you something after you pass, they must give it to a player who has not passed, or discard it if nobody can take it.

## Ties

If players tie, the player with the most Events wins. If still tied, the most leftover resources wins. That is the one time a spare pebble matters.

## Counting cleanly

Final scoring is where small errors creep in. The World Boardgaming Championships event uses score sheets and asks another player to confirm each category before cards go back in the box. It works at home too:

1. Count base points on every card.
2. Add point tokens.
3. Add each purple bonus, one card at a time.
4. Add trail workers and Events.

## A last-turn checklist

1. Do I have open city spaces, and a card for each?
2. Is there a trail space I can still reach with the cards in my hand?
3. Can a green card or an Event turn my leftover resources into points?
4. Am I ready to pass, or is there one more action worth taking?
