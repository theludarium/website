The World Boardgaming Championships Everdell event plays four players to a table where possible, with seats assigned at random, base game only, and two hours per round, according to its [2026 preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/evd.html). If you want to compete, this is the game to learn. It is the same game you play at home, but four players changes its rhythm.

## What changes at four

From the rulebook setup and placement rules:

- **4 Forest cards, and each opens a second space.** But never two workers of the same color on one Forest card.
- **Starting hands run 5, 6, 7, and 8 cards** by seat.
- **Three rivals** for every exclusive space, every Meadow card, and every Event.

What follows:

- **The Meadow turns over fast.** A card you leave for next turn has three chances to be taken before you return.
- **Events go early.** Three other cities are counting colors too.
- **Spaces open and close constantly,** as players prepare at different times.

## Seat order

The tournament randomizes seats, so plan for any of them.

- **First seat** starts with 5 cards and moves first. You get first pick of the open spaces on the opening turn, but the fewest cards to choose from.
- **Fourth seat** starts with 8 cards, a full hand. You move last in the opening, so the spaces you want may be gone, but you have the most cards to choose from and the most to discard at the Haven or spend on the autumn trail later.

Read your seat as a plan. With 5 cards, the first placements often should draw cards. With 8, your hand already has options; your early placements can buy resources for them.

## Reading three cities

At two players you watch one city. At four, the skill is tracking three without slowing the game. Keep it simple and track only what decides races:

1. **Colors toward basic Events.** Who has 3 green, 2 red, 2 blue?
2. **Cards named by the special Events.** Who holds one half of a pair?
3. **Open spaces in each city.** Who is filling up, and who is still building?
4. **Workers out.** Who is about to prepare and free their spaces?

When two opponents race for the same Event, a third player often benefits. Let them spend their turns on each other, and take something uncontested.

## Events at four

With three rivals, basic Events are often gone by summer. If one fits your city, aim to claim it in spring or early summer. Special Events usually last longer, because they need exact cards, but every player can see them, so treat the last card of a pair as urgent.

## Open cards and point tokens

At four players, an Open Destination in your city gets more visitors, and you gain a point each time. And when you visit an opponent's Open card, the point you give them goes to one of three rivals, not the only one. That makes visiting cheaper than at two, and building Open cards more rewarding.

## Pace

The WBC preview asks for a brisk pace and notes that many tables ran close to the time limit the year before. Two hours at four players leaves room for a full game, but not for long pauses. Three habits:

- **Plan on other players' turns.** Pick your next move, and a backup, before your turn comes.
- **Keep payments clear.** The preview asks players to make each resource gain and payment clear. It also speeds the game.
- **Know the cards.** Every card you have to read during the game costs time. Reading every card in the base deck at home, using the reading-a-card lesson, saves minutes at the table.

## Scoring by placing

The WBC event ranks players by heat wins first, then by placing points: 1,000 for a first, 100 for a second, 10 for a third, and 1 for a fourth. Among players with the same record, the game master's tiebreakers include the margin of your wins and, in games you did not win, how close you finished to the winner.

So at a tournament table:

- **A clear second beats a close third.** The 100 points for second are ten times the 10 for third.
- **Margins matter.** When you win, win by as much as you can. When you lose, finish as close as you can.
- **Keep scoring all game.** A game you will not win is still worth playing hard to the last action.

The full ranking format and game-level tiebreakers are in the tournaments lesson.

## Tie at the table

The rulebook breaks a tie by most Events, then most leftover resources. The WBC adds three more for the tournament: cards left in hand, then fewest cards in your city, then a die roll. In a close game, a spare card in hand at the end is worth keeping if you have no better use for it.
