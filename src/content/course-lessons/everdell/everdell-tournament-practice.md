You know the rules, the openings, the combos, the scoring, the strategy paths, and how four-player tables play. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: four players, base game only, two hours, and a score sheet checked by someone else. This lesson gives you a six-week plan to get there.

The plan assumes about three to four hours a week. Scale it up or down, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The main in-person event is the Everdell tournament at the [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/), covered in the [tournaments lesson](/academy/everdell/everdell-tournaments/). It welcomes beginners, plays the base game at four-player tables, and runs two heats, a semifinal, and a final. Check the next year's event list for its dates and rules, because formats can change from year to year.

If WBC is not in reach, a local store's tournament or a group of friends playing a short series under the same rules works just as well as a target.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play as many base-game Everdell games as you can, at three or four players where possible. The [digital edition](https://direwolfdigital.com/everdell/) plays fast, scores for you, and has opponents at any hour. Use it for reps, and track your score in each game.

**2. The clock.** At least one session a week, play a four-player game across a real table, base game only, with a two-hour limit. Use the tournament habits from the tournaments lesson: plan on others' turns, make each gain and payment clear, and remind each other of easy-to-miss rules like the 2 Meadow cards at the summer prepare.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Scoring drill.** Take the end of any game, physical or digital, and score each city by hand, category by category: base points, point tokens, purple bonuses, the autumn trail, Events. Check your total against the real one. The WBC event provides score sheets and asks another player to confirm each category, so practice both roles.
- **Card drill.** Shuffle the deck, flip a card, and say its cost, color, Common or Unique, points, and the card it links to, using the routine from the [reading-a-card lesson](/academy/everdell/how-to-read-an-everdell-card/). Then name one card it combos with.
- **Opening drill.** Deal a starting hand of 5 to 8 cards, a Meadow, and Forest cards. Without playing, decide your strategy path and your first three placements. Do it again with a new deal. It builds the read that the [strategy paths lesson](/academy/everdell/everdell-strategy-paths/) describes.

**4. Review.** After every game, ask three questions, and write the answers down:

1. Which two cards in my city earned the least for their space, and what could have gone there?
2. Which Event did I miss, and who claimed it, when?
3. When did I prepare each season, and would a different turn have been better?

Patterns show up fast. A mistake you see three times is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: foundations.** Volume and review, plus the card drill every session. Focus on the space test from the [card evaluation lesson](/academy/everdell/everdell-card-evaluation/): every card should add more than an average space.

**Week 3: solo pressure.** Add the solo game against Rugwort. Year 3, Rugwort the Rapscallion, takes one of your workers in autumn, so you have to plan autumn with 5 workers. Beating him is a good test of whether your city is efficient.

**Week 4: the clock.** Two timed four-player games this week. Your goal is to finish every game inside two hours, and to know where the time went. Watch for long turns early in the game that leave the table rushing in autumn.

**Week 5: event rules.** Play every practice game exactly as the event plays: base game only, the rulebook and its FAQ, random seats, score sheets with a checker, and the tournament's tiebreakers. If your group wants to try the event's one optional change, remove the Fool only if everyone agrees.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Play a few games in the first half of the week, then ease off. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers from week to week:

- **Average score** in four-player games.
- **Average margin,** win or loss, since tournament tiebreakers reward it.
- **Empty city spaces** at the end. The target is zero.
- **Game length** in timed games, to be sure you finish comfortably inside two hours.

If your margin is rising and your city is full at the end, the plan is working, whatever your results.

## The table around the tree

Tournament prep can make a cozy game feel like work. Keep one thing in view. Every game in this plan is a game with other people, often people who love this valley as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the closest finishes afterward. If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

That is the end of the course. You can play, teach, and read any card; open with green cards and make every worker pay; race for Events; build combos and count your score; choose a path, value each space, and time the seasons; win at two and at four; and walk into a tournament ready. The game is the reason. The people around the tree are the point.
