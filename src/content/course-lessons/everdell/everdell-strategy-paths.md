By Level 3 you know the cards, the combos, and the scoring. The next step is choosing, early, where most of your points will come from. Everdell scores in five categories: base points, point tokens, purple bonuses, the autumn trail, and Events. A city that tries to max all five usually finishes thin in each. A city that leans hard into two usually wins.

This lesson describes four paths that come up again and again in the base game, what feeds each one, and how to tell which one your cards are offering.

## Why one path beats a balanced city

You have about 15 placements and 15 spaces. Each path needs a few specific cards to come together, and the payoff grows with each piece. A Castle with two Common Constructions scores a little. A Castle with seven scores a lot. Splitting your city between a half-built Castle plan and a half-built Event plan gets you the small version of both.

The rulebook's own scoring example makes the point: the 62-point city got 22 points from printed card values, 14 from point tokens, 10 from purple bonuses, 12 from Events, and 4 from the trail. No single category was more than about a third of the total, but tokens, purple, and Events together beat the printed points.

## Path 1: the Production engine

**The idea:** green cards early, so they pay three times, then turn their output into a big city in summer and autumn.

**Key cards:** Farm, General Store, Barge Toad, Mine, Resin Refinery, Twig Barge, Chip Sweep, and a converter like the Woodcarver or Doctor to turn late resources into points.

**Scores through:** base points from a full city, Harvest Festival, point tokens from converters, and often a Castle if many of your greens are Common.

**Signals:** two or more cheap green cards in your opening hand, or a Forest card that pays the resources your greens cost.

## Path 2: the Event hunter

**The idea:** points that never take a city space. Build toward two basic Events and at least one special Event, then cash them in with the King.

**Key cards:** whichever colors your Events need, the cards named on the special Events, and the King, who scores 1 per basic Event and 2 per special Event.

**Scores through:** Events, the King's bonus, and the tiebreaker if it comes to that.

**Signals:** a card in your opening hand that is named on a special Event, or a hand already strong in one color.

**The risk:** you are racing the table. Watch every city, and claim the turn you qualify.

## Path 3: the Prosperity city

**The idea:** pick a purple card that counts something, then build the city it counts.

**Key cards:** Castle with Common Constructions, Palace with Unique Constructions, School with Common Critters, Theater with Unique Critters, and the Ever Tree, which counts purple cards, including itself.

**Scores through:** purple bonuses on top of base points. Two purple cards that count the same kind of card stack well. A Castle and a School both like Common cards; a Palace and a Theater both like Unique ones.

**Signals:** a purple card in hand or the Meadow early, and a hand that already leans Common or Unique.

## Path 4: the token city

**The idea:** points as tokens, earned again and again, rather than printed points.

**Key cards:** converters like the Woodcarver, Doctor, and Monk; the Chapel, which grows point tokens on itself; the Clock Tower; and Open red cards in your city that pay you a point each time an opponent visits.

**Scores through:** point tokens, often a lot of them. The rulebook example's 14 token points show how fast they add up.

**Signals:** a converter and its matching producer, or a strong Open Destination that the rest of the table will want.

## Reading your opening

At setup and through winter, look at four things:

1. **Your hand.** Which path do two or three of your cards point toward together?
2. **The Forest cards.** Which resources are easy this game? A Forest card full of berries favors Critter-heavy cities.
3. **The four special Events.** Do any name cards you hold?
4. **The Meadow.** Is a purple card already showing?

Pick the path with the most signals, and keep a second in reserve. Most strong cities are one main path plus a natural partner: Production with Harvest Festival, Prosperity with a matching second purple card, Events with the King.

## When to switch

Commit by summer. Before that, the Meadow can still hand you a better path. After it, a switch usually leaves two half-built plans. A good test in late spring: count how many of the cards your path needs are already in your city or hand. Fewer than half, and a switch is still cheap.
