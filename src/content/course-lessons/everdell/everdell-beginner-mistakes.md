Your first three games of Everdell are where habits form. Some of them are rules mistakes, played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that quietly cost a lot of points. This lesson covers both, checked against the current rulebook and the consolidated rules from the Complete Collection.

## Rules slips about the seasons

**Preparing too early.** You can prepare for season only when all of your workers are deployed. A worker still at home means you place it, or play a card, first.

**Producing in summer.** Green Production cards activate when you prepare for spring and for autumn. Preparing for summer gives you a worker and Meadow cards, but no Production.

**Forgetting the summer draw.** When you prepare for summer, you draw up to 2 cards from the Meadow, if you have room in your hand. It is easy to miss: the World Boardgaming Championships event preview uses it as its example of a rule players should remind each other about.

**Forgetting that green cards pay when played.** A green card activates the moment you play it, in any season, as well as during the spring and autumn Production.

**Waiting for each other.** Players do not change seasons together. One player can be in autumn while another is still in summer.

## Rules slips about cards

**Paying for a free Critter.** If the Construction named on a Critter is already in your city, that Critter is free. Check the corner before you pay berries.

**Using one Construction twice.** Each Construction can bring in its Critter for free only once. The occupied token stays on it, even if that Critter later leaves your city.

**Two of a Unique card.** You can have only one copy of each Unique card in your city. Common cards can repeat.

**Drawing past 8.** Your hand limit is 8, always. If a power tells you to draw more, you stop at 8. If you must give cards to an opponent, pick one with room, and discard whatever nobody can take.

**Refilling the Meadow too soon.** When a power lets you draw several Meadow cards, take them all first, then refill.

## Rules slips about workers

**Claiming an Event you have not earned.** You can place a worker on an Event only if your city meets its requirements at that moment, and you pay any listed cost then. Once it is yours, you keep it, even if the required cards later leave your city.

**Two of your workers on one Forest card.** Never allowed, at any player count. The second space on a Forest card is only open in four-player games.

**Visiting a red card without an Open sign.** You can visit Destination cards in your own city. In someone else's city, only cards with an Open sign, and the owner gets a point token.

**Giving things to a player who passed.** Once a player has passed, nobody can give them cards or resources.

## The big strategy trap: the spring stockpile

New players love gathering. By the end of spring they have a heap of twigs and resin and nothing built. Resources score nothing at the end, except as a tiebreaker or for a card that counts them.

The fix: before you place a worker, name the card that worker is paying for. If you cannot name one, take cards instead of resources.

## The second trap: a city full of small cards

Fifteen spaces feels like plenty in spring. By summer it isn't. Every early card that scores 1 point and does little else is taking a space a better card wanted in autumn.

The fix: in summer, count your open spaces and decide what fills the last few. Remember that Events don't use a space, the Wanderer takes no space, and a Harvester and Gatherer share one.

## The third trap: ignoring Events

New players forget that Events exist. Each basic Event is worth 3 points and asks for a set of one color in your city:

- **Harvest Festival:** 4 green Production cards.
- **Grand Tour:** 3 red Destination cards.
- **City Monument:** 3 blue Governance cards.
- **Cartographers' Expedition:** 3 tan Traveler cards.

Only one player can claim each, so the first one there wins it. Special Events ask for specific cards and score what their card says.

## The fourth trap: treating the Meadow as background

The Meadow is a shared hand of 8 cards. The card you are saving resources for can be gone before your next turn. If a Meadow card is the one you need, buy it now or plan a way to take it, like the summer draw.

## A checklist for game three

Before each prepare for season, ask:

1. Are all my workers out?
2. Is there a card I can still afford and want, especially a green one?
3. Going into summer: do I have room in my hand for 2 Meadow cards?

And when autumn starts, ask one more: which cards will fill my last spaces?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong early. If Everdell feels heavier than your group's usual games, our guide to [what board game weight means](/articles/board-game-weight-explained/) shows where it sits and why the first game is a learning game.
