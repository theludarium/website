Everdell looks like a cozy game, and it is one. It is also a game people play for a title, with an annual tournament at the World Boardgaming Championships and a digital version for playing strangers online. You never need to enter either. But the way tournaments run tells you a lot about how to play well at home.

## Is there an official Everdell circuit?

Not that we could find. The publisher's site lists the conventions where its team exhibits each year, but no organized play program, qualifiers, or championship series for Everdell. The main competitive event is run by players, at the World Boardgaming Championships.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. Everdell has had an event there since 2023. According to the [event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/evd.html), it drew 71 players in 2023, 68 in 2024, and 59 in 2025, and it was on the WBC's 2026 winners list.

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/evd.html) lays out the format:

- **Class B: beginning players welcome.** You do not need a ranking or a long record to enter.
- **Two heats, then a semifinal and a final.** In 2026 the heats ran on July 26 and July 28, with the semifinal and final on July 30.
- **Two hours per round.** The preview asks players to keep a brisk pace, because many tables ran close to the limit the year before.
- **Four players per table** when possible, with seating and seat order randomized.

## How a tournament game runs

The WBC rules are short, and most of them are good habits:

- **Base game only.** No expansions in any round.
- **The rulebook plus the FAQ.** Rules are played as written in the rulebook and the FAQ posted on BoardGameGeek.
- **One optional change.** All copies of the Fool may come out of the deck if every player at the table agrees.
- **Normal setup**, except for how the first player is chosen.
- **Shared rules duty.** Every player helps keep the game state right, with friendly reminders. The preview's own example is the one tables miss most: drawing 2 Meadow cards when you prepare for summer.
- **Careful scoring.** Each table fills in a score sheet, and another player confirms each category before cards or tokens go back.

Ties follow the rulebook first: most Events, then most leftover resources. After that come cards left in hand, then fewest cards in your city, then a die roll.

## How players advance

The event uses a format called Heats Most Wins, Points. Players are ranked first by heat wins. Then each finish earns points: 1,000 for first, 100 for second, 10 for third, and 1 for fourth. A player who took a first, a second, and a fourth would have 1,101.

The top 16 move on to the semifinal, and semifinal winners meet in a four-player final. Among players with the same record, the game master's tiebreakers reward winning by a wide margin and, in games you did not win, finishing close to the winner's score. Every point still matters after first place is settled.

## Online play

The digital edition of Everdell from Dire Wolf launched on Steam in 2022 and is also sold on the Apple App Store, Google Play, and the Nintendo eShop for Switch. It offers cross-platform online multiplayer, AI opponents, and solo challenges. It is the quickest way to play many games against new opponents, and it does the scoring for you.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many stores run open game nights where Everdell comes off the shelf. The publisher's [store locator](https://tycoongames.com/pages/buy-our-games) lists shops that carry its games.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play four-player games, base game only.** That is the tournament format.
- **Play inside two hours.** Decide fast, and plan your next turn while others take theirs.
- **Score on paper.** Fill in each category for every player and check each other.
- **Review your city.** After each game, look at your 15 spaces. Which card scored the least for the space it took, and what could have gone there instead?
