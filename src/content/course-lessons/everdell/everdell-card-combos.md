Most cards in Everdell are fine alone. The game rewards you when they are not alone. A Farm is a berry. A Farm next to a General Store, a Barge Toad, and a Harvester is a small economy. This lesson groups the combos in the base game into five families, so you can spot them in a fresh Meadow instead of memorizing lists. Every card effect here follows the rulebook's card index.

## Family 1: the free Critter pair

Every Critter names a Construction. Build the Construction, and the Critter comes in for free. That is the simplest combo in the game, and it is everywhere.

The rulebook's example is the Courthouse and the Judge. The Courthouse gives you a twig, resin, or pebble after each Construction you play. The Judge lets you swap one resource in any card's cost for another. Together, you build more often and you can almost always pay.

Many pairs are named so you can guess them: the Castle hosts the King, the Palace the Queen, the Inn the Innkeeper, the Post Office the Postal Pigeon, the General Store the Shopkeeper, the Twig Barge the Barge Toad. The Ever Tree is special: it can host any one Critter for free.

What makes a pair a real combo is when both halves help each other beyond the free play. Castle and King both score at the end. Inn and Innkeeper both make Critters cheaper in different ways. Look for pairs like that first.

## Family 2: the Farm chain

The Farm is the center of the most common engine in the base game. On its own it produces 1 berry. Then other cards count it:

- **General Store:** 1 berry, or 2 berries if you have at least one Farm.
- **Barge Toad:** 2 twigs for each Farm in your city.
- **Harvester:** 1 resource of your choice when paired with a Gatherer and with a Farm in your city.

All of these are green, so they produce when played, in spring, and in autumn. Two Farms and a Barge Toad played before your spring prepare is 4 twigs from the Toad at spring and again in autumn, on top of its first payout. That is the payoff of the openings lesson's rule: green cards played early pay three times.

The berries feed your Critters, and the twigs feed your Constructions. The Farm chain pays for the rest of your city.

## Family 3: the copy cards

Two green Critters copy other green cards:

- **Chip Sweep** activates any one green Production card in your city, when played and at each Production.
- **Miner Mole** copies any one green Production card in an opponent's city, and gives you the reward.

Their value is exactly the best green card they can point at. A Chip Sweep next to a strong Barge Toad is a second Barge Toad. With a weak green card, it is a weak card. So play copy cards after the card worth copying, and check opponents' cities before you buy a Miner Mole.

## Family 4: converters that turn resources into points

Some cards change leftover resources into point tokens:

- **Woodcarver:** pay up to 3 twigs for 1 point each, when played and at each Production.
- **Doctor:** pay up to 3 berries for 1 point each, the same way.
- **Monk:** give up to 2 berries to an opponent for 2 points each.

A converter is only as good as the producer behind it. The Woodcarver wants a Twig Barge or a Barge Toad. The Doctor wants Farms. Put a producer and its converter together, and every Production turns into points.

The Monk is the strongest rate here, but it feeds an opponent. More on that trade in the two-player lesson at Level 3.

## Family 5: the reward stack

Blue cards that reward you for playing cards stack with each other:

- **Courthouse:** a twig, resin, or pebble after each Construction.
- **Shopkeeper:** a berry after each Critter.
- **Historian:** a card after each Critter or Construction.

With all three, every card you play gives you something back, and the Historian's card often becomes the next play. Three blue cards also make City Monument. This is the engine the midgame lesson described, and it is strongest when it comes together before summer, the season you play the most cards.

## Payoffs: where combos end

A combo needs somewhere to land. Purple cards are the landing spots:

- **Castle** counts Common Constructions, like Farms and Mines.
- **School** counts Common Critters.
- **Palace** and **Theater** count Unique Constructions and Unique Critters.
- **Ever Tree** counts purple cards.

A Farm chain lands well on a Castle. A city of Unique blue cards lands on a Palace. When you start a combo, glance at which purple card would cash it in, and leave a space for it.

## How to spot a combo in the Meadow

Each time the Meadow changes, ask three questions:

1. Does any card here name a Construction already in my city?
2. Does any card here count something I already have, like Farms?
3. Does any purple card here reward the shape my city is taking?
