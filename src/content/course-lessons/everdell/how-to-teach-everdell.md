Everdell looks busy. There is a tree, a river, four piles of resources, eight cards in the Meadow, and Forest cards on both sides. Hosts see all that and feel they must explain every piece before anyone moves. They don't. The game has three actions and three season changes. Almost everything else is printed on a card.

Here is a script in the order you would say it, with the moments to stop talking. Print the [Everdell teach sheet](/academy/everdell/teach-sheet/) and put it where everyone can see it.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up completely, so the teach starts with the game ready to play. Tree on the stump, deck inside it, 8 cards in the Meadow, Forest cards out, Events placed, resources in piles.

Then pick the first player. The rulebook says the most humble player goes first, which is a fun ten seconds of argument. Deal starting hands in turn order: 5 cards, then 6, 7, and 8. Give each player 2 workers and put their other 4 on the tree.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "We are each building a critter city. The cards you play are your city, and you have room for 15. The game runs from late winter to the end of autumn. Most points wins."

Hold up a worker. "You start with two of these. You will get more each season."

## Minutes 2 to 4: three actions and one example turn

Say: "On your turn you do one thing. Send out a worker, play a card, or, once all your workers are out, bring them home for the next season."

Then do a real turn. Place a worker on the 3 twigs space and take the twigs. "My worker stays here until I prepare for the next season. This space holds one worker, so nobody else can use it until then. Spaces with an open ring are shared."

Point at the Forest cards and the Haven, but don't explain them yet. "These are more places to send workers. The card tells you what you get."

Stop here. Ask: "Questions on placing a worker?"

## Minutes 5 and 6: playing a card

Hold up any Critter and any Construction.

- "To play a card, pay its cost. Critters cost berries. Buildings cost mostly twigs, resin, and pebbles."
- "You can buy from your hand or from these eight cards in the Meadow."
- Point at the corner of the Critter. "See this building name? If you already have that building, this Critter moves in free. Put one of these occupied tokens on the building. Each building does that once."

## Minute 7: the two colors to remember

Don't teach all five colors. Teach two.

- "Green cards are Production. They pay you when you play them, and again every spring and autumn. Green cards played early pay more."
- "Purple cards score bonus points at the end."

Then add: "Red cards are places only you can send workers, unless they show an Open sign. Everything else, read the card."

## Minutes 8 and 9: the seasons

Point at the tree. "When all your workers are out, you take a turn to prepare for the next season. All your workers come home, plus the new ones waiting up here."

- "Spring: one new worker, and your green cards pay out."
- "Summer: one new worker, and you take up to 2 cards from the Meadow."
- "Autumn: two new workers, and your green cards pay out again."

Then the line that saves the most confusion: "We don't change seasons together. You move on when you are ready."

## Minute 10: the end, and go

Say: "Autumn is the last season. When you are out of moves, you pass. Then we add up card points, point tokens, purple bonuses, and Events." Then start.

## Teach the rest live

Explain each of these the first time it matters, not before:

- **Events.** When someone's city first meets an Event's requirements, show how they send a worker to claim it. Basic Events want a set of one card color; special Events name specific cards, like a Woodcarver and a Chapel.
- **Open signs.** The first time someone visits an opponent's red card, give the owner their point token.
- **The Haven.** When someone has cards they cannot use: 2 cards in, 1 resource out.
- **The autumn trail.** Mention it when the first player reaches autumn. Discard cards equal to a space's number, and that worker scores those points.
- **The hand limit.** The first time someone would draw a ninth card, they stop at 8.

## What to leave out

- **Card powers.** The cards explain themselves. Read one aloud only when someone asks.
- **Every Forest card.** Players will read them when they want them.
- **Special Event requirements.** Point to them. Don't list them.
- **Strategy.** Let people find their own first game.

## Coaching during the first game

Help with the rules, not the choices. "That Critter is free because you have its building" is helpful. "You should build the Inn" is playing for them.

Two moments are worth a gentle question. The first is a player in spring sitting on a big pile of resources: "What could you build with that?" The second comes in summer, when a city fills up fast: "How many spaces do you have left?"
