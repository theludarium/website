Cities & Knights has a reputation as a long teach. It does not need to be one. Your players already know how to settle, roll, trade, and build. You only teach what is new, and you teach it in the order the game will throw it at them.

This script runs about ten minutes with the board already set up. Keep the [Cities & Knights teach sheet](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/teach-sheet/) on the table so nobody has to remember costs. If anyone at the table has never played Catan, teach the base game first; our [Catan course](/academy/catan/) has you covered.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up the full board, including the barbarian ship on its start and the robber parked beside the track's last space. Give each player their improvement board with the three cubes at the bottom, their knights, their walls, and a player aid. Pull the development cards and the Largest Army tile out of sight now, so nobody asks.

Leave the starting placement for after the teach. Players place better once they know cities matter more.

## Minute 1: the hook

Say this: "It is Catan, to 13 points instead of 10. But a barbarian fleet is sailing at us. Every city on the island makes it stronger. If we do not have enough knights standing up when it lands, someone loses a city."

Point at the ship and the track. That one picture carries the whole game.

## Minutes 2 to 3: the third die

Hold up the event die. "We roll three dice now. Read this one first."

- "Half its faces are a ship. A ship moves the barbarians one step closer."
- "The other faces are three city gates, one per color. A gate means progress cards might come out."

Then point at an improvement board. "If the gate matches a track and the red die is in the range next to your cube, you draw a card from that deck. Everyone starts at the bottom, so for now, nobody draws. That changes once you build improvements."

## Minutes 3 to 5: cities and commodities

"Settlements produce like always. Cities on forest, pasture, and mountains now take one resource and one commodity: paper, cloth, or coin. Fields and hills still give two of the resource."

Then: "Commodities pay for city improvements. Paper climbs science, cloth climbs trade, coin climbs politics. Level 1 costs one card, level 5 costs five. Level 3 on a track gives you a permanent power, which is printed right on the board. The first to level 4 takes that track's metropolis, worth 2 points. You need a city to improve."

Now explain the starting placement: "You place a settlement and road, then in reverse order a **city** and road. Your city is your first commodity source, so place it with that in mind."

## Minutes 5 to 7: knights

Hold up a basic knight lying down. "Wool and ore buys a knight. It arrives lying down: inactive. One grain stands it up. Only standing knights fight barbarians."

"Wool and ore again promotes a knight one step, to strength 2. Strength 3 needs level 3 in politics."

"An active knight can also do one thing on your turn: move along your roads, push out a weaker enemy knight, or chase the robber. Doing that lays it down again. You cannot stand it up and use it in the same turn."

Keep this short. Lesson 5 goes deep, and your table will learn the knight actions by watching them happen.

## Minutes 7 to 9: the attack

"When the ship lands, count every city on the board. That is the barbarians' strength. Count every standing knight's strength. That is ours."

- "If we match or beat them, whoever gave the most knight strength takes a 1-point token."
- "If they win, whoever gave the least loses a city back to a settlement. A metropolis is safe."
- "Either way, every knight lies down after the attack."

Then the robber: "The first attack also wakes the robber. Until then a 7 still makes you discard if you hold more than 7 cards, but nobody moves the robber or steals. City walls let you hold 2 more cards each."

## Minute 10: progress cards and the finish

"Progress cards are your new development cards. Hold 4 at most. Play them on your turn, any time, and you can play one the turn you draw it. Alchemy is the only one you play before rolling. Victory point cards go face up the moment you draw them."

"First to 13 on their own turn wins. Questions? Let's place."

## While you play

Run the first two rounds out loud. Name the event die result every roll. When the ship is two or three steps out, say so and count the cities. That single habit teaches the defense better than any rule.
