Your first games of Cities & Knights will run long, and that is fine. The bigger risk is quietly playing a rule wrong and learning bad habits from it. This lesson covers the rules slips nearly every new table makes, checked against the 6th edition rulebook, and then the strategy traps that cost the most points.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Resolving production before the event die.** The event die comes first. Move the ship or draw progress cards, and only then hand out resources. If the ship lands, the attack happens before anyone collects.

**Using a knight the turn you activate it.** Activation stands a knight up, but a knight can only take an action if it was already active at the start of your Action phase. You can do it the other way around: use an active knight, which lays it down, then pay a grain to stand it back up.

**Forgetting that attacks lay every knight down.** After each attack, win or lose, every knight on the board goes inactive. A table that forgets this defends the next attack for free.

**Moving the robber before the first attack.** On a 7 before the first attack, players over the limit still discard, but the robber does not move and nobody steals. The first attack sends it to the desert.

**Counting only resources on a 7.** The limit counts resources and commodities together. Seven cards is the line; more than 7 and you discard half, rounded down. Each city wall adds 2.

**Holding progress cards past the limit.** You may hold 4. If you draw a fifth on someone else's turn, discard down right away. On your own turn, you have until the end of your Action phase to play or discard down.

**Hiding a victory point card.** Printing and Constitution go face up the moment you draw them, even on another player's turn. They do not count toward your 4.

**Playing progress cards before the roll.** Only Alchemy is played before rolling. Everything else waits for your Action phase.

**Improving without a city.** You need at least one city on the board to make an improvement. A player whose only city was pillaged keeps their levels but cannot climb until they rebuild.

**Skipping a level.** Cubes climb one space at a time, paying each space's cost. Level 3 science costs 1 paper, then 2, then 3, over three improvements.

**Promoting to mighty too early.** Strong knights become mighty only once you reach level 3 in politics. And any one knight can only be promoted once per turn.

## The big strategic mistake: cities with no knights

New players arrive from base Catan wanting cities, and Cities & Knights rewards cities. So everyone builds them. Each city adds 1 to the barbarians' strength, and nobody has stood up a knight. The ship lands, the island loses, and the players who gave the least each lose a city.

Count before the ship arrives. If there are 6 cities on the board, the knights need a total strength of 6 to hold. If you are the player with zero active knights, you count as giving the least, whoever else is short.

The fix is not to stop building cities. It is to keep one or two knights in play early and stand them up before the ship is close. A basic knight costs 1 wool and 1 ore, and standing it up costs 1 grain. A lost city costs 2 grain and 3 ore to rebuild, plus the point and the commodities it would have made in between.

## Hoarding commodities

Commodities feel precious, so new players sit on them. Then a 7 comes and half the hand goes back. Spend commodities on improvements when you can afford the next level. Early levels are cheap: the first level of any track costs 1 commodity, and it starts your progress card draws.

## Ignoring the tracks

Some players treat improvements as a late-game luxury. But an improvement is permanent, it draws you cards for the rest of the game, and level 4 is the doorway to a metropolis. A player who never climbs a track has few ways to reach 13.

## Spreading thin

The opposite trap is a little of everything: one level of science, one of trade, one of politics. Each track pays more as it climbs, and the level 3 abilities and the metropolises go to depth, not breadth. Lesson 9 covers choosing a track.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before each turn, ask:

1. How far is the ship, and how many cities are on the board?
2. Is my knight strength enough that I will not give the least?
3. Can I afford the next level on my best track?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong early. For the base-game version of these habits, our Catan course lesson on [beginner mistakes](/academy/catan/beginner-mistakes/) is a good companion.
