You have the rules, the strategy, and now the tournament shape: [scoring](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-tournament-scoring/), the [clock](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-under-the-clock/), and [table talk](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-table-talk/). This lesson turns them into a plan. It assumes you have about eight weeks before an event and can play one live game a week, plus some online play. Stretch or squeeze it to fit your calendar.

## Where to play online

Online games give you volume, and volume is how the routine from the clock lesson becomes automatic. Three places to look:

- **CATAN Universe,** the official digital edition, lists Cities & Knights among its expansions. You can play other people or computer opponents.
- **Board Game Arena.** Catan's own site describes a one to one adaptation of Catan and the Cities & Knights expansion there, and lists access to tournaments among the Premium perks.
- **Colonist,** a popular fan alternative, offers a Cities & Knights mode with its own rules page.

A caution: online versions may differ from the 6th edition rulebook in small ways, from card names to timing. Colonist, for one, uses its own names for some things. Treat online play as practice for decisions and speed, and check any rule that surprises you against the [official rulebook](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/CN3087%20CATAN%E2%80%93Cities%26Knights_%20Rulebook.pdf). The event will play the physical game.

Online play also misses two skills: handling real cards and pieces quickly, and talking to people across a table. That is why the plan keeps a live game every week.

## Weeks 1 and 2: rules fluency

Goal: no rule question stops a game.

- **Live game each week,** untimed. Every time anyone checks the rulebook, write the question down.
- **Online:** two games a week against any opponents. Focus on the barbarian count and progress card timing.
- **Homework:** read the barbarian attack page and the progress card list once more. Most rule arguments at a Cities & Knights table come from those two places.

By the end of week 2, your list of questions should be short and answered.

## Weeks 3 and 4: speed

Goal: your turns are fast without being rushed.

- **Live game each week** with a turn timer. Use the setup from the [base course timer lesson](/academy/catan/beating-the-turn-timer/), 90 seconds a turn plus a 10 minute bank, unless your event has announced its own.
- **Time the whole game.** Write down start and finish. You want to know how long your table takes before an organizer tells you.
- **Online:** play with the fastest turn settings you are comfortable with. Practice the "if 7, if ship" plan on every opponent's turn.

## Weeks 5 and 6: scoring and talk

Goal: you play the format, not just the game.

- **Live game each week,** scored the way your event scores. If you do not know the format yet, score it two ways, wins first and points plus a win bonus, and talk about where the two would differ.
- **Play one game for points only.** Pick a moment when you clearly cannot win, then try to finish with the most points you can, and protect your cities through the last attack.
- **Practice the barbarian call.** Every time the ship moves, someone says the count out loud. Rotate who does it.

## Week 7: the mock event

Goal: feel the whole day once before it counts.

Play two or three games in one sitting, under event conditions: the timer, the scoring, the house rules your event uses, and short breaks between rounds. Keep a running standings sheet. Notice when you get tired and what your play looks like after the second game. Eat and drink the way you plan to on the day.

## Week 8: rest and review

Play one relaxed game, or none. Then go through your notes with the [reviewing your games](/academy/catan/reviewing-your-games/) method from the base course: find the turn you lost each game, and the one habit you most want to keep. Write those on a card you will bring to the event.
