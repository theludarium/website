Welcome to Level 4. Everything you learned in Levels 0 to 3 still holds at a tournament. What changes is how a game turns into a result on a standings sheet. In a home game, there is a winner and everyone else. At an event, your 11 points in a loss may count for a lot, or for nothing, depending on one paragraph in the event rules.

So the first skill of a tournament player is boring and vital: find that paragraph and read it.

## Two shapes of scoring

Cities & Knights events are not part of the official Catan championship ladder the [base course describes](/academy/catan/how-catan-tournaments-work/). That ladder plays the base game, and its standings rank wins first, then points. Cities & Knights events are run by conventions and organizers, and each one sets its own format. Most fall into one of two shapes.

**Wins first.** Standings sort by games won, and points only break ties. This is how the base Catan ladder works, and the [play for points lesson](/academy/catan/play-for-points/) covers it in depth.

**Points with a win bonus.** Every victory point you score counts toward a running total, and the table winner gets a bonus on top. The Mind Sports Olympiad's 2026 Cities & Knights event used this shape: victory points scored in the game, up to 13, counted toward your tournament score, the winner of each table got two bonus points, and the player with the most tournament points over the event won. Four players sat at each table, with two rounds and then an evening final for the qualifiers.

## The math of points plus a bonus

Under that format, a table win is worth 13 plus 2, so 15. A loss is worth whatever you finished with. Now compare two players after two rounds:

| Player | Round 1 | Round 2 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Wins: 13 + 2 = 15 | Collapses: 7 | 22 |
| B | Loses with 12 | Loses with 11 | 23 |

Player B never won a game and is ahead. In a wins first format, A would lead by a full win.

The lesson is not "stop trying to win." The win bonus is real, and 15 is the most any one game can give you. The lesson is that the bonus is small next to the game itself. Two points of bonus is the same as two points you scraped together in a game you were never going to win. Every round, your floor matters as much as your ceiling.

Everyone starts a Cities & Knights game with 3 points already on the board: a settlement worth 1 and a city worth 2. So the real range at a table runs from about 3 to 13, and the difference between a bad game and a decent one is often 5 or 6 tournament points.

## Points the barbarians can take back

Here is what makes Cities & Knights scoring different from base Catan. Some of your points are safe, and some are not.

- **A city can be pillaged.** When the barbarians win, the player or players who contributed the least active knight strength lose a city, which drops to a settlement. That is 1 point gone.
- **A metropolis cannot be pillaged.** A city with a metropolis is worth 4 points in all, and the barbarians cannot touch it.
- **Defending pays.** When the knights win, the player who contributed the most strength alone takes a VP token worth 1 point.
- **Victory Point progress cards are played face up** the moment you draw them, so those points are banked at once.
- **The merchant and the Longest Route tile move.** Both can be taken from you by another player.

At a home game, losing a city in the last barbarian attack is a shrug if you were not going to win anyway. In a points format, that shrug costs you a tournament point. So late in a game you cannot win, the cheapest point you can protect is often worth more than a new point you might build. Activate a knight before the ship lands. Keep your share of the defense above the lowest player's. The [barbarians lesson](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarians-and-defending-catan/) and the [barbarian timing math](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarian-timing-math/) give you the tools.

## What to ask before round one

Event pages are often thin. Catan's own Gen Con 2025 page lists a Cities & Knights Casual Tournament in the organized play hall, but says nothing about format or scoring. So ask at check-in, or write to the organizer before the event:

1. Do standings sort by wins first, or by total points?
2. Is there a bonus for a table win, and how big?
3. Are points capped at 13, and what happens if a game ends on time?
4. Is there a final, and do earlier points carry into it?
5. Any house rules? The Mind Sports Olympiad, for one, re-rolls 7s until every player has taken two turns, to keep games from running long.
6. Which rulebook edition is in use?

Write the answers on the inside cover of your notebook. They decide how you play every close call that day.
