Every Cities & Knights game has a clock built into it: the barbarian ship. It moves when the event die shows a ship, and when it lands, the whole island is tested at once. This lesson covers the attack rules from the 6th edition rulebook, the odds of the ship moving, and how to make sure you are never the one who pays.

## How the ship moves

The event die shows a ship on 3 of its 6 faces, so on any roll there is a 1 in 2 chance the ship moves one space. Over a full round of a 4-player game (4 rolls), the ship moves about 2 spaces on average.

<!-- VERIFY: the 6th edition rulebook does not print the die faces; confirm on the physical event die that 3 of 6 faces show the ship and 1 face each shows the science, trade, and politics gates. -->

When the ship reaches the last space, the barbarians attack during the Roll Dice phase, before anyone collects resources. Then the ship goes back to the start and the clock begins again.

Alchemy does not help or hurt here. It sets the two production dice, but the event die is still rolled as normal.

## The math of an attack

Two numbers decide everything.

**The barbarians' strength** is the number of cities on the board, counting every player's cities and metropolises. Settlements do not count.

**The defenders' strength** is the total strength of every active knight on the board: basic 1, strong 2, mighty 3. Inactive knights count for nothing.

If defenders are greater than or equal to barbarians, the island holds. Ties go to the knights.

## When the island holds

The player who gave the most active knight strength takes a VP token worth 1 point. These are often called Defender points.

If two or more players tie for the most, nobody takes the token. Instead, each tied player, starting with the player whose turn it is, draws a progress card from the deck of their choice.

That tie rule matters. If you are chasing the point, you need to beat the next best defender outright, not match them.

## When the barbarians win

The player who gave the least active knight strength has one city pillaged: it becomes a settlement. If several players tie for the least, each of them loses a city.

Some details the rulebook spells out:

- **Zero active knights always counts as the least.** If you have none standing, you are in the target group.
- **Metropolises cannot be pillaged.** A player whose only city is a metropolis is skipped, and the next lowest player is pillaged instead.
- **No city, no loss.** A player with only settlements cannot be pillaged, so the next lowest player is checked, and so on, until a city is pillaged.
- **Walls fall with the city.** A pillaged city's wall goes back to its owner's supply, ready to build again.
- **Out of settlements?** Turn the city piece on its side to show a settlement. You must rebuild that one into a city before upgrading any other settlement.

## After every attack

Win or lose, two things happen. The ship returns to its starting space. And every knight on the board lies down. Everyone who wants to defend the next attack has to pay grain again.

The first attack also moves the robber from beside the track onto the desert. From then on, 7s move the robber and steal as in base Catan.

## A worked example

Four players, 7 cities on the board, so the barbarians have strength 7. Active knights:

| Player | Cities | Active strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 3 | 3 |
| B | 2 | 2 |
| C | 1 | 1 |
| D | 1 | 0 |

Defenders total 6, less than 7. The barbarians win. D has zero active knights, so D gives the least and loses a city. If D had stood up one basic knight, the total would be 7, the island would hold, and A would take the Defender point for giving the most.

That one grain would have been worth a city to D and a point to A. That is the whole game in miniature.

## How to never be the target

1. **Count every time the ship moves.** Cities on the board, active strength on the board. Know the gap.
2. **Know who is lowest.** If the island will lose, you only need to not be the lowest. One more strength than the weakest defender is enough.
3. **If the island will hold anyway, decide about the point.** Beating the top defender by 1 earns a point. Matching them earns a progress card each, if you are tied for most. Neither earns you anything if you are somewhere in the middle.
4. **Stand knights up late, but not too late.** Activation costs grain. Paying it the turn before the ship is likely to land keeps your grain flexible, but a lucky run of ships can catch you. When the ship is two spaces out, every roll has a 1 in 2 chance to move it.
5. **Remember the reset.** After each attack, everyone pays again. The player who keeps grain coming in can defend every attack.
