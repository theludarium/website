The [table talk lesson](/academy/catan/table-talk-and-negotiation/) in the base course teaches the core skills: persuade with public facts, form short alliances, talk the robber away from you, and stay likable. All of that carries over. This lesson covers what is different at a Cities & Knights table, where the score is out in the open, a shared enemy sails toward the island, and a handful of cards punish whoever is ahead.

## The score is public. The speed is not.

In base Catan, the hard part of [threat assessment](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/) is guessing hidden Victory Point cards. Cities & Knights removes most of that guessing. Victory Point progress cards are played face up the moment they are drawn, and every other point sits on the board: buildings, metropolises, the merchant, the Longest Route tile, and defender VP tokens. Anyone can count anyone's score.

So the question moves from "how many points do they have?" to "how fast can they get the next ones?" Watch for:

- **A track close to level 4.** That player may be one build from a metropolis, which is 2 points that can never be pillaged.
- **A big pile of one commodity.** It usually means a city improvement is coming.
- **A full hand of progress cards.** Up to four cards stay hidden, and that is where surprises live.
- **Cheap points nearby:** a settlement spot, a city upgrade, the merchant.

The leader on the board is not always the threat. The player at 9 with a metropolis coming next turn is more dangerous than the player at 10 with nothing to build.

## The cards that punish the leader

Several politics and trade cards in the 6th edition aim at players with more points than the one who plays them:

- **Wedding:** each player with more points than you gives you 2 cards of their choice.
- **Sabotage:** each player with as many points as you, or more, discards half their resource and commodity cards.
- **Guild Dues:** you look at the hand of a player with more points and take any 2 cards.

That has a real effect on tactics. Moving one point ahead of the table early does not just make you a target for talk. It makes you a legal target for these cards, and Sabotage hits ties too. Sometimes the strong play is to hold a point you could take, such as a city upgrade, until the turn it gets you somewhere. And when you are behind, keep these cards. They are your comeback.

## Talking about the barbarians

The barbarian defense is a shared problem with a selfish twist. If the defense holds, the one player who contributed the most strength takes a VP token, unless there is a tie. If it fails, the players who contributed the least lose a city. Everyone wants Catan defended. Everyone would like someone else to pay for it.

Good table talk here is plain arithmetic said out loud. "Five cities on the board, four knights active. One more holds it." That turns a quiet standoff into a decision the table can see. If you are the one who can activate cheaply, you can offer it, and ask a trade for it.

Know the traps. Treason makes a player remove a knight of their choice, and the player of the card may then place a knight of the same strength or lower. Intrigue lets a player displace a knight without using one of their own. A defense that holds by exactly one point can collapse on the turn before the attack. Build a margin when it is cheap.

## Kingmaking

Late in a game, a player who cannot win can still decide who does: by whom they trade with, where the robber goes, and whether they help defend. In Cities & Knights the defense is a big lever. A trailing player who refuses to activate can turn a held defense into a lost one, and the lowest contributors lose cities.

Points formats soften this. When every point counts toward the standings, as in the [scoring lesson](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-tournament-scoring/), the trailing player has a reason to keep scoring, and protecting their own cities usually means helping the defense. When you are the one behind, play for your points, not for a person. Our article on [kingmaking in board games](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) covers why that line matters.

## Event rules on talk

Table talk rules differ from event to event. The official base Catan tournament rules, summarized in the base course, limit trades between two players who are not taking their turn. A Cities & Knights event may follow those rules or set its own. Ask before round one, along with the scoring questions.
