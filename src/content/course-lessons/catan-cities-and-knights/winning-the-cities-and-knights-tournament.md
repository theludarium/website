By now you know how events score, how to play under a clock, and how to talk at the table. This lesson puts it together across a whole day. The examples use the format the Mind Sports Olympiad published for its 2026 Cities & Knights event: four players a table, two rounds, then a final for qualifiers, with victory points up to 13 counting toward your total and two bonus points for each table win. Swap in your own event's format; the method is the same.

## Keep your own standings

Do not wait for the organizer to post results. After each game, write down your score and, if you can, the scores of the players near you. Then answer one question: what do I need from the next round?

That answer sets your risk. A player who needs a win plays differently from a player who needs 9 points, and both play differently from a player who needs only to avoid a disaster.

## Round one: build a high floor

In a points format, a strong loss is worth almost as much as a weak win, as the [scoring lesson](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-tournament-scoring/) showed. So your first round has two jobs: give yourself chances to win, and make sure the bad case is still decent.

The habits that raise your floor are the ones you already know:

- **Open for ore and wheat,** as the [openings lesson](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-openings/) teaches, so your next cities come early. Cities are points and commodities at once.
- **Never be the lowest defender** when an attack might fail. A pillaged city is a point gone.
- **Aim for a metropolis when your track is close.** Its 2 points cannot be pillaged.
- **Take the defender's VP token when it is cheap.** One active knight can be the difference.

None of those is timid. Each one either adds a point or keeps one you already have.

## The middle: let the standings decide

After round one, compare where you are with where you need to be.

**Comfortably placed.** Keep the floor high. Protect your cities, take safe points, and do not chase a long shot that leaves you exposed to the barbarians.

**Short of where you need to be.** Take more risk on purpose. Push for a metropolis race you might lose. Skip the city walls and put everything into the race. Accept a thinner defense if it buys a faster path to 13. The point is to give yourself a real chance at the win bonus, because a safe 8 will not get you there.

Either way, decide before the game, not during it. Write the plan in one line at the top of your notes.

## The final table

Ask the organizer one question before the final: do earlier points carry in, or does the final start fresh? The Mind Sports Olympiad's page says the winner is the player with the most tournament points over the event, so read your event's rules closely and ask if it is unclear.

**If the final starts fresh,** only the win matters. Play for 13. Points in a loss help only if places behind the winner are ranked by score.

**If points carry in,** do the math at the start. Say you lead the next player by 4. If they win the final with 13 plus 2, that is 15, and you need 11 to stay level. So you can play for a high floor, and you only need to stop them from running away. If you trail by 4, you need the win and then some.

At a final, everyone is good. Expect tighter defenses, faster metropolis races, and quicker reads on who is ahead. Two things matter more than usual:

- **The catch-up cards.** Wedding, Sabotage, and Guild Dues all aim at players with more points than the one playing them. At a strong table, expect them to be saved for the leader. Do not climb one point ahead until the climb gets you somewhere.
- **The barbarian math.** A city with a metropolis cannot be pillaged, and the city lost in a failed attack comes from the lowest contributors. Know exactly who is exposed before each attack, including you.

## Closing it out

You win only by reaching 13 on your own turn. At a final table, the other players will do everything legal to stop the turn before that. So plan your last turn early:

1. Count the points you need and the builds that get you there.
2. Hold more than one path. If a Sabotage halves your hand, a second route to the last point saves the game.
3. Watch the ship. A city you lose to an attack on someone else's turn can take you from 13 back to 12.

Then make the turn quickly and cleanly. Say your points out loud and let the table check them.

## Win or lose, be someone people want to play again

Final tables remember how you play. Be generous with rules questions, honest about the score, and gracious at the end. Our piece on [how to be a good loser](/articles/how-to-be-a-good-loser/) applies to winners too.
