At Level 1 you learned the four knight moves: recruit, activate, promote, and act. Most new players treat knights as defense only, standing them up before the ship lands and forgetting them between attacks. Knights do much more than that. They sit on intersections, and anything that sits on an intersection can guard, block, and threaten.

## The rules that matter for tactics

- Only knights that are **active at the start of your Action phase** can take an action. A knight you activate this turn cannot act until next turn.
- After any action, a knight lies down. You may stand it back up the same turn for 1 wheat.
- Knights act like buildings for routes. Nobody may build a road past another player's knight, a knight blocks a settlement site, and a knight breaks a rival's road into shorter pieces for the Longest Route.
- A new knight goes on any empty intersection connected to your road. It does not follow the Distance Rule.
- After every barbarian attack, all knights lie down.

## Guard posts: chasing the robber

Once the first attack brings the robber onto the board, an active knight on an intersection of the robber's hex may chase it away. You move the robber and steal a card, just as if you had rolled a 7.

The best place for a knight is often an empty corner of your best hex. Call it a guard post. When a rival puts the robber there, you chase it on your next turn, steal a card, and send the robber somewhere that hurts someone else. Then pay 1 wheat to stand the knight up again, ready for the barbarians and the next robber.

The math is simple. Your best hex might be a 6 or 8 under a city, 5 of 36 rolls and 2 cards each time. At four players that is about 0.56 payouts a round, or 1.1 cards. Without a guard post, the robber can sit there for rounds. With one, it leaves on your next turn, and you take a card on the way out. One basic knight and a wheat a chase is cheap insurance.

Remember the timing. You chase in your Action phase, after production. The roll that started your turn is already lost if the robber was on your hex. A guard post limits the damage to about one round. It does not prevent it.

## Displacing: when it removes a knight

Your active knight may move along your routes onto a rival knight's intersection, as long as yours is stronger. Their knight then has to move along their own continuous routes to an empty intersection. If it cannot, it comes off the board.

Removal is the goal. Look for rival knights on the end of a short spur, or boxed in by your pieces, with no empty intersection they can reach. Displacing one of those removes it. That costs your rival the 1 wool and 1 ore they paid to recruit it, plus any promotions, and takes away its strength before the next attack.

A displaced knight keeps its status. So displacing an active knight that can escape only moves it, and it stays active for defense. Weigh that before you spend your action.

Also think defensively. A basic knight on an exposed spot is an invitation. A mighty knight cannot be displaced at all, because nothing is stronger. If politics level 3 is in your plan, those are your posts that must hold.

## Blocking roads and sites

Because knights block like buildings, a knight is often the cheapest way to stop a rival's plan:

- **A building site:** a knight on the intersection keeps a rival from settling there. Since knights ignore the Distance Rule, you can block a site even right next to your own buildings.
- **A road:** a knight on an intersection stops a rival from building past that point. Block the one path toward the open coast or the good port.
- **The Longest Route:** a knight on an intersection along a rival's road splits it into two shorter routes. If you can reach an intersection their road passes through, one knight can drop their route below yours. The Longest Route is worth 2 points, and a basic knight costs 2 cards.

[Expansion Planning](/academy/catan/expansion-planning/) from the base course teaches you to see which open site a rival is reaching for. In Cities & Knights you can answer it with a knight, not just a race.

## Keeping knights both busy and ready

The tension in every knight decision is this: an action lays the knight down, but the barbarians only count active knights. The fix is a habit. After you act, stand the knight back up for 1 wheat if the ship is close. That keeps your defense and your board presence at the same time. [Barbarian timing math](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarian-timing-math/) shows you when the ship is close enough to pay for it.

Two cards help. **Encouragement** stands all your knights up for free, best right before an attack or after a turn of several actions. **Smithing** promotes up to two knights for free, which can turn a basic guard into a strong knight that only a mighty knight can push off.
