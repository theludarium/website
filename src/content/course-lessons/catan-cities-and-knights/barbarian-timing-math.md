The barbarian ship is the clock of Cities & Knights. New players watch it with dread; strong players watch it with numbers.

## How fast the ship moves

The event die is rolled on every turn, and three of its six faces show the ship. So the ship moves on half of all rolls. From its starting space, it needs 7 moves to reach Catan. <!-- VERIFY: event die faces (3 ship, 1 each track) and the number of spaces from start to attack on the 6th edition barbarian track -->

On average, that is 14 rolls from one attack to the next. At four players, about three and a half rounds. The spread is wide, though:

| Rolls since the ship reset | Chance the attack has come |
| --- | --- |
| 9 | about 9% |
| 11 | about 27% |
| 13 | about 50% |
| 16 | about 77% |

So an attack in under 10 rolls is rare, and a long wait past 16 rolls happens about one cycle in four. Do not plan around the average alone.

## The question that matters: before my next turn?

You can only activate knights on your turn. Once your turn ends, the next chance you get comes after every other player has rolled, and after your own next roll, since the attack resolves before your Action phase. At four players that is 4 rolls. At three, it is 3.

Here are the chances that the ship lands within that window, by how many moves it has left:

| Moves left | 3 players (3 rolls) | 4 players (4 rolls) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 88% | 94% |
| 2 | 50% | 69% |
| 3 | 13% | 31% |
| 4 | 0% | 6% |

Read it like this. At four players, with the ship 2 moves out, the attack comes before you can act again more than two times in three. Activate now. At 4 moves out, it almost never does. Save your wheat. At 3 moves out it is a judgment call: about one time in three, which is where the rest of this lesson comes in.

## Count both sides

The barbarians' strength is the number of cities on the board, metropolises included. The defenders' strength is the total of every active knight: 1 for basic, 2 for strong, 3 for mighty. If the defenders match or beat the barbarians, Catan is safe.

Count both numbers before you spend. Say there are 7 cities and the active knights add up to 5. Catan needs 2 more strength or the barbarians win.

- **If Catan will lose,** the players who contributed the least each lose a city (not one with a metropolis). A player with no active knights always counts as contributing the least. Your goal is not to save Catan, it is to not be the lowest. One active basic knight is often enough, if someone else has zero.
- **If Catan will win,** the player who contributed the most strength alone gets a victory point token. If two or more tie for the most, nobody gets the point, and each tied player draws a progress card of their choice instead.

## When not to pay

Every activation costs 1 wheat, and after each attack all knights lie down again. So a knight you keep active costs a wheat each cycle, roughly every three or four rounds at four players.

Do not pay when it changes nothing:

- Catan already wins comfortably, and you have no chance at the most strength.
- Catan will lose anyway, and you are already above the lowest contributor who has a city that can be pillaged.
- Your only city is a metropolis, so you cannot lose a city at all.

Do pay when it changes the outcome:

- One more strength flips Catan from losing to winning, and you hold a city at risk.
- One more strength makes you the sole top contributor, which is a full victory point for 1 wheat.
- A rival is about to tie you for most, which would cost you the point.

## Building into an attack

Every city you build adds 1 to the barbarians. Building your third city when the ship is 1 move out can turn a safe Catan into a losing one, and your new city is a fine target. If you are about to upgrade, check whether the defenders still cover the new total. If they do not, activate first or wait a turn.

## The first attack is special

The first attack also releases the robber onto the desert. Before it, 7s cause discards but no theft. After it, every 7 steals, and the Taxation card becomes playable. A lost city in the first attack also sets you back early, when each city matters most. That is why the [opening lesson](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-openings/) puts a knight ahead of almost everything.

Encouragement is the ideal card for the last turn before an attack: it stands all your knights up for free. If you hold one, play it on your last turn before the attack instead of paying wheat knight by knight.
