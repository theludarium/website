Every city improvement you build moves you up one of three tracks, and each track feeds a different deck of progress cards, a different level 3 ability, and a different metropolis. You can climb all three. You cannot climb all three quickly. This lesson is about choosing where your commodities go first.

## Each track has one commodity

The tracks are paid in one commodity each:

| Track | Commodity | Made by a city on | Level 3 ability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Science | Paper | Forest | If you get no production on a roll (other than a 7), take 1 resource of your choice |
| Trade | Cloth | Pasture | Trade any 2 identical commodities for any 1 other commodity or resource |
| Politics | Coin | Mountains | Promote strong knights to mighty knights |

The cost climbs by one each level: 1 commodity for level 1, then 2, 3, 4, and 5. Reaching level 3 costs 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 commodities of one type. Reaching level 5 costs 15.

That is why your board decides so much. A city next to a forest on 6 (5 pips) makes about 0.56 paper per round at four players (5 pips x 4 rolls / 36). Six paper from that city alone takes about 11 rounds. Two commodity sources on the same track halve that. A track with no source means trading 4 resources at the bank for each commodity, or 24 cards to reach level 3.

## Start where you already produce

Your first track is almost always the one your best city already feeds. Add up the pips for each commodity across all your cities:

- **Paper pips** from forests next to your cities
- **Cloth pips** from pastures next to your cities
- **Coin pips** from mountains next to your cities

The biggest number is your main track. If two are close, look at what else you need. Mountains also give ore for knights and cities, so a coin-heavy board often plays well as a politics and knights game. Pastures give wool for knights too. Forests give wood for roads, which fits a science player who wants to spread out.

## What each track is good at

**Science** rewards a board with thin coverage. Its level 3 ability pays you on rolls where you get nothing, so it helps most when your numbers are few. The science deck is about building and production: cheaper cities, cheaper improvements, extra ore or wheat, free roads, free knight promotions, and the Alchemy card that sets the dice.

**Trade** rewards a player with surplus commodities and good ports. Its level 3 ability turns 2 of any one commodity into anything. Its deck holds 6 Merchant cards out of 18, plus monopolies, so trade players often hold the merchant and its victory point. [The merchant and trade](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/the-merchant-and-trade/) goes deeper in Level 3.

**Politics** rewards a knight player. Its level 3 ability allows mighty knights, the only knights that nobody can displace with a stronger one. Its deck is the most aggressive: cards that move or remove rival knights, take cards from leaders, and make everyone ahead of you discard half.

## Read the rivals before you commit

Every player's cubes are public, and so are their cities. Before you commit to a track, look at who else produces that commodity.

If one rival has two cities on strong pastures, you will not beat them to the trade metropolis by trading for cloth. You can still climb trade for the ability and the cards, but plan your metropolis on a track where you are the best producer. [Metropolis races](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/metropolis-races/) covers that math.

Shared tracks have one upside: every player who climbs a track draws from the same deck. Two strong trade players split the Merchant cards. A track nobody else is climbing is a deck you have mostly to yourself.

## When to open a second track

Open a second track when your main track reaches level 3, or when a cheap step is sitting in your hand. Level 1 on any track costs a single commodity and lets you draw from that deck on a red die of 1 or 2. That is the best value on the board for one card, so most strong players take level 1 on a second track early.

Avoid spreading evenly. Three tracks at level 2 give you no level 3 ability and no shot at a metropolis. One track at level 3 and one at level 1 is usually a better use of the same 7 commodities.

## Commodities go to tracks, not to the bank

Commodities count toward your hand limit and the robber can steal them, so a pile of paper is at risk. Spend as you go. When you are one commodity short of the next level, trade for it rather than waiting a round. Tempo matters: the [base course's tempo lesson](/academy/catan/cities-first/) applies here too, because a track level pays on every event roll from the moment you build it.
