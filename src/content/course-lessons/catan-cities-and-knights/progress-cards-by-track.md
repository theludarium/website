Progress cards replace base Catan's development cards: 54 of them, 18 per deck, and you draw from a deck only once you have level 1 on its track. Know what is in each deck and fewer surprises are yours to suffer.

## How often you draw

When the event die shows a track's symbol, every player checks the red die against their level on that track. Each level widens the range by one number.

| Your level | Red die range | Chance when the symbol shows | Cards per round (4 players) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | 2 in 6 | about 0.22 |
| 2 | 1 to 3 | 3 in 6 | about 0.33 |
| 3 | 1 to 4 | 4 in 6 | about 0.44 |
| 4 | 1 to 5 | 5 in 6 | about 0.56 |
| 5 | 1 to 6 | always | about 0.67 |

The last column assumes each track's symbol is on one face of the event die, so each symbol shows on 1 roll in 6. <!-- VERIFY: the 6th edition event die has 3 ship faces and 1 face each for science, trade, and politics -->

So a player at level 3 draws from that deck about once every two rounds. Level 1 is the cheapest step on the table: one commodity buys about a fifth of a card every round for the rest of the game.

## The science deck (18 cards)

Alchemy (2), Crane (2), Engineering (1), Invention (2), Irrigation (2), Medicine (2), Mining (2), Printing (1, a victory point), Road Building (2), Smithing (2).

- **Alchemy** is the only card played before you roll. You set both production dice. You also set the red die, which decides who draws progress cards if the event die shows a track. To roll an 8 that only you can draw on, pick a red die above every rival's range: a red 5 and a white 3 pays only level 4 or 5 players.
- **Medicine** builds a city for 1 wheat and 2 ore instead of 3 ore and 2 wheat. Hold it until you have the 3 cards.
- **Crane** cuts one improvement by 1 commodity. Use it on your biggest step, not on a level 1 that costs 1 anyway.
- **Mining** and **Irrigation** pay 2 ore or 2 wheat for each mountains or fields hex next to your buildings. Count your hexes. With three of them, either card is 6 cards.
- **Smithing** promotes up to two knights for free, worth 4 cards. **Invention** swaps two number discs other than 2, 6, 8, and 12.

## The trade deck (18 cards)

Commercial Harbor (2), Guild Dues (2), Merchant (6), Merchant Fleet (2), Resource Monopoly (4), Trade Monopoly (2).

- **Merchant** is a third of the deck. Each one moves the merchant to you and with it 1 victory point. Expect it to change hands several times.
- **Resource Monopoly** takes up to 2 of one resource from every player. Play it after a big roll of that resource, when hands are full.
- **Trade Monopoly** takes 1 of one commodity from each player. Use it to finish an improvement step.
- **Merchant Fleet** gives 2:1 trades in one resource or commodity for the rest of your turn. It is best when you hold a pile of one type.
- **Guild Dues** lets you look at the hand of a player with more points and take 2 cards. **Commercial Harbor** swaps one of your resources for a commodity of each rival's choosing.

## The politics deck (18 cards)

Constitution (1, a victory point), Diplomacy (2), Encouragement (2), Espionage (3), Intrigue (2), Sabotage (2), Taxation (2), Treason (2), Wedding (2).

- **Encouragement** activates all your knights for free. The right moment is just before the barbarians land, or just before a turn of knight actions.
- **Treason** forces a rival to remove a knight of their choice, and you may place one of yours of the same strength or lower. **Intrigue** displaces a rival knight next to your routes without using a knight.
- **Wedding** makes every player with more points give you 2 cards. **Sabotage** makes everyone with as many points as you or more discard half their cards. Both are best when you trail.
- **Taxation** moves the robber and steals from every player on the hex. It cannot be played before the first barbarian attack.
- **Espionage** looks at a rival's progress cards and takes one. **Diplomacy** removes an open road.

## Timing rules that shape your hand

- Victory point cards go face up as soon as you draw them, even on another player's turn. They do not count toward your hand limit, and Espionage cannot take them.
- You may hold 4 progress cards. When you draw on another player's turn and go over, you discard at once. On your own turn you have until the end of your turn.
- You may play a card the turn you draw it.

So holding 4 means your next off-turn draw forces a discard. Keep 3 or fewer. [Development Card Math](/academy/catan/development-card-math/) from the base course covers the same habit of counting what is left in a deck.
