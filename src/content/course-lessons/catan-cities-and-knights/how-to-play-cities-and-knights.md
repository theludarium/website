Cities & Knights is the deep end of the Catan pool. You still settle, roll, trade, and build, but cities now make commodities, those commodities buy improvements and powerful progress cards, and every few turns a barbarian fleet lands and tests whether the island built enough knights. The goal is 13 points, not 10.

This lesson teaches the Cities & Knights rules from the 6th edition rulebook (2025). You need the [Catan base game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and the [Cities & Knights expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). It plays 3 to 4, or 5 to 6 with its own extension. If base Catan is new to you, start with our [Catan course](/academy/catan/) and come back.

## What comes out of the box, and what goes back

From the base game, put away the development cards, the Largest Army tile, and the sea frame piece marked 1-2. Cities & Knights replaces that frame piece with one that shows the barbarian track.

The expansion adds commodity cards (paper, cloth, and coin), three progress card decks, an improvement board per player, knights, city walls, metropolises, the barbarian ship, the merchant, VP tokens, and a third die: the event die.

## How do you set up?

![The board in the barbarian track frame, dice, metropolises, merchant, and Longest Route beside it, the supply of cards, and each player's improvement board, knights, walls, and player aid](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/setup.svg)

1. Build the frame with the barbarian track piece. Lay out the hexes and number discs. The rulebook shows a balanced map for your first game; after that, use the base game's variable setup.
2. Put resource and commodity cards face up in eight stacks. Shuffle each progress deck and set all three face down. Pile the VP tokens nearby.
3. Put the 3 metropolises, the merchant, the dice, and the Longest Route tile beside the board.
4. Put the barbarian ship on the track's starting space. Put the robber beside the track's last space, not on the desert.
5. Roll for first player. Everyone takes their pieces, a player aid, a city improvement board, and 3 cubes, one at the bottom of each track.
6. In turn order, place a settlement and a road. Then, in reverse order, place a **city** and a road. The distance rule applies.
7. Take 1 resource for each hex next to your city.

That city at the start matters. Only cities make commodities.

## What happens on a turn?

Three phases, in order.

**Roll Dice.** If you hold an Alchemy card, play it now; it is the only progress card you play before rolling. Then roll all three dice and read the event die first.

- **Ship:** move the barbarian ship one space toward the island. If it reaches the last space, the barbarians attack (below).
- **City gate** (science, trade, or politics): every player checks that track on their improvement board. If the red die's number falls in the range beside your cube, draw the top card of that progress deck. Draw in turn order, starting with the player rolling.

**Production.** Settlements produce as usual. Cities on three terrains now split their take:

| City on | Takes |
| --- | --- |
| Forest | 1 wood and 1 paper |
| Pasture | 1 wool and 1 cloth |
| Mountains | 1 ore and 1 coin |
| Fields | 2 grain |
| Hills | 2 brick |

On a 7, nothing produces. Anyone holding more than 7 cards (resources and commodities together) discards half, rounded down. Until the first barbarian attack, that is all a 7 does: the robber stays off the board and nobody steals.

**Action.** Trade, build, use knights, improve, and play progress cards, in any order, as often as you can pay. Commodities trade like resources with other players. With the supply, you can buy commodities with resources at your usual rates, and trade commodities away at 4:1 or through a 3:1 port.

## The new things you can build

- **Knights** cost 1 wool and 1 ore and arrive inactive, lying down, on an empty intersection connected to your road. Activating one costs 1 grain. Promoting one a step costs 1 wool and 1 ore. Lesson 5 covers what they do.
- **City walls** cost 2 brick and sit under a city. Each wall lets you hold 2 more cards before a 7 makes you discard. Three at most.
- **City improvements** move a cube up a track by paying that track's commodity: paper for science, cloth for trade, coin for politics. Level 1 costs 1, level 5 costs 5. You need at least one city to improve.
- **Metropolises** go to the first player to reach level 4 on a track, but only for now: the first player to reach level 5 takes it for good. A metropolis sits on one of your cities and is worth 2 more points.

## The barbarian attack

When the ship lands, compare two numbers. The barbarians' strength is the number of cities on the board, metropolises included, everyone's. The defenders' strength is the total of every active knight on the board: basic knights count 1, strong 2, mighty 3.

If the knights match or beat the barbarians, the player who gave the most strength takes a VP token. If the barbarians win, the player who gave the least loses a city, reduced to a settlement. Then the ship goes back to the start and every knight lies down, inactive. After the first attack, the robber moves to the desert and plays as normal.

## How do you win?

Points come from settlements (1), cities (2), metropolises (2 more), Longest Route (2), the merchant (1), VP tokens (1 each), and two victory point progress cards. The first player with 13 or more on their own turn wins.
