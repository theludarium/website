Welcome to Level 2. You know the pieces now: knights, the barbarian ship, commodities, and the three improvement tracks. This level is about turning that knowledge into a plan, and every plan starts at placement.

Cities & Knights changes setup in one way that matters more than any other. Your second placement is a city, not a settlement. That city pays double from the very first roll, and it is the only kind of building that makes commodities. Pick it well and your whole game is easier.

## The city spot is the real first pick

In round 1 everyone places a settlement and a road. In round 2, going in reverse order, everyone places a city and a road, and takes one starting resource for each hex next to that city.

So when you look at the board, look for your city spot first. A city on a forest, pasture, or mountains hex collects 1 resource and 1 commodity (paper, cloth, or coin) when that number rolls. A city on fields or hills collects 2 wheat or 2 brick and no commodity. Commodities are what move your cubes up the improvement tracks, so a city with no commodity hexes leaves you trading for every step.

Use the pip math from the base course in [The Dots Matter](/academy/catan/number-probability/). Say your city touches a forest on 6, mountains on 9, and a pasture on 10. That is 5 + 4 + 3 = 12 pips. In a four-player game, a spot pays 12 x 4 / 36, about 1.3 times per round. As a city, each payout is 2 cards, so this spot brings about 2.7 cards a round, and about half of them are commodities.

Round 1 picks should be made with round 2 in mind. If the best city spot will not survive until your round 2 pick, take it now as your settlement and plan to upgrade it soon. Your round 2 city then goes on the best spot left. The last player in turn order places a settlement and a city back to back, which makes their two picks easy to plan together. [Seat Position Changes Everything](/academy/catan/seat-position/) covers how pick order shapes these choices.

## Why ore and wheat carry the opening

Look at what the early game asks you to buy:

- **A city:** 3 ore and 2 wheat, the same as base Catan.
- **Recruit a knight:** 1 wool and 1 ore.
- **Activate a knight:** 1 wheat.
- **Promote a knight:** 1 wool and 1 ore.

Ore shows up in every one of them, and wheat in most. A mountains hex under a city also pays coin, the politics commodity. Base Catan players learn that [ore and wheat win late](/academy/catan/resource-value-over-time/). In Cities & Knights, the game starts late. You already have a city, and the game asks for knights within the first few rounds.

Brick and wood still matter for roads and new sites. But a start with no ore is hard here: 4 cards at the bank for every ore, and you need ore every turn.

## Be ready for the first attack

The barbarian ship moves when the event die shows the ship. On average it takes about 14 rolls to reach Catan, which is three to four rounds at four players. <!-- VERIFY: 14 rolls assumes 3 ship faces on the event die and 7 moves to the attack space --> The odds of when it lands are in [Barbarian timing math](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarian-timing-math/) later in this level.

When it lands, the barbarians' strength is the number of cities on the board. At the start that is one per player. If the active knights on the board add up to less, the barbarians win, and the players who contributed the least lose a city. A player with no active knights always counts as contributing the least.

So the cheapest insurance in the opening is one basic knight, recruited and activated before the ship arrives. That is 1 wool, 1 ore, and 1 wheat, three cards to protect a city that cost you nothing at setup but would cost 5 to rebuild. If the defenders win, the player who contributed the most gets a victory point, so one knight can also be the start of a score.

## The robber sleeps until the first attack

The robber stays next to the barbarian track until the first attack. Until then, a 7 still makes players with big hands discard half, but nobody moves the robber or steals.

So a city on a 6 or 8 pays in full for the first few rounds, and the main risk of saving cards is the discard on a 7. City walls (2 brick each) raise your limit by 2 each, which matters once you save for improvements.

## An opening checklist

1. **City spot first:** 10 or more pips, at least two commodity hexes if you can.
2. **Ore somewhere:** on your city or your settlement, ideally on a number that is not a 2, 3, 11, or 12.
3. **Wheat somewhere:** activations and cities need it all game.
4. **Room to grow:** a road toward an open site, because a second city gives a second commodity engine.
5. **A knight before the ship lands.**

The base course's [five-point opening checklist](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/) still applies on top of this. Cities & Knights only changes which spot you should look at first.
