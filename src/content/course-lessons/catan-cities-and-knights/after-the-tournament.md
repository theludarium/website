The scores are signed. Maybe you won your table, maybe you made the final, maybe you lost a city to the last barbarian attack of the day and finished a point short. Whatever happened, the most useful hour of the tournament is still ahead of you: the one you spend looking back.

This last lesson covers that review, what to log that is special to Cities & Knights, and how to plan the next event.

## Review that night

The base course's [reviewing your games](/academy/catan/reviewing-your-games/) lesson gives you the method: find where the game turned, and write a three line review. Do it the same day, before the details blur. One review per game, even the games you won.

Then add a few lines that only Cities & Knights asks for.

## What to log for Cities & Knights

**The barbarian attacks.** For each attack: the cities on the board, the active knight strength, the result, and who lost a city or took the VP token. Did you ever lose a city you could have saved with one activation? That is the most common cheap point lost in this game.

**Your improvement tracks.** When did you reach level 3 on each track, and did you get a metropolis? If another player beat you to one, by how many turns?

**Progress cards.** Which did you draw, which did you play, and which did you discard or lose? A card held too long is a quiet loss, and Espionage can take one from your hand.

**The catch-up cards.** Were you ever hit by Wedding, Sabotage, or Guild Dues? Were you a point ahead when it happened, and did you need to be?

**The clock.** How long did each game take, and did any turn of yours run long? Note which turns, and why.

**Your final score in each game.** In a points format, every point counted. Look at the games you lost and ask how many more points were on the table.

## Find the pattern

One game is a story. Three or four games are data. Once your reviews are written, read them side by side and look for the same mistake twice. Common ones:

- Losing a city to an attack you saw coming.
- Falling behind in a metropolis race by a single turn.
- Leading by one point too early and drawing every catch-up card at the table.
- Running short on time in the endgame and making a rushed last turn.

Pick one. Not three. One habit to fix is a plan; three is a wish.

## Watch strong players too

If you sat at a table with someone who played better than you, write down one thing they did differently. When games from the top tables are streamed or recorded, the base course's [study championship finals](/academy/catan/study-championship-finals/) lesson shows how to watch them like a coach.

## Plan the next event

Cities & Knights events do not form a season the way the base game's ladder does, so you build your own. The base course's [planning a season](/academy/catan/planning-a-season/) lesson covers budgeting time and travel; here is how it fits this game.

1. **Mark the recurring events.** Recent years have had Cities & Knights tournaments at Gen Con, UK Games Expo, and the Mind Sports Olympiad. Gen Con 2027 runs August 5 to 8 in Indianapolis. For the rest, check each event's site in the spring, as the [where tournaments happen](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/where-cities-and-knights-tournaments-happen/) lesson explains.
2. **Fill the gaps.** Online play and local store events give you games between conventions.
3. **Work back from the date.** Put the [eight week practice plan](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-practice-plan/) on the calendar before your next event, with your one habit as the focus.
4. **Find your group.** Players who train together improve together. The [long game](/academy/catan/the-long-game/) lesson covers building a training group, and the [Seafarers course](/academy/catan-seafarers/) has more maps to explore when you want a change of scene.

## What this course gave you

You started at Level 0, learning to set up a board with a barbarian track. You learned to recruit and activate knights, read the progress card decks, choose a track, race for metropolises, and time the barbarians. Then you learned to take all of that to a tournament: the scoring, the clock, the table, and the final.

That is the whole path. Congratulations, Champion.

The game is the reason. The people are the point. The best thing a tournament gives you is not the result. It is a room full of people who love this game as much as you do, and a reason to see them again next year.
