If you have played a lot of base Catan, you bring good habits into Cities & Knights: count pips, guard your ports, trade with purpose. Some of your instincts, though, now point the wrong way. This lesson walks through each big change and what it does to strategy, using only the 6th edition rules and some simple counting.

## Change 1: 13 points instead of 10

Three more points sounds like a longer race on the same track. It is a different track. Base Catan's extra points come from Longest Road, Largest Army, and development cards. Here, Largest Army and the development deck are gone. In their place are metropolises (2 points each), the merchant (1), Defender VP tokens from barbarian attacks (1 each), and two victory point progress cards.

Most of those new points come through city improvements, and improvements need commodities, and commodities need cities. So the road to 13 runs through your cities.

## Change 2: cities make commodities

In base Catan, upgrading a settlement doubles its production. In Cities & Knights, that is only true for fields and hills. A city on forest, pasture, or mountains takes 1 resource and 1 commodity instead of 2 resources.

That changes how you read a city spot. A mountain city no longer gives 2 ore. It gives 1 ore and 1 coin. If you were counting on that city for ore to build more cities, you get half as much ore as you are used to, and a coin you can only spend on politics or trade.

Count it in pips, the way our [number probability lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/) teaches. A city on a mountain marked 8 (5 pips) touches that hex on about 5 of every 36 rolls. Over 36 rolls, that is about 5 ore and 5 coin, not 10 ore.

## Change 3: the starting city

You place a settlement, then a city, and collect from the city. The city is in play from turn one, which means you can make improvements from turn one, and it makes commodities from the first roll.

Place your city where it will produce a commodity you plan to spend, on a strong number. Placing it only for brick and wood, the way you might in a base game road race, gives you no commodities at all from your best building.

## Change 4: ore, grain, and wool rise in value

Look at what the new pieces cost:

| Action | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Recruit a knight | 1 wool, 1 ore |
| Promote a knight | 1 wool, 1 ore |
| Activate a knight | 1 grain |
| City (unchanged) | 2 grain, 3 ore |
| City wall | 2 brick |

Knights pull on wool and ore, and keeping them active pulls on grain every time an attack resets them. Cities still cost grain and ore. In base Catan, wool is often the card everyone has too much of. Here it has a steady job. Lesson 8 builds an opening plan around this.

## Change 5: knights replace Largest Army

There are no knight cards to buy. Knights are pieces on the board, and their main job is defense. They also block roads and settlement spots, break up an opponent's Longest Route, and chase the robber. You will have fewer surprise robber moves from a hand of knight cards and more open, visible board fights.

## Change 6: the barbarians tax cities

The barbarians' strength is every city on the board, yours and everyone else's. Defense is every active knight, also everyone's. So defense is shared, but the punishment is not. If the barbarians win, the player who gave the least knight strength loses a city.

That creates the central tension of the game. Every city you build makes the next attack harder for everybody. Every knight you activate helps everybody, but only rewards you if you give the most. The table that builds cities quickly and ignores knights gets pillaged. The player who overbuilds knights spends wool, ore, and grain the others spent on growth.

## Change 7: the robber sleeps at first

The robber does not move or steal until after the first attack. A 7 still forces discards over the limit. So the opening is calmer than base Catan, and the early hand limit is a bigger risk than the robber. City walls raise your limit by 2 each. They also cost brick that a road builder wants.

## Change 8: progress cards come to you

You do not buy progress cards. They arrive when the event die shows a gate in a track where you have built, and the red die falls in your range. Climbing a track is buying a steady stream of cards. Lesson 7 shows how fast that stream gets.

## What to keep from base Catan

Pip counting, port value, and reading your opponents all still work. So does the advice to build toward a plan instead of grabbing whatever you can afford. Our [opening settlements lesson](/academy/catan/opening-settlements/) and the [How to Win at Catan](/articles/how-to-win-at-catan/) article still apply, with one change: now you are placing a city.
