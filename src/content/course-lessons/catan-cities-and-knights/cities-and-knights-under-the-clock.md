A tournament runs on a schedule. The Mind Sports Olympiad's 2026 Cities & Knights event fit two rounds into one day session from 10:00 to 18:00, with the final in the evening. The organizers also re-rolled 7s until every player had taken two turns, and said why: without that rule, games run longer and the event overruns. When an organizer changes the rules to save time, that tells you how long this game can get.

You cannot make the game shorter. You can make your share of it shorter, and you can stop the clock from deciding your close calls for you.

## Why the game runs long

Two things stack up. First, there are more points to earn. You start at 3 of 13 instead of 2 of 10 in base Catan, so you need 10 more points instead of 8. That is a quarter more scoring, before the barbarians knock any of it back down.

Second, each turn has more steps. Count what the rulebook adds:

- **Three dice,** with the event die resolved before production.
- **Progress card draws,** in turn order, for every player whose track level matches the red die, followed by discards down to four cards.
- **Commodities** to collect, sort, and trade.
- **Knights** to recruit, activate, promote, and move.
- **Barbarian attacks,** with strengths to count, cities to pillage or a VP token to award, and every knight to lay down after.

None of that is slow on its own. Together, a table that hesitates at each step can add minutes to every round.

## What the clock looks like

There is no single Cities & Knights tournament timer. The official base Catan rules suggest 90 seconds a turn plus a 10 minute time bank, and the [beating the turn timer lesson](/academy/catan/beating-the-turn-timer/) teaches you to play inside it. A Cities & Knights event may use a timer like that, a round time limit, or just a schedule and a judge. Ask which, and ask what happens if a round runs out of time. That answer changes your last few turns.

## Plan on other turns

The base course's rule holds: think earlier, not faster. In Cities & Knights you have more to plan, so give each other player's turn a job.

1. **After your turn:** decide your next build and the two cards you are missing.
2. **On the next player's roll:** update for what came in. If the ship moved, recount the barbarians.
3. **One player before you:** pick the progress card you will play, if any, and when. Alchemy only works before you roll, so decide on it now.

When the dice reach you, you should already know your "if 7," "if ship," and "if I get ore" answers.

## A routine for the repeated steps

**Sort your hand the same way every time.** Resources in one group, commodities in another, progress cards apart and face down. A glance tells you what you can build, and discards on a 7 take seconds.

**Keep a running barbarian count.** Barbarian strength is the number of cities on the board. Defender strength is the total of all active knights. Update both numbers when they change, not when the ship lands. When the attack comes, you only need to confirm.

**Resolve in order, out loud.** "Ship moves. No attack. Politics on a 3: who draws?" Naming each step keeps the table moving and stops the mistakes that take longest to undo.

**Know your knights' state.** Active or inactive, basic, strong, or mighty. Your knight actions should be decided before your turn, not discovered during it.

## Where to spend your time

Most turns are simple: collect, maybe build, pass. A few decide the game. In Cities & Knights those are:

- **The turn before an attack,** when activating one knight might save a city or win the VP token.
- **A metropolis race,** when spending commodities now or next turn is the whole question.
- **The turn you could reach 13.** You can only win on your own turn, so count every point twice.

If there is a time bank, spend it there. If there is no bank, save your long thinks for those turns and make every other turn fast.

## Keep the table kind

A clock makes some players anxious. Never rush anyone yourself; that is the timer's or the judge's job. Offer to track the barbarian count for the table, and say "done" clearly when your turn ends.
