Knights are the most flexible pieces in Cities & Knights. They defend the island, they block, they fight each other, and they chase the robber. They also cost wool, ore, and grain that you could spend on cities. This lesson covers every knight rule in the 6th edition, then what each one means at the table.

## The pieces

Each player has 6 knights: 2 basic (strength 1), 2 strong (strength 2), and 2 mighty (strength 3). A knight lying down is inactive. A knight standing up is active.

## Recruit: 1 wool, 1 ore

You may only recruit basic knights. Place it, lying down, on an empty intersection connected to one of your roads. Knights ignore the distance rule, so a knight can sit right next to a settlement.

If both your basic knights are already on the board, you cannot recruit until you promote one of them, which frees a basic knight back to your supply.

## Activate: 1 grain

Pay 1 grain and stand an inactive knight up. You can recruit a knight and activate it in the same turn. You can also take an action with an active knight (which lays it down) and then pay to stand it up again. What you cannot do is activate a knight and then act with it in the same turn: knights can only act if they were active at the start of your Action phase.

## Promote: 1 wool, 1 ore

Swap the knight for the next stronger one, in the same spot. It keeps its status: an active knight stays active. You can promote a given knight only once per turn. You cannot promote if both of your next-stronger knights are already on the board. And strong to mighty needs level 3 on the politics track.

Promotion is the cheapest strength in the game. Going from basic to strong adds 1 strength for 1 wool and 1 ore, the same price as a new basic knight, and it does not need a new intersection or a second grain to activate.

## The three actions

An active knight can take one action on your turn. After the action, lay it down.

**Move.** Travel along your own connected roads to an empty intersection. You may pass your own buildings and knights, never another player's pieces.

**Displace.** Move along your roads onto an intersection holding an opponent's knight that is weaker than yours. Their knight must move to an empty intersection along their own roads, keeping its status. If it has nowhere to go, it returns to their supply. Equal strength cannot displace.

**Chase the robber.** If your active knight sits on a corner of the robber's hex, you can move the robber as if you had rolled a 7: put it on a new hex and steal a random card from a player with a building there. This only matters after the first barbarian attack, when the robber enters the board.

A knight that needs to reach the robber first spends a turn moving, which lays it down, and then you pay a grain to stand it back up. It chases on a later turn.

## Knights as walls

Knights act like buildings on the road network, active or not:

- No one may build a road past another player's knight.
- A knight blocks that intersection as a settlement spot.
- A knight breaks up an opponent's road when counting Longest Route.

That makes a lying-down knight useful. A basic knight placed at the end of an opponent's road can stop them reaching a port or a settlement spot. Its cost is the same 1 wool and 1 ore, and it does not need grain to block.

## How much strength do you need?

The barbarians' strength equals the number of cities on the board. In a 4-player game where everyone has 2 cities, that is 8. If every player brings 2 active strength, the island holds exactly. Any one player who brings less becomes the likeliest target.

Your fair share is roughly your own number of cities. Whether you bring more depends on the Defender VP token. Lesson 6 does that math.

## Common knight plans

- **The early blocker.** One basic knight, recruited early to block a key intersection, then activated before the first attack.
- **The defender.** Two knights promoted to strong, active before each attack. That is 4 strength from two intersections.
- **The robber hunter.** A knight kept near your best hex after the first attack, ready to chase the robber off it.

Each costs wool and ore. That is why the [art of the trade](/academy/catan/art-of-the-trade/) matters more here than in the base game: wool you used to dump at 4:1 now buys strength.
