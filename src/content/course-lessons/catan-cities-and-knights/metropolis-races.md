Welcome to Level 3. At Level 2 you learned to choose a track and reach its level 3 ability. Now the tracks become a race. There are three metropolises, one per track, and each one is worth 2 points to whoever holds it. In a game to 13, that is a big share of a win.

## How control works

- The first player to reach **level 4** on a track takes that track's metropolis and puts it on one of their cities. That control is **temporary**.
- The first player to reach **level 5** on a track takes the metropolis for good. That control is **permanent**.
- A metropolis is worth 2 points, so a city with one is worth 4 in all.
- Each metropolis must sit on a different city. If you have no city without a metropolis, you may not buy that level 4 or level 5 improvement.
- A city with a metropolis cannot be pillaged by the barbarians.

So a metropolis can change hands only once: from the first player at level 4 to the first player at level 5. A second player reaching level 4 takes nothing.

## The commodity math

From level 3, the next two steps cost 4 and then 5 commodities. Reaching level 4 costs 10 commodities in all, and level 5 costs 15.

The step from 3 to 4 is the best deal in the game: 4 commodities for 2 points and a safe city. The step from 4 to 5 costs 5 more for no new points. You buy it for protection: to stop a rival from taking the metropolis away, and to draw from that deck on every red die result.

## Count the pace

Every player's cubes are public. So are their cities and the numbers they sit on. That is enough to estimate how fast a rival climbs.

Use the pip count from the base course's [Dots Matter](/academy/catan/number-probability/) lesson. A city next to a forest on 8 makes paper on 5 of 36 rolls. At four players, that is 5 x 4 / 36, about 0.56 paper a round. Two such cities make about 1.1 a round.

Now picture the race. A rival holds the trade metropolis at level 4. You are at level 3 on trade.

- They need **5 cloth** to reach level 5 and lock it.
- You need **4 + 5 = 9 cloth** to reach level 5 first and take it.

If you both make cloth at the same rate, they get there first almost every time. To take it, you need nearly twice their pace, or cards that change the count. That is the rule of thumb: **the leader on a track keeps the metropolis unless the chaser is about twice as fast.**

Now flip it. You hold a metropolis at level 4 and a rival is at level 3. They need 9 and you need 5. You do not have to rush to level 5, but you do need to watch them. When their cube moves to level 4, they are 5 commodities from taking it, the same as you. From that moment it is a straight race, and you should spend on it first.

## Cards that change the count

- **Crane:** one step costs 1 commodity less. Use it on the 5.
- **Trade Monopoly:** 1 of a named commodity from each player. It speeds you up and slows the rival on the same turn.
- **Commercial Harbor:** your resources for rivals' commodities, which they choose. Weaker, since they will not give you what you need if they can help it.
- **Sabotage** and the robber: a rival saving 5 commodities is holding a big hand. Sabotage makes every player with as many points as you or more discard half. The robber steals from the hand directly.
- **Espionage:** look at their progress cards. If you find a Crane, take it.

The [Card Counting](/academy/catan/card-counting/) lesson from the base course helps here: track how many commodities each rival has collected and spent, and you know how close they are.

## When to let it go

Sometimes the right move is to stop. If a rival is at level 4 with two strong commodity cities and you are at level 3 with one, the 9 commodities you would spend chasing could buy two steps on another track instead. A metropolis on a track nobody else is climbing is cheaper than a contested one.

A good habit: before you spend the first commodity of a level 4 step, ask whether you can win this race. If the answer is no, put those commodities toward a different metropolis, a knight, or a city.

## Metropolis and defense

Because a metropolis cannot be pillaged, it changes your barbarian risk. If your only city is a metropolis, you cannot lose a city to a barbarian win, and the pillage passes to the next weakest defender. The rulebook's own example shows exactly that. But every metropolis is still a city, so it still adds to the barbarians' strength. [Barbarian timing math](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarian-timing-math/) shows how to plan defense around that.
