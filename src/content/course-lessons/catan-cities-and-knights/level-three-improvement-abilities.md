Levels 1 and 2 on a track mostly widen your progress card draws. Level 3 is different. It gives you a permanent ability that works whether or not the event die cooperates. Every track's ability is different, and on the right board each one is worth more than a card a round.

All three cost the same: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 commodities of the track's type. The question is which one pays you most, and how fast you can get there.

## Science: the Aqueduct

If you collect no cards in the production phase, you may take 1 resource of your choice. It does not work on a 7.

Its value depends on how many rolls miss you. Count the numbers your buildings touch and add their dice combinations. Say you touch 5, 6, 9, and 10: that is 4 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 16 of the 36 combinations. A 7 is 6 more. That leaves 14 of 36 rolls, about 39%, that give you nothing. At four players, that is about 1.6 free resources a round, and you choose each one.

Two things make it stronger. First, it works on every player's roll, not just yours. Second, the free card is any resource you like, so it is worth more than a random card. On a thin board it is the best ability in the game. On a board where you touch ten numbers, it rarely fires, and you should look elsewhere. (A blocked hex counts as a miss, so the robber hurts you a little less, too.)

## Trade: the Merchant Guild

You may trade any 2 identical commodities for any 1 other commodity or resource.

Without it, trading a commodity at the bank costs 4, or 3 or 2 at a port. With it, every commodity is worth half a card of anything. If your cities produce one commodity well, you can now convert the surplus into the commodities your other tracks need, at half the bank price.

A worked example: you make plenty of cloth and need 4 coin for a politics step. At the bank that is 16 cloth. With the Merchant Guild it is 8. That is the difference between a step you never reach and one you reach this game. It also means you rarely need a Merchant Fleet card just to convert, so you can save it for a bigger turn.

## Politics: the Fortress

You may promote strong knights to mighty knights. A mighty knight has strength 3.

The value shows up in two places. On defense, each mighty knight is one more strength than a strong knight for the same activation cost of 1 wheat. Without politics level 3, your maximum on the board is two strong and two basic knights, strength 6. With it, two mighty knights add 6 more, for a possible 12. That helps you claim the defender's victory point.

On the board, a mighty knight cannot be displaced by another knight, because nothing is stronger. It can hold a road block or a robber-chasing post for the rest of the game. Only cards like Treason and Intrigue can move it. [Knight tactics](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/knight-tactics/) later in this course puts that to work.

## Racing there

Abilities are not exclusive. Every player can build the Aqueduct. So the race is not against rivals, it is against the clock. Each round you have the ability, it pays. An ability reached in round 6 pays for many more rounds than one reached in round 12.

Speed comes from three habits:

1. **Pick the track you produce.** Use the pip count from [Choosing a track](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/choosing-your-improvement-track/). Your main commodity is the cheapest path.
2. **Build several levels at once if you can.** You may not skip a level, but you may climb more than one in a turn. Saving 5 commodities to go from level 1 straight to 3 in one turn is fine, as long as a 7 is not likely to cost you half your hand first. A city wall makes that wait safer.
3. **Use the cards that shorten the climb.** Crane takes 1 commodity off one step. Trade Monopoly and Commercial Harbor pull commodities from rivals. Merchant Fleet turns a pile of one type into another at 2:1 for a turn.

## Level 3 also widens your draws

Level 3 lets you draw on a red die of 1 to 4, two thirds of the time when your track's symbol shows. So the climb pays twice: once in the ability and once in cards. In a four-player game that is about 0.44 cards a round from that deck, double what level 1 gives.

## Which one first?

There is no best ability, only the best one for your board. A short way to decide:

- **Few numbers, scattered production:** science.
- **One commodity in surplus, ports nearby:** trade.
- **Lots of ore and wool, a knight plan, a rival you need to hold off:** politics.

And remember that level 3 is the doorway to level 4 and the metropolis. The ability you reach first is often the metropolis you will fight for next.
