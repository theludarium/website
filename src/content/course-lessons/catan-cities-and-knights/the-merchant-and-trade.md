In base Catan, trade happens with other players and with ports. Cities & Knights keeps all of that and adds two things: commodities, which trade by their own rules, and the merchant, a small wooden figure worth a victory point that moves every time someone plays a Merchant card. This lesson is about the economy around both.

## The trade rules for commodities

Commodities trade almost like resources, with a few limits worth knowing by heart:

- **With other players:** any mix of resources and commodities, on any terms you agree. [The Art of the Trade](/academy/catan/art-of-the-trade/) from the base course applies in full.
- **With the supply at 4:1, or 3:1 at a 3:1 port:** you may trade resources for commodities, commodities for resources, or commodities for other commodities.
- **At a 2:1 port:** only that port's resource, as usual. You may receive a commodity, but you cannot pay with commodities at that rate.
- **Progress cards** can never be traded.

On top of those, two rates belong to the trade track. The level 3 ability (Merchant Guild) lets you trade any 2 identical commodities for any 1 commodity or resource. The Merchant Fleet card gives you 2:1 trades in one resource or commodity of your choice for the rest of your turn.

## What the merchant does

When you play a Merchant card, you take the merchant and place it on a land hex next to one of your buildings. While you control it:

- You may trade that hex's resource at 2:1 with the supply. Resources only, not commodities.
- You hold 1 victory point.

You keep it until someone else plays a Merchant card. There are 6 Merchant cards in the 18-card trade deck, a third of it, so the merchant moves often in a game where several players climb trade.

## Where to put it

The merchant's rate is only as good as the resource under it. Put it on the hex whose resource you will have the most of over the next few rounds. That is usually a hex where you have a city, or two buildings, on a good number.

A worked example: your city and a settlement both touch a pasture on 6. That pasture pays you 3 wool each time it rolls (2 for the city, 1 for the settlement). With the merchant on it, every 2 wool becomes any resource you need. That turns your strongest hex into a 2:1 port for the cards you lack, such as ore for knights.

Avoid parking it on a hex you rarely collect from just to block a rival. The merchant does not stop production. Its value is in your trades.

## How long will you keep it?

You can estimate it. Each time the event die shows trade, every player on the trade track draws if the red die is in range. With three players on trade at levels 3, 2, and 1, the chance of a draw per roll adds up to (4 + 3 + 2) / 36, so about one trade card a round at four players. A third of those are Merchants. That is roughly one Merchant every three rounds, and some players will hold theirs instead of playing it at once.

So treat the merchant as a few rounds of use, not a permanent engine. Make the trades it allows while you have it.

## The merchant as a finishing move

The real power of the merchant is its point. You win the moment you have 13 points during your turn. A Merchant card held in your hand is a point you can add on any turn of your choosing, and taking the merchant also takes a point away from whoever had it.

That makes the last Merchant card a classic finisher. If you are at 12 points and hold a Merchant, you can win on your turn even with no resources at all. Keep that in mind for two reasons:

- **When you hold one,** keep it if it can be your 13th point. Play it earlier only if the trade rate or the point matters more right now.
- **When a rival is at 12 or close,** count how many Merchants are gone. If the deck still has several and the rival draws trade cards, they may be holding one already. Espionage can check. Wedding or Guild Dues will not help, because those take resources, not progress cards.

## The merchant and the leader

Because control swings a full point, the merchant is also a tool against the leader. Playing a Merchant while the leader holds it costs them a point and gives you one, a two-point swing in a single card. In the late game, that can be worth more than any build. [Threat Assessment](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/) from the base course helps you decide who that leader is.
