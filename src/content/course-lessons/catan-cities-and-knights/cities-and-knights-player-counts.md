Cities & Knights plays three or four out of the box. With the 5 to 6 extension it plays up to six, and it combines with Seafarers for a bigger map. The rules barely change between these setups. What changes is how fast everything moves around you, and that is what this lesson is about.

## Three players versus four

With three players there are three starting cities, so the first barbarian attack needs only 3 strength to hold off. There is also more room on the board, fewer rivals on each improvement track, and fewer trade partners.

The main thing to adjust is the clock. Between the end of your turn and your next chance to act, only 3 rolls happen instead of 4. From [Barbarian timing math](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarian-timing-math/): with the ship 2 moves out, the chance it lands before you act again is 50% at three players and 69% at four. You can wait a little longer on activations at three.

Fewer rivals also means a merchant tends to stay with you longer, and a metropolis race often has one rival at most.

## Five and six players: the extension

To play five or six, you need the [Cities & Knights 5 to 6 Player Extension](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87CXSQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). The rulebook asks for components from base CATAN, the [CATAN 5 to 6 Player Extension](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1LYDT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and Cities & Knights as well. You build the larger board from the CATAN 5 to 6 extension, using the Cities & Knights sea frame with the barbarian track, and you add the extension's 18 commodity cards to the supply.

The 6th edition uses **paired player turns**, which replace the special building phase from older 5 to 6 player sets:

1. **Player 1** takes a full turn: roll, production, and action. If player 1 reaches 13 points, the game ends at once.
2. **Player 2**, the third player to the left of player 1, then takes an Action phase. Player 2 may not trade with other players.
3. Both markers pass to the left, and the next player 1 rolls.

## What paired turns do to timing

Every player is player 1 once per round and player 2 once per round. So at five or six, you get **two Action phases every round**, and you only roll on one of them.

That matters most for the barbarians. Between your chances to act, only two or three rolls pass, even at six players. You can wait on activations about as long as at three players. You also get a second window to stand knights up after you see the ship move.

But each round has more rolls, so the ship crosses the track in fewer rounds. On average it still needs about 14 rolls, which is under two and a half rounds at six players. There are also more cities on the board, so the barbarians are stronger.

Since player 2 cannot trade with players, plan player trades for your player 1 turn.

## More players, faster decks

The decks stay at 18 cards each, but more players draw from them. The 6 Merchant cards come out faster and the merchant changes hands more often. A Merchant card held for your winning point, as in [The merchant and trade](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/the-merchant-and-trade/), is a better plan at six than at four, because you cannot count on holding the merchant itself for long.

## Combining with Seafarers

Cities & Knights also combines with [CATAN Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). The Cities & Knights rulebook gives the combined rules:

- **Ships** follow the rules that apply to roads. Knights can move along routes of roads and ships, and can stop on an empty intersection between sea hexes.
- **Knights must stay connected** to a route of their color. You may not move a ship if that would cut a knight off.
- **The pirate** starts on the final space of the barbarian track and enters play after the first attack, placed as the scenario says. Knights on sea intersections can chase away the pirate the same way knights on land chase the robber.
- **Barbarians** attack every island at once, counting every city and every active knight on the board.
- **Gold fields** produce resources only, never commodities, and the merchant may not go on one.
- **Taxation** moves only the robber, not the pirate.
- **Diplomacy:** if you remove one of your own ships, you may rebuild only a ship. If you remove one of your own roads, you may rebuild only a road.
- **Winning** takes 2 more points than the Seafarers scenario normally asks.

The rulebook recommends scenarios without hidden tiles or many small islands, because those make the barbarians hit harder. It names [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/) and [Through the Desert](/academy/catan-seafarers/through-the-desert/) as good fits, and The Fog Islands and The Four Islands as poor ones. The Seafarers course covers the [combined game from the Seafarers side](/academy/catan-seafarers/seafarers-player-counts-and-combinations/).

For five or six players with both expansions, check the official combination rules on [catan.com's rules page](https://www.catan.com/understand-catan/game-rules) before you set up.

## Which count to choose

Four is the count most tables in this course assume. Three is the most forgiving for learning. Five and six are the most social and the most chaotic. If you want to compare other Catan add-ons first, see [The Best Catan Expansions](/articles/best-catan-expansions/).
