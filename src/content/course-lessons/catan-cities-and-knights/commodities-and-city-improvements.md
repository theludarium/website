Commodities are the new currency of Cities & Knights. They come only from cities, they buy only a few things, and what they buy lasts all game. This lesson covers how commodities work, what each improvement costs and gives you, and the odds behind the progress cards, all from the 6th edition rules.

## Where commodities come from

A city produces a commodity on three terrains:

| Terrain | Commodity | Improvement track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forest | Paper | Science |
| Pasture | Cloth | Trade |
| Mountains | Coin | Politics |

The city also takes 1 of the terrain's resource. Settlements never make commodities, and cities on fields and hills make none.

You can also get commodities by trading. Other players will trade them like any card. With the supply, you can trade resources for commodities at your usual rates, including ports. You can trade commodities away at 4:1, or 3:1 with a 3:1 port.

## How fast they come

Count pips, as our [number probability lesson](/academy/catan/number-probability/) teaches. A city on a mountain marked 6 has 5 pips, so it produces a coin on about 5 of every 36 rolls. In a 4-player game, that is 4 rolls a round, or about 0.56 coin a round.

Now look at the cost of climbing:

| Level | Cost | Total to reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 3 | 6 |
| 4 | 4 | 10 |
| 5 | 5 | 15 |

From that one strong city alone, level 3 politics (6 coin) takes about 11 rounds. That is slow. It is why a second city on the same commodity, a 2:1 port on that terrain's resource, or trading at the table make such a difference. One good commodity source gets you started. Two get you to a metropolis.

## Climbing a track

On your turn, pay the commodities shown on the next space up and move your cube up one. You cannot skip spaces. You can make as many improvements as you can afford, on any tracks, as long as you have at least one city on the board.

If you lose your last city to the barbarians, you keep your levels but cannot climb again until you build another city.

## The progress card odds

Your level sets the range on the red die that draws you a card when the event die shows that track's gate:

| Level | Red die draws on | Chance per roll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | never | 0 |
| 1 | 1 or 2 | 2 in 36 |
| 2 | 1 to 3 | 3 in 36 |
| 3 | 1 to 4 | 4 in 36 |
| 4 | 1 to 5 | 5 in 36 |
| 5 | 1 to 6 | 6 in 36 |

The chance comes from two things lining up: the gate in your color (1 face in 6) and the red die in your range. At level 1 that is 1/6 times 2/6, or 2 in 36. In a 4-player game, that is about one card every 4 or 5 rounds. At level 3 it doubles, to about one card every 2 rounds. At level 5 every roll of that gate pays you.

Every player rolls, so these cards come on other players' turns too. Each level is a permanent raise.

## Level 3: a permanent ability

Reaching level 3 on a track gives you that track's ability for the rest of the game:

- **Science:** if a roll gives you no cards in the Production phase, take 1 resource of your choice. Not on a 7.
- **Trade:** trade any 2 identical commodities for any 1 other commodity or resource.
- **Politics:** promote strong knights to mighty knights.

Science smooths bad rolls. Trade turns spare commodities into whatever you need. Politics gives you strength 3 knights, the best defenders and the only knights that can displace a strong one. Lesson 11 compares them in depth.

## Levels 4 and 5: metropolises

The first player to reach level 4 on a track takes that track's metropolis and puts it on one of their cities. That control is temporary: the first player to reach level 5 on that track takes it for good, even from someone else.

A metropolis is worth 2 points on top of the city, so that city counts 4. It cannot be pillaged. It still counts toward the barbarians' strength, because it is still a city. You can hold several metropolises, but each needs its own city. If you have no free city to put it on, you cannot buy level 4 or 5 on that track.

Three metropolises at 2 points each are 6 of the 13 points you need, and they are the most stable points in the game. That is why the tracks matter so much, and why lesson 12 is all about metropolis races.

## Putting it together

Pick the track your cities feed best. Climb it early, while the levels are cheap, so the cards start coming. Spend commodities instead of holding them through 7s. And plan a second source before you need it, the way our [expansion planning lesson](/academy/catan/expansion-planning/) plans your next settlement.
