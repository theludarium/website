Two-player Lost Ruins of Arnak is fast, tight, and very direct. It shows your mistakes clearly because there is only one other player to blame. This lesson covers what the rules change at two players and how strong players use those changes.

All rules here follow the current English rulebook from Czech Games Edition.

## What changes at two players

- **Starting sites hold one archaeologist each.** You block the two-boot space at all 5 starting sites, so each one has room for a single archaeologist for the whole game.
- **Fewer research bonus tiles.** Some spaces on the research track get a bonus tile only in three- or four-player games. At two, those spaces start empty.
- **Smaller temple stacks.** Every temple tile stack has one tile per player, so each holds 2. There are only two 11-point tiles in the game.
- **Two Lost Temple arrivals.** The Lost Temple bonus stack has 2 tiles. The first glass to arrive chooses one, and the second takes what is left.
- **The first turn alternates.** The starting player marker passes left each round, so in a two-player game the first player starts rounds I, III, and V, and the second player starts rounds II and IV. The second player begins with a coin and a compass instead of 2 coins.

## Every placement is a block

With one space per starting site, sending your archaeologist somewhere closes it to your opponent for the round. That turns each dig into two questions: what does this site give me, and what does my opponent need from it?

Look at their research path. The bridges above their tokens show what they must pay next. If a starting site is their only cheap source of that resource this round, taking it first can cost them a whole research step.

You cannot touch their cards, figures, or tokens. No effect in the game allows it. Denial in Arnak always works through shared things: spaces, the card row, bonus tiles, and temple tiles.

## The race is the game

With fewer bonus tiles on the track and only two of each temple tile, the research race matters even more.

- **Bonus tiles** go to whoever arrives first. Look at which ones sit on both your paths and decide which you will fight for.
- **The Lost Temple** rewards the earlier arrival with more points, and the first arrival also breaks ties.
- **The 11-point tiles** are only two. If both of you are in the temple, each one either player takes is one fewer for the other.

A useful habit: each round, count how many research steps each of you needs to reach the temple, and what those steps cost. The [next lesson](/academy/lost-ruins-of-arnak/lost-ruins-of-arnak-lost-temple-race/) shows how.

## The card row moves slowly

With only two buyers, cards stay in the row longer. You can often see which card will sit next to the moon staff at the end of the round and be exiled. If your opponent clearly wants it and cannot afford it this round, it may be leaving on its own. If they can afford it, buying it first is a block that also improves your deck.

## Passing with one opponent

When your opponent passes, you take every remaining turn of the round in a row. That is a real swing:

- **Passing first** hands your opponent free rein over the spaces you both wanted. Pass first when you have nothing useful left, or when the spaces you care about are already yours.
- **Staying in late** lets you take what they left. But cards saved in hand cut into your fresh draw next round, so do not hold cards only to outlast them.

Look at who starts next round too. Passing order does not change it, since the marker simply alternates, but the starting player gets first pick of the single-space starting sites. If you start next round, you can afford to leave a contested site for now.

## Starting sites in round I

The first player has the first pick of the five single-space starting sites. The second player answers with a compass in place of a second coin. Use it: a compass is a step toward an artifact or a discovery, which gets you off the shared starting sites and onto your own.

## Tiebreaks in a duel

Ties are more common when two careful players count every point. The rulebook breaks them in favor of whoever reached the Lost Temple first. If neither did, the higher research score wins. Being first to the temple is worth points and the tie.
