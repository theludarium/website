Some games have world championships and national qualifiers. Lost Ruins of Arnak's competitive life is quieter. Most of it happens online, plus whatever you organize yourself. That is good news for a new player: the path from your kitchen table to strong opponents is a browser tab away.

## Is there an official Arnak championship?

Not that we could find. Czech Games Edition's [events page](https://czechgames.com/events) lists the conventions where it appears, such as Gen Con, Origins, UK Games Expo, and SPIEL Essen, but no Arnak championship series or organized play program.

## Board Game Arena

[Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=arnak) has hosted Lost Ruins of Arnak since 2021. It plays in your browser for 2 to 4 players, with friends or with players from around the world. Its game page lists a tutorial, real-time and turn-based play, and ranked games.

There is no solo mode there, so solo practice stays on the table with the rival tiles.

An official digital [Lost Ruins of Arnak](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3387300/Lost_Ruins_of_Arnak/) from CGE Digital is listed on Steam, and the publisher links to it from its own Arnak page. The listing promises scenarios with their own goals, AI opponents, and ranked online play. As of this writing, its release date is listed as to be announced.

## Run your own Arnak tournament

Since official events are rare, the best tournament may be the one you host. A few choices to make up front:

- **Board side and expansions.** Bird Temple side, base game only, is the fairest starting point. Everyone knows it.
- **Table size.** Two-player tables are faster and more direct. Four-player tables take longer, about 30 minutes per player by the publisher's estimate, but feel more like the game most people play at home.
- **Format.** A few Swiss rounds, where players with similar records meet, works well for a small group. Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) walks through Swiss, brackets, seeding, and tiebreaks.
- **Tiebreaks.** The rulebook already has one: first to the Lost Temple wins a tied game, and if no one reached it, the higher research score wins.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights where mid-weight euros like Arnak come out. Ask the staff. If you are in central Indiana, our guide to [Indianapolis board game stores](/articles/indianapolis-board-game-stores/) lists places to sit down and play.
- **Conventions.** Czech Games Edition's events page lists its convention appearances, which in 2026 included Gen Con in Indianapolis. Our [Gen Con first-timer guide](/articles/gen-con-first-timer-guide/) helps if you have never been.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play many two-player games.** They are fast, they show your mistakes clearly, and they are easy to find online.
- **Read your score breakdown.** After each game, compare your research, temple tiles, idols, guardians, card points, and Fear with the winner's. The gap usually lives in one or two categories.
- **Play the solo rival at higher difficulty.** Add red action tiles until it beats you about half the time. It is a patient sparring partner.
- **Switch boards.** The Snake Temple side tests whether you know the game or only one layout of it.
