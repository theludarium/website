The Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series is the biggest step on the ladder that any Duelist can walk into. No invite needed: register, bring a deck, and play the same Main Event as everyone else in the hall. This lesson walks through the weekend, from Konami's [YCS FAQ](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/ycs-faqs/) and Tournament Policy, and ends with a plan to get ready.

## The shape of the weekend

A YCS runs Friday to Sunday, and each one hosts two Main Events:

- **Genesys Main Event:** Day 1 on Friday, Day 2 on Sunday.
- **Advanced Main Event:** Day 1 on Saturday, Day 2 on Sunday.

If you make Day 2 in both, you choose one. Public Events, Regional Qualifiers, and Dragon Duel events for younger players run around them. The next North America YCS on Konami's calendar is **YCS Orlando**, November 6 to 8, 2026, at the Orange County Convention Center, followed by the **North America Remote Duel YCS**, December 4 to 6, 2026, on Discord.

## Before you travel

From the YCS FAQ:

- **Pre-register online** at Konami's registration site. That is the main way in. On-site registration happens only if space is left.
- **Entry means buying booster packs.** The number and price are set per event, so check the event page.
- **Submit your deck list** on the registration site by the deadline on the event page. You can update it until then. After that, it is final.
- **Link NEURON.** NEURON is required for on-site check-in for both Main Events. Make sure the CARD GAME ID it shows is yours.
- **Check in the day before Day 1.** Thursday for Genesys, Friday for Advanced, with photo ID. Miss it without telling the organizer, and your pre-registration can be canceled.

## How the rounds work

Swiss rounds are 50 minutes unless the event announces a change before it starts, and the double loss rule from lesson 17 applies to every one. The Tournament Policy sets the number of rounds by attendance. For example:

| Players | Day 1 rounds | Day 2 rounds | Top cut |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 257 to 512 | 8 | 4 | Top 8 |
| 513 to 1,024 | 8 | 5 | Top 8 |
| 1,025 to 2,048 | 9 | 5 | Top 8 |

How many players reach Day 2 depends on the event, so check its page. The Top 8 after all Swiss rounds play single elimination. The final Match is untimed, and the event FAQ says whether any other Top 8 Matches are too. Prize splits are not allowed at Tier 3 events.

## What earns an invite

In the Advanced Main Event, finishing in the Top 32 earns an invite to your region's next World Championship Qualifier. If attendance reaches 2,049 or more, places 33 to 64 earn one too. The winner also gets travel and lodging to that WCQ, for winners from North, Central, and South America. Invites go to your home region's WCQ, wherever you earned them.

The Genesys Main Event does not lead to a WCQ. Konami's YCS FAQ refers to a separate Genesys Championship and says more Genesys news is coming, so check the event page for what a Genesys finish earns. <!-- VERIFY: what a Top finish in a YCS Genesys Main Event earns, and any Genesys Championship dates. -->

You do not need a Top 32 to have a great weekend. Many players go for the Public Events, the trading, and the room itself.

## A four-week plan

**Four weeks out: lock the deck.** Pick the deck you will play, using lesson 18's advice: the deck you know best, unless it has a clear, lasting weakness. Play it at every locals.

**Three weeks out: build your forecast.** Name the three decks you expect most. Plan your Side Deck against them with lesson 15's three groups, and practice the swaps until they are fast.

**Two weeks out: train the clock.** Play full Matches on a 50-minute timer. Practice going first and going second in equal numbers, as lesson 10 suggests. Track which side you lose on, and fix it.

**One week out: freeze and prepare.** Stop changing the deck except for a new list update. Submit your deck list. Check sleeves for wear, and pack spares. Print or save the Tournament Policy. Book food and sleep as seriously as testing.

## On the day

- **Bring:** your deck, Side Deck, and Extra Deck in matching legal sleeves, spare sleeves, paper and pen for Life Points, any dice or counters you need, photo ID, and your phone with NEURON.
- **Eat and drink between rounds.** A Day 1 of eight or nine rounds is a long day. The FAQ says some events schedule a 30-minute meal break, and late arrivals to the next round earn tardiness penalties.
- **Reset after each round.** Restore your Main and Extra Decks after siding. It is a rule, and it saves you from starting a Match with the wrong cards.
- **Reset your head too.** A loss in round two does not end a Swiss event. With eight or more rounds on Day 1, one loss still leaves plenty of room.
