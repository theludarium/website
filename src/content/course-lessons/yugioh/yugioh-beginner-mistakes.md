Welcome to Level 1. You can play a full Duel now, and you can teach one. The next step is playing clean ones. A clean Duel is not about winning. It is a Duel where nobody has to rewind three plays because a rule slipped by.

Returning players make a predictable set of mistakes. Some come from rules that changed while you were away. More come from habits that were never quite right, even back then. Here are the ten that show up most, with the fix for each. Every rule below comes from Konami's Official Rulebook, Version 10, as changed by the 2021 Rules Update.

## Turn one slips

**1. Drawing on the first turn.** The player who goes first skips their draw on turn one. Old muscle memory says draw, so say it out loud instead: "Going first, no draw."

**2. Attacking on the first turn.** The first player cannot conduct a Battle Phase on their first turn either. Their first turn is Main Phase 1, then straight to the End Phase. This matters more than it sounds, because it shapes how modern decks play first. Lesson 10 covers that choice.

## Spell and Trap slips

**3. Springing a Trap the turn you Set it.** A Trap cannot be activated the turn it is Set. The same goes for a Quick-Play Spell you Set face down. Set it now, use it from your opponent's turn on. The exception is a card whose own text says otherwise, such as a Trap that can be activated from the hand.

**4. Resolving before your opponent can respond.** The rulebook warns against this directly: never resolve a card before asking whether your opponent wants to make a Chain. Activate, pause, then resolve. A simple "Okay?" is enough.

## Monster slips

**5. Two Normal Summons.** You get one Normal Summon or Set per turn. Setting a monster face down uses it. So does a Tribute Summon. Special Summons are unlimited unless a card says otherwise.

**6. Putting Link Monsters anywhere.** Under the 2021 rules, Fusion, Synchro, and Xyz Monsters from the Extra Deck can go into any of your Main Monster Zones. Link Monsters, and face-up Pendulum Monsters from the Extra Deck, cannot. They go into your Extra Monster Zone, or a Main Monster Zone that a Link Arrow points to. If you have no open zone like that, you cannot make the Summon.

**7. Link Monsters in Defense Position.** Link Monsters have no DEF. They can never be in Defense Position, not even by a card effect, and they can never be Set.

**8. Treating every trip to the Graveyard as "destroyed."** In the rulebook's glossary, a card is destroyed by battle or by an effect that destroys. A monster that is Tributed, or sent to the GY as a cost, is not destroyed. So an effect that triggers "if this card is destroyed" does not trigger when you Tribute it. Read the exact verb on the card.

## Effect slips

**9. Using a once-per-turn effect twice.** Many modern cards say "You can only use this effect of" a named card once per turn. That limit is on the name, not the copy. A second copy of the same card cannot use that effect again that turn. Some cards go further and say you can only activate one copy of that card per turn. Read the last line of every text box.

**10. Playing an old card by its old text.** Konami updates card text over time. At events, the Tournament Policy says you must play every card by its most recent text, even an old printing. Look up anything older than your starter in Konami's [card database](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/) before you trust the printing.

## Two habits that prevent most of these

**Say your phases.** "Draw. Standby. Main Phase 1." It catches slips 1 and 2 by itself, and it gives your opponent a clear moment to respond, which is how the rulebook says priority passes between players.

**Narrate your Extra Deck Summons.** Before each one, say the zone and why it is legal: "Link 2 into my Extra Monster Zone." Lesson 7 drills this.

## Hand size and other small things

A few smaller slips round out the list:

- **Hand size.** In your End Phase, discard down to 6. Not 5, not 7.
- **Battle position changes.** Once per monster per turn, and never on a monster the turn it arrived.
- **Public questions.** Your opponent may ask your hand size, your Deck count, what is in your Graveyard, or your Life Points. You must answer truthfully.
- **Tracking Life Points.** Use paper and a pen. At official events the Tournament Policy requires both players to keep Life Points on paper, even if they also use an app.

## When you are not sure

Pause the Duel, open the card's entry in the card database, and read the current text together. At home, agree on a call and move on. At a store, call a judge. Asking for a ruling is normal, and it is how everybody gets better at the rules.

None of these slips makes you a bad player. Each one just costs a little more than it should. Clean them up now, and everything you learn at Levels 2 and 3 will land on solid ground.
