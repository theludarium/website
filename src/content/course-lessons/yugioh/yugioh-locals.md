Walking into a card store after years away can feel like walking into a party where everyone already knows each other. Most of that feeling is just not knowing the routine. This lesson is the routine.

## Find an Official Tournament Store

Konami runs organized play through **Official Tournament Stores** (OTS). Find one near you with the official [OTS locator](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/ots-locations/).

Konami's new-player page suggests a gentle first step: drop in on a tournament night just to watch and see whether the store suits you. Most stores post their weekly schedule online or on a board by the register.

**Learn to Play events.** Konami's Duel Day page invites new players to learn the game at listed store locations. If your store runs one, it makes a gentle first visit, because teaching is the point. If not, ask the staff whether a regular can walk you through a game before the tournament starts. <!-- VERIFY: the Duel Day locations list showed no stores in October 2026; check it, or ask Konami how Learn to Play events are scheduled now. -->

## Before you go: get a CARD GAME ID

Konami tracks players through a **CARD GAME ID** in the official **NEURON** app. For sanctioned events like Yu-Gi-Oh! Day, Konami requires one, and you register for the event inside NEURON by scanning the store's code or searching for the event. Set it up at home. It saves time at the counter.

For Yu-Gi-Oh! Day, in-store registration also asked for a photo ID matching your legal name, and Duelists under 16 needed a parent or guardian to sign. Your store can tell you what its own events need.

## What to bring

- **Your deck,** in matching sleeves, in a deck box.
- **Your Extra Deck and Side Deck.** The Side Deck holds up to 15 cards you can swap in between Duels.
- **A way to track Life Points.** A notepad and pen are best. The rulebook suggests tracking LP on paper during important Duels, even if you also use a calculator or app.
- **A coin, a die, and counters,** if any of your cards need them.
- **Your phone,** with NEURON signed in.

## Which format is it?

Ask before you sit down. Many stores run **Advanced**, the standard format with the current Forbidden and Limited list. Some run **Genesys**, which bans Link and Pendulum Monsters and uses a point cap instead of the usual list. Your deck must fit the format of that night.

## How the night runs

Most store tournaments use **Swiss rounds**. You are paired against someone with a similar record each round, and you play every round whether you win or lose, so nobody goes home after one match.

- **Each round is a best-of-three Match.** Win two Duels and you win the Match.
- **Rounds have a time limit.** At Yu-Gi-Oh! Day, Matches had 50 minutes in store, and if time ran out before the Match was decided, both players took a loss. Play at a steady pace.
- **The number of rounds depends on attendance.** At Yu-Gi-Oh! Day, 4 to 8 players meant 3 rounds, and the count grew with the crowd.
- **Between Duels, you may side.** Swap cards between your Side Deck and your Main or Extra Deck. Your Side Deck count must stay the same, and you show your opponent the count.
- **The loser of each Duel decides** who goes first in the next one.

## Table etiquette

Most of good etiquette comes straight from the rulebook:

1. **Greet your opponent.** Shuffle your deck well, then offer it to be shuffled or cut. Handle your opponent's cards with care when it is your turn to cut.
2. **Say your phases.** "Draw. Standby. Main Phase." Announcing the end of a phase is how you pass the chance to respond. It also prevents arguments.
3. **Pause before you resolve.** After you activate something, give your opponent a moment to respond.
4. **Answer public questions truthfully.** Hand size, cards left in the Deck, the cards in each Graveyard, and Life Points are all public. If asked, you must answer truthfully.
5. **Ask before touching.** You may look through your opponent's Graveyard, but ask first and keep the cards in order.
6. **Call a judge** when you disagree on a rule. It is normal, not rude, and it keeps the game friendly.

## Win or lose, stay a minute

Shake hands, record the result the way the store asks, and if the game was close, ask what your opponent would have done differently. Regulars remember the new player who asked good questions.
