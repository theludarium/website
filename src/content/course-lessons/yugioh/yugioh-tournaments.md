Yu-Gi-Oh! organized play is built as a ladder. Each step earns invitations to the next, all the way to a World Championship. Most players never go past their store, and that is a fine way to enjoy the game. But knowing the ladder exists changes how you look at a Tuesday night tournament.

## Step 1: locals at your store

Everything starts at an **Official Tournament Store**. Weekly store tournaments are where you learn your deck, meet regulars, and find out how you handle a clock. Lesson 11 covers your first night. Find a store with Konami's [OTS locator](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/ots-locations/).

## Step 2: OTS Championships

The **OTS Championship** is a store-level championship that runs at Official Tournament Stores at the same time across North and Latin America. Konami's [OTS Championship page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/otschampionship/) describes it as a way to earn invites toward a World Championship Qualifier. It is the natural first "big" event: same store, same faces, higher stakes.

In the US and Canada, the most recent one ran on August 22 or 23, 2026, as a Tier 2 Advanced event with 50-minute rounds. At each store, the Top 4 Duelists earned an invite to the 2027 World Championship Qualifier. Konami posts the next date and its FAQ on the same page.

## Step 3: Regional Qualifiers

**Regional Qualifiers** are Tier 2 tournaments too, where Duelists compete for invites to their World Championship Qualifier and for exclusive prizes. The [Regional Qualifier page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/regional-qualifier/) links to the current locations and FAQ.

## Step 4: the Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series

The **YCS** is Konami's traveling weekend event, with hundreds of Duelists, exclusive prizes, and invites to a World Championship Qualifier. It is also a festival: Dueling, trading, and hanging out with people who love the game.

Upcoming dates on Konami's [YCS page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/events/ygo-championship-series):

- **YCS Orlando,** Florida: November 6 to 8, 2026.
- **North America Remote Duel YCS,** online over Discord: December 4 to 6, 2026.
- Latin America has its own YCS events, including Santiago, Chile (November 27 to 29, 2026), Lima, Peru (February 5 to 7, 2027), and Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic (February 26 to 28, 2027).

Plenty of players go to a YCS for the crowd and the trading, not just the main event. If one comes near you, it is worth a day trip just to see the scale of it.

## Step 5: World Championship Qualifiers and Worlds

**World Championship Qualifiers** are invite-only. Duelists who earned invites at the earlier steps compete for a chance to represent their region at the **Yu-Gi-Oh! World Championship**. The 2026 North America WCQ was held at the Minneapolis Convention Center, July 10 to 12, 2026.

## Remote Duel: play from home

Official Tournament Stores can also run **Remote Duel** tournaments, hosted on Discord, with a webcam, phone, or tablet showing your field. Konami has also scheduled a Remote Duel YCS for December 2026. For players far from a store, it is a real way onto the ladder. See the [Remote Duel page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/remoteduel/) for setup rules.

## Two formats

- **Advanced** is the standard format, using the current Forbidden and Limited list.
- **Genesys** drops Link and Pendulum Monsters, plays on the original field with no Extra Monster Zones, and caps decks with points instead of the usual list. Konami calls it a fully supported tournament system, and stores can host Genesys events. The August 2026 OTS Championship was Advanced only, so check the format of any ladder event before you build. For a returning player, Genesys can be a gentle way back into competition, and lesson 19 covers building for it.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play the same deck for a season.** Knowing every line of one deck beats owning five.
- **Practice going first and going second.** Many decks play very differently on each side of the coin flip. Know your plan for both.
- **Play on a clock.** Time a few practice matches at 50 minutes to learn your pace.
- **Learn the rules deeply.** The Chain rules from lesson 6 and the Damage Step rulings from lesson 16 decide close games. Konami takes rules questions by email, and judges at events are there to help.
- **Review your losses.** Which card did you wish you had drawn? Which play did you rush?

## Finding people to play with

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents:

- **Your local OTS.** Ask the staff which nights are casual and which are competitive.
- **Master Duel,** Konami's free-to-play digital card game, for practice any time.
- **Friends with old binders.** Lesson 4's teach script and the 2-Player Starter Set make it easy to bring a friend back with you.
