Returning players make great teachers. You remember what it felt like to learn, and you know the classic turn by heart. The trap is wanting to teach everything you just relearned, Link Arrows and Chains included, before the new player has drawn a card.

This script keeps the first teach to ten minutes. It works best with the two decks from the 2-Player Starter Set, and it pairs with the printable [Yu-Gi-Oh! teach sheet](/academy/yugioh/teach-sheet/). For the general method behind it, see our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/).

## Before you sit down

Set the table so the teach starts with the game ready:

- One deck per player, shuffled, face down in the Deck Zone.
- Extra Decks to the side, face down.
- A notepad or calculator for Life Points.
- The teach sheet in the middle.

Then decide one thing: comic or script. If the new player likes reading, hand them the starter's 64-page teaching comic and play its scripted Duel together, card by card. It covers everything up to Synchro and Xyz. If they would rather just play, use the script below.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "We each start with 8000 Life Points. Get mine to zero and you win. Monsters fight, Spells help, Traps surprise."

Hold up one card of each kind. Point at the colors: "Orange and yellow are monsters. Green are Spells. Pink are Traps."

## Minutes 2 to 4: the field and one turn

Point at your half of the table. "Monsters go in this row of five. Spells and Traps go in the row behind. The pile on the right is the Graveyard, and anyone can look through it."

Then play a real turn while you narrate:

1. "I draw a card." (If you are going first, skip it, and say why: "The first player doesn't draw on their first turn.")
2. "Once per turn, I can Normal Summon one monster. Upright is attacking. Or I can Set it face down sideways, hidden, to defend."
3. "Big monsters cost Tributes. Level 5 or 6, I give up one of my monsters. Level 7 or more, two."
4. "I can Set a Trap face down. I can't use it this turn, but from the start of your turn, I can spring it."
5. "Going first means no attacks on my first turn. So I end my turn."

Stop. Ask: "Questions on the turn?"

## Minutes 5 to 7: the first battle

Let the new player take their turn with a little coaching. When they attack, teach battle with their own cards:

- "Attack against attack: higher ATK wins, and the loser takes the difference."
- "Attack against a defender: beat its DEF and it's destroyed, but nobody takes damage. Fall short and you take the difference."
- "No monsters on my side? You hit me directly for your full ATK."

Battle is the moment the game clicks. Let it land before you say anything new.

## Minutes 8 to 9: Spells, Traps, and responses

Wait until a Trap gets activated, then explain the one idea that matters:

"When someone plays a card, the other player can respond. The newest card resolves first." That is a Chain, in one sentence. Skip Spell Speeds for now.

Then name the hand limit: "At the end of your turn, if you have more than 6 cards, discard down to 6."

## Minute 10: the Extra Deck, in one breath

Only now, point at the Extra Deck. "These are special monsters. You don't draw them. You make them from other monsters, usually ones already on your field, following the recipe on the card. I'll show you the first time one of us does it."

Then play. Teach each Extra Deck Summon the first time it is legal, using the cards on the table.

## What to leave out of the first Duel

- **Link and Pendulum.** The starter's teaching comic stops at Synchro and Xyz, and so should you. Save Link Arrows and the Extra Monster Zone rule for Duel three, with lesson 2 open.
- **Spell Speeds** and Damage Step timing. Teach them when a real question comes up.
- **Deck building and ban lists.** Nobody needs them to enjoy Duel one.

## Coaching during the first Duel

Help with reading, not deciding. "That card's effect only works when it's Summoned" is helpful. "You should attack with that one" is playing for them.

Say your phases out loud on your own turns. It teaches the rhythm without a lecture, and it is good store etiquette too.
