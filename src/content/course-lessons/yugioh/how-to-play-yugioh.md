Maybe you had a binder in middle school. Maybe you dueled on the bus, or on a bedroom floor, with a deck held together by a rubber band. Then life happened, and Yu-Gi-Oh! kept going without you. This course is for that player: someone who knows what a Trap Card is, and wants to know what everything else means now.

Good news first. The heart of the game has not moved. You still start at 8000 Life Points. You still get one Normal Summon a turn. A Level 7 monster still needs two Tributes, and Traps still have to be Set before you can spring them. What changed is everything built on top of that.

## What changed while you were away

Here is the short list. Lesson 2 walks through each one in full.

- **More ways to Summon.** Fusion and Ritual are still here. Synchro Monsters (built from a Tuner plus other monsters, adding up Levels) and Xyz Monsters (built from monsters of the same Level) arrived later. Then came Pendulum Monsters, which can sit in your Spell & Trap Zone as Spells, and Link Monsters, which point arrows at zones on the field.
- **A new zone in the middle.** The Extra Monster Zone sits between the two players. It controls where some monsters from your Extra Deck can go.
- **The first player does not draw.** The player who goes first skips their draw on turn one, and cannot attack that turn either.
- **New words for old ideas.** "Removed from play" is now "banished." The Graveyard is written "GY" on cards. What some remember as Multi-Trigger effects are now called Quick Effects.
- **A faster game.** Modern decks Special Summon many monsters in a single turn. A strong first turn can build a board full of monsters that stop your plays. Lesson 2 shows how the rules allow it, and lesson 11 shows what to expect at a store.

None of this is as hard as it sounds. It just helps to relearn it in the right order.

## The best first buy: the 2-Player Starter Set

Konami's starter for new Duelists is the **Yu-Gi-Oh! TRADING CARD GAME 2-Player Starter Set**. It holds two different 44-card decks, each a 40-card Main Deck plus a 4-card Extra Deck. The star of the box is a 64-page teaching comic that walks two players through a scripted Duel, card by card. It starts at the first draw and ends with Synchro and Xyz Summons from the Extra Deck.

That scripted Duel suits a returning player well. You already know half of it, so you move fast through the familiar parts and slow down where the new rules kick in. Once the teaching Duel is done, the two decks work as complete decks you can play again and again.

It is the right pick when:

- Two of you are learning or relearning together, such as a parent and a kid.
- You want to play at a kitchen table before you play at a store.
- You want to know the modern turn before you spend money on singles.

Konami lists it on the [2-Player Starter Set product page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/products/2-player-starter-set/). It launched in January 2024 at a list price of about $20.

The comic covers Synchro and Xyz, but not Link or Pendulum. Lessons 2 and 3 fill that gap.

## Free before you buy

Two free ways to see the modern game first:

- **Konami's beginner videos.** The official [Yu-Gi-Oh! For Beginners](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/forbeginners/) page has a five-chapter beginner series and an eight-chapter advanced series that ends with Link Monsters.
- **Master Duel.** Konami's digital card game is free-to-play, with optional in-game purchases, and lets you Duel other players online. It is the fastest way to watch a modern combo turn happen, with the game enforcing every rule for you.

## What about your old cards?

Most old cards are still legal to play. Three things to check first:

1. **The Forbidden and Limited list.** Some cards are Forbidden (no copies), Limited (1 copy), or Semi-Limited (2 copies) at official events. Konami posts the [current list](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/limited/) on its site, and it changes regularly.
2. **The current card text.** Rules and wording have shifted over the years. Look up any old card in Konami's official card database before you rely on what the printed text seems to say.
3. **The pace.** A deck of old favorites can still be a joy at home. Against a modern store deck, it will likely struggle. That is fine. Lesson 8 covers how to upgrade.

## What to skip for now

**Booster packs as a deck plan.** Packs are random. Open them for fun, not to build a deck.

**Structure Decks as a first buy.** Konami's own [New to Yu-Gi-Oh! page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/about/new-to-ygo/) aims Structure Decks at more advanced players, while noting that customizing one is a good way to learn deck building. That makes them a great second step, which lesson 8 covers.

**Speed Duel products,** unless you want them on purpose. Speed Duel is a simplified format based on the Duel Links ruleset, with its own Skill Cards. It is fun, but it is not the full game this course teaches.

## Keep the first purchase small

Buy the starter, play both decks until they feel easy, and stop there for a few weeks. Once you know which cards you wish you had, the upgrades in lesson 8 will make far more sense.
