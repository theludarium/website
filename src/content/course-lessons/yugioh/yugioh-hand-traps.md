Welcome to Level 3. At Level 2 you built a deck that opens its plan most games. Now you learn to stop your opponent's. In modern Yu-Gi-Oh!, the main tool for that is the hand trap: a card you activate from your hand, usually on your opponent's turn, to stop part of their play.

## What makes a hand trap

A hand trap is not an official card type. It is a player term for cards that can be used straight from the hand, often during the opponent's turn. Most are monsters with a Quick Effect that you discard or send to the GY. A few are Traps with text that lets you activate them from the hand.

Their strength is surprise. A Set Trap is visible on the field. A hand trap is just one more card in a hand of five or six.

## Common hand traps, in plain words

All texts below are from Konami's [card database](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/), and the list status is from the Forbidden and Limited List effective October 19, 2026.

- **Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring.** Discard it to negate an effect that adds a card from the Deck to the hand, Special Summons from the Deck, or sends a card from the Deck to the GY. Once per turn. Not on the list, so 3 copies are allowed.
- **Effect Veiler.** During your opponent's Main Phase, send it from your hand to the GY to negate the effects of one face-up Effect Monster they control until the end of the turn. Not on the list.
- **Infinite Impermanence.** A Trap that negates a face-up monster's effects until the end of the turn. If you control no cards, you can activate it from your hand. Not on the list.
- **Ghost Ogre & Snow Rabbit.** When a monster on the field, or a face-up Spell or Trap already on the field, activates its effect, send this from your hand or field to destroy that card. Once per turn. Not on the list.
- **Nibiru, the Primal Being.** During the Main Phase, after your opponent has Normal or Special Summoned 5 or more monsters that turn, Tribute as many face-up monsters on the field as possible to Special Summon it, and give your opponent a Token. Once per turn. Not on the list.
- **Droll & Lock Bird.** Limited, so 1 copy at most.
- **Maxx "C".** Forbidden in the Advanced Format.

## The core question: which card?

Every combo turn has cards it can replace and cards it cannot. Modern decks often run several starters that do the same job, so stopping one may just send the player to the next.

Ask three questions before you respond:

1. **Can they replace this?** If they have shown several ways to do the same thing, stopping the first one may cost them very little.
2. **What does it lead to?** A search that finds the card that opens their whole turn is worth more than a search for a backup piece.
3. **Will I get a better window?** Some hand traps can only answer certain actions. Ash Blossom only answers Deck searches, Summons from the Deck, and sends from the Deck. If the turn's key card does one of those, wait for it.

This is often called finding the choke point: the action that, once stopped, leaves the rest of the turn with nowhere to go. Learning a deck's choke points is how you get better against it, and it is one reason practice against the decks at your store pays off.

## When early is right

Waiting is not always better. Stopping the very first card is right when:

- **The first card is the only starter in their hand,** as far as you can tell. Stop it, and the turn may end there.
- **Your hand trap only works early.** Effect Veiler, for one, needs a face-up Effect Monster to target during the Main Phase. If the key monster resolves its effect on Summon and leaves, there may be nothing to target later.
- **Every later step is protected.** Some boards end with cards that stop your hand traps. Use yours before those land.

## Playing around your opponent's answers

Your opponent knows hand traps exist. Two cards were built to blunt them:

- **Called by the Grave** banishes a monster from your GY and negates its effects. Lesson 6 showed it stopping Ash Blossom.
- **Crossout Designator** names a card, banishes a copy of it from your own Deck, and negates the effects of that card name for the rest of the turn.

Both became Forbidden in the Advanced Format on the October 19, 2026 list. In Genesys they are still legal, at 20 points each, so expect them there. In Advanced, your opponent's answers are now mostly their own hand traps and the protection their board builds.

If your opponent holds an answer back, consider making them use it on a smaller threat first. Two hand traps in one turn are much harder to answer than one.

## Hand traps going first

Hand traps are not only for going second. When you go first, they sit in your hand during your opponent's turn and stop their first big play. That is why a block of them fits most decks. From lesson 9's table, 9 hand traps in 40 cards puts at least one in your opening hand about 74% of the time going first.

## The habit

Before your opponent's turn, name what you are waiting for. "Ash for the search into their field spell." Then wait for it, unless one of the reasons to go early shows up. A named target beats a nervous reflex every time.
