Most Duels are decided by decks and decisions. Close ones are often decided by rulings: a card that could not be activated when someone thought it could, or a trigger that went on the Chain in the wrong order. This lesson covers the ones that come up most, from Konami's rulebook, Version 10.

## The Damage Step

When you declare an attack, play moves from the Battle Step into the Damage Step, where damage is calculated. The Damage Step has strict limits.

- **Only two kinds of cards can be activated:** Counter Traps, and cards with effects that directly change a monster's ATK or DEF.
- **Only until damage calculation starts.** After that, those windows close.

A Trap that destroys an attacking monster, for example, is not something you can activate once the Damage Step has begun. Play it in the Battle Step, when the attack is declared. That timing is one of the most common rulings returning players miss.

The rulebook points to a longer Damage Step guide on Konami's site for the finer cases. <!-- VERIFY: the Damage Step page linked in the Version 10 rulebook (yugioh-card.com/en/gameplay/damage_step/) returned an empty page in October 2026; link a current Konami page if one exists. -->

## Attacking a face-down monster

If you attack a face-down Defense Position monster, it is flipped face up in the Damage Step, and damage is calculated using its DEF. Its Flip effect activates and resolves after damage calculation. If that Flip effect targets, it cannot target a monster already destroyed in that battle.

## Replays

Say you declare an attack, and before the Damage Step a card removes the monster you targeted, or a new monster appears on your opponent's side. That is a replay. You may attack again with the same monster, attack with a different one, or not attack at all. If you switch to a different monster, the first one still counts as having declared an attack and cannot attack again that turn.

## Did it battle?

The rulebook says a monster has "battled" only if the attack reached damage calculation. If an effect stops the attack before then, the monster did not battle, though it did declare an attack. That matters for any card that cares whether a monster battled.

## Triggers that happen together

Sometimes two or more Speed 1 effects, such as Trigger Effects, activate at the same moment. They go on one Chain in this order:

1. The turn player's mandatory effects, in any order they choose.
2. The opponent's mandatory effects, in any order.
3. The turn player's optional effects, in any order.
4. The opponent's optional effects, in any order.

Then the Chain resolves from the top down, as always. So the opponent's optional effect, added last, resolves first. Mandatory effects say what happens with no "You can." Optional ones say "You can."

Separately, when one effect makes both players do something at the same time, such as "each player targets 1 monster," the turn player acts first.

## Costs are not effects

Anything before the semicolon in an effect's text is done on activation. Costs, such as discarding, paying Life Points, or Tributing, are paid before the card is activated. The rulebook is firm: if the activation is negated, the cost is not refunded. That is why negating a card that cost its owner two cards can swing a Duel.

## Destroyed, sent, banished, Tributed

The exact verb matters:

- **Destroyed** means sent to the GY by battle or by an effect that destroys.
- **Tributed** or **sent as a cost** is not destroyed.
- **Banished** cards go out of play, not to the GY. A banished card that later moves to the GY is not "sent to the GY" for card effects.
- **Leaves the field** includes going to the GY, being banished, or returning to the hand or Extra Deck. A monster shuffled into the Main Deck or turned into Xyz Material leaves the field, but its "leaves the field" effects do not activate.

## When versus if

Optional Trigger Effects come in two common wordings. "When ... : You can" and "If ... : You can." They look alike. They are not.

A "When ... : You can" effect can only activate if the event that triggers it was the last thing to happen. If something else happened after it, such as another cost or another part of the same effect resolving, the effect cannot activate. Players call this missing the timing. An "If ... : You can" effect does not have this problem. It can activate in the next Chain even if other things happened after its trigger. Mandatory effects do not miss the timing either.

Most modern cards use "If" for exactly this reason. Older cards with "When" are where the ruling still bites.

## When the rulings get hard

- **Use the card database.** Konami's [card database](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/) lists current text, and NEURON's card search does the same at the table.
- **Call a judge at events.** At Tier 2 and higher events, KONAMI Judges are required, and the Tournament Policy lets you appeal a floor judge's ruling to the Head Judge.
- **Ask Konami.** For questions that come up at home, Konami takes rules questions by email through its site.
