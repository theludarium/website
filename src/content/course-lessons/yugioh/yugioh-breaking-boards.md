Your opponent went first and ended with a field full of monsters and Set cards. Many returning players see that and feel the Duel is over. Often it is not. A board is not a wall. It is a fixed number of chances to stop you, and you can spend them down.

## Count the interruptions

Before you play a card, read the board. For each card your opponent controls, ask:

- **Can it stop me?** Look for Quick Effects, Set Spell and Trap Cards, and monsters that negate or destroy.
- **How often?** Most effects say once per turn. A few can be used more than once.
- **What can it stop?** Some stop only Spells. Some stop only monster effects. Some answer anything.

Add those up. If your opponent has three ways to stop you, your turn needs four good actions, or a way to remove their interruptions without triggering them.

Public knowledge helps here. The Tournament Policy says printed card text is always public, and your opponent must answer truthfully about cards in the GY, Life Points, and hand size. Read their face-up cards in the card database, or ask a judge, before you commit.

## Bait first, real play second

Order your plays from least important to most. If your opponent has one negation and you have two starters, lead with the weaker one. If they use the negation on it, your stronger starter resolves. If they save it, you have still made progress.

This works best with cards that are threats your opponent cannot ignore. A bait they can safely let resolve is not a bait.

## Board breakers, and their conditions

A board breaker removes or shuts off several cards at once. Every one has a condition, and the condition decides when you can play it. Texts are from Konami's [card database](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/), and list status from the list effective October 19, 2026.

- **Harpie's Feather Duster.** Destroys all Spells and Traps your opponent controls. Limited to 1.
- **Lightning Storm.** Only if you control no face-up cards. Choose: destroy all your opponent's Attack Position monsters, or all their Spells and Traps. You can only activate 1 per turn. Since it needs you to control no face-up cards, play it before you Summon anything.
- **Dark Ruler No More.** Negates the effects of the face-up monsters your opponent currently controls until the end of the turn, and neither player can activate monster effects in response to it. The cost: for the rest of the turn after it resolves, your opponent takes no damage. Use it to build your own board, not to win by attacking.
- **Evenly Matched.** A Trap. At the end of the Battle Phase, if your opponent controls more cards than you, make them banish cards from their field face down until they control as many as you. Your opponent chooses which ones go. If you control no cards, you can activate it from your hand, so to use it on your first turn you need to enter your Battle Phase with an empty field.

None of these is on the list except Harpie's Feather Duster, so the others can run 3 copies.

## Which breaker for which board

Match the breaker to what the board relies on:

- **Interruptions mostly on Set Spells and Traps?** Harpie's Feather Duster or the Spell and Trap mode of Lightning Storm.
- **Interruptions mostly on monster Quick Effects?** Dark Ruler No More, which also stops monster effects in response to it.
- **A wide board of many cards?** Evenly Matched cuts it down to your count, though your opponent keeps their best cards.

Many board breakers can be stopped by the very cards they target. A Set card that negates Spells can answer Harpie's Feather Duster. Bait it with something else first if you can.

## A sample sequence

Say your opponent has two monsters with negation effects and one Set card. You hold Lightning Storm, a starter, and a hand trap. One sensible order:

1. **Lightning Storm** first, while you control no face-up cards. Choose the mode that removes the most interruptions.
2. **Your starter** next. If something survived, it may use its last interruption here.
3. **Keep the hand trap** for anything they activate in response, or for their next turn.

The exact best order depends on the cards. The principle holds: clear the way, then commit.

## When to stop

Sometimes the board cannot be broken this turn. Then the goal changes to surviving. Keep cards that matter for later, Set defense, and make your opponent use resources on their next turn. A Duel lost slowly sometimes turns around. A Duel where you spent everything into a board you could not break rarely does.
