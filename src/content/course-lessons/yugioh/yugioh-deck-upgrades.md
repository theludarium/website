Your starter decks are a fine way to learn. Sooner or later, though, you will want a deck of your own. The good news: a strong, fun deck does not have to cost much, as long as you spend on purpose. This lesson is about spending on purpose.

## Pick one plan

Modern Yu-Gi-Oh! decks are built around a strategy, usually a family of cards that share a name or theme and help each other. Players call these archetypes. Cards in an archetype often search each other out of the Deck, which is how a modern deck finds its best plays so often.

Pick one. Not two, not a pile of favorites. One plan, with enough copies of its key cards to see them every game.

How to choose:

- **Play it first.** Konami's digital Master Duel is a cheap way to try a strategy before you buy it in paper.
- **Look at new sets.** Each booster set adds new themes and support. The current set, Beyond the Brave (October 2026), is built around Joey Wheeler's cards and a few other themes.
- **Pick what you like looking at.** You will shuffle this deck a few hundred times. Love it.

## Structure Decks: a strong starting point

A Structure Deck is a ready-made deck built around one strategy, and Konami's own new-player page notes that customizing one is a good way to learn deck building. That makes it the natural second purchase after the starter.

Konami's Structure Deck list also includes all-foil Chronicles Decks, but the most recent standard Structure Deck on it is [Structure Deck: Blue-Eyes White Destiny](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/products/sdwd/), from February 2025. That was still true on the page in October 2026, so check it for anything newer before you buy.

A common budget approach is to buy more than one copy of the same Structure Deck, so you can run 3 copies of its best cards. Before you do, compare the price of the extra copies against just buying those few cards as singles.

## Why singles beat packs

A booster pack is a surprise. A single is a decision. If your deck needs one specific card, buying packs to find it is the most expensive way to get it.

Where to buy singles:

- **Your local store.** Many Official Tournament Stores sell singles over the counter. Buying there also helps keep the place you play in business.
- **Online marketplaces,** where sellers list cards by name and printing.
- **Trades** with other players at the store.

Three habits save money:

1. **Buy the cheapest printing.** Every TCG printing of a card with the same name plays exactly the same. Rarity and edition change the price, not the game. One catch: cards printed for Asian markets, including Asian English cards, are not legal at TCG events.
2. **Check the card number** in the listing so you get the card you meant to buy.
3. **Buy for the deck, not the binder.** Shiny versions can come later.

## Check the list before you buy

Some cards are Forbidden, Limited to 1, or Semi-Limited to 2 at official events. Konami updates the [Forbidden and Limited list](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/limited/) regularly. Check it before every purchase. Nothing hurts like buying three copies of a card the week it drops to one.

## Keep it at 40

The rulebook's own advice is to keep your deck close to the 40-card minimum. More cards means you draw your best ones less often. When you add a card, take one out.

A rough shape many decks follow:

- **Starters:** cards that begin your main plan on their own. You want to see one almost every game.
- **Extenders:** cards that keep the plan going after your opponent stops the first piece.
- **Interaction:** cards that stop your opponent, especially ones with Quick Effects you can use on their turn.

## Build the Extra Deck last

Your Extra Deck holds up to 15 cards: Fusion, Synchro, Xyz, and Link Monsters. Many decks also use general Link and Xyz Monsters that fit any strategy with the right materials. Fill the Extra Deck once the Main Deck is settled, because the Main Deck decides which Extra Deck monsters you can reach.

## A format that rewards older cards

Some Official Tournament Stores now run **Genesys**, an official format with no Link or Pendulum Monsters and no Extra Monster Zones. Instead of the usual ban list, some cards cost points and a deck must stay within a point cap, usually 100. If your old collection is mostly Fusion, Synchro, and Xyz, Genesys can be a friendly way back in. The [Genesys page](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/genesys/) has the point list.

## Sleeves and the rest

Sleeve your deck. The rulebook says that if you use sleeves, they all have to match, so no card stands out. A sturdy deck box and a pad of paper for Life Points finish the kit.
