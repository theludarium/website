Spirit Island has one of the most loved solo modes in the hobby. Our [best solo board games](/articles/best-solo-board-games/) guide calls it excellent at one player. It is also the best practice there is: every decision is yours, and every mistake is easy to trace.

## The solo rules

The core rulebook's solo rules are short:

- **One Island board is the whole Island.**
- **You may target yourself** with Powers that say "another Spirit." You do not get any extra benefit a Power gives for helping someone else.
- Everything else is a normal game. The Fear Pool holds 4 Fear markers, one player's worth, and the Fear Deck is the usual 9 cards in three groups.

The rulebook adds a warning: luck matters more in a solo game, and you have no fellow Spirits to cover your Spirit's weaknesses.

## What changes with one board

One board has 8 lands, two of each terrain. So each one-terrain Invader card hits two lands, and each Stage III card hits four, half the Island. Setup Invaders are fewer, the Fear Pool is smaller, and a single Spirit's Range covers much more of the Island than it would on four boards.

The pressure is different, not lighter. You are the only source of Damage, Defend, movement, and Fear, and the Invaders do not care which jobs your Spirit is bad at.

## Choosing a solo Spirit

Use the three early jobs from the [starter Spirits lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-starter-spirits/): stopping Builds, stopping Ravages, and destroying buildings for Fear. In a team game, each Spirit can specialize. Solo, you need all three, or a plan for the one you lack.

Among the starters:

- **Lightning's Swift Strike** destroys buildings well, but has to find answers for spreading Explorers.
- **Vital Strength of the Earth** holds lands safe, and needs time and Power Cards to clear them.
- **River Surges in Sunlight** moves Invaders and Dahan, which covers a lot of ground on a small Island.
- **Shadows Flicker Like Flame** stops Builds and generates Fear, and can struggle when Towns and Cities pile up.

Whatever you choose, use your Power Card draws to fill the gaps. A Spirit that destroys buildings should value a Minor that stops a Build. A defensive Spirit should value one that deals Damage.

## Playing two Spirits

Many solo players control two Spirits on a two-board Island. By the rules, that is simply a two-player game run by one person: two boards, 8 Fear markers, two Spirits, two hands. Our solo guide notes that one Spirit is a tight hour, while two make a full evening.

Two-handed play has real advantages for learning:

- **Teamwork practice.** You learn which Spirits pair well, and how to stack Powers on one land.
- **Less luck.** Two Spirits cover each other's weak spots, as the rulebook says teammates do.
- **Easier tracking.** Keep each Spirit's cards, Energy, and Elements in separate areas.

Run the Spirit Phase one Spirit at a time, then resolve Fast Powers together, then the Invaders, then Slow Powers. Do not try to hold both hands in your head at once.

## Thematic and solo

For the thematic map, the rulebook's solo layout uses the Northeast board. Save it for later; the thematic map adds 3 to a game's Difficulty, and the [thematic map lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-thematic-map-and-high-difficulty/) at Level 5 covers it.

## Solo as practice

A few habits turn solo games into practice for the group table:

- **Play the same Spirit three games in a row.** You learn its Growth rhythm and its best turns.
- **Name the clock that beat you.** Deck, Blight, or a lost Spirit. The [endgame lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-endgame/) shows how to read them.
- **Score every game** with the rulebook's scoring, so you can compare games over time.
- **Try the easier settings** if a Spirit keeps losing early: a bonus Growth at setup, or skipping the first Explore.
