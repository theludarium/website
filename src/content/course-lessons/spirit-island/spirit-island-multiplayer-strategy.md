Spirit Island works solo, but it is built for a team. Two to four Spirits share one Island, one Invader board, and one fate. This lesson is about turning a table of separate engines into one plan, and then about where to find more people to play with.

## Talk is part of the game

Everyone acts at the same time in every phase, and the rulebook encourages talking it through. It goes further: table talk is likely necessary for victory. Plans, warnings, and requests ("can you Push that Explorer out of my Jungle?") are all normal play.

What you cannot share is your engine. Energy and Card Plays belong to one Spirit and cannot be transferred. Help each other with Powers, not with resources.

## Split the Island by board

Each Spirit starts on its own Island board, though once play begins, Presence and Powers can go anywhere. A board is still a natural home base.

The rulebook offers a habit that helps new players: each player runs the Invader Actions on their own starting board. You learn how the Invaders behave by moving them yourself, and nobody accidentally Explores twice.

Boards also settle arguments. When a choice is not covered by a Power, such as where Blight cascades, the table tries to agree. If it cannot, decisions in a land go to the Spirit whose starting board holds it, and anything else goes to the game's owner or organizer. When a choice comes from a Power, the Spirit using it decides.

## Talk in objectives, not details

The rulebook warns that trying to remember every detail of every player's turn is a recipe for overload. Focus on objectives, and get into details only when they matter.

A good planning sentence names a threat and an owner: "I have the Sands Ravage. You take the new Jungle Explorers." Each player then works out their own cards. That keeps the talk short and keeps every player in charge of their own Spirit.

## Stack your Damage

Damage from several Powers combines within a turn. The rulebook's example: three Powers that each deal 1 Damage can destroy a City. Damage on Invaders that survive heals when Time Passes, so stacking only works if it lands in the same turn.

That is why the rulebook tells you to spread your Presence across the Island: Spirits whose Ranges overlap can combine on one land.

Order matters too. The table resolves Fast Powers in any order it chooses, as long as no Power interrupts another partway through. The rulebook shows one Spirit Gathering an Explorer into a land, then a second Spirit Gathering Dahan in, and that Spirit's Power then destroys the Explorer with the Dahan's help. Neither Power works alone.

## Use the Dahan as shared allies

The Dahan belong to nobody, so they belong to everyone. Move them into lands with a few Invaders, where Fear effects and their own fight-back help most. Move them out of lands where a big Ravage would wipe them out. One Spirit Pushing Dahan into place for another Spirit's Ravage defense is teamwork in its purest form.

## Keep every player playing

Each Spirit panel is a big puzzle, which limits how much one player can run the whole table. Our article on the [quarterback problem](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/) uses Spirit Island as an example of exactly that. Still, a confident veteran can take over. A few habits help:

- Each player decides their own cards. Others suggest; they do not dictate.
- Agree on pace. The rulebook says any player may put a time limit on the Spirit Phase, and that very fast and very careful players at one table can frustrate each other.
- Do not rewind. If you discover a rule was played wrong, the rulebook says not to sweat it: note the right rule and keep going.

Our guide to [co-ops without a quarterback problem](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/) has more habits for open-information games.

## Playing solo

In a solo game, one Island board is the whole Island. You may target yourself with Powers that target another Spirit, though you do not get any extra benefit those Powers give for helping someone else. The rulebook notes that luck matters more solo, with no teammates to cover your Spirit's weak spots. Our guide to the [best solo board games](/articles/best-solo-board-games/) calls it excellent at one player.

## Beyond your table

Spirit Island is cooperative, so there is no tournament ladder in the usual sense. It is not among the current events in the [World Boardgaming Championships event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/index.html). The challenge lives in the adversary levels and the rulebook's scoring, covered in the [adversaries lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-adversaries-and-scenarios/) and the [practice plan](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-practice-plan/).

Big events do exist. At Gen Con 2024, Greater Than Games ran three Spirit Island Mega events, where many tables each played their own Island against one shared Invader board. The [publisher's report](https://shop.greaterthangames.com/blogs/news/spirit-island-at-gen-con-more) says all three ended in victory.
