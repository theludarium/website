Spirit Island has a reputation as a hard game to teach. Part of that is fair. Every player has a panel full of icons, the Invaders run on their own schedule, and there are three ways to lose. But most teaches go wrong for a simpler reason: the host explains the Spirits first, and nobody yet knows what the Spirits are fighting.

Flip the order. Teach the threat, show it move once, then hand each player the tools to stop it. Print the [teach sheet](/academy/spirit-island/teach-sheet/) and put a copy where everyone can see it.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up the whole game first, following [how to play](/academy/spirit-island/how-to-play-spirit-island/), except for the Invaders' first Explore. Then make the first game easier on purpose. The rulebook's advice for groups where everyone is new:

- Use the printed Blight space on the Invader board, not a Blight Card.
- No adversary and no scenario.
- Each player takes a low-complexity Spirit and its Power Progression card.

Choose Spirits for the people, not at random. The rulebook names four low-complexity Spirits and calls Shadows Flicker Like Flame the trickiest of them. Give Vital Strength of the Earth or River Surges in Sunlight to the most cautious player, Lightning's Swift Strike to the one who likes big plays, and Shadows to a confident gamer. The [starter Spirits lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-starter-spirits/) has more on each.

## Minute 1: the story and the goal

Say: "We are the spirits of this island. Invaders have landed. Together, with the Dahan who live here, we grow stronger and drive them off. We all win or all lose."

Then the three ways to lose, pointing at each: "Too much Blight leaves this card. Any one of us loses all our Presence. Or the Invader Deck runs out." Three sentences, and the table knows what failure looks like.

## Minutes 2 to 6: the Invaders

Point at the Invader board. This is the part to slow down for.

1. "These three spaces are what the Invaders do every turn: Ravage, Build, Explore, in that order."
2. "Each card names a land type. They only act in lands of that type."
3. Show the pieces. "Explorers, Towns, Cities. They deal 1, 2, and 3 Damage when they Ravage."
4. "After they act, every card slides one space left. What Explores now Builds next turn and Ravages the turn after."

Save the last step of setup for this moment: reveal the first Invader card and Explore with the table watching, then put the card on Build. Now walk through turn one's Invader Phase with your finger, without moving pieces. Nothing Ravages yet, because the Ravage space is empty. The lands on the Build card will Build. A new card will Explore. Then ask: "Which lands Ravage on turn two?" When someone points to the right ones, the hardest idea in the game has landed.

## Minutes 7 to 9: Blight and Fear

Teach the two Blight rules the rulebook warns are easy to misplay, word for word if you can:

- "A Ravage adds only 1 Blight to a land, however much Damage it does."
- "If that land already had Blight, 1 more cascades into one adjacent land, not all of them."

Then add: "Blight in a land destroys one of each Spirit's Presence there. So keep Presence out of lands about to Ravage."

Fear takes thirty seconds: "Destroying Towns and Cities, and some of our Powers, scare the Invaders. Enough Fear earns a Fear Card. More Fear Cards make winning easier." Point at the three victory conditions on the board and stop there. The [Fear lesson](/academy/spirit-island/spirit-island-fear-and-terror-levels/) can wait for game two.

## Minutes 10 to 15: your Spirit

Now each player turns to their own panel, and you teach the Spirit Phase once, for everyone:

1. "Grow: pick one Growth option on your panel and do all of it."
2. "Gain Energy: the highest number showing on your top track."
3. "Play cards: up to the highest number showing on your bottom track. Pay for them all now, even the Slow ones."

Then Fast and Slow: "Fast cards happen before the Invaders. Slow cards happen after." Point back at the Invader board. "So Fast cards stop this turn's Build. Slow cards hit next turn's."

Do not explain Innate Powers, Elements, or Major Powers in detail. Say "your panel's Innate Powers are free if you have the right Elements, and we'll point them out when you have them."

## Turn one, out loud

Play the first turn together, slowly. Each player says what they are Growing and which cards they will play, and the table helps with targets. Let each player run the Invader actions on their own starting board, a habit the rulebook suggests for new players. It keeps everyone involved and stops anyone from Exploring twice.

After turn one, step back. Answer questions as they come, and resist the urge to plan everyone's turn for them.

## Keep the night fun

Two rulebook habits help a first game:

- **Do not rewind.** If you find a rule was played wrong, note the right rule and keep going.
- **If it is too hard,** give every Spirit a bonus Growth at the end of setup, skip the Invaders' first Explore, or both.

A first game will run long. Plan for it, and our guides to [board game weight](/articles/board-game-weight-explained/) and [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) can help you set the evening's expectations.
