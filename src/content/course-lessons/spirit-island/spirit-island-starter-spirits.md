The base game has eight Spirits. The rulebook singles out four as low complexity and recommends them for your first game or two: Lightning's Swift Strike, Vital Strength of the Earth, River Surges in Sunlight, and Shadows Flicker Like Flame. The box includes a Power Progression card for each of them, and the rulebook calls Shadows the trickiest of the four.

This lesson looks at what each one is built to do, so your table can pick a team instead of four strangers.

## How to read a Spirit panel

Before you choose, flip the panel. The back holds the Spirit's Setup and a Play Style and Complexity section: a short summary of its strengths, weaknesses, and Powers. It is the fastest way to learn a Spirit.

The front holds four things that define how it plays:

- **Growth options:** how it spreads, gains Powers, and Reclaims.
- **Presence Tracks:** how fast its Energy and Card Plays rise.
- **Innate Powers:** free Powers triggered by Elements.
- **Special Rules:** anything that works differently for this Spirit.

Read the Special Rules twice. They are where each Spirit breaks the normal rules, and they shape every turn.

## Lightning's Swift Strike

Lightning is speed and destruction. Its Special Rule lets it use some Slow Powers as if they were Fast, which means hitting the Invaders before they Ravage instead of after. Its Innate Power destroys Towns and Cities when it has enough Fire and Air.

The rulebook names Lightning as a Spirit good at wrecking Towns. Use that. Aim it at the buildings that are about to Ravage, and let a teammate handle the scattered Explorers. Watch your Energy: playing many cards is only useful if you can pay for them.

## River Surges in Sunlight

River is a Spirit of Wetlands and movement. Its Special Rule treats its Presence in Wetlands as Sacred Sites, so a single Presence in a Wetland can use Powers that need a Sacred Site. Its Innate Power, fueled by Sun and Water, Pushes Invaders out of a land and, at higher thresholds, Damages them too.

Pushing is a quiet superpower. Push the lone Explorer out of a land before it Builds, and that land gets no Town. Move Dahan into a land that is about to Ravage, and they fight back, as in the rulebook's own Gather and Push example. River rewards players who like to think about where pieces should be, not just which ones to destroy.

## Vital Strength of the Earth

Vital Strength is the Island's shield. Its Special Rule gives Defend 3 in every land where it has a Sacred Site, so its strongholds shrug off Ravages that would Blight other lands. Its Innate Power lets any Spirit, itself included, Repeat one cheap Power Card, and more Elements raise the cost limit.

The rulebook names it as a Spirit that can prevent Ravages, and for that reason calls Sweden easier for it. Build Sacred Sites in the lands the Ravage card is heading for, and keep Dahan alive there: Defend protects them too, and surviving Dahan fight back. A team with Vital Strength can afford to let its damage dealers grow.

## Shadows Flicker Like Flame

Shadows is fear and trickery. Its Special Rule lets it pay Energy to reach any land with Dahan, however far away, so it can show up where nobody expects. Its Innate Power, fueled by Moon and Fire, picks off Explorers and generates Fear.

The rulebook calls Shadows the trickiest starter and notes that it relies on moving and destroying Explorers to stop Invader Builds. That makes it great at keeping lands clean early, and the rulebook marks England, which builds relentlessly, as harder for it. Shadows also feeds the Fear race from lesson 3, so the whole team reaches higher Terror Levels sooner.

## Pairing them

A team works best when its Spirits cover different jobs. Three jobs matter most early: stopping Explorers before they Build, stopping Ravages before they Blight, and destroying Towns and Cities for Fear.

- **Vital Strength with Lightning:** one holds the line while the other breaks buildings.
- **Shadows with Lightning:** Shadows keeps new lands clean while Lightning tears down the old ones, and both feed Fear.
- **River with Vital Strength:** River moves Invaders and Dahan into place, and Vital Strength makes those lands safe.

With four players, the four starters make a natural first team.

## Leaving the Power Progression behind

The Power Progression is for introductory games. Once your table knows the turn, switch to the normal method: draw 4 from the Minor or Major deck and keep 1. Your Spirit will grow differently every game, which is where the replay value lives.

## Beyond the starters

The other four Spirits in the box are not on the rulebook's low-complexity list, so save them for later games. One of them, A Spread of Rampant Green, is named in the rulebook alongside Vital Strength as a Spirit that can prevent Ravages.

If you want more beginner-friendly Spirits, [Horizons of Spirit Island](https://shop.greaterthangames.com/products/horizons-of-spirit-island) is a standalone game for 1 to 3 players, ages 12 and up. The publisher describes its five Spirits as easy to learn, and the box includes Power Progression cards and works with the rest of the Spirit Island line.
