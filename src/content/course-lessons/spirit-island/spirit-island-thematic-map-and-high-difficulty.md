Welcome to Level 5, the top of the ladder. Spirit Island has no tournament circuit, so mastery here means something else: beating the hardest games your box can build, and building a table that can keep doing it. This lesson is about the hard games.

## The difficulty chart, extended

The core rulebook gives every adversary level and scenario a Difficulty number, and adds two tools for going further:

- **Add them up.** Play an adversary and a scenario together, and their Difficulties add for a very rough estimate of the combined challenge.
- **The thematic map adds 3.**

The rulebook is careful to call this a rough guide. Some Spirits are better or worse against certain adversaries and scenarios, and some combinations work together more than others. But it gives you a dial with no fixed top.

## The thematic map

The back of each Island board shows the thematic map, the Island as its story imagines it. The rulebook describes the differences:

- **Terrains cluster** the way real geography would place them, with wet lands on the windward side of the mountains.
- **Some boards have more lands** and more starting Invaders.
- **Terrains are harder to spot** in the more realistic art.

The thematic boards have fixed places. One player uses Northeast. Two use West and East, touching on the side opposite the Ocean. Three add Northeast above East. Four use West, East, Northwest, and Northeast. Some lands spill slightly onto other boards; a land belongs to the board that shows its number and setup icons, and loose fragments are not in play. Ignore setup icons for Branch & Claw tokens if you only have the base game.

**Why clustering is harder:** on the balanced boards, each terrain is spread out, so one Invader card hits lands far apart. On the thematic map, the same card can hit a block of neighboring lands. Explorers then sit next to Towns, Builds feed Explores, and one Ravage can set up a cascade into the land next door.

**How to play it:** read each board's terrain clusters before turn one. Put Presence where it can reach a whole cluster, and plan to break up the biggest one before its Stage III card arrives.

## Stacking an adversary and a scenario

You can play an adversary, a scenario, or both. When their rules disagree, the scenario wins. The four core scenarios carry their own Difficulty: Blitz and Guard the Isle's Heart are 0, Rituals of Terror is 3, and Dahan Insurrection is 4.

A Difficulty 0 scenario is a way to change the game without making it harder. Adding one to an adversary you have already beaten gives a fresh puzzle at the same rough Difficulty, before you climb again.

## How the numbers stack

Some examples, using the core rulebook's chart:

| Game | Difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| Sweden level 6 | 8 |
| Sweden level 6 on the thematic map | 11 |
| Brandenburg-Prussia level 6 | 10 |
| Brandenburg-Prussia level 6, Dahan Insurrection | 14 |
| England level 6, Rituals of Terror, thematic map | 16 |

Treat the last rows as stretch goals. The rulebook suggests moving up one point at a time, or two if the Blight Card never flips.

## Scoring a hard game

The rulebook's optional scoring rewards hard wins and fast ones:

- **Victory:** 5 times the Difficulty, plus 10, plus 2 for each Invader card left in the deck.
- **Defeat:** 2 times the Difficulty, plus 1 for each Invader card out of the deck.
- **Either way:** plus 1 for every X living Dahan, and minus 1 for every X Blight on the Island, where X is the number of players.

So a win at Difficulty 10 with 2 cards left scores 5 times 10, plus 10, plus 4: 64 before Dahan and Blight. A loss at the same Difficulty after 10 cards are out scores 20 plus 10: 30. Scores let you compare games across different setups, which is the point of keeping them.

## Choosing the next challenge

At this level, choose challenges that teach something, not just ones with bigger numbers:

- **A Spirit against the adversary it is weakest against.** The rulebook's notes give you pairs: Shadows Flicker Like Flame against England, or a slow-developing Spirit against Brandenburg-Prussia.
- **A new map.** The same adversary on the thematic map plays very differently.
- **A different player count.** Solo and four-player games stress different skills.
