Sushi Go! is a gift to anyone who hosts. The core rule fits in one breath, everyone plays at once so no one waits, and a whole game is done before a heavier game would finish its setup. The risk is a host who reads every scoring rule before anyone has touched a card. Teach the move, deal, and let the cards do the rest.

Here is a script in the order you would say it. Print the [Sushi Go! teach sheet](/academy/sushi-go/teach-sheet/) and put it in the middle of the table. Our general method for [teaching any game in five minutes](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) fits this game well.

## Before anyone sits down

Shuffle the deck well and count out the hands: 10 cards each for 2 players, 9 for 3, 8 for 4, 7 for 5. Have paper and a pencil ready, since the box has no scorepad. Pick the scorekeeper now, ideally someone who is not also teaching.

## Minute 1: the picture

Say: "This is a sushi conveyor belt. Your hand is the belt in front of you. Every turn, you take one dish and pass the rest to your left. After three rounds, the most points wins."

That one image answers most questions before they come up. Plates go by. You grab one. The belt moves on.

## Minute 2: the move

Hold up your hand and walk through it:

1. "Pick one card and put it face down."
2. "When everyone's ready, we all flip at the same time."
3. "Pass the rest of your hand to your left."

Then say the line that makes the game click: "The hand you get next is someone else's leftovers. Whatever you pass, your neighbor gets to choose from."

## Minutes 3 and 4: how things score

Do not recite the list. Point at the scoring printed on the cards and group them:

- **"Some cards need a set."** Tempura scores 5 for a pair. Sashimi scores 10 for three. One alone is worth nothing.
- **"Some cards grow."** Dumplings score more the more you have, up to 15 for five.
- **"Some cards are a race."** Maki rolls: most roll icons this round scores 6, second scores 3.
- **"Some cards just score."** Nigiri: squid 3, salmon 2, egg 1.

## Minute 5: dessert

"Pudding is different. You keep it all game. At the very end, the most pudding scores 6 and the fewest loses 6." In a two-player game, leave off the second half: no one loses points for pudding with two.

Then stop and deal. Wasabi and chopsticks wait until someone holds one.

## Teach the rest live

Explain each of these the first time it comes up. All come from the official rules.

- **Someone looks at a wasabi.** "Take it, and the next nigiri you take goes on top of it, worth triple. A squid on wasabi is 9 points."
- **Someone looks at chopsticks.** "They do nothing now. On a later turn, say 'Sushi Go!' and take two cards. Then the chopsticks go back into your hand and get passed on."
- **The last card.** "When one card is passed to you, just keep it. That ends the round."
- **Scoring round one.** Score together, one card type at a time, starting with maki. New players learn the scoring best by watching their own cards turn into points.
- **Between rounds.** "Keep your pudding out. Everything else goes to the discard pile."

## Help them score round one

The first scoring is where new players get lost. Run it the same way every time:

1. **Maki first,** because it compares everyone. Count icons, not cards.
2. **Then sets:** tempura pairs, sashimi threes.
3. **Then dumplings** on the 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 ladder.
4. **Then nigiri,** tripled on wasabi.
5. **Say the total out loud** for the scorekeeper.

Once the table has seen one round scored, rounds two and three score themselves.

## Families, kids, and big groups

Sushi Go! is listed for ages 8 and up, and it suits mixed ages because everyone moves at once and nobody can attack anyone. A few tips:

- **Seat a new player to the left of a patient one.** The hands they receive will have been picked over by someone who leaves good options.
- **Let kids keep their cards spread out.** Grouping matching cards is half of scoring.
- **Praise a good pass, not just a good pick.** The game is about the table, not one hand.

## The one tip worth sharing

New players ask what to take first. One answer helps without taking over: "Look at what you already have. A card is worth the most when it finishes something." A second tempura is worth 5 points right now. A first tempura is worth nothing unless another comes along. That idea is the heart of the next lessons.
