Duel is the easiest kind of teach: one learner, one table, and a game that rewards playing over listening. The trap is the icons. A host picks up a card, explains every symbol on it, then does the same for twelve Wonders, and the learner has heard ten minutes of rules before taking a card.

The fix is to teach the turn, name the three ways to win, and start. Below is a script in the order you would say it. Print the [7 Wonders Duel teach sheet](/academy/7-wonders-duel/teach-sheet/) and leave it beside the board. The box also has a helpsheet that explains every symbol, so you do not have to.

## Before your opponent sits down

Set up everything first, using the [how to play lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/how-to-play-7-wonders-duel/):

- Board to one side, Conflict pawn in the middle, Military tokens out, 5 Progress tokens face up.
- 7 coins each.
- 3 cards from each Age returned to the box, and 3 guilds shuffled into Age III.
- The Age I layout dealt and ready.

For a first game, skip the draft and hand out the rulebook's fixed Wonders. One player gets The Pyramids, The Great Lighthouse, The Temple of Artemis, and The Statue of Zeus. The other gets Circus Maximus, Piraeus, The Appian Way, and The Colossus. Put the helpsheet between you.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "We each build an ancient city over three rounds called Ages. There are three ways to win. Push this pawn into my capital with shields. Collect six different science symbols. Or have more points after the third Age."

Point at the pawn, then at a green card. That is all the goal needs.

## Minute 2: the turn

Put your hand on the bottom row.

1. "On your turn, take any card that nothing else is covering. Right now that is this bottom row."
2. "You can do one of three things with it. Build it in your city. Sell it for coins. Or tuck it under one of your Wonders to build that Wonder."
3. "If taking a card uncovers a face-down one, flip it. Careful: whatever you uncover, I can take next."

That third line is the whole game in one sentence. Say it slowly.

## Minute 3: paying

Hold up a card with a resource cost.

- "Brown and grey cards make resources. You never use them up."
- "Missing one? Buy it from the bank for 2 coins. It costs 1 more for each of that resource my brown and grey cards make. So when you build resources, you also make them more expensive for me."
- "Some cards cost coins. Some are free."

## Minute 4: shields, science, and Wonders

- "Red cards have shields. Each shield moves the pawn one space toward my capital. When it crosses into one of these zones, I lose coins."
- "Green cards have science symbols. Match two of the same symbol and you take one of these Progress tokens. Six different symbols wins."
- "Your Wonders are big effects you build with a card. Only seven of the eight can be built, so the last one gets left out."

## Minute 5: the end of an Age

"When all twenty cards are gone, we lay out the next Age. Whoever the pawn is leaning toward picks who starts it." Then start playing.

## What to leave out of the first teach

- **Every Progress token.** Read the tokens aloud the first time someone makes a science pair.
- **Military token details.** Explain them the first time the pawn crosses a line.
- **Chains.** They begin in Age II. Point one out when it comes up.
- **Guilds.** Explain them when Age III is laid out.
- **Civilian scoring.** Many first games end early. If yours reaches the end of Age III, walk the scorebook together.
- **The Wonder draft.** Save it for the second or third game.
- **Strategy.** One tip only: "Before you take a card, look at what it uncovers."

## Coaching during the first game

Help with costs and icons, not choices. "That card costs you 3, because I make clay" is helpful. "Take the red card" is playing for them.

There is one thing worth saying out loud each turn for the first few games: the shield gap and how many different science symbols each of you has. New players lose to sudden wins they did not see coming, and a game that ends with "wait, that is it?" is less fun to learn from than one that ends with a fight. Calling the numbers teaches the game's two clocks without a lecture.

## After the first game

Play again right away while the rules are fresh, and switch Wonder sets. The second game is where your opponent starts to plan, and the third is a good time to add the Wonder draft.
