New Duel players rarely get the big ideas wrong. They take cards, build cities, and push the pawn. The slips happen in the small rules, the ones that come from playing big 7 Wonders first or from teaching from memory. Each one below is checked against the [7 Wonders Duel rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-rprod/storage/downloads/games/7wonders-duel/en/7du-rules-us-15990558193s5I6.pdf) and the publisher's [online FAQ](https://www.rprod.com/en/7-wonders-duel/faq).

## Money and trading

**Mistake: paying your opponent for resources.** In Duel, every purchase goes to the bank. Your opponent's city sets the price, but they never see the coins. (The Economy Progress token is the one exception: with it, the coins your opponent spends on trading come to you.)

**Mistake: a flat price of 2.** Each missing resource costs 2 coins plus 1 for every matching symbol on your opponent's brown and grey cards. If they show two stone symbols, stone costs you 4 coins each, and three stone costs you 12.

**Mistake: counting yellow cards and Wonders in the price.** Only brown and grey cards raise prices. Resources from your opponent's yellow cards, The Great Lighthouse, or Piraeus do not.

**Mistake: using a discount the turn you build it.** Yellow cards like the Stone Reserve or the Customs House fix a resource's price at 1 coin, starting on the following turn.

**Mistake: selling for 3.** Selling a card pays 2 coins plus 1 per yellow card in your city. The big game's flat 3 coins does not apply.

**Mistake: spending resources.** Resources are never used up. Each symbol on your brown and grey cards pays once per turn, every turn, for the whole game.

## The layout

**Mistake: taking a card that is partly covered.** A card is accessible only when no other card covers any part of it. Even a corner counts.

**Mistake: forgetting to flip.** When your pick uncovers a face-down card, turn it face up before your turn ends. If a Wonder gives you a second turn, flip first, then take the second turn.

**Mistake: hiding the discard pile.** Discarded cards go face down, but both players may look through the pile at any time. That matters for The Mausoleum, which builds a card from it.

## Wonders

**Mistake: paying the card's cost.** When you build a Wonder, you pay the Wonder's cost. The card you tuck under it is only a marker, and its own cost and effect are ignored.

**Mistake: building the eighth Wonder.** Only seven Wonders can be built in a game. The moment the seventh is built, the last unbuilt Wonder returns to the box, whoever owns it.

**Mistake: a play-again on the last card.** If you build a play-again Wonder with the last card of an Age, the extra turn is lost. The layout is empty, and the rulebook does not carry it over.

**Mistake: thinking The Mausoleum reaches the setup cards.** It takes the cards discarded during play. The 3 cards per Age returned to the box at setup are not part of the discard.

## Military and science

**Mistake: comparing shields at the end of each Age.** That is the big game. In Duel, the pawn moves the moment shields are built, one space per shield, and it moves back and forth all game.

**Mistake: Military tokens every time.** Each Military token works once. The player who enters its zone applies it, the opponent loses the coins (or all their coins, if they have fewer), and the token leaves the game.

**Mistake: missing a pair.** Each pair of identical science symbols earns a Progress token at once. The Law token counts as a science symbol toward six different ones. In the base game no green card shares its symbol, so it never makes a pair.

**Mistake: forgetting the sudden wins.** Check the pawn and both science counts after every card. Supremacy ends the game on the spot, mid-Age included.

## The end of an Age and the game

**Mistake: the stronger player choosing who starts.** The player with the weaker military chooses who starts the next Age: the one the pawn is leaning toward. If the pawn is on the middle space, the player who took the last card of the Age chooses.

**Mistake: big-game science scoring.** Duel does not square anything. At the end, green cards score the points printed on them.

**Mistake: ties go to coins.** A Duel tie goes to the player with more points on blue cards. If that ties too, you share the win.

## The one habit behind all of this

Most of these slips come from the big game. If both of you know 7 Wonders, say it at the start: "No neighbors, no passing, the bank sets prices from my brown and grey cards, and selling is 2 plus yellows." That one sentence prevents half this list.

The other half is the layout. Before each pick, ask what it uncovers. The [Wonders lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-wonders/) is next, and the [layout control lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-layout-control/) in Level 3 turns that question into a count.
