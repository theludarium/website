The [Wonders lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-wonders/) gave you a plan before the first card. This lesson is about paying for it. Duel's economy runs on one formula from the [rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-rprod/storage/downloads/games/7wonders-duel/en/7du-rules-us-15990558193s5I6.pdf): a missing resource costs 2 coins plus 1 for each matching symbol on your opponent's brown and grey cards. Almost every economic decision in the game comes back to that line.

## Resources do two jobs

Every brown or grey card you build does two things at once. It gives you that resource every turn for the rest of the game. And it raises your opponent's price for that resource at the bank by 1 coin per symbol.

The rulebook's own example shows how fast that adds up. A player with a card that makes 2 stone sets stone at 4 coins each for their opponent. If that opponent needs 3 stone for a card and makes none, it costs them 12 coins. One brown card turned an Age II card into a turn of saving up.

So when you weigh a brown or grey card, ask two questions: do I need this resource, and does my opponent? A resource they need and you also use is the best kind. A resource only they need is still worth a look as a price raise, though it is weaker than a card that helps you directly.

## Build early, because Age III has none

The Age III deck has no brown or grey cards. Whatever production you have at the end of Age II is what you finish with, and Age III cards are the most expensive in the game. A player who skipped resources early pays the bank for nearly everything in the last Age, at prices their opponent's city has been raising all game.

That makes Age I and Age II resources more valuable than their small effect suggests. A common shape for a solid opening: a few brown cards that cover what your Wonders need, and at least one grey card, since glass and papyrus appear on many blue, green, and Wonder costs.

## Yellow cards that fix prices

Some yellow cards set the price of one or two resources at 1 coin for you, starting the turn after you build them. The Stone Reserve, Clay Reserve, and Wood Reserve each fix one raw material; the Customs House fixes glass and papyrus. Others, like the Forum and the Caravansery, produce one resource of your choice each turn.

A fixed price pays back quickly when your opponent has made that resource expensive. If stone costs you 4 and you expect to buy 3 more stone over the game, a stone fix saves 9 coins. If stone costs you 2 and you rarely need it, the card is weak.

Yellow cards also raise your sale price. Each one adds 1 coin to every card you sell, which matters more than it sounds in a game where selling is a real move.

## Coins are tempo

You start with 7 coins. Coins become points at 1 per 3, so as a score they are small. Their real value is what they let you build on the turn you need to.

- **Keep a buffer.** Military tokens take 2 or 5 coins when the pawn enters their zones. Being drained to zero right before a card you must build to stop a sudden win can lose the game.
- **Selling is not a wasted turn.** A sale gives 2 coins plus your yellow cards. More important, it removes a card your opponent wanted, without opening anything new if you pick the right one.
- **Big coin effects arrive in lumps.** The Temple of Artemis, The Hanging Gardens, Agriculture, and Urbanism all pay at once. Plan the expensive card you will buy with them.

## Tempo in Age I and Age II

Age I starts with 6 accessible cards along the bottom row. Early picks there are fairly safe: each card you take opens at most a little of the row above, which is face down. You are gambling on hidden cards for both players.

Age II is the Age I shape upside down and starts with only 2 accessible cards. Choices are narrow, and the player who must open the next row is often the one handing over good cards. A play-again Wonder breaks that rhythm: two cards in a row change who opens what for the rest of the Age. The [layout control lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-layout-control/) in Level 3 turns this into a count.

## A quick money check each turn

Before each pick, run three numbers:

1. **What does this card cost me now,** at today's bank prices?
2. **What does taking it do to my opponent's prices,** if it is brown or grey?
3. **How many coins will I have left,** and is a Military token close?

None of this replaces the question from Level 1, what this card uncovers. It sits beside it. For the bigger picture across both games in the family, our [7 Wonders strategy guide](/articles/7-wonders-strategy/) covers economy first, then points.
