Level 3 was about the games that end early. This lesson is about the ones that do not. When neither player reaches a capital or six different symbols, the game ends after Age III and goes to the scorebook. Tournament players treat that count as a live number all through the last Age, and so should you.

Rules here come from the [7 Wonders Duel rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-rprod/storage/downloads/games/7wonders-duel/en/7du-rules-us-15990558193s5I6.pdf).

## The scorebook, row by row

A civilian victory adds:

1. **Military.** The pawn's zone: 0 in the middle, then 2, 5, or 10 to the player it leans toward. Only one player scores here.
2. **Blue cards.** Their printed points.
3. **Green cards.** Their printed points. Nothing is squared.
4. **Yellow cards.** Any printed points.
5. **Purple cards.** The guilds, below.
6. **Wonders.** The points printed on each one you built.
7. **Progress tokens.** Agriculture (4), Philosophy (7), and Mathematics (3 per token you hold, itself included).
8. **Treasury.** 1 point per full set of 3 coins.

Most points wins. A tie goes to the player with more points on blue cards, then a shared win.

## Military is points, too

The pawn scores even when it never reaches a capital. Moving it out of the middle into the first zone on either side is worth 2; the next zones are worth 5 and 10. That makes the board's dotted lines into point thresholds.

One shield can be worth a lot late in Age III. If the pawn sits one space from entering the 5 zone toward your opponent, a single shield swings their military row from 2 to 5 for you. Going the other way, one shield of theirs can drop you a zone. Count the space to the next line, not just the shield gap.

## The guilds

Three of the seven guilds are shuffled into Age III, so every one drawn will be in the layout. Most give coins when built and points at the end, and each one counts from **the city with the most** of something. That city can be yours or your opponent's.

| Guild | Counts |
| --- | --- |
| Builders Guild | 2 points per Wonder in the city with the most Wonders |
| Moneylenders Guild | 1 point per 3 coins in the richest city |
| Scientists Guild | Green cards in the city with the most |
| Merchants Guild | Yellow cards in the city with the most |
| Magistrates Guild | Blue cards in the city with the most |
| Tacticians Guild | Red cards in the city with the most |
| Shipowners Guild | Brown and grey cards, together, in the city with the most |

The last five pay 1 coin per card when built and 1 point per card at the end. The rulebook adds that you can choose a different city for the points than you chose for the coins, and that the Shipowners Guild must count brown and grey from one city.

The card is printed as the Merchants Guild. The rulebook's description page calls it the Traders Guild; it is the same card.

Because a guild can score off your opponent, read each one from both sides. A Tacticians Guild in a game where your opponent has six red cards is worth 6 to whoever builds it. If you cannot use it, it may still be worth taking so they cannot.

## The seventh Wonder race

Eight Wonders start the game and only seven can be built. In Age III, if both players have unbuilt Wonders, the race is on: the seventh one built sends the last one to the box.

Count what the race is worth. If your last Wonder is The Pyramids (9 points) and theirs is The Mausoleum (2 points plus a free card), building first protects 9 points and wipes out their option. If you cannot afford yours yet, look at what they need to afford theirs. Any card can be tucked under a Wonder, so you cannot starve them of cards, but a brown or grey card that raises their price for a key resource, or a Military token that drains their coins, can buy you the turn you need.

## The tiebreak changes blue cards

Ties go to blue-card points. In a close game that makes every blue card worth slightly more than its printed number. Between a blue card and another card worth the same, take the blue.

Late in Age III, when the totals are within a few points, count the blue rows separately for both players. A game you are tied in on points may already be won or lost on blue.

## Counting Age III as you go

You do not need to total everything every turn. Keep two running numbers:

- **The settled score:** cards and Wonders already built, Progress tokens, and the pawn's current zone.
- **What is left in reach:** the accessible cards worth the most to each player, and the guilds already face up.

Then, before each pick, ask what the card is worth to you and to your opponent, and what it opens. Selling a card that would score 6 for them is often better than building one that scores 3 for you.

## Do not forget the sudden wins

All of this sits on top of Level 3. A civilian lead means nothing if your opponent reaches their sixth symbol. Keep the [supremacy counts](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-supremacy/) running even while you count points.
