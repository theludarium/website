You can play and teach Duel, draft Wonders with a plan, run an economy, choose Progress tokens, control the layout, count both sudden wins, and count a civilian finish. The last step is practice that looks like the event. This lesson gives you six weeks.

The plan assumes three to four hours a week. Scale it, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-tournaments/) covers the main options:

- **The World Boardgaming Championships** Duel event, marked beginning players welcome. Check the current [WBC site](https://www.boardgamers.org/) for dates and the event preview.
- **A local tournament** at a game store or convention near you.
- **A bracket you run yourself.** Our guide on [how to run a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers the format.

Then read that event's rules, and practice under them.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play online, where games are fast. [Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwondersduel) is the easy option. Aim for five to ten games a week, alternating first and second seat, base game only unless your event allows expansions.

**2. Format.** At least once a week, play across a real table under the event's rules: the Wonder draft, a timer for the round, and, for elimination-style games, the balanced draft where the second player chooses who starts each set of four.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week. Rotate these:

- **Layout drill.** Lay out Age II from your own copy. Count the safe picks, then say who takes the last card if nobody plays again. The [layout lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-layout-control/) explains the count.
- **Supremacy drill.** At random points in an online game, pause and say the shield gap and both science counts before you look. The [supremacy lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-supremacy/) explains why.
- **Price drill.** Pick any Age II or Age III card in a game and say what it costs you right now at the bank, and what it would cost your opponent. The [strategy lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-strategy/) has the formula.
- **Draft drill.** Deal four Wonders from your box. Decide, as second player, whether you would start the draft or pass it, and which Wonder you would leave your opponent. Then deal four more and do it again.
- **Count drill.** In Age III, keep a running civilian score for both players, as in the [scoring lesson](/academy/7-wonders-duel/7-wonders-duel-scoring/), and check it against the final total.

**4. Review.** After every loss, write short answers to three questions:

1. Did the game end by military, science, or points?
2. Which pick opened the card that beat me?
3. When did the shield gap, the science count, or the points race first turn against me, and did I see it?

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: volume.** Play and review. Record each result by seat and by how the game ended. Do not change how you play yet; just collect the numbers.

**Weeks 3 and 4: format.** Add the event's rules to every game. Play two in-person games a week under a timer. Start the layout and price drills.

**Week 5: the hard parts.** Play only under event rules. Treat every game as an elimination game: as first player, a tie loses, so count blue-card points in Age III. Run the draft drill before each session.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Read your notes for the pattern you see most, and fix that one thing. Arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep a few numbers from week to week:

- **Win rate as first player and as second player,** tracked separately.
- **How your games end:** your wins and losses by military, science, and points.
- **Surprise losses:** sudden wins you did not see coming a turn early. This number should fall toward zero.

If the numbers move the right way, the plan is working, whatever the event result.

## Bring a copy

The WBC preview asks players to bring a copy of the game, and warns that players can be turned away if there are not enough. If you are buying, the base box is **[7 Wonders Duel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B014DMSTXK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** on Amazon.

## The person across the table

A tournament can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view: Duel is a game for two, and every game in this plan is time across a table from someone who loves it as much as you do. Learn their names. Talk through the close finishes afterward. Play the rematch.
