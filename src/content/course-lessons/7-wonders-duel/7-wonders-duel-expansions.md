7 Wonders Duel has two expansions, both by the same designers, both for exactly two players, and both playable together. That is a short shelf, which makes choosing easy. This lesson covers what each adds from the publisher's own rulebooks for [Pantheon](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-rprod/storage/downloads/games/7wonders-duel-pantheon/en/7dpa-rules-en-1624535175WPszB.pdf) and [Agora](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-rprod/storage/downloads/games/7wonders-duel-agora/en/7dag-rules-en-1624535505VDrdL.pdf), then how to choose.

## First: player count

Duel is a game for two, and both expansions keep it that way. There is no official solo mode or third seat. For 3 to 7 players, the publisher's answer is the big game, [7 Wonders](/academy/7-wonders/), which has its own course here. Duel does not mix with the big game's expansions.

**[7 Wonders Duel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B014DMSTXK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** is the base box you need for either expansion.

## Pantheon: Divinities and Grand Temples

**[7 Wonders Duel: Pantheon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01K9MNAOG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds 15 Divinity cards from five mythologies, each with one focus: Greek (civilian), Phoenician (commercial), Mesopotamian (scientific), Egyptian (Wonders), and Roman (military). It also adds a Pantheon board, two Wonders, three Progress tokens, and five Grand Temples.

How it plays:

- **Age I seeds the Pantheon.** Five Mythology tokens sit on face-down cards in the Age I layout. When your pick flips one of those cards, you take its token, draw two Divinities of that mythology, and place one face down in a Pantheon space of your choice.
- **Age II opens it.** The Divinities are revealed, a Gate card fills the last space, and three Offering tokens sit on face-down Age II cards as one-time discounts.
- **A new action.** From Age II on, instead of taking a card, you can pay to activate a Divinity and use its power at once. Each Pantheon space shows two costs, one per player, so the same god is cheaper for one of you than the other.
- **Grand Temples replace the guilds** in Age III. A Mythology token with a matching symbol lets you build one free. At the end, 1, 2, or 3 Grand Temples score 5, 12, or 21 points.

The powers are big: Aphrodite is 9 points, Tanit gives 12 coins, Mars is 2 shields, Ra steals an unbuilt Wonder. Pantheon makes the game more explosive and gives the civilian race a large new prize in Age III.

## Agora: Senators, Conspiracies, and political supremacy

**[7 Wonders Duel: Agora](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08PQ17FG1?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds a Senate board of six Chambers, 13 Senator cards (Politicians and Conspirators) shuffled into the Age decks, 16 Conspiracy cards, 16 Decree tokens, 24 influence cubes, two Wonders, two Progress tokens, and four Military tokens of its own.

How it plays:

- **Recruit Senators** from the layout like any card. Each costs coins equal to the Senators you already have, so the first is free.
- **Politicians** let you place or move influence cubes in the Senate. The number of actions grows with your blue cards: 1 for 0 to 1 blue cards, 2 for 2 to 3, and 3 for 4 or more.
- **Conspirators** place one cube, or draw 2 Conspiracy cards and keep 1. You prepare a Conspiracy by tucking a card from the layout under it, then trigger it at the start of a later turn, on top of your normal turn.
- **Chambers and Decrees.** Strictly more cubes in a Chamber than your opponent controls it and gives you its Decree. Each controlled Chamber scores 1 to 3 points at the end.
- **Political supremacy.** Control all six Chambers at once and you win immediately.

Agora adds a third front to a game that already has two. It suits players who like hidden plans and fights over a shared board.

## Playing both

Agora's extra cards make each Age bigger. Its rulebook shows the new layouts: 25 cards in Age I (rows of 3 to 7) and in Age II (rows of 7 to 3), and 23 in Age III.

The two expansions combine. The Agora rulebook gives the order for a combined turn: trigger a Conspiracy if you want, then either take a card from the layout or activate a Pantheon card. For the card layouts with both expansions, it points to the player aid card in the Agora box.

## Promo extras

The publisher's [Duel page](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/7-wonders-duel) also posts rules for promotional cards, such as a Statue of Liberty Wonder. They are small additions; the base game and the two boxed expansions are the core.

## How to choose

- **You love the base game and want more of it:** Pantheon. It keeps the same three wins and adds power and a stronger Age III.
- **You want more interaction and a new way to win:** Agora.
- **You mostly play casual games with a partner who is still learning:** neither yet. The Wonder draft is the first expansion, and it is already in the box.
- **You are preparing for a tournament:** stay with the base game. The WBC Duel event plays without expansions by default, as the next lesson covers.

Online, the [Board Game Arena Duel page](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=sevenwondersduel) includes both expansions, so you can try each before buying.
