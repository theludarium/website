Doubles is how most people first meet crokinole: four people, one board, two teams. It is also a serious tournament format. At the World Crokinole Championship, the doubles events run alongside singles, and in 2026 the Competitive Doubles title went to Ryan Buhr of Fort Wayne, Indiana, and Shawn Hagarty of Guelph, Ontario, according to the [championship's results](https://worldcrokinole.com/results.html).

## The doubles rules

From the [NCA Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf), rule 5, and the [World Crokinole Championship rules](https://worldcrokinole.com/thegame.html):

- **Seating.** Partners sit across from each other and play the same color. Around the board, the seats go team A, team B, team A, team B.
- **Discs.** Each player begins with 6 discs, so each team shoots 12 per round.
- **Turn order.** Play goes clockwise, so the teams alternate shots.
- **Who starts.** If your team is chosen to start, you and your partner decide which of you shoots first. In each later round of the game, the obligation to start moves one player clockwise. Over a four-round game, every player starts once.
- **Talking.** The NCA coaching rule says a player may not be coached except by other players at the table (rule 8a). The World Championship rules say the same thing more plainly: doubles players may talk to their partner during the game.

Everything else is the same as singles: the valid-shot rule, line calls, the Spinning Disc and Damage Stays rules, and scoring.

**At home:** the most common difference is the disc count. Some sets do not split neatly into four stacks of 6. Any even split works for a family game, as long as both teams shoot the same number.

## How four seats change the board

In singles, your opponent sees the board from one angle, directly across from you. In doubles, there are four angles, and your opponents sit on **both** sides of you.

That changes three things:

1. **Hiding is harder.** A disc hidden behind a peg from one seat is often open from the seat next to it. The NCA No Hiding Rule already keeps open-board shots near the middle, so most discs are within reach of someone.
2. **Hide from the next shooter.** The player who shoots right after you is always an opponent, sitting clockwise from you. That is the angle to hide from. The opponent after that will have to deal with whatever your partner leaves.
3. **Your partner is a second angle.** A disc you cannot reach may be an easy takeout for your partner. Sometimes the right shot is to leave it and set up something else.

## A simple team plan

Strong doubles teams agree on roles before the game, then adjust.

- **The stronger 20 shooter** takes the open boards when they fall to them. Neither partner can choose when that happens, but a team can play for it, by trading discs so the board clears on the right turn.
- **The steadier hitter** takes on the busy boards and the must-hit shots.
- **Both** count the score and the shots left, out loud, before the last few shots of each round.

None of this means one partner sits out. It means the team talks about which seat has the better angle, and plays its turns in that light.

## What to say between shots

Talking is allowed, so use it well:

- **Before the shot:** "Their 15 behind the peg is open from my side. Can you take the 10 instead and stay on the board?"
- **The count:** "We're up a 10. They have three shots, we have two. They have the hammer."
- **After the shot:** short and kind. A miss is a shared problem.

Keep it quick. Events run on a clock, and the World Championship rules say game time limits still apply to doubles teams who talk.

## The hammer in doubles

Turns alternate by team, so the team that does not start the round shoots last. Because the start moves one seat clockwise each round, the hammer passes back and forth between the teams, and each team holds it in two of the four rounds. Lesson 7's hammer plans work the same way with four players, but you now have two shooters to set up the final shot.

## Doubles for new players

Doubles is the gentlest way to bring someone new into crokinole. Pair them with the strongest player, and let the partner talk them through every shot. The rules allow it, and new players learn the game's shots faster when someone names them in the moment. Lesson 2 has more on seating a mixed table.
