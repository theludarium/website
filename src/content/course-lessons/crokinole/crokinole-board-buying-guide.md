Welcome to Level 4. Crokinole skill is partly a feel for speed, and speed depends on the board, the discs, and the powder. A shot that is perfect on one board flies off another. If you want your practice to carry over to a club or tournament, play on equipment built to the same standard. This lesson explains what that standard is and what to check before you buy. It names no prices, since they change often.

## Board size: tournament or traditional

Board makers sell two broad sizes:

- **Tournament size.** A playing surface about 26 inches across, with a ditch around it and a rail outside that. Boards sold as meeting National Crokinole Association standards list this size. [Tracey Boards](https://traceyboards.com/faq/), which calls itself the official board builder of the World Crokinole Championship, is one such maker.
- **Traditional or smaller boards.** Many older and octagonal boards have a smaller playing surface, often listed around 22 to 24 inches, with a smaller 20 hole and smaller discs.

Both play the same game. The difference shows when you move between them: distances, angles, and the speed you need all change. If tournaments interest you at all, buy tournament size.

## The 20 hole and discs must match

Disc size and 20 hole size go together. Disc sellers list two common pairings:

| Board | 20 hole | Disc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tournament size, about 26 inches | About 1 3/8 inches | About 1 1/4 inches |
| Smaller traditional boards | About 1 1/4 inches | About 1 1/8 inches |

A disc that is too big for the hole will never score 20. One that is much smaller drops in too easily. Measure your hole before you buy replacement discs.

Tournament discs are flat on both sides, and many are hard maple. Full sets are commonly sold as 26 discs: 12 per color plus a spare of each.

## What to check on a board

- **Flatness.** Lay a straightedge across the surface in a few directions. A warped board pulls every slow shot to one side.
- **Smooth lines.** Lines should be printed or inlaid flush with the surface, not raised. A raised line can bump a slow disc.
- **Pegs.** Eight pegs, firmly set, often with a rubber sleeve. Loose pegs give odd bounces.
- **The 20 hole.** Clean edges and a flat bottom, so a sunk disc lies flat. A disc must lie flat to score 20 (NCA rule 6c).
- **A deep enough ditch.** Discs knocked out should stay out, not bounce back in.

## Powder: less than you think

The NCA rules allow granular shuffleboard wax, placed in the ditch so players can rub their disc in it before shooting. Only tournament officials put it on the playing surface, and no other lubricant is allowed (rule 8b). At the World Crokinole Championship, the boards are waxed with paste wax, and powdered shuffleboard wax, CAPO fast speed, is set in the ditch, according to its [FAQ](https://worldcrokinole.com/faq.html).

How to use it at home, following that model:

1. **Put a small pinch in the ditch**, in one spot near your seat. Tracey Boards gives the same advice in its FAQ.
2. **Rub the bottom of your disc through it** before each shot.
3. **Do not dust the whole board.** Too much powder makes a board fast and unpredictable, and you cannot practice touch on it.
4. **Wipe the surface** now and then. The NCA rules allow players to agree to remove wax between rounds (rule 8c).

Skip old household remedies. Stick to products sold for shuffleboard or crokinole.

## Caring for a board

- **Store it flat or upright and steady,** indoors, away from heat vents, damp basements, and hot cars. Wood moves with heat and moisture.
- **Keep it covered** when it is not in use, so dust does not build up in the powder.
- **Refresh the surface wax** when discs start to feel slow, not on a schedule.

## Where this leads
