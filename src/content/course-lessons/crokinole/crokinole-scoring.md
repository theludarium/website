Welcome to Level 2. You can play a whole game without knowing the score until the end, and many families do. But the players who win close rounds count before every late shot, because the score tells them which shot to take. This lesson covers how a round is counted, the two common ways to turn rounds into a game, and how to count fast mid-round.

## Ring values

From the [NCA Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf), rule 6:

| Where the disc rests | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Completely in the center hole, lying flat | 20 |
| Inside the 15 line | 15 |
| In the 10 ring, outside the pegs | 10 |
| In the 5 ring, inside the outer line | 5 |
| Touching a line | The lower of the two regions |
| Touching or over the outer line | Out of play, 0 |

Two details settle most disputes:

- **The bottom edge decides a line call.** A disc touches a line if its bottom edge is over any part of the line, not by how it looks from above (rule 6e). Get your eye down to board level.
- **Count before you touch.** At the end of the round, count the board and the 20s before anyone moves a disc (rule 2a iii).

## Way one: points per round (NCA and tournaments)

Under the NCA rules and the [World Crokinole Championship rules](https://worldcrokinole.com/thegame.html):

- Each side totals its discs, including 20s.
- The higher total wins the round and takes **2 points**. A tie gives each side **1 point**. A loss is 0.
- A game is **four rounds**. The first shot alternates each round in singles, and in doubles it moves one seat clockwise.
- How many games make a match is set by the event. The World Championship rules say a match is normally 10 games, with a championship final played best two out of three.

So a game is out of 8 points. A 2 to 2 split of rounds is a 4 to 4 game.

**What it means for play:** the size of your win does not matter. Winning a round 85 to 80 earns the same 2 points as winning it 140 to 20. Once you are ahead late in a round, protecting the lead is worth more than padding it.

## Way two: the race to 100 (common at home)

Many families play differential scoring:

- Each side totals its discs.
- Only the higher side scores, and it scores the difference. A round of 65 to 40 gives 25 points to the winner and 0 to the other side.
- Play rounds until one side reaches 100, or whatever target you agree.

This is a house rule, not the NCA rule. It is a fine one, especially when players of different strengths share a board, because a strong round from a weaker player moves them a lot.

**What it means for play:** margin matters. A risky shot that could add 20 points to a lead is worth more here than under points per round, because the extra points carry over.

## Counting mid-round: cancel out

Adding up every disc is slow. Use pairs instead:

1. Match each of your discs with an opposing disc in the same region. A 10 cancels a 10.
2. Set aside matched 20s the same way.
3. Count only what is left over.

If you have a 15 and two 10s, and they have a 15 and one 5, the 15s cancel, and you are ahead by 10 plus 10 minus 5, which is 15.

Then add one more thing: **discs left to shoot.** If you are up 15 but your opponent has two shots to your one, your lead is not safe. Lesson 7 covers how the last shot of a round, the hammer, changes the math.

## Recording the score

At tournaments, players fill in their own scorecards in ink, record points and 20s each round, and swap cards with their opponent to check after each game, according to the World Championship rules. The count of 20s matters there: in the World Championship's preliminary rounds, a tie in points goes to the player with more 20s in the match.

At home, a notepad with two columns works. If you play the race to 100, write the running total, not just each round.

## Pick one, and say it

The most common scoring argument is not about lines. It is two players who each thought the game was scored a different way. Agree before the first shot. Our [crokinole rules](/articles/crokinole-rules/) article and the teach sheet both list the two methods side by side.
