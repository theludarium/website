Welcome to Level 1. Crokinole has few rules, which is why the edge cases cause so many arguments. A disc rolls over the line and back. A shot hits your own disc, which hits theirs. A 20 drops on a shot that missed. This lesson settles each one with the [NCA Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf), ratified May 6, 2026, with rule numbers so you can point at the page.

## 1. Touching a line

**Mistake: scoring a disc on a line at the higher value.** A disc touching a line scores the lower of the two regions (rule 6d). A disc touching the 15 line scores 10.

**Mistake: judging the line from above.** The NCA rule is about the bottom edge of the disc: is it over any part of the line? Not where the edge seems to be when you look straight down (rule 6e). Crouch to board level to call a close one.

**Mistake: playing a disc on the outer line.** A disc touching or crossing the outer line is out of play and goes into the ditch (rule 8e). Touching that line is not a 5. It is gone.

## 2. Touching the 15 line on an open board

**Mistake: requiring the disc to be fully inside.** When no opposing discs are in play, your shot needs a disc that ends "touching or within the 15 line" (rule 3a ii). Touching counts as reaching the middle, even though that same disc will score only 10. If your group requires fully inside, that is a house rule.

## 3. Hitting through your own disc

**Mistake: calling a combination hit illegal.** If your shooter hits one of your own discs, and that disc hits an opponent's, the shot is valid (rule 3a i). You do not need to touch the enemy disc with the shooter itself.

**Mistake: forgetting what comes off after a miss.** When a shot is not valid, the shooter and every disc it struck come off, and none of them score (rule 3b). If your shooter missed every enemy disc but bumped two of your own, all three go to the ditch.

## 4. The 20 on a bad shot

**Mistake: keeping a 20 from an invalid shot.** A 20 is a struck disc like any other. If the shot that sank it was not valid, the 20 comes off with the rest (rule 3b). The usual case: an opponent has one disc hiding in the 5 ring, you shoot for the open 20 instead of hitting it, and you sink it. It does not count.

**Mistake: counting a 20 that is tilted or half in.** It must be completely in the hole and lying flat (rule 6c). A disc leaning on the rim of the hole stays where it is and scores 15.

**Mistake: leaving 20s in the hole.** After each turn, sunk 20s come out and sit where everyone can see them (rule 2a ii). Leaving them in changes the board for the next shot.

## 5. Discs that leave and come back

**Mistake: putting every returning disc in the ditch.** If a disc touches or crosses the outer line, hits nothing but the playing surface, and comes back in on its own, it stays in play. This is the Spinning Disc Rule (rule 8g).

**Mistake: undoing what a returning disc did.** If a disc crosses the line, bounces off the rail or the discs in the ditch, and comes back onto the board, that disc is out. But anything it hit stays where it ended up, and any 20 it knocked in counts. This is the Damage Stays Rule (rule 8h). Do not reset the board.

## 6. What counts as a shot

**Mistake: a do-over for a slipped finger.** Once your finger and disc are set and the disc leaves your finger, the shot has happened (rule 7h). A flub is a shot.

**Mistake: starting the disc anywhere in your quarter.** The disc starts flat and still, touching your quarter's outer line, or touching both that line and one of your quarter lines (rule 7e). It may not start out in the middle of the 5 ring.

**Mistake: the other hand on the table.** During a shot, only your shooting hand, wrist, and forearm may touch the board or table (rule 7c). Your free hand stays in your lap or at your side.

## 7. Posture and the board

**Mistake: standing up to reach a shot.** At least part of your seat must stay on the chair while you shoot, all four chair legs stay on the floor, and nothing but your feet touches the floor (rules 7k to 7m). Players call this the one cheek rule.

**Mistake: turning the board.** The board may not be moved during a game. The one exception: at the end of a round you may move it to fish out a disc lost underneath. If one goes under during a round, reuse a disc you already shot and tell your opponent (rule 7i).

## Home tables

Most families play looser than this, and that is fine. The point of knowing the NCA answer is that you can choose your house rules on purpose, name them, and play by the same ones every time. Lesson 10 lists the common ones. For the short version of the full rules, keep our [crokinole rules](/articles/crokinole-rules/) article handy.
