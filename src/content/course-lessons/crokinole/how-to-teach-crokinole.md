Crokinole is one of the easiest games to teach, and one of the easiest to over-teach. A new player does not need the Spinning Disc Rule before their first flick. They need a seat, a disc, and a reason to aim. This lesson gives you a host script in the order you would say it, built from the NCA rules taught in [lesson 1](/academy/crokinole/how-to-play-crokinole/).

For the general craft of teaching any game, see our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/). Print the [crokinole teach sheet](/academy/crokinole/teach-sheet/) and leave it next to the board.

## Before anyone sits down

- **Pick the format.** Four people: doubles, partners across from each other. Two people: singles. Five or more: play doubles and rotate a player in after each game.
- **Count the discs.** 6 each for doubles, 8 each for singles. Hand each player their stack.
- **Powder in the ditch.** A small pinch in one spot. Show it once; people copy what they see.
- **Clear the table.** Drinks off the table that holds the board. A bumped board moves discs.

## The script, in order

Say these three sentences and nothing else:

1. "Take turns flicking one disc from the line in front of you, toward the middle."
2. "If the other side has a disc on the board, you have to hit one. If they don't, you have to reach this line." Point at the 15 line, the ring with the pegs.
3. "Closer to the middle is worth more, and the hole is 20."

Then shoot one demo disc. Place it touching the outer line in your quarter, flick it softly, and let it stop somewhere in the 10 ring. Say, "On an empty board, that doesn't count. It has to reach the 15 line, so it comes off." Take it off. You have just taught the No Hiding Rule in four seconds.

Now hand the first disc to the youngest or newest player, and start.

## Teach the rest as it happens

Every other rule is easier to learn in the moment it comes up. Have these lines ready:

| When this happens | Say this |
| --- | --- |
| A shot misses every enemy disc | "Missed, so that one comes off, and any of yours it moved." |
| A shot hits your own disc into theirs | "That counts. Hitting through your own disc is legal." |
| A disc stops touching the outer line | "Touching the outside line means it's out. Into the ditch." |
| A disc drops in the hole | "Twenty! Take it out and set it where we can all see it." |
| Someone half-stands to reach | "Keep part of your seat on the chair. It's a real rule, I promise." |
| Someone asks about scoring | "We count at the end of the round. Lower number if you're on a line." |

Keep the posture rule light at home. The point of saying it is so nobody is surprised at a club or tournament later.

## Model the first-round habits

New players copy the host. A few things to do on purpose in round one:

- **Shoot soft.** Your first hits should knock a disc off and leave yours on the board. People will copy the touch.
- **Wait for stillness.** Do not reach for a disc until everything has stopped. Rule 7d in the NCA rules, and good manners besides.
- **Count out loud.** When the round ends, count your own discs aloud, then theirs. It teaches the values faster than any table.

## Seating mixed ages

Crokinole is a favorite for families because a child and a grandparent can share a board. A few honest adjustments help:

- **Small hands.** Let young kids shoot from inside the 5 ring at first, or use a crokinole cue. The World Crokinole Championship runs cue categories, and its junior divisions welcome young cue players, so this is a known path, not a cheat.
- **Partner the newest player with the strongest.** In doubles, partners may talk during play, so the experienced partner can coach every shot.
- **Skip the must-hit rule for the very young** if it frustrates them, and say plainly that it is a house rule for now.

## After the first round

Now is the time for the details: score both sides together, explain that a tie round still counts, and decide how you will score the game (lesson 5 has both ways). Then play a full game of four rounds. By round three, nobody will need the script.

## When someone wants more

If a player asks how to aim, hand them [lesson 4 on how to shoot](/academy/crokinole/how-to-shoot-crokinole/). If they ask whether people really play this seriously, the answer is yes, every June in Tavistock, Ontario, and lesson 11 covers how to join them.
