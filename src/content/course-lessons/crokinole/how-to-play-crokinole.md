Crokinole is a flicking game on a round wooden board. You flick small discs toward the center, knock your opponent's discs off, and try to leave yours near the middle when the last disc stops. It plays two (singles) or four (doubles), takes minutes to learn, and rewards years of practice.

This lesson teaches the game the way tournaments play it, from the [Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf) ratified by the National Crokinole Association (NCA) on May 6, 2026, checked against the [World Crokinole Championship rules](https://worldcrokinole.com/thegame.html). Where home games often differ, this lesson says so. For a shorter read with a little history, see our article on [crokinole rules](/articles/crokinole-rules/).

## The board

![A crokinole board from above: the 20 hole in the center, the 15 line where the eight pegs stand, the 10 ring, the 5 ring split into four quarters, the ditch around the edge, and two players across from each other with one disc each on the outer line](/academy/crokinole/setup.svg)

From the center out:

- **The 20 hole.** A shallow hole just bigger than a disc.
- **The 15 ring.** The circle around the hole, bounded by the 15 line. Eight pegs stand on that line and guard the middle.
- **The 10 ring.** The next band out.
- **The 5 ring.** The wide outer band. Four short lines split it into quarters, one quarter for each seat.
- **The outer line.** The edge of the 5 ring. It is also your shooting line.
- **The ditch.** The gutter between the playing surface and the rail. Discs knocked out land here and stay for the rest of the round.

## Setting up

1. **Seats and colors.** In singles, two players sit across from each other. In doubles, partners sit across from each other and share a color, so the teams alternate seats around the board. The NCA rules leave first shot, colors, and seats to chance or the organizer.
2. **Discs.** Each player shoots 8 discs in singles and 6 in doubles. Keep the ones you have not shot off the board, near your seat.
3. **Powder.** If you use it, put a pinch of shuffleboard powder in the ditch and rub your disc in it before you shoot. Do not sprinkle it across the board.

## Taking a shot

Play goes clockwise, one disc per turn. Wait until every disc has stopped moving. Then:

- Place your disc flat and still, touching the outer line in your own quarter. It may also touch one of your quarter lines.
- Flick it with one or more fingers. No finger guards or other aids.
- During the shot, only your shooting hand, wrist, and forearm may touch the board or table.
- Once your finger is set and the disc leaves it, the shot counts, even if you flub it.

## The valid-shot rule

This is the heart of crokinole, and it has two halves.

**If any opposing disc is in play, you must hit one.** Your shooter can hit it directly, or it can knock one of your own discs into it. Either way counts.

**If no opposing discs are in play, you must play to the middle.** When everything stops, your shooter, or a disc it struck, must be touching or inside the 15 line. A 20 counts. The NCA calls this the No Hiding Rule, because it stops you from parking a disc out of reach in the 5 ring.

If a shot breaks the rule, remove the shooter and every disc it struck, including any 20 it made. They do not score.

## Out of play, 20s, and fouls

- **Out of play.** A disc that touches or crosses the outer line is out of play and goes into the ditch before the next turn.
- **The Spinning Disc Rule.** If a disc touches or crosses the outer line, hits nothing but the playing surface, and comes back inside on its own, it stays in play.
- **The Damage Stays Rule.** If a disc goes over the line, bounces off the rail or discs in the ditch, and comes back onto the board, that disc is out. Anything it moved stays moved, and any 20 it knocked in counts.
- **20s.** After each turn, take any disc lying flat and fully in the center hole and set it aside where everyone can see it. It scores 20 at the end of the round.

## Scoring

When every disc has been shot, count before anyone moves a disc: 20 for each disc set aside, 15 inside the 15 line, 10 in the 10 ring, 5 in the 5 ring. A disc touching a line scores the lower region.

In NCA play, the higher total wins the round for 2 points, a tie gives 1 each, and a game is four rounds with the first shot alternating. Many families instead score the difference and play to 100. Agree before the first shot. Lesson 5 covers both.

## Home rules versus tournament rules

This course teaches the NCA column and flags the other as a house rule.

| Question | NCA rule | Common at home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open board: does touching the 15 line count? | Yes | Some groups require fully inside |
| When do 20s come out? | Right after the turn | Sometimes left in until the end |
| How is a round scored? | 2 points for a win, 1 for a tie | The difference, first to 100 |
| Discs in singles | 8 per player | Often 12 each, from a set of about 26 |
| Discs in doubles | 6 per player | Whatever the set holds, split evenly |
