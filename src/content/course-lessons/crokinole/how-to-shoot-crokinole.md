New players flick the way they would flick a bottle cap: a different hand, a different finger, and a different force every time. Some shots land, most do not, and nobody can say why. The fix is not talent. It is a routine. This lesson builds one from the ground up, inside the rules on how a shot must be taken.

## What the rules allow

The NCA rules shape the technique, so start there ([Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf), May 6, 2026):

- The disc starts flat and still, touching the outer line of your quarter (rule 7e).
- It is struck by one or more fingers, with no finger guards or other aids (rule 7f).
- Only your shooting hand, wrist, and forearm may touch the board or table during the shot (rule 7c). Resting them on the board is fine.
- Part of your seat stays on the chair, the chair stays flat on the floor, and only your feet touch the floor (rules 7k to 7m).
- You may not shake the board on purpose or excessively (rule 7g).

Inside those limits, every grip and stance below is legal.

## Step 1: Choose your finger

Players use two common flicks. Try both for a week and keep the one you can repeat.

- **The index flick.** Set the back of your index fingernail just behind the disc and flick it forward. Many new players start here because it feels natural.
- **The middle-finger flick.** The same motion with the middle finger. Plenty of players prefer it. Try it before you settle.

Whichever you pick, strike with the nail side of the finger, not the soft pad. The nail gives a firm, consistent contact.

A note on contact: some players start with the finger touching the disc, while others start a small gap behind it. Both are common. Pick one and keep it the same on every shot.

## Step 2: Set your hand

Your hand is the base the whole shot rests on.

- **Anchor it.** Rest the heel of your hand or your forearm on the board or rail so it cannot drift. The rules allow it, and a still hand is a straight shot.
- **Line up behind the disc.** Your finger, the disc, and the target should sit on one line. If your finger is off to one side, the disc will be too.
- **Use your quarter.** You may set the disc anywhere along your quarter's outer line, right up to the quarter lines. Sliding left or right is the cheapest way to change your angle. Before you aim, ask where on the line gives the straightest path.

## Step 3: Aim with your eye low

- **Get your eye down.** Lower your head toward the board, behind the disc, so you see the path the disc will take rather than the board from above. Your seat must still touch the chair, so lean from the waist.
- **Pick a spot, not an area.** Aim at one edge of the target disc, or at one point on a peg, not at "over there." Small targets make small misses.
- **Pause, then shoot.** Set, look, breathe out, flick. A rushed flick is where most misses come from.

## Step 4: Control the strength

Strength matters more than aim for most new players. The usual miss is not off line. It is too hard: the shooter hits the target and keeps going into the ditch.

Think of three speeds:

| Speed | Use it for | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft | Reaching the 15 line, the open 20 | The disc dies near where it lands |
| Medium | A takeout where you want to stay on the board | Target goes, shooter slows and stays |
| Hard | Clearing a disc behind others, or a double | Everything moves, including your shooter |

Learn soft first. On a fast, well-powdered board, a soft flick carries much farther than you expect.

## Your seat and body

- **Sit square to your quarter.** Your shoulders should face the center. You can turn your body to reach a side of your quarter, but keep part of your seat on the chair.
- **Use the same chair height every time.** A different height changes your eye line and your feel.
- **Free hand still.** Keep it off the table (rule 7c) and away from the board.

## Common faults

- **Shooter flies off after the hit.** Too hard. Drop a speed.
- **Disc wobbles or drifts off line.** Check that the disc starts flat and that your finger meets it squarely, nail side first.
- **Misses to the same side every time.** Your finger is not behind the line. Move your hand, not your aim point.
