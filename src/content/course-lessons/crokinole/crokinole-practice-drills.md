Crokinole has a rare gift for anyone who wants to improve: you can practice alone. Every important shot can be set up on an empty board and repeated fifty times in a sitting. This lesson gives you six drills and a weekly plan that ties them together, so the skills from Levels 1 to 4 hold up under pressure.

A word on targets. Top players are startlingly good. In one 2025 tournament final, Devon Fortino and Shawn Hagarty made a combined 85 of 90 open 20s, about 94 percent, according to the [World Crokinole Championship's 2026 report](https://worldcrokinole.com/headline%20news/winner2026.html). Nobody starts there. Your target is your own last week.

## Keep a log

Every drill below ends in a number. Write it down with the date: a notebook, a phone note, or a sheet taped under the board. Without a log, practice drifts toward the shots you already like. With one, you can see which shot is lagging.

Use the same board, the same powder, and the same chair for practice when you can. Lesson 9 explains why: speed changes with equipment, and you are training a feel for speed.

## Drill 1: Open 20s

**Why:** the open 20 is the highest-value shot in the game, and when every other tiebreak fails, the World Championship settles ties with a shoot-out of 12 shots at the 20 hole ([rules](https://worldcrokinole.com/thegame.html)).

**How:** shoot 24 discs at the empty hole, from your normal seat, using the full width of your quarter. Remove each disc after it stops. Count the 20s.

**Make it harder:** shoot your 24 in sets of 8, the singles count for a round, and record each set separately. Look for a drop-off late in each set.

## Drill 2: Reach the line

**Why:** on an open board, a disc must end touching or inside the 15 line (NCA rule 3a ii). A soft shot that reliably gets there is your safety net when the 20 is not on.

**How:** shoot 20 discs at the 15 line, aiming to stop just touching it behind a peg. Score 1 point for touching or inside, 2 points for touching behind a peg from the opposite seat's view.

## Drill 3: Takeout and stay

**Why:** the hit and stick is the shot that separates new players from good ones (lesson 6).

**How:** place one disc of the other color in the 10 ring. Shoot at it 20 times, resetting each time. Score 1 point if it leaves the board, 2 if it leaves and your shooter stays in play, 3 if your shooter stays inside the 15 line.

**Make it harder:** move the target to the 5 ring, then behind a peg.

## Drill 4: Hit and roll to a spot

**Why:** a takeout that ends with your shooter hidden is one of the best results a shot can have (lesson 7).

**How:** set a target disc in the 10 ring. Before each shot, name the peg you want your shooter to finish behind. Shoot 15. Score 1 for each takeout, plus 1 if the shooter ends near the named peg.

## Drill 5: Doubles and in-offs

**Why:** these shots are rare, so you rarely get to practice them in games.

**How:** set two target discs close together, and shoot 10 times for the double. Then set one target disc near the center and shoot 10 times for the in-off 20. Count successes. Move the targets around the board between sets, so you learn which angles work from your seat.

## Drill 6: The last shot

**Why:** the hammer decides close rounds, and pressure changes your touch.

**How:** set up a late-round position: a few discs of each color, with you behind by one disc. You get one shot. If it wins the round by your count, score it. Reset to a new position and repeat 10 times. Have a friend set the positions if you can, so you do not build easy ones.

## A weekly plan

Four short sessions a week beat one long one. Each session is about 20 to 30 minutes.

| Day | Drills | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | 1, 2 | Open board touch |
| Day 2 | 3, 4 | Hitting and staying |
| Day 3 | 1, 5 | 20s and rare shots |
| Day 4 | 6, then a full game | Pressure and decisions |

Every fourth week, replace the drills with full games against the strongest player you can find. Then compare your log to four weeks earlier and pick next month's focus: the drill whose number moved least.

## Play real games too

Drills build shots; games build judgment. Play under the NCA rules as often as you practice: 8 discs each, four rounds, points per round, seated. If you can, play people better than you, at an NCA club or event (lesson 11).
