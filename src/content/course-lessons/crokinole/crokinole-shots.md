Once your shot is repeatable, the question changes from "can I hit it?" to "which shot is this?" Crokinole players use a small set of names for the shots that come up again and again. This lesson walks through them, with the rule that limits each one.

The rule behind all of them is the valid-shot rule from the [NCA Official Rules of Crokinole](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf), rule 3: with an opposing disc in play, your shot must hit one, directly or through your own disc. With none in play, a disc must end touching or inside the 15 line. Every shot below has to pass that test, or it comes off.

## The open 20

**When:** no opposing discs are in play.

A soft, straight shot at the empty center hole. Top players make it most of the time: the final of the 2026 World Crokinole Championship ended on a perfect round of eight 20s, according to the [championship's report](https://worldcrokinole.com/headline%20news/winner2026.html). For everyone else, the open 20 is also the safest shot on an empty board, because a near miss still lands near the 15 line and satisfies the No Hiding Rule.

**Shoot it:** slide along your line until you have a clean path between the pegs, then shoot at soft speed. A disc that is too hard hits the back of the hole and pops out.

## The takeout

**When:** an opposing disc is in play and in sight.

Hit their disc hard enough to send it into the ditch. This is the bread-and-butter shot of crokinole. A takeout scores nothing by itself, but each disc you remove is points they no longer have.

**The catch:** a full-speed takeout usually sends your shooter into the ditch too. That leaves the board empty, and your opponent gets an open 20.

## Hit and stick (or hit and roll)

**When:** you want the takeout and a scoring disc at once.

A takeout at medium speed, aimed so your shooter stops after contact (hit and stick) or rolls to a chosen spot (hit and roll). Aim at the center of their disc to make yours stop. Aim off center to send yours sideways, the way a cue ball moves in pool.

This is the shot that separates new players from good ones. A good hit and roll leaves your shooter tucked behind a peg, where the next shot cannot reach it easily.

## The raise

**When:** your own disc sits short of where you want it.

Hit your own disc to push it forward, into a higher ring or behind a peg. **Mind the rule:** if an opposing disc is in play, a raise is only legal if the push carries your disc into theirs. A pure raise that touches no enemy disc is a failed shot, and both of your discs come off. When the board has no opposing discs, a raise is legal if a disc ends touching or inside the 15 line.

## The rebound

**When:** their disc is hidden behind a peg or another disc.

Bank your shooter off a peg, or off one of your own discs, to reach a disc you cannot hit straight. Rebounds off pegs are hard to control, and a miss costs you the shot. Try one only when the hidden disc is worth it, such as a 15 that would win them the round.

## The double takeout

**When:** two opposing discs sit close together, or lined up.

One shot that removes two of their discs. Hit the first so it drives into the second, or hit one and let your shooter carry on to the other. A double is a big swing, but the angle has to be right. If it is not, take the single and keep your shooter on the board.

## The in-off 20

**When:** an opposing disc sits near the center.

Hit their disc so that your shooter deflects into the 20 hole. It is a takeout and a 20 at once, and because you hit an opposing disc, the shot is valid and the 20 counts. It needs an angle where the deflection points at the hole, so look for it, but do not force it.

## Choosing between them

A quick order of questions before every shot:

1. **Must I hit?** If an opposing disc is in play, every option must touch one.
2. **Can I hit and stay?** A hit and stick usually beats a hard takeout.
3. **Is there a double or an in-off?** Take it if the angle is clean.
4. **Is the board empty?** Then the open 20, or a soft shot that stops touching the 15 line behind a peg.
5. **Am I ahead, with the last shot to come?** Lesson 7 says when to play safe.
