Welcome to Level 3. At Levels 1 and 2 you learned to make shots. Now the question is which shot the round needs. Crokinole strategy is mostly about the end of the round: who shoots last, what the board will look like then, and how to leave the other side with nothing good to do.

## The hammer

The **hammer** is the last shot of the round. The NCA rules use the word in their coaching rule, listing "who has hammer" among the things spectators may not tell a player ([NCA rules, 8a](https://nationalcrokinoleassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NCA-Official-Rules-of-Crokinole.docx.pdf)).

Who has it: in singles, both players shoot the same number of discs and alternate, so the player who did **not** shoot first shoots last. In doubles, the same works out by team: the team that did not start the round has the hammer. The start alternates every round, so each side gets the hammer twice in a four-round game.

Why it matters: the last shot faces no reply. Whatever it does, stays done. A side with the hammer can afford to let the round stay close, because it gets the final word.

## Playing with the hammer

- **Keep the board simple.** A busy board gives your opponent more chances to hide. With the hammer, trade takeouts early and keep your shooters on the board.
- **Let the empty board work for you.** If the board clears late in the round, whoever shoots next must play into the open and reach the 15 line. With the hammer, you still get the last look at whatever they leave.
- **Save your best shot for last.** If you are behind, your hammer is your chance to take out their best disc or sink a 20. Plan the round around that final shot.

## Playing without the hammer

- **Make the last shot hard.** Leave your discs where a single shot cannot remove them: behind pegs, or spread apart so no double exists.
- **Build a lead before the end.** A one-disc lead is fragile when the other side shoots last. A two-disc lead is much harder to erase with one shot, as long as you leave no double.
- **Do not leave an easy in-off.** A lone disc near the center with an open angle to the hole is a gift to the hammer.

## Hiding behind pegs

A disc that sits just behind a peg, from the next shooter's point of view, is hard to hit. They must bank off the peg, use one of their own discs, or try an angle from the far end of their quarter. A miss costs them the shot.

Two rules shape hiding:

- **On an open board you cannot hide in the 5 ring.** The No Hiding Rule says your disc must end touching or inside the 15 line (rule 3a ii). A common open-board hide is a disc that just touches the 15 line behind a peg. It scores only 10, but it is safe and legal.
- **On a contested board you must hit first.** A hit and roll that ends behind a peg is one of the best results a shot can have: it removes theirs and hides yours.

Hide from the next shooter, not from everyone. A disc hidden from your opponent's seat may sit wide open to your own. That is fine.

## When to play safe

"Safe" means the shot that keeps the round under control rather than the one with the biggest upside. When to choose it depends on how you score (lesson 5):

**Points per round (NCA).** Only the round result counts. If you are ahead late, the right shot is usually the one that protects the lead: a soft takeout that stays on the board, or a hit that leaves your shooter hidden. A 20 attempt that could leave you empty is a poor trade.

**The race to 100 (home).** Margin counts. A lead of 5 and a lead of 40 are different results. Taking a 20 attempt while ahead can be right here, because the extra points carry into the next round.

In both systems, when you are behind with few shots left, take the risk. A safe shot that loses by 10 is still a loss.

## Counting shots left

Before each of the last four or five shots, count:

1. The score now, with the cancel-out method from lesson 5.
2. How many shots each side has left.
3. Who has the hammer.

A simple test: if you are ahead and the other side has more shots left, assume each of their shots can remove one of your discs. Are you still ahead after that? If not, you need another disc on the board or one of yours hidden.

## Reading your opponent

- **Watch their speed.** A player who keeps flying off the board after hits will struggle to hit and stick. Leave them takeout chances that empty the board for you.
- **Watch their 20 rate.** If they sink open 20s reliably, do not clear the board for them late in a round.
- **Notice their side.** Most players have one side of their quarter they shoot from best. Hide on the angle they avoid.
