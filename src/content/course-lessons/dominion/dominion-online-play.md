The fastest way to get better at Dominion is to play a lot of it, and the fastest way to play a lot of it is online. The computer shuffles, counts, and sets up every kingdom in a second. A game that takes half an hour at the table goes much faster on a screen. This lesson covers where to play and how to turn those games into skill.

## The official app

The officially licensed digital edition is [Dominion by Temple Gates Games](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1131620/Dominion/), on PC through Steam and on phones and tablets through the app stores. According to its Steam page:

- **The base set is free.** That is the same 26 Kingdom cards this course teaches.
- **Ranked and unranked matchmaking**, in real time or asynchronously, so you can take turns over a day.
- **Solo play against the computer** at four difficulty levels.
- **Online games for up to 6 players**, with cross-platform play.
- **Leaderboards**, and game summaries you can export and share.
- **Expansions** sold as add-ons, for when you are ready. <!-- AFFILIATE: Dominion digital expansions (Temple Gates Games) -->

The app left early access and fully launched on February 1, 2024.

There is also [Dominion Online](https://dominion.games/), the browser version from Shuffle iT that many long-time players learned on. <!-- VERIFY: whether dominion.games is still running in 2026, what it offers free, and whether it still hosts official championships. -->

## A path through the modes

**1. The computer first.** Play several games at the hardest computer level. It is patient, never annoyed by a slow turn, and it lets you try plans you would be shy to test against a person. Try Big Money plus one card in a kingdom, then try an engine in the same kingdom, and compare.

**2. Unranked games next.** Real opponents play differently from any computer. They attack, they rush, and they make the endgame decisions this course has taught you to watch for.

**3. Ranked when you are ready.** Ranked games count toward the app's leaderboards, which give you a rough measure of progress over months. Do not chase the number game to game. Look at the trend.

**Asynchronous play** is a good fit for busy weeks. A slow game lets you think hard about each decision, which is useful practice for the reading and counting skills from Levels 3 and 4.

## Turning volume into skill

Fifty games played on autopilot teach less than ten played with attention. Give each session one focus:

- **Kingdom reading.** Before turn 1, say your plan and both openings, using the [two-minute routine](/academy/dominion/dominion-kingdom-reading/).
- **Tracking.** Keep a silent count of your opponent's points and Golds, then check it at the end.
- **Endgame.** Pay special attention to every buy once 4 Provinces are left.
- **One card.** Pick a card you do not understand and play it in every game it appears.

After each game, take one minute: what decided it? The [game review lesson](/academy/dominion/dominion-game-review/) at Level 5 gives you a fuller method.

## Simulators, for the curious

The Dominion community has built simulators that play bot strategies against each other thousands of times, to measure how fast a plan reaches its Provinces. The [Dominion Strategy wiki](https://wiki.dominionstrategy.com/index.php/Simulators) lists the main ones. They are good for testing money plans such as Big Money plus one card. They are weaker at judging complex engines, which depend on decisions a simple bot does not make well.

## The community

The [Dominion Strategy](https://dominionstrategy.com/) site has articles, card discussions, and coverage of online events. Reading a strong player's explanation of a game you just lost can teach more than ten more games.

## Keep the table too

Online play is practice. The table is where Dominion is a night out: friends, a shared kingdom, and someone groaning at a Witch. Use the app to get sharper, then bring it back to the people.
