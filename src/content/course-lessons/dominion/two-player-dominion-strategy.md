Welcome to Level 4. The rules of Dominion are the same at two players as at four. The game is not. With one opponent, the Supply is smaller, every attack hits your only rival, and every point either of you scores moves the one number that matters: the gap. Two-player games are also the fastest way to get many games in, especially online.

## The two-player Supply

With 2 players, the rulebook uses 8 Estates, 8 Duchies, and 8 Provinces, and 10 Curses. Gardens, if it is in the kingdom, also uses 8.

Eight Provinces are 48 points. If they split 4 and 4, the Provinces alone decide nothing, and the game comes down to Duchies, Estates, and Curses. That is why two-player endgames so often turn into a dance of Duchies when the Provinces get low: each one is 3 points that the other player cannot have.

## Only the gap matters

In a four-player game, a card that hurts one opponent helps the other two. With one opponent, everything is a swing.

- **Your Province** is +6 for you, and one fewer Province your opponent can take.
- **A Curse you give** is minus 1 for them and makes their deck worse.
- **A contested Duchy** is +3 for you and 3 they cannot get. When both of you want it, its real value is close to 6.

Before any Victory card buy in a close game, ask: what does this do to the gap, and how many turns are left?

## The first player's edge

The first player takes the first turn, so when the game ends on their turn, they have taken one more turn than their opponent. That extra turn is worth something. In a simple simulation of two identical money decks, playing Big Money with late Duchy and Estate rules, the first player wins about 52% of games, the second about 41%, and about 7% are shared wins.

The rulebook's tiebreaker pushes back a little. If the scores tie, the player with fewer turns wins. So when the first player ends the game on a tie, the second player wins, and the first player must be strictly ahead to end it. When the second player ends the game on a tie, both have taken the same number of turns, and they share the win.

The rulebook has one more balancing rule: in a rematch, if the last game was not a tie, the winner goes last. Some tournaments go further and let players bid points for their seat, covered at Level 5.

## Playing each seat

**As the first player:** you can often end the game on your own turn, but only when you will be strictly ahead. Count before you take the last Province.

**As the second player:** you take the last turn of every round, so you always know exactly what your opponent just did. Ending the game on a tie shares the win rather than losing it. Watch for the turn where one buy from you empties the Province pile with you ahead.

## Attacks with one rival

Every attack lands on your only opponent, so its full cost comes back to you as gap. Militia every other turn costs them a few dollars each time. In a Witch game, the 10 Curses are split only between the two of you. Five each is a draw; seven to three is a big lead, worth both the points and the weaker hands.

Council Room gives each other player a card. At two players, that gift goes to your only rival every time, so weigh it more carefully than at four.

## Split piles

In a mirror, where both players want the same Kingdom card, the 10 copies get split. Whoever gets the larger share of a key card, such as a Laboratory or a Witch, usually has the stronger deck. Notice when a pile you depend on is shrinking because your opponent wants it too, and buy it a turn earlier than you planned.

## The duel mindset

Two-player Dominion rewards counting. Count the Provinces left. Count the gap. Count the turns. The [endgame lesson](/academy/dominion/dominion-endgame-strategy/) gave you the penultimate Province rule; in a duel, you will use it every game.
