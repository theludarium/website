Walk down the trading card aisle and the Pokémon section looks like a wall of shiny boxes. Booster packs, tins, Elite Trainer Boxes, collections with a foil card in the window. Almost none of them teach you to play. This lesson sorts the wall into what helps a new player and what can wait.

It is written for two readers at once: the new player, and the parent standing in the aisle with them.

## Collecting and playing are two different hobbies

Many people meet the Pokémon TCG as a collecting hobby. Packs are fun to open, and a binder of favorites is its own reward. That is a great hobby. It is just not the same as learning the game.

A pile of cards from random packs rarely makes a deck that plays well. A playable deck needs the right mix of Pokémon, Trainer cards, and Energy, in the right numbers. So if the goal is to play, start with a product where that work is already done.

## The best first buy: Battle Academy

The official Play! Pokémon getting-started page points new players to **Battle Academy**: balanced decks with a board that leads you through the game.

The current box is the 2024 edition. It has three ready-to-play 60-card decks, a game board that shows where every card goes, and two guided tutorial books that teach the game step by step. The decks are balanced against each other, so a parent and a kid, or two friends, start on even footing. <!-- VERIFY: Battle Academy 2024 contents (three 60-card decks, board, two tutorial guides, counters and coin) on the official product page, and confirm no newer edition has replaced it. -->

[Pokémon TCG Battle Academy](https://tcg.pokemon.com/en-us/battleacademy/) is the right pick when:

- Two people are learning together.
- Nobody in the house has played before.
- You want the game to sit on a table like a board game, with a clear place for every card.

## A good second option: a Mega Battle Deck

If one player is learning and already has someone to play against, like a sibling with cards or a store's League, a single deck may make more sense. The current rulebook names the **Mega Battle Deck** as a great way to learn: a preconstructed 60-card deck that comes with a strategy sheet and everything you need to play.

The official product guide lists [Mega Battle Decks](https://tcg.pokemon.com/en-us/product-guide/) one step up from Battle Academy, for players ready for a little more challenge. ## Free before you buy

Two free ways to try the game first:

- **Pokémon TCG Live.** The official free online game has an interactive tutorial. It is the fastest way to see whether the rhythm of a turn clicks.
- **Pokémon League.** Play! Pokémon stores run League, a casual play time where everyone is welcome. The official parents' page notes that Leagues often have cards for beginners. The [League night lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-league-first-night/) covers it in full.

## What to skip for now

**Booster packs as a way to build a deck.** Packs are random. Open them for fun, not as a plan.

**League Battle Decks.** These are strong, tournament-style decks. The official Play! Pokémon getting-started page pitches them at players with a bit more experience, built on higher-power cards common in tournament play. They are a great second or third deck and a big step for a first one.

**Build & Battle Boxes.** These hold a smaller 40-card deck plus packs. They are made for prerelease events, which the [prerelease lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-prerelease-guide/) covers. Not the best first product.

**Elite Trainer Boxes.** These are collector boxes with packs, sleeves, Energy cards, and a player's guide. A lovely gift for a kid who already plays. As a way to learn, they do less than they appear to.

## A note for parents

You do not need to know the rules to buy well. Three questions do the work:

1. Will my kid play with someone at home? Battle Academy.
2. Will they play at a store, or with friends who already play? A Mega Battle Deck.
3. Do they mostly love opening packs and keeping favorites? Then collecting may be the hobby, and that is fine too.

If you are unsure, buy for playing first. A kid who knows the game gets more joy out of every pack later, because they know what the cards do.

## Keep the first purchase small

Whatever you choose, stop there for a few weeks. Play the decks until everyone knows them. The [budget upgrades](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-deck-upgrades/) at Level 1 make far more sense once you know which cards you wish you had.
