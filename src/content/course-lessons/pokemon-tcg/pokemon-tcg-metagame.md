The metagame, or meta, is the set of decks people are actually playing. It changes with every new set and every rotation. You do not need to know every deck in it. You need to know the families decks come from, and which members of each family are popular right now.

## Why families matter

A deck's family tells you how it wins and how fast. Once you know that, you know what to protect, what to race, and how long the game is likely to run.

The families below are not official categories. They are a plain-language way to sort what you will see. Many decks mix two.

## Fast Basic attackers

These decks put strong Basic Pokémon, often Pokémon ex, straight into play and attack from the first turns. No evolution needed.

- **How they win:** big early damage, taking Prize cards before slower decks set up.
- **What they fear:** single-Prize decks that trade one Prize for their two, and anything that slows their early turns.
- **How to play against them:** keep two Pokémon on your Bench, and make each of their Knock Outs cost them something.

## Evolution engines

These decks build a board of Stage 1 or Stage 2 Pokémon over the first few turns, often with Abilities that draw cards, move Energy, or power up attacks.

- **How they win:** once set up, they produce a big attack every turn, sometimes for several turns running.
- **What they fear:** pressure in the first turns, before their Evolution lines are complete.
- **How to play against them:** attack the Basics on the Bench before they evolve, if you have cards that reach the Bench.

## Single-Prize decks

These decks avoid Pokémon ex and play Pokémon that give up only 1 Prize card each.

- **How they win:** the Prize trade. Their opponent needs six Knock Outs; they may need only three.
- **What they fear:** running out of attackers, or meeting a deck that knocks out one Pokémon every turn with little effort.
- **How to play against them:** knock out their attackers efficiently, and look for chances to take two Knock Outs in one turn if your deck can.

## Control and disruption

These decks win by stopping you from playing: limiting the cards in your hand, locking your Items or Abilities, discarding your Energy, or making you run out of cards to draw.

- **How they win:** you cannot do what your deck needs, so it never gets going, or you lose to an empty deck.
- **What they fear:** decks that need very few cards to function.
- **How to play against them:** keep a lean plan, count your deck in long games, and do not give them easy targets.

## Spread decks

These decks place damage across several of your Pokémon at once, often on the Bench, and then collect several Knock Outs together.

- **How they win:** a big turn late in the game that takes many Prize cards at once.
- **How to play against them:** heal or remove damaged Pokémon, and keep your Bench as small as is safe.

## Learning the meta

**At your store.** League is the best scouting there is. Watch games, ask what people are playing, and notice the decks that show up every week. Local metas often differ from big events.

**At big events.** Decklists from major tournaments are published after the event. The official Play! Pokémon site posts news from its Championship Series, and community sites such as [Limitless TCG](https://limitlesstcg.com/) collect results and decklists from many events. Look at which decks finish near the top, and which appear often.

**When it changes.** New sets arrive several times a year. Once a year, usually in spring in recent seasons, the Standard format rotates out older regulation marks, and the meta resets. The weeks after a rotation or a new set are the best time to watch and learn. <!-- VERIFY: the next Standard rotation date after the 2026 to 2027 season. -->

## Choosing a deck in a known meta

Knowing the meta helps you choose a deck: one with good matchups against the popular decks, and no matchup it simply cannot win. But the deck you know well usually beats the deck you just picked up.
