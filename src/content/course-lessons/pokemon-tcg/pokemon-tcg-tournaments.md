Welcome to Level 5. You can play a clean game, build and test a deck, and handle a timed match. Some players stop at League for years, and that is a great place to stay. Others feel the pull of the bigger events. This lesson maps the whole path so you can choose how far to go.

## Formats: which cards are allowed

At home you can play any cards you both agree on. Sanctioned events use a **format**.

- **Standard** is the main format and, as the Play! Pokémon rules page puts it, the most popular way to play. It allows only recent cards, identified by the **regulation mark** in each card's bottom left corner. Each Championship Series season, older marks rotate out. Reprints with a current mark stay legal.
- **Expanded** allows a much larger pool of older cards, with its own banned list. Fewer local events use it.
- **Alternative formats**, such as Gym Leader Challenge, are described in the official Alternative Play Handbook. They are fan favorites for casual nights.

## Age divisions

Play! Pokémon tournaments split players into three age divisions, set by birth year at the start of each season. For the 2027 season:

- **Junior:** born 2015 or later.
- **Senior:** born 2011 to 2014.
- **Masters:** born 2010 or earlier.

Your division stays the same all season, and younger players face others near their own age. The official parents' page names age divisions as one of the safety measures at local tournaments.

## The ladder, rung by rung

**1. Pokémon League.** Casual play. Where everyone starts, and where many happily stay.

**2. League Challenges.** Small local tournaments at Play! Pokémon stores. Play! Pokémon calls them entry-level events: a good first tournament, and a chance to earn your first **Championship Points**.

**3. League Cups.** Local tournaments similar in scale to League Challenges, but often with more players, tougher opponents, and more Championship Points.

**4. Regional and Special Championships.** The first level of major events, with bigger crowds and more Championship Points than store events. They need advance registration and can sell out.

**5. International Championships.** A few huge events a season, each serving a part of the world.

**6. The Pokémon World Championships.** The season's final event. For 2027, a set number of top Championship Point earners in each region and age division get invitations. Winning a Regional or Special Championship, or finishing in the top 4 at an International, also earns one.

The official [Championship Series site](https://championships.pokemon.com/en-us/) lists the events and the season's structure. <!-- VERIFY: League Challenge and League Cup match structure this season (best of one or best of three, round times). -->

## What changes at a tournament

- **You need a Play! Pokémon Player ID.** The [League night lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-league-first-night/) covers it.
- **You write a deck list.** Every card in your 60-card deck, submitted before play. Official deck list sheets are on the Play! Pokémon documents page.
- **Your deck must be legal** in the event's format, card by card.
- **Rounds are timed,** and you play several rounds against different opponents.
- **Judges handle rules questions.** Raise your hand and ask. It is what they are there for. The [tournament rules lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-tournament-rules/) covers time, ties, and deck lists in detail.

Read the Play! Pokémon Standards of Conduct before your first event. It sets the tone for every sanctioned table.

## A quick checklist before any event

1. **Play the same deck for a few weeks.** Knowing your deck beats a stronger deck you have never played.
2. **Check legality.** Regulation marks first, then the banned list.
3. **Practice your setup.** Shuffle, draw, place, set Prizes, smoothly, without rushing.
4. **Practice on Pokémon TCG Live,** the free official game, against decks you will see.
5. **Go with a goal** that is not winning: play every round, learn one new card, meet one new player.

## The point of it all

Tournaments are one way to enjoy the game. The shared table is the reason it lasts. The best players at any League remember what it was like to be new, and the best thing you can do, once you know the game, is teach it to the next person who walks in nervous.
