Your first League Challenge, League Cup, or Regional is a big step. The games are the same game you have played all along. What changes is everything around them: registration, deck lists, pairings, a clock. This lesson walks through the day in order so nothing about it is a surprise.

## The week before

- **Register.** Store events often fill up, and big events need advance registration. Find events on the official [Event Locator](https://events.pokemon.com/EventLocator/?locale=en-us) or the store's own page.
- **Check your Player ID.** You need one to play. The [League night lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-league-first-night/) covers how to get it.
- **Check legality.** Every card legal in the format, and no banned cards.
- **Write your deck list** at home, with the deck in front of you. Count it twice. It must total exactly 60.

## What to bring

- Your deck, sleeved, plus a few spare sleeves of the same kind.
- Your deck list, or confirmation that you submitted it online if the event uses that.
- Damage counters, markers for Special Conditions, and a coin or a see-through die.
- A pen and a blank sheet for notes.
- Water and a snack. Food and drinks stay off the play table.
- A phone charger, if the event posts pairings online.

## Arrival and registration

Arrive early. Check in, hand in your deck list if you have not, and find where pairings are posted. Most events open with a short announcement about the number of rounds, the time per round, and whether matches are single games or best of three.

## How the rounds work

Most Play! Pokémon events use **Swiss rounds**: in each round, you play someone with a similar record. You are never knocked out of Swiss for losing, so a bad first round is not the end of your day.

Larger events then cut to a **top cut**: the best Swiss records play single-elimination matches until one player is left. Smaller events may be Swiss only.

Players are split into **Junior, Senior, and Masters** divisions by birth year. When a division has fewer than six players, the rules combine divisions for Swiss pairings, but each division keeps its own standings and its own top cut.

## Each round

1. Find your table on the pairings.
2. Shake hands, shuffle, offer your deck to cut, and set up.
3. Play at a steady pace. Track Prize cards and time.
4. Call a judge for any rules question, or if something goes wrong. You will not get in trouble for asking.
5. When the match ends, both players fill in and sign the match slip or report the result as the event asks. Check it before you sign.

## Between rounds

- **Eat, drink, and step outside if you can.** A short walk resets your head.
- **Do not replay the last game for an hour.** Write one line in your notes and let it go.
- **Watch your time.** Be back before the next round starts. Arriving late can cost you a game.

If you need to leave early, tell the organizer and follow the drop procedure. You cannot drop in the middle of a match, so finish or concede your current match first.

## Keeping your head

Everyone loses games at a tournament, including the winner most of the time. Judge your day by the goals you set: play every round, play at a good pace, make fewer of the mistakes in your notes. A win is the bonus.

Be the opponent you would want across the table. Say good game, mean it, and if your opponent is new, help them feel welcome. The Play! Pokémon Standards of Conduct sets that expectation for everyone.

## For parents of Junior and Senior players

Juniors and Seniors play against players near their own age, and adults are present at every sanctioned event. Stay nearby on the first day. Let your player handle their own games, and let judges handle rules questions. Bring snacks, keep the mood light between rounds, and celebrate effort and good sportsmanship over results. The site's [parent's guide to the Pokémon TCG](/articles/pokemon-tcg-parents-guide/) covers the hobby more broadly.
