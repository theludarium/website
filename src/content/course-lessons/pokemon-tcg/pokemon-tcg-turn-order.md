The rulebook lets you do almost everything on your turn in any order: Bench Basics, evolve, attach an Energy, play Trainers, retreat, use Abilities. Then you attack and the turn ends. "Any order" sounds like freedom. In practice it is a puzzle, and strong players solve it the same way every time.

This lesson gives you the method and a five-word checklist to go with it.

## The principle: open options, then commit

Some cards give you information or more choices. Search cards show you your deck. Draw cards put new cards in your hand. Others lock in a decision you cannot take back: the one Energy attachment, an evolution, a retreat, your one Supporter.

So the order is:

1. Play the cards that open options.
2. Then make the choices that close them.

If you attach your Energy first and then draw into a better target for it, that attachment is wasted for the turn. If you draw first, you attach with full knowledge.

## The checklist: look, find, draw, commit, attack

**Look.** Before you touch a card, look at the board. What is your opponent's Active Pokémon, and how much damage does it have? What could it do to you next turn? What do you need this turn: a Knock Out, a safer Active Pokémon, a Bench that survives?

**Find.** Play Items that search your deck for specific cards, and put Basic Pokémon on your Bench. Searching early also tells you which cards are left in your deck, and which are missing because they are in your Prize cards.

**Draw.** Play cards that draw. This is usually where your Supporter goes. Some Supporters draw a few cards, some shuffle your hand away first, some discard. Read yours and ask what it does to the cards already in your hand (see below).

**Commit.** Now make the locked-in choices. Evolve. Attach your Energy to the Pokémon that needs it most this turn or next. Retreat if you need to. Play a Stadium or a Pokémon Tool.

**Attack.** Check the attack cost, Weakness, and Resistance. Then announce it. The turn is over.

## Protect your hand from your own Supporter

The one place the simple order flips is a Supporter that changes your hand. Read it carefully.

- **A Supporter that shuffles your hand into your deck before drawing.** Play out the useful cards in your hand first: Bench your Basics, play your Items, maybe even attach Energy. Anything left in your hand gets shuffled away.
- **A Supporter that makes you discard cards.** Use what you can first, so you are discarding cards you did not need.
- **A Supporter that only draws.** Play it early, before you commit to anything, so you see the most cards before choosing.

## Abilities belong in the order too

Abilities can be used as many times as the cards allow, and they are not attacks, so using one does not end your turn. Place each one where it fits the checklist. An Ability that draws or searches belongs in find or draw. An Ability that moves Energy or damage belongs in commit. Some Abilities only work when a Pokémon is in the Active Spot, or only once per turn, so read them before you start.

If a Pokémon gains an Ability when it evolves, evolve it first, then use the Ability.

## Think one turn ahead

The best turns also set up the next one. Before you attach, ask: which Pokémon will attack next turn, and how much Energy will it need then? Before you fill your Bench, ask: which of these do I want to evolve next turn? A Basic Pokémon has to be in play at the start of your turn to evolve, so the Bench you build this turn is next turn's evolution plan.

## Keep it moving

A good order should make turns faster, not slower. At first, say the checklist words in your head. After a few weeks, you will notice you simply play draw cards before attaching without thinking about it. That is the goal: the method becomes habit, and your thinking time goes into the hard choices.

At a store, take your time when it matters, but not on every card. Tournament play has guidelines for how long common actions should take, which the [tournament rules lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-tournament-rules/) covers at Level 4.
