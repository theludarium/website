The Pokémon TCG has a reputation for being complicated. Part of that is the card pool: thousands of cards, each with its own text. But you never teach the card pool. You teach a turn, and the turn is short.

This script is built for a parent teaching a kid, an older sibling teaching a younger one, or one friend teaching another. Print the [Pokémon TCG teach sheet](/academy/pokemon-tcg/teach-sheet/) and put it on the table between you. For the general craft of teaching any game, our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) pairs well with this one.

## Before you sit down

Set up both decks before you start talking. If you have Battle Academy, use its board and follow its first tutorial guide, which walks two new players through a game step by step. <!-- VERIFY: how the Battle Academy 2024 tutorial guides run the first game (for example, whether decks start in a set order). --> If you have two decks, pick simpler ones for the first game: fewer Abilities and fewer Trainer cards that need explaining.

Lay out damage counters and a coin. Decide now that the first game is a practice game with both hands face up.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "Your Pokémon battle my Pokémon. Every time you Knock Out one of mine, you take one of these face-down Prize cards. Take all six and you win."

Point at the Prize cards. Then the two other ways to win, quickly: "You also win if I have no Pokémon left to fight, or if I can't draw a card at the start of my turn."

## Minutes 2 and 3: the play area

Point at each zone on the board or table as you say it:

- "This is your Active Pokémon. It's the one fighting."
- "This is your Bench. Up to five Pokémon wait here."
- "These are your Prizes. Your deck goes here, your used cards go here."

Then the one Bench rule that saves new players: "Always keep something on your Bench. If your Active Pokémon is Knocked Out and your Bench is empty, you lose on the spot."

## Minutes 4 to 6: one turn, out loud

Take a real turn while you narrate.

1. "First I draw a card."
2. "Now I can do things, in any order. Put Basic Pokémon on my Bench. Evolve a Pokémon that's been in play since my turn started. Attach one Energy card, only one per turn."
3. "I can play Item cards as much as I want, but only one Supporter a turn."
4. "Last, I attack. Attacking ends my turn."

Then point at an attack: "To use this, these symbols have to match the Energy attached to my Pokémon. The star means any Energy works."

Stop and ask: "What could you do on your turn?" Let them answer with their own hand.

## Minutes 7 and 8: damage and Knock Outs

- "Each counter is 10 damage."
- "When the damage reaches this HP number, it's Knocked Out. I take a Prize card."
- "Look down here: Weakness. If my Pokémon is that type, the damage doubles."

Then one sentence on ex: "A Pokémon with ex after its name is extra strong, but if it's Knocked Out, the other player takes two Prizes instead of one. A Mega ex gives up three."

## Minutes 9 and 10: start, and teach the rest live

Flip the coin and play. These come up later, and each is easier to teach when it happens:

- **Retreat.** The first time a Pokémon is in trouble, show the Retreat Cost: discard that much Energy, swap it with a Benched Pokémon.
- **Special Conditions.** The first time an attack makes a Pokémon Asleep, Poisoned, or Paralyzed, turn the card or place the marker and read the rule from the teach sheet.
- **First-turn limits.** If the teacher goes first, explain on the spot: the first player can't attack or play a Supporter this turn.
- **Stadiums and Tools.** Explain them the first time one is played.

## What to leave out

- **Deck building.** The decks are already built. Save it for the [budget upgrades lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-deck-upgrades/).
- **The mulligan rule,** unless someone has no Basic Pokémon in their opening hand.
- **Formats, regulation marks, and tournaments.** None of it matters at the kitchen table.
- **Every card's text in advance.** Read cards when they are played.

## Coaching during the first game

With hands face up, you can coach without taking over. Ask questions instead of giving answers: "Which of your Pokémon could attack next turn?" "What happens if mine gets Knocked Out?"

For young kids, attaching Energy is the step they forget most. A gentle "Did you attach an Energy?" each turn builds the habit fast.
