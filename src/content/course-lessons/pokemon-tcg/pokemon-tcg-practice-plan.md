You know the rules, the decks, the odds, and the tournament procedures. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: best-of-three matches, a clock, a room full of tuned decks, and no notes from earlier rounds. This plan gets you there in six weeks.

It assumes about four to six hours a week. Scale it up or down, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The official [Championship Series site](https://championships.pokemon.com/en-us/) lists Regional and Special Championships for the season. Big events need advance registration and can sell out, so register as soon as you choose.

If a Regional is too far, run the same plan for a League Cup. The structure works for any event.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play your deck against the decks you expect. Pokémon TCG Live is the fastest way to get many games in; League nights give you in-person games against your local meta. Aim for fifteen to twenty-five games a week, and log every one as in the [testing lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-playtesting/).

**2. The clock.** At least once a week, play a full best-of-three match with a 50-minute timer, using the [tournament rules](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-tournament-rules/) for time and the +3 turns. Shuffle and set up by hand. Track your Prize cards on a note sheet that starts blank.

**3. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Setup drill.** Shuffle, draw 7, set up, and set Prizes, against a timer. Smooth beats fast.
- **Prize check drill.** Draw 7, set 6 Prizes face down, then search the deck and name the 6 Prized cards. Flip them to check.
- **Damage drill.** Lay out a board and work out the biggest attack your opponent could use next turn, as in the [reading your opponent lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-reading-your-opponent/).

**4. Review.** After every loss, write the answers to three questions:

1. Where did the Prize trade turn against me, and when?
2. What did I do on the turn before it turned?
3. Was this the list, the matchup, or my play?

Patterns show up fast, and a mistake you see three times is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: choose and learn.** Settle on a deck using the [metagame lesson](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-metagame/). Play volume and review, plus the setup drill every session. Count your Knock Outs, not your damage, every turn.

**Weeks 3 and 4: the matchups.** Focus your games on the three or four decks you expect most. Add a second timed match each week. Decide your [tech cards](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-tech-cards/) and your plan for going first or second against each deck.

**Week 5: lock the list.** No more big changes. Play every practice match under the event's rules. Write your final deck list and check it card by card.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Pack your bag from the [tournament day checklist](/academy/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-tcg-tournament-day/) two nights before. Sleep well. Arrive early.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers from week to week:

- **Win rate** against each of your top matchups.
- **The turn your main attacker first attacks,** on average.
- **Matches finished on time,** out of your timed matches.
- **Repeat mistakes** in your review notes.

If your attacker is arriving on time, your matches finish, and the same mistakes stop showing up, the plan is working, whatever your results on the day.

## At the event

Play one round at a time. Eat between rounds. Call a judge whenever you are unsure. Win or lose, write one line after each match and let it go. A Regional is a long day, and the players who finish it well are usually the ones who stayed calm in round three.

## The table across from you

Competition can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view: every match in this plan, and every match at the event, is a game against another person who loves this game too. Learn their names. Talk through the close games afterward. Help the nervous first-timer find their table.

The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
