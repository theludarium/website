A **prerelease** is a store event held before a new set reaches shelves. You get a box of the new cards, build a deck from it, and play a few casual games. Everyone starts on the same footing, which makes it one of the friendliest events in the game for new players.

Prerelease events run at individual stores on their own schedules and with their own entry fees. Find them on the official [Event Locator](https://events.pokemon.com/EventLocator/?locale=en-us), then call or check the store to sign up. ## What is in the box?

The prerelease product is the **Build & Battle Box**. The official product guide describes its core as a smaller 40-card deck you can customize for quick games, and recent expansion pages list it as a 40-card deck plus 4 booster packs of the new set. <!-- VERIFY: Delta Reign Build & Battle Box contents, including any promo card and Basic Energy. -->

So at the event you have two piles: a ready-to-play deck, and four packs of fresh cards.

## How is a prerelease game different?

A prerelease uses **Limited** rules: you play with only the cards from your box, plus Basic Energy. The decks are 40 cards, not 60. The basic turn is the same game you learned in [how a turn works](/academy/pokemon-tcg/how-to-play-pokemon-tcg/).

The Play! Pokémon Tournament Rules Handbook sets Limited games at **4 Prize cards** instead of 6, which keeps them quick, and it drops the usual limit of 4 copies of a card, unless a card's own text sets a limit. The organizer will confirm the details at the start. <!-- VERIFY: Limited rules (40 cards, 4 Prize cards, no copy limit) were read in the December 2024 Tournament Rules Handbook; confirm in the current Play! Pokémon TCG rules document. -->

## Building your deck in under half an hour

The safest method for new players: **start from the 40-card deck in the box, and swap.**

1. **Open your packs and sort them.** Pokémon by type, then Trainers, then Energy.
2. **Look for Evolution lines.** A Basic with its Stage 1 is worth far more than either alone. A full line in one type is the best thing your packs can give you.
3. **Pick your types.** Stay with the box deck's type, or switch to one if your packs gave you a strong line in it. One type is easiest; two at most.
4. **Swap one for one.** For every card you add, take one out. The deck must stay at exactly 40.
5. **Cut first:** off-type Pokémon, single Stage 2 cards without their earlier steps, and attacks that need Energy you are not playing.
6. **Keep the Trainers that find cards.** Search and draw Trainers matter even more in a small deck.
7. **Check your Energy count** against the box deck's. If you changed type, change the Energy with it.

Done is better than perfect. A sensible deck built in twenty minutes beats a clever one finished as round one starts.

## During the games

- **Read every new card out loud** the first time it is played. Nobody knows the set yet, including your opponent.
- **Ask questions.** Prerelease is relaxed, and players expect to look things up.
- **Fill your Bench.** With fewer Prize cards, one Knock Out matters more, and an empty Bench loses games fast.
- **Trade after, not during.** Many players trade the cards they did not use.

## What to bring

- Card sleeves for 40 cards, plus a few spares.
- Damage counters and a coin.
- A pen, and a Player ID if you have one.
- A bag to carry home the box and the cards you did not play.

## After the prerelease

Keep the deck together for a week and play it at League. A 40-card deck is a great teaching deck for a younger sibling, and the cards you opened become your first collection from the new set.
