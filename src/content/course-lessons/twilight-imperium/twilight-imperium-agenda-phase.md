Welcome to Level 5, the championship path. At the top of Twilight Imperium, games are won as often at the council table as on the map. The agenda phase is where the galaxy writes its own rules, and a player who reads the room can gain points, lock rivals out, or rewrite a law that was about to hurt them.

Everything below follows the Living Rules Reference, version 2.0, sections 7, 8, 28, and 94.

## When the agenda phase starts

The agenda phase is skipped at first. The round someone removes the custodians token from Mecatol Rex, it joins the round, and it stays for the rest of the game (27.4). So the first landing on Mecatol is also a political choice: it starts the votes.

## How a vote works

Each agenda phase has two agendas. For each one:

1. **The speaker reveals it** and reads every outcome aloud.
2. **Players vote**, starting left of the speaker and going clockwise. The speaker votes last.
3. **The outcome with the most votes resolves.** On a tie, or if nobody votes, the speaker chooses among the tied outcomes (8.19).

To vote, exhaust any number of your planets. Your votes equal their combined influence (8.6). All your votes go to one outcome (8.7). Trade goods cannot be spent as votes (8.13), and you may abstain.

## Why votes are almost free

Look at the order of the phases. The status phase readies all your cards just before the agenda phase. You exhaust planets to vote. Then the last step of the agenda phase readies every planet again (8.4), before the next round begins.

So the planets you vote with are back in time for the next action phase. Voting costs you nothing on the map. That changes how to think about influence: a high-influence planet is a voting weapon every round, at no cost to your economy.

## Laws and directives

**Laws** can change the rules for the rest of the game. If a law's "For" outcome wins, or it elects someone, the law stays in play. If "Against" wins, its effect resolves and the card is discarded (7.3, 7.6).

**Directives** happen once and are discarded (7.7).

Some agendas elect a player or a planet. You can vote for yourself (8.10), and you can only elect a planet that a player controls (8.11). Read every outcome before the vote: an "Against" result is often a penalty, not just a rejection.

## The speaker's seat

The speaker reveals agendas, votes last, and breaks ties. Voting last means seeing every other vote before you commit. The Politics strategy card chooses the next speaker, and its primary also lets the player look at the top two agendas and put each on the top or bottom of the deck (66.2). A player who knows the next agenda can make deals in advance that nobody else understands yet.

## Riders and predictions

Some action cards, called riders, let you predict an outcome after an agenda is revealed and before anyone votes. Playing one means you cannot vote on that agenda. If your prediction is right, you gain the card's reward: the Imperial Rider, for example, gives 1 victory point. A player who has played a rider wants that outcome to win, which tells the table where their votes will go.

## Deals and trust

Deals can happen at any time between any players (28.1). The rules split them in two:

- **Binding deals** are ones that resolve immediately, such as "give me two trade goods and I vote For now." You must keep them (28.3).
- **Non-binding deals** are promises about the future, such as "I will not attack you next round." Nobody has to keep them (28.4).

During the agenda phase, each player may make one transaction with every other player for each agenda, neighbors or not (94.6). That is when trade goods, commodities, and promissory notes change hands for votes.

Breaking a non-binding promise is legal. But the table remembers, and a player whose word holds will get better deals all game. Strong players keep most of their promises and break one only when it wins the game. For more on how table politics shapes play, see [negotiation board games](/articles/negotiation-board-games/) and [kingmaking in board games](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/).

## Winning a vote

A practical routine for each agenda:

1. **Count the influence** every player has ready. It is public: their planet cards are face up.
2. **Find who cares.** Most agendas matter to two or three players. The rest can be bought.
3. **Offer something small and immediate** for votes: a trade good, a commodity, a promissory note. Binding offers are worth more than promises.
4. **Watch the speaker.** On a close vote, the speaker's tie-break decides. Make your deal with them first.

## Faction politics

A few factions change the agenda phase. The Xxcha Kingdom can discard a revealed agenda and reveal a new one. The Argent Flight votes first with bonus votes. The Nekro Virus cannot vote at all, but predicts outcomes for technology. Know who at the table bends these rules before the first agenda is revealed.

## The leader problem

Late in the game, every vote is really about one question: does this help the player closest to 10? Groups that track the score vote together against the leader. If you are the leader, offer the table something real, or expect the council to turn on you.
