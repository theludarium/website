Welcome to Level 4. By now your group can play a full game and plan a route to 10 points. This lesson is about what goes in the box: the expansions, the free updates, and how the player count changes the game.

The general case for and against expansions is in [are expansions worth it](/articles/expansions-worth-it/). For Twilight Imperium, the answer depends on how often your group plays.

## The base game

[Twilight Imperium: Fourth Edition](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074YPSTRP?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 6 players with 17 factions. Fantasy Flight lists 4 to 6 hours. It is a complete game, and plenty of groups never need more.

## Prophecy of Kings

[Prophecy of Kings](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F5M99DR?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the big expansion, and the Living Rules Reference version 2.0 is written for the game with it. It adds:

- **Seven factions**, for 24 in all (lesson 7 covers them).
- **Room for 7 and 8 players**, with new setups and pieces.
- **Exploration.** The first time you take a planet, you draw from its exploration deck: cultural, hazardous, or industrial. Cards give trade goods, attachments, or relic fragments, and fragments turn into relics.
- **Leaders.** Each faction has an agent, ready from the start; a commander, which switches on when you meet a faction-specific condition; and a hero, which becomes usable once you have scored three objectives and is then used once.
- **Mechs**, a heavy ground unit for every faction, many with a Deploy ability.
- **New systems**, including legendary planets, hyperlanes for some setups, and the wormhole nexus.
- **More cards**: action cards, agendas, objectives, and technologies.

Many experienced groups play with it, because the extra factions and leaders give each faction more identity. The cost is more rules to teach, so learn on the base game first.

## Thunder's Edge

[Thunder's Edge](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FSNZMNTF?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) came out in October 2025. It needs only the base game, though it also works with Prophecy of Kings. Fantasy Flight's announcement and the expansion's rulebook list:

- **Five new factions**: the Crimson Rebellion, the Deepwrought Scholarate, the Firmament, the Last Bastion, and the Ral Nel Consortium.
- **Breakthroughs**, a new card for every faction, gained during the game.
- **The Thunder's Edge expedition**: players claim its slices, and when it is done, the new planet goes to the player who claimed the most.
- **The Fracture**, an extra set of systems reached through ingress tokens, with relics.
- **Neutral units** that fight anyone entering their space.
- **Galactic events**, optional cards chosen at setup that change the rules for that game.
- **Twilight's Fall**, a separate mode in which each player builds a faction.
- **The Council Keleres** and other content from the free Codex, now printed.
- **Two revised strategy cards**, Construction and Warfare, which replace the base game versions, and a new Mecatol Rex tile.

This course follows the base game and Prophecy of Kings as the Living Rules Reference version 2.0 describes them. If your group plays with Thunder's Edge, read its rulebook for the changes, starting with the two revised strategy cards. Fantasy Flight posts it, and the Twilight's Fall rulebook, on the [Twilight Imperium product page](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/product/twilight-imperium-fourth-edition/).

## The free Codex updates

Fantasy Flight has released four Codex volumes as free downloads on the same page. Volumes II to IV are named Affinity, Vigil, and Liberation. They include revised cards, the Council Keleres faction in Vigil, and alternate versions of some technologies. Agree as a group which Codex changes you use before the game starts.

## Player counts

**Three players.** Everyone takes two strategy cards. Each player gets a large share of the galaxy, so early conflict is rarer and the game leans toward economy and racing.

**Four players.** Everyone still takes two cards, and all eight are taken each round, so no trade goods pile up on unchosen cards (84.2). Initiative uses your lower card.

**Five players.** The standard five-player map leaves three seats at a slight disadvantage, so the Rules Reference gives those players trade goods at the start. With Prophecy of Kings, a hyperlane setup is another option.

**Six players.** The standard map: six home systems around a three-ring galaxy. Each player takes one card, and two cards collect trade goods each round. This is the count the game is built around.

**Seven and eight players** (Prophecy of Kings). Larger galaxies, set up with hyperlanes or extra tiles. At seven, one card goes unchosen each round; at eight, all eight are taken. Expect a much longer game.

## Accessories worth knowing about

Twilight Imperium has a lot of tokens. A component organizer or a set of bags cuts setup time more than any rules change. Choose what fits your shelf, and see [how long game night should last](/articles/how-long-should-game-night-last/) for planning a long session.

## A buying order

1. The base game, played until the shared rules feel easy.
2. Prophecy of Kings, if the group plays several times a year.
3. Thunder's Edge, once your group wants new modes and events.
