The classic Twilight Imperium mistake is a ninety-minute teach followed by a nine-hour game. Nobody remembers a rule they heard two hours before they needed it. A better teach is short and ordered: the goal, the round, the three actions, then play. Everything else waits until it happens.

This script runs about 30 minutes for a table of new players. The general method behind it is in [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/).

## Before game day

The teach starts before anyone sits down.

- **Assign factions in advance.** Pick ones with simple abilities for new players (lesson 7 has a list). Send each player their faction's name.
- **Build the galaxy the night before.** Use the first-game setup from the Learn to Play booklet, or any map you like. Setting up from scratch can take close to an hour.
- **Sort each faction's pieces** into its own bag or tray: units, command tokens, control tokens, starting technologies.
- **Ask players to read lesson 1** of this course. Even one reading means the teach is a reminder, not a first look.

## The script, in order

### 1. The goal (two minutes)

"We are rival empires racing to 10 victory points. Points come mostly from objectives. These five face-down cards are worth 1 point each, these are worth 2, and one more turns over every round. You also have a secret objective only you can score. The objectives tell you what to do."

Point at the two revealed objectives and read them aloud.

### 2. The round (five minutes)

Walk through the four phases with the strategy cards in hand.

"Every round, we each take a strategy card. The number on it sets turn order. Then we take turns, one action at a time, until everyone passes. Then we score, clean up, and do it again. Later, once someone takes Mecatol Rex from the custodians, we add a voting phase."

Lay out the eight strategy cards and read each one's top line only. Do not read every secondary ability yet.

### 3. The three actions (ten minutes)

"On your turn you do one thing. Use your strategy card. Or take a tactical action. Or play a card that says Action."

Spend most of the teach on the tactical action, because it is most of the game. Use real pieces on the board:

1. Put a command token from your tactic pool in a system.
2. Move ships in from systems that do not have your token.
3. Fight if someone else's ships are there.
4. Land ground forces on planets.
5. Build with your space dock if you have one there.

Then explain the cost: "That token stays there until the end of the round. You cannot activate the same system twice, and you only have so many tokens. Tokens are your time."

### 4. Your sheets (five minutes)

Have everyone look at their command sheet together: tactic pool, fleet pool, strategy pool. Then their faction sheet: read your faction abilities to yourself, and ask about anything unclear.

### 5. Money (three minutes)

"Planets have two numbers. The left one pays for ships and technology. The right one buys command tokens and votes. Spend a planet by flipping its card. It comes back next round."

### 6. Start

That is enough to begin. Draft strategy cards and start the first action phase.

## What to leave out

- **Combat details.** Teach them at the first battle, with dice in hand. The rolls are simple: each ship rolls a die and hits on its combat number or higher.
- **The agenda phase.** It does not start until someone removes the custodians token. Teach voting then.
- **Action cards.** Players read their own as they draw them.
- **Technology trees.** Show where prerequisites appear on a card. Let players browse their decks during other turns.

## Running the first round

Read each strategy card's full text aloud when its owner uses it. That way everyone learns the secondary abilities with a real decision in front of them.

Keep the status phase on the [teach sheet](/academy/twilight-imperium/teach-sheet/) and read its steps aloud the first time. It is where most first games go wrong: forgotten action cards, tokens left on the board, or two objectives scored at once.

Answer rules questions from the Living Rules Reference rather than by vote. It is organized by topic, so a search usually takes less than a minute.

## When the first battle happens

Stop the table and walk through it together: space cannon fire after movement, then anti-fighter barrage in the first round, then announcing retreats, rolling, assigning hits, and retreating. Lesson 8 covers the full order and the odds. Teach it once, with everyone watching, and nobody will need it taught again.
