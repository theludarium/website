Twilight Imperium is a game of galactic empires: six or so great factions racing to claim the empty throne at the center of the galaxy. It has a reputation as the longest, densest game on the shelf, and the box backs that up. But the rules have a clear skeleton. Every round has the same four phases, and on almost every turn you take one of three kinds of action.

This lesson follows the current Fantasy Flight Games documents: the Learn to Play booklet and the Living Rules Reference, version 2.0. The Rules Reference wins whenever the two disagree, and a card wins over the Rules Reference. Fantasy Flight posts both on its [Twilight Imperium product page](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/product/twilight-imperium-fourth-edition/). This course covers the base game and Prophecy of Kings. The newer Thunder's Edge expansion revises two strategy cards and adds more; lesson 9 covers it.

## How do you win?

The first player to reach 10 victory points wins, the moment they reach it. Groups can agree to use the 14-point side of the track for a longer game.

Most points come from objectives. Five stage I public objectives (1 point each) and five stage II public objectives (2 points each) are dealt face down, and one more is revealed every round. Each player also holds secret objectives that only they can score. A few other things score too, like removing the custodians token from Mecatol Rex.

## How do you set up?

![An abstract six-player galaxy: Mecatol Rex in the center with the custodians token, three rings of systems, six home systems on the outer ring, and a side panel with the objectives, strategy cards, and command tokens](/academy/twilight-imperium/setup.svg)

1. **Pick a speaker** at random. The speaker chooses strategy cards first.
2. **Choose factions and colors.** Each player takes a faction sheet and its components, plus the plastic units and command sheet of one color.
3. **Build the galaxy.** Mecatol Rex goes in the center. Home systems sit in the third ring. Players take turns placing the other system tiles ring by ring. The Learn to Play booklet has a simpler first-game setup, so use it for a first game.
4. **Place the custodians token** on Mecatol Rex and shuffle the action, agenda, objective, and secret objective decks.
5. **Set out the eight strategy cards** near the speaker.
6. **Take your starting units and technologies** from the back of your faction sheet. Put 3 command tokens in your tactic pool, 3 in your fleet pool, and 2 in your strategy pool.
7. **Prepare objectives.** Each player draws 2 secret objectives and keeps 1. The speaker deals 5 stage I and 5 stage II objectives face down and reveals the first 2 stage I.

## The four phases of a round

**Strategy phase.** Starting with the speaker and going clockwise, each player takes one strategy card. With 3 or 4 players, everyone takes a second card. Each unchosen card gets a trade good, which makes it sweeter next round.

**Action phase.** Players take turns in initiative order, lowest strategy card number first. On your turn you take one action, then play passes. This repeats until everyone has passed. You cannot pass until you have used your strategy card.

**Status phase.** Score up to one public and one secret objective. The speaker reveals the next public objective. Everyone draws an action card, takes their command tokens off the board, gains 2 new ones, readies exhausted cards, repairs damaged units, and returns strategy cards.

**Agenda phase.** Skipped at first. Once someone removes the custodians token from Mecatol Rex, every round ends with two votes on agendas, laws and one-time effects that change the game.

## The three actions

**Strategic action.** Use the primary ability of your strategy card. Then each other player may spend a token from their strategy pool to use its secondary ability.

**Tactical action.** This is where the galaxy changes hands. Place a command token from your tactic pool in a system to activate it. Move ships into it. Fight if enemy ships are there. Land ground forces on its planets. Then produce new units from your space docks there. Every tactical action costs a command token, and you cannot activate a system that already holds one of yours, so tokens are your real limit.

**Component action.** Use any card or ability marked "Action," such as an action card or a technology.

## Planets, resources, and influence

Each planet card shows two numbers. Resources (the left value) pay for units and technology. Influence (the right value) buys command tokens and, later, casts votes. You spend a planet by exhausting its card, and it comes back in the status phase. Trade goods work as either one resource or one influence, but never as votes.

## Command tokens

Your command sheet has three pools. The tactic pool pays for tactical actions. The fleet pool sets how many ships, not counting fighters, you can have in any one system. The strategy pool pays for other players' secondary abilities. You gain 2 tokens each status phase, and the Leadership strategy card gives more.

## How the game ends

When a player reaches 10 points, the game ends right then. If the speaker must reveal a public objective and none are left, the game also ends, and the player with the most points wins.
