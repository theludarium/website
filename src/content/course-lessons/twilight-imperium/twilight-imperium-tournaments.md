This is the last lesson of the course. You can teach the game, finish it in a day, plan a route to 10, play any faction, fight with known odds, draft a fair galaxy, and work the council. The next step is playing against people who do the same.

Twilight Imperium has no single official circuit. Its competitive scene is mostly community events, local store tournaments, and online play. This lesson covers how those events tend to run, where to find a game, and a plan to get ready.

## How events tend to run

Formats vary by organizer, so always read the event's own rules. Common patterns:

- **Qualifying games, then a final table.** Many events seat players at several tables, then bring the top finishers to one final game.
- **A clock.** A full game in a fixed time slot is the main difference from a home game. Events often set a round or time limit and score the game as it stands when time runs out.
- **A set rules package.** Organizers say in advance which expansions and Codex updates are in play, and whether the galaxy is drafted. Slice drafts like the Milty draft from lesson 10 are a common choice.

The World Boardgaming Championships ran Twilight Imperium tournaments from 2009 to at least 2013, before this edition existed. Check the [Boardgame Players Association](https://www.boardgamers.org/) for the current event list.

## What changes under a clock

A time limit rewards different habits than a lazy Saturday:

- **Score early.** If the game may end on time rather than at 10 points, a point in round two is worth as much as a point in round six. Do not save scoring for a big finish.
- **Know where you stand every round.** You need to know who is ahead if time runs out now.
- **Plan on other turns.** Players who decide quickly get more out of a fixed time than players who calculate on their own turn.
- **Keep deals simple.** Long negotiations cost the whole table time, and a timed table runs out of patience fast.

## Where to play online

- **Board Game Arena.** An official version of the base game arrived on Board Game Arena on October 14, 2026, free to play. It covers the base game only, without Prophecy of Kings. Board Game Arena tables can be real-time or turn-based, and turn-based games run for weeks, which suits busy schedules.
- **Tabletop Simulator.** A long-running community mod is widely used by online groups and leagues.
- **Twilight Imperium Digital.** A separate official PC version for Steam was announced in 2025. Its Steam page lists the release date as to be announced.

Online communities, from the [BoardGameGeek Twilight Imperium forums](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/233078/twilight-imperium-fourth-edition) to fan Discord servers, organize leagues and find players for both online and in-person games.

## Where to play locally

Ask at your local game store, since many run long-game days and know who plays. BoardGameGeek's regional forums are another good place to post. In Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## A six-week practice plan

Each week has one focus. Online turn-based games fit this plan well, because you can play a few rounds a week.

**Week 1: Rules.** Reread the [rules mistakes](/academy/twilight-imperium/twilight-imperium-rules-mistakes/) and the Living Rules Reference sections on tactical actions and the status phase. Play one game, online or at home, and look up every rule you were not sure of.

**Week 2: Objectives.** In every round, write down the objectives you will score and the ones each opponent can score. Afterward, compare your plan with what happened. The [objectives lesson](/academy/twilight-imperium/twilight-imperium-objectives/) has the questions to ask.

**Week 3: Strategy cards.** Before every pick, name the card each other player wants. Track how often you were right.

**Week 4: Factions.** Play two factions you have never played. Read each sheet with the three questions from the [factions lesson](/academy/twilight-imperium/twilight-imperium-factions/).

**Week 5: Combat and tokens.** Before every attack, say the expected hits for both sides. Count your command tokens at the end of each round and note any you wasted.

**Week 6: The full game under a clock.** Play one game with a time limit, using the event's rules package if you know it. Then review your score log: the round you fell behind is your next focus.

## Keep a log

After every game, write three lines: your faction, your score each round, and the one decision you would change. After six games, the pattern in your log will teach you more than any guide.

## The point of it all

Twilight Imperium is the longest game many players will ever finish, and a finished game is a day spent around one table with the same people. Tournaments are a way to find more of those people. Win or lose, the hours are the good part.
