Welcome to Level 3. You know the rules, the cards, and the race. Now the factions. The base game has 17, and Prophecy of Kings adds 7, for 24. Each faction sheet lists its abilities, and each one bends a rule of the shared game. Find that bend, and you have your plan.

The ability summaries below are in our own words. Your faction sheet is the final word.

## Good factions for a first game

These have abilities that are always on or easy to use, so a new player can focus on the shared rules. This is a common community list, not an official one.

- **Federation of Sol.** Gains an extra command token every status phase (Versatile), and can drop two infantry onto a planet it controls for a strategy token (Orbital Drop). More tokens means more actions.
- **Barony of Letnev.** Its fleet limit per system is 2 more than its fleet pool (Armada), and it can spend trade goods to reroll in space combat. Big, durable fleets.
- **Sardakk N'orr.** Every unit gets +1 on combat rolls (Unrelenting). Simple and strong: fight.
- **Emirates of Hacan.** The trading faction. Free Trade secondaries, transactions with anyone, and action cards in trades. A good pick for a player who likes deals.
- **Xxcha Kingdom.** After it uses Diplomacy, it can take an empty planet next to its own (Peace Accords), and it can replace a revealed agenda (Quash). A calm, political faction.

## The other base factions

- **Arborec.** Places a free infantry on a planet each status phase, but its space docks cannot build infantry (Mitosis). Spread out and hold ground.
- **Ghosts of Creuss.** Treats alpha and beta wormholes as adjacent, and its home system sits off the board, reached through its gate. Mobile and tricky, but its geography confuses new players.
- **Universities of Jol-Nar.** -1 to every combat roll (Fragile), but it can use the Technology primary when following the secondary (Brilliant), and it ignores one prerequisite on technologies that are not unit upgrades (Analytical). Out-tech everyone, avoid fair fights.
- **L1Z1X Mindnet.** Takes over enemy PDS and space docks when it conquers a planet (Assimilate), and bombards during ground combat (Harrow). An invasion faction.
- **Mentak Coalition.** Up to two of its cruisers or destroyers fire before space combat starts (Ambush), and it takes trade goods from neighbors (Pillage). A pirate that profits from everyone's trades.
- **Embers of Muaat.** Places two fighters or a destroyer for a strategy token wherever its war sun is (Star Forge), and flies through supernovas. A war sun faction from the start.
- **Naalu Collective.** Goes first in initiative order with its 0 token (Telepathic), and can slip away from attacks (Foresight). Fast, elusive, and great at timing.
- **Nekro Virus.** Cannot research technology and cannot vote. It steals technologies by destroying enemy units and by predicting agendas. Rewarding, but it plays against the normal rules, so save it for experienced players.
- **Clan of Saar.** Scores objectives without controlling its home system (Nomadic), and gains a trade good with each new planet. A spread-out, hard-to-kill empire.
- **Winnu.** Removes the custodians without spending influence (Blood Ties), and builds on Mecatol Rex after taking it. Its plan is the center of the galaxy.
- **Yin Brotherhood.** Converts enemy infantry in ground combat (Indoctrination) and sacrifices ships for hits (Devotion). Aggressive and swingy.
- **Yssaril Tribes.** No action card limit, draws extra action cards, and can stall by discarding one as an action. The card master, but its many options slow learners down.

## The Prophecy of Kings factions

Prophecy of Kings adds leaders, mechs, and exploration, and these factions use them heavily.

- **Argent Flight.** Votes first with bonus votes (Zeal), and its anti-fighter barrage can damage ships once fighters are gone (Raid Formation).
- **Empyrean.** Ignores nebulae when moving, can let others pass through its ships, and holds two faction promissory notes. A diplomat that trades favors.
- **Mahact Gene-Sorcerers.** Takes an opponent's command token after winning a combat (Edict), which grows its fleet and lets it use that player's commander (Imperia).
- **Naaz-Rokha Alliance.** When it explores a planet with one of its mechs, it draws an extra card and picks one (Distant Suns). It can turn two matching relic fragments into a relic (Fabrication). Exploration is its engine.
- **Nomad.** Starts with three agents instead of one (The Company), and gains a trade good after each agenda outcome it voted for or predicted (Future Sight).
- **Titans of Ul.** Places sleeper tokens on planets it explores (Terragenesis), which wake into PDS units when it activates their systems (Awaken).
- **Vuil'raith Cabal.** Captures enemy units destroyed in combat (Devour) and builds them back as its own for free (Amalgamation).

## How to read any faction

Three questions work for every sheet:

1. **What do I do better than anyone?** Combat (Sardakk, Letnev), economy (Hacan, Saar), technology (Jol-Nar), politics (Xxcha, Argent), or tempo (Naalu, Sol).
2. **What is my weakness?** Jol-Nar fights badly. Nekro cannot research. Arborec builds infantry slowly from space docks. Plan around it from round one.
3. **What do my neighbors want?** Your plan meets the board through the players next to you. A combat faction beside a technology faction will be tempted to attack early.

## Commodities and neighbors

Each faction sheet shows a commodity value. Commodities turn into trade goods only when traded to another player, so factions with high values, like Hacan at 6, want trade partners. Factions with low values, like Letnev at 2, lean less on trading and more on planets.

## Picking factions as a group

For a first game, the host can assign factions from the beginner list. Later, many groups deal each player two or three factions at random and let them choose one. Lesson 10 covers the faction and map draft that tournaments use.

Thunder's Edge, released in 2025, adds five new factions, prints the Council Keleres, and gives every faction a breakthrough card. Lesson 9 covers it.
