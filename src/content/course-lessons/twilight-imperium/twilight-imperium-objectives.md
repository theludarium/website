Many new players treat Twilight Imperium as a war game and spend the first rounds building fleets. Then someone quiet scores their way to 10 while everyone else fights. Points win the game, and almost all points come from objectives. This lesson shows how to read them and plan a route.

## Where the points come from

**Stage I public objectives.** Five are dealt at setup, two are revealed, and they are worth 1 point each. They tend to ask for things you do anyway: control planets, spend resources, own technologies.

**Stage II public objectives.** Five more, worth 2 points each. The speaker cannot reveal any until all stage I objectives are face up (61.14), so they arrive in the middle of the game and ask for more.

**Secret objectives.** You start with one, and the Imperial card draws more. Each is worth 1 point. You can hold at most three in total, scored and unscored (61.21). Some score in the status phase, some in the action phase, often for a specific kind of combat.

**Other points.** These are smaller but often decide close games:

- Removing the custodians token from Mecatol Rex: 1 point.
- The Imperial primary while you control Mecatol Rex: 1 point.
- Support for the Throne: each player starts with this promissory note. Give it to someone and they gain 1 point, which they lose if they activate a system with your units.
- Some agendas grant or move points.

## The scoring limits

In each status phase, in initiative order, you may score at most one public and one secret objective (61.6). Action-phase objectives can be scored any time during that phase, though only one during or after each combat (61.7). You must control every planet in your home system to score any public objective (61.16).

The status phase limit is the heart of the race. If you are counting objectives you meet, count only one public per round, plus any you can score with the Imperial card.

## How many rounds is that?

A simple count shows why the race is tight. Suppose you score one stage I objective every status phase from round one. That is 1 point a round. Add a secret objective now and then, the custodians, and a 2-point stage II objective when they appear, and a strong player can reach 10 points in five or six rounds. A player who skips a round of scoring, because a home planet was lost or the fleet was busy, falls a full round behind.

The game cannot run past nine rounds anyway, because the ninth status phase has no objective left to reveal (81.2). So the race is not to the end. It is to 10 points before anyone else.

## Reading the board

Each status phase, after a new objective is revealed, look at all the revealed ones and ask three questions:

1. **Which can I score next status phase?** Plan this round's actions around those.
2. **Which can nobody stop me from scoring?** Objectives about spending resources or owning technologies are safe. Objectives about controlling planets can be taken away by an invasion.
3. **Which is my opponent closest to?** Sometimes the best action is to take one planet they need.

A public objective can be scored by every player who meets it, once each (61.8). So an easy objective does not separate you from the table. The objectives you score and others miss are what put you ahead.

## Secret objectives are a plan, not luck

You see your secret objective at the start. Treat it as a hint about your game: an objective about a certain kind of battle tells you which fights to pick. If a secret is far out of reach, the Imperial secondary can draw another, and you can return an unscored one when you have more than three.

Hidden objectives also make you hard to read. A player who suddenly attacks a strange system might be finishing a secret, and that is worth noticing.

## Timing the Imperial card

Imperial lets you score a public objective in the action phase, then gain a point for Mecatol Rex or draw a secret. Used well, it gives two scoring chances in one round: one from Imperial, one in the status phase. That is why it becomes the most contested card as players near 10.

Use it late in the round, after you have completed what you need, so the objective you score is one you just finished. Taking it also denies it to a leader.

## Ties at the end

If two players would reach 10 at the same moment, the one earlier in initiative order wins (98.7). In the status phase, players score in initiative order, so a low strategy card number is a tiebreaker in a close final round.

## The habit

Count points every round, for everyone. The scoreboard is public, the objectives are public, and the player who knows who is closest to 10 makes better choices than the player with the biggest fleet.
