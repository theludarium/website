Combat in Twilight Imperium looks like a pile of dice, and it is. But each die follows one rule: roll a ten-sided die, and it hits if the result is equal to or higher than the unit's combat value (18.1). The 0 counts as 10. So a unit with combat value C hits with a chance of (11 minus C) in 10.

That one formula, plus the order of steps, is most of what you need. Values below are the standard units from the faction sheets. Your faction may have special units, so check your sheet.

## The odds per die

| Unit | Combat | Chance to hit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| War sun (needs its technology) | 3, three dice | 80% each | Sustain Damage |
| Dreadnought | 5 | 60% | Sustain Damage, Bombardment 5 |
| Cruiser | 7 | 40% | 50% once upgraded |
| Infantry | 8 | 30% | 40% once upgraded |
| Destroyer | 9 | 20% | Anti-Fighter Barrage 9 (two dice) |
| Carrier | 9 | 20% | Capacity 4 |
| Fighter | 9 | 20% | 30% once upgraded |

## The order of a tactical action's fights

1. **Space cannon offense.** After the active player moves ships in, every player with Space Cannon units in the active system may fire, starting with the active player and going clockwise (77.2). Other players must target the active player's ships.
2. **Space combat**, if two players have ships there. Each round:
   - **Anti-fighter barrage**, in the first round only (78.3). Hits can only destroy fighters.
   - **Announce retreats**, defender first. If the defender announces one, the attacker cannot.
   - **Roll dice.** One die per ship, the attacker first.
   - **Assign hits.** Use Sustain Damage to cancel hits first, then destroy one of your ships per remaining hit. You choose which of your own ships die.
   - **Retreat**, if announced. Retreating ships go to one adjacent system that holds your units or planets and no enemy ships, and you place a command token there.
3. **Invasion.** Bombard planets, commit ground forces, then **space cannon defense**: PDS units on the invaded planet fire at the landing ground forces. Then ground combat, in rounds, until one side is gone.

## Space cannon

A PDS hits on 6 with one die, so 50%. Two PDS on a planet, the most allowed (63.3), give a 75% chance of at least one hit. Space cannon fires even if no ships moved in, and a PDS with its upgrade can fire into adjacent systems.

Space cannon offense happens before combat, so its hits come off before the enemy fleet rolls a single die. A PDS on a border planet is a tax on every fleet that stops there. The Antimass Deflectors technology applies -1 to space cannon rolls against your units, which drops a PDS to 40%.

## Anti-fighter barrage

A basic destroyer rolls two dice at 9, an expected 0.4 fighters destroyed, and only a 36% chance of destroying at least one. The upgraded destroyer rolls three dice at 6: an expected 1.5 fighters, and an 87.5% chance of at least one. Against a fleet built on fighters, upgraded destroyers change the fight before it starts.

Barrage hits can only be assigned to fighters, and Sustain Damage cannot cancel them (87.4).

## Sustain Damage is worth a ship

A unit with Sustain Damage can cancel one hit, and it is then damaged and cannot do it again until the status phase repairs it. In practice, a dreadnought absorbs two hits before it dies. That is why dreadnoughts anchor fleets: for 4 resources you get a 60% die and two hits of protection, in one fleet pool slot.

## Value per resource

The fleet pool limits non-fighter ships in a system, and resources limit everything. Two simple measures help:

| Unit | Cost | Expected hits per resource | Hits absorbed per resource |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fighter (2 for 1) | 1 | 0.40 | 2 |
| Destroyer | 1 | 0.20 | 1 |
| Cruiser | 2 | 0.20 | 0.5 |
| Dreadnought | 4 | 0.15 | 0.5 |
| Carrier | 3 | 0.07 | 0.33 |

Fighters are the cheapest hits and the cheapest bodies, but they need capacity and die to barrage. Dreadnoughts are expensive per hit, but they make the most of a small fleet pool. Most strong fleets mix the two: a few heavy ships for the fleet pool, and fighters to soak hits.

## Ground combat

Infantry hit on 8, 30% per die. Ground combat repeats until one side is gone. If both sides are wiped out, the defender keeps the planet (49.5).

| Attacking infantry vs defending infantry | Attacker takes the planet |
| --- | --- |
| 2 vs 1 | 87% |
| 3 vs 2 | 81% |
| 4 vs 3 | 78% |
| 3 vs 3 | 48% |
| 5 vs 3 | 93% |

The pattern: bring two more than the defender for a near-sure invasion. And remember space cannon defense: each PDS on the planet may remove a landing infantry before ground combat begins.

## Bombardment

Before landing, a dreadnought may bombard: one die at 5, 60%, and each hit destroys a defending ground force. A planet with a PDS has Planetary Shield and cannot be bombarded, except by a war sun (65.3). Shields matter: they protect exactly the planets you most want to hold.

## When to attack

An attack spends a command token, and the fleet that fought stays put until the status phase. So the question is never only "will I win?" It is "is this worth a token and a stuck fleet?" Attack when the fight scores an objective, takes a planet someone needs, or removes a fleet that threatens your home. Otherwise, a fleet that sits and threatens often does more than one that fights.
