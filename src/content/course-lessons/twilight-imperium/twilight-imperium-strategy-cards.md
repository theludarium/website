Welcome to Level 2. At Level 1 you learned to play the rules right and finish the game. Now you start choosing on purpose, and the first choice of every round is the strategy card.

Every card has an initiative number from 1 to 8. Lower numbers act first in the action phase and score first in the status phase. Every card has a primary ability for its owner and a secondary ability that each other player can use, usually by spending a token from their strategy pool. The text below follows the Living Rules Reference, version 2.0, including its errata to Diplomacy. Thunder's Edge replaces Construction and Warfare with revised versions, so if you play with it, read those two cards from its box.

## The eight cards

| # | Card | Primary (owner) | Secondary (others) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Leadership | Gain 3 command tokens, then 1 more per 3 influence spent | 1 token per 3 influence spent, no strategy token needed |
| 2 | Diplomacy | Choose a system with your planet (not Mecatol Rex); others place a token there. Ready up to 2 planets | Ready up to 2 planets |
| 3 | Politics | Choose a new speaker (not the current one), draw 2 action cards, look at the top 2 agendas | Draw 2 action cards |
| 4 | Construction | Place a PDS or space dock, then a PDS if you like | Place a token in a system, then a PDS or space dock there |
| 5 | Trade | Gain 3 trade goods, refill commodities, let any players take the secondary free | Refill commodities |
| 6 | Warfare | Take one of your tokens off the board, then redistribute your tokens | Use one home-system space dock's production |
| 7 | Technology | Research 1 technology, and a second for 6 resources | Research 1 technology for 4 resources |
| 8 | Imperial | Score a public objective you meet; then 1 point if you hold Mecatol Rex, or draw a secret objective | Draw a secret objective |

## When each card is worth taking

**Leadership.** Command tokens are actions. Three free tokens is the equal of three extra turns of tactical actions or three more secondary abilities. It also goes first, which matters when two players want the same system. Many players take it in a round where they plan a lot of moves.

**Diplomacy.** Two jobs. Its token drop stops everyone else from activating one of your systems this round, which protects a home system or a key planet when a neighbor's fleet is close. Readying two planets is also extra spending money. It is a defensive card in a round where you expect trouble.

**Politics.** The speaker picks strategy cards first next round, votes last, and breaks ties. Politics lets you hand that seat to anyone except the current speaker, including yourself. The two action cards are good, and the agenda peek is useful once the agenda phase starts.

**Construction.** Space docks are where your units come from. An early second space dock in a rich system speeds up everything after it. PDS units protect planets with space cannon and block bombardment. Note the secondary costs you a command token placed in that system, which locks your ships there.

**Trade.** Three trade goods is real money, and you decide who else gets free commodities. That choice is a bargaining chip: players will offer deals to be included. The card tends to matter more to factions with high commodity values.

**Warfare.** Its primary pulls a command token back off the board. That lets a fleet that already moved this round move again, or frees a system you need to activate twice. It is the card for a big push.

**Technology.** Two technologies in one action, the second for 6 resources. Technology prerequisites build on each other, so falling behind early is hard to fix. Objectives also often ask for technologies of certain colors.

**Imperial.** The only card that scores points directly. Its primary lets you score a public objective during the action phase, separate from the status phase limit, and adds a point if you control Mecatol Rex. It is often the strongest card in the late game, and the last to act in turn order.

## How the pick works

The speaker picks first, then clockwise. With 3 or 4 players, everyone picks a second card in the same order, and your initiative is your lower card. Any card nobody picks gets a trade good on it, and those goods pile up round after round. An ignored card with three trade goods on it can be the best pick on the table.

A pick is also a denial. If one player is a point away from an objective that Imperial would score, taking Imperial yourself may be worth more than any other card. Reading which card each player needs is part of the skill.

## Secondaries are half the value

Following another player's card costs a strategy token. Your strategy pool holds only a few, and each token you put there is a token you cannot use for tactical actions. Pick the secondaries that matter to your plan, usually Technology, Leadership, and Imperial late in the game, and skip the rest without guilt.

## A rule worth knowing

You must use your card's primary before you can pass, and you can only do it once per round. Plan when to use it. Using Imperial late in the round lets you score an objective you completed earlier in that same action phase. Using Leadership early gives you more tokens to work with all round.
