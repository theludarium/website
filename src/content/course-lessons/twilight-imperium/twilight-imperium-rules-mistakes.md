Welcome to Level 1. Twilight Imperium is long enough that a small rules slip in round one can bend the whole game. The good news: the slips cluster in a few places. This list checks each one against the Living Rules Reference, version 2.0, with the rule numbers so you can look them up.

## Command tokens and movement

**Mistake: reading the fleet pool as a total.** The tokens in your fleet pool cap how many non-fighter ships you can have in each system, not across the galaxy (37.1). Fighters and ground forces count against your ships' capacity instead. If a system ever has too many ships, you remove the extras.

**Mistake: moving ships out of a system with your own token.** A ship cannot move if it starts in a system that holds one of your command tokens (58.4). It can move through such systems. This is why a fleet that just fought is stuck until the next round, unless something like the Warfare strategy card removes the token.

**Mistake: flying past enemy ships.** Ships cannot move through a system that contains another player's ships (58.4). Fighters count: the FAQ confirms fighters block movement.

**Mistake: picking up troops from a locked system.** Fighters and ground forces cannot be picked up from a system with your command token, other than the active system (95.3).

**Mistake: producing ships under a blockade.** You cannot produce ships in a system that holds other players' ships, though you can still produce ground forces (67.6).

**Mistake: forgetting that new planets arrive exhausted.** When you gain control of a planet, its card comes in exhausted (25.1). You cannot spend it until the status phase readies it.

## The action phase

**Mistake: passing early.** You cannot pass until you have used your strategy card's primary ability. In a 3- or 4-player game, you must use both of your cards first (3.4).

**Mistake: paying for the Leadership secondary.** Every strategy card's secondary costs a token from your strategy pool, except Leadership's (20.5).

**Mistake: trading with anyone, anytime.** Transactions happen during the active player's turn, at most one with each neighbor (94.1). Neighbors are players with units or planets in the same or adjacent systems. Only commodities, trade goods, promissory notes, and relic fragments can change hands, and at most one promissory note each way. During the agenda phase, anyone can trade with anyone, once per agenda.

**Mistake: spending your own commodities.** You cannot spend commodities. They become trade goods only when you give them to another player (21.7).

## Scoring

**Mistake: scoring two public objectives in one status phase.** Each status phase, you may score at most one public and one secret objective (61.6). In the action phase you can score any number of action-phase objectives, but only one during or after each combat.

**Mistake: scoring with a lost home planet.** You cannot score public objectives unless you control every planet in your home system (61.16). Defend home.

**Mistake: hoarding secret objectives.** You can have at most three secret objectives in total, scored and unscored together (61.21). Draw a fourth and you must return an unscored one.

**Mistake: thinking the custodians cost only influence.** To remove the custodians token, spend 6 influence and commit at least one ground force to land on Mecatol Rex in the same invasion (27.2). You gain 1 point, and the agenda phase begins that same round.

## Combat and abilities

**Mistake: firing anti-fighter barrage every round.** It happens only in the first round of a space combat (78.3).

**Mistake: using space cannon only on your turn.** Any player with a PDS in the active system can fire at the active player's ships after movement (77.2). A PDS on an invaded planet also fires at landing ground forces.

**Mistake: reading a 0 as zero.** The 0 on the ten-sided die is a 10, the best roll (FAQ).

**Mistake: sustaining damage twice.** A unit that uses Sustain Damage is damaged and cannot use it again until it is repaired in the status phase (87.3).

## Votes

**Mistake: voting with trade goods.** Trade goods can be spent as influence, but never to cast votes (8.13).

**Mistake: splitting votes.** All your votes on an agenda go to one outcome (8.7). The speaker votes last and breaks ties.

## A rules habit for the table

Keep the Living Rules Reference open on a phone or laptop. It is sorted by topic and numbered, so a question takes a minute to settle. Look rules up rather than vote on them: a quick lookup keeps a six-hour game fair.
