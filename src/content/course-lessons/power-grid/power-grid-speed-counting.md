The World Boardgaming Championships runs Power Grid in three-hour rounds. The 2026 preview expects most games to finish in under two and a half hours, and games still going at three hours are adjudicated, which the event wants to avoid. Five players, a new map, and every Elektro counted, in under two and a half hours. That takes a different kind of skill from playing well with no clock.

The good news: speed comes mostly from when you think, not how fast you think.

## Think on other players' turns

Each phase has long stretches where you are waiting. Use them.

- **During the auction,** while others bid on a plant you do not want, work out your fuel bill for each plant you might still buy.
- **During the fuel phase,** while players before you buy, plan your build. Their fuel buys rarely change your cities.
- **During the build phase,** while players before you build, price your route, and keep a backup in case someone takes your city.
- **During bureaucracy,** while the banker pays out, look at the plant market that is about to change and think about next round's auction.

By the time it is your turn, you should be checking a plan, not making one.

## Fuel shortcuts

Coal, oil, and garbage sit three to a space. That makes fuel bills quick:

- **A full space of three costs three times its price.** Three coal at 4 is 12.
- **Count spaces, not markers.** "Two from the 3 space and all three at 4" is 6 plus 12, which is 18.
- **Uranium is one per space,** so add the prices directly.

Say the bill out loud as you take the markers. Others can check it as you go.

## Route shortcuts

The [route math lesson](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-building-costs/) has the method. For speed:

- **Price from the network outward.** Start with the nearest target and route the next from it.
- **Group the space costs.** "Three cities, one at 10 and two at 15, is 40, plus lines of 6, 5, and 0." Then add.
- **Show the route.** Set the new houses beside the board, point along the route, and name the total. At WBC this is the expected habit, and a route paid as named stands even if a cheaper one existed.

## Know the payout card by heart

Bureaucracy is quick when nobody looks anything up. Learn a few anchors: 5 powered pays 64, 10 pays 105, 15 pays 134, 20 pays 150. The steps between them shrink by one every two cities, as the [payout card lesson](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-payout-table/) shows. From the anchors you can find any row in a second.

## The banker's two minutes

Bureaucracy can drag. A clean routine:

1. **Payouts,** in player order, each player naming their powered cities and taking the cash.
2. **Refill,** fuel by fuel, from the card column, read out loud.
3. **Plant market,** last.

With practice it takes two minutes. Without one, it can take ten.

## Calculators

The WBC preview allows calculators if other players can see the screen, and only for adding, not for taking notes. That is a sensible rule at home too. A calculator saves time on long routes and big fuel buys. It does not replace knowing roughly what the answer should be.

## Where time goes

Most slow games lose time in the same places:

- **Opening a plant.** The leader stares at the market. Fix: decide during bureaucracy.
- **Big builds.** A player prices five cities on their turn. Fix: price them during the fuel phase.
- **Arguments about route cost.** Fix: show the route before paying.
- **The final round.** Everyone recounts. Fix: run the [endgame count](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-endgame-strategy/) a round early.

## Practice with a timer

Play a few games where you track time per round. For five players, aim to finish inside two and a half hours. If a round runs long, note which phase and who was thinking. Then fix that habit, whether it is yours or the table's.

Fast is not the goal. Clean decisions are. But a clock shows quickly who has a plan before their turn, and that is the skill tournament players build.
