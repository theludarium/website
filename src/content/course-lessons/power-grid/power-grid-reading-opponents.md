Strong Power Grid players seem to know what everyone will do. They are not guessing. Power Grid puts almost all of a company on display, and the few hidden things can be estimated. This lesson is a routine for reading the table, and the moves it opens up.

## What is public

- **Plants.** Face up, with fuel type, fuel needed, and cities powered.
- **Stored fuel.** It sits on each player's plants.
- **Cities.** On the board and on the cities track.
- **Turn order.** On the turn order track.
- **What they paid.** Every bid, fuel purchase, and build happens in the open.

## Money: open or hidden?

The German Recharged rulebook does not say whether players' money must be hidden, so tables differ. Agree before the game. If money is open, ask. If it is hidden, estimate it. You can get close.

## Estimating a rival's cash

Everyone starts with 50. After that, money comes from one place and goes to three:

- **In:** the payout card, by cities powered. You can see how many cities they power each bureaucracy.
- **Out:** plant bids, fuel, and cities. You watch every one.

A quick way to keep it: estimate each rival's cash at the end of bureaucracy, when everyone has just been paid. That is the number that matters for the next auction. Round to the nearest 5. Being within 10 is enough to know who can outbid you.

## Capacity and need

For each rival, work out three numbers:

1. **Cities owned.**
2. **Capacity:** cities their plants can power on full fuel.
3. **Fuel short:** markers they must buy to run every plant this round.

Then read the gaps:

- **Cities above capacity:** they need a plant. Expect them to bid hard on anything that closes the gap.
- **Capacity above cities:** they will build this round. Expect them in the cities near their network.
- **Fuel short on a scarce fuel:** they are exposed. If you buy before them, you can raise their bill.

## Predicting the auction

Put the numbers together before the leader opens:

- Who **must** buy? Players with a capacity gap.
- Who **can** pay the most? Your cash estimate.
- Who will **avoid** which plants? Players already burning a crowded fuel will look elsewhere.

Now you can choose what to open. If the player who must buy cannot afford the big plant, opening it may win it for you cheap. If they can, opening it drains them before you open the one you want. The [auction lesson](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-auction-strategy/) covers both moves.

## Predicting the build

Build order is reverse turn order, so the trailing players build first. Before your build, look at the players who build after you and ask:

- Which cities are cheapest for them to add?
- Do any of those cities sit in your path?

If a city is cheap for you and important to them, take it. In Step 1, it blocks them. In Steps 2 and 3, it fills a space they wanted, or forces them to pay the higher space.

## Reading intent

Some signals carry meaning:

- **A player who stops building** while cash piles up is saving. Often for a big final round. Count their finish.
- **A player who stores double** of a fuel expects it to get expensive, or plans to starve someone.
- **A player who skips the auction** expects a better market next round, or needs every Elektro for cities.

None of these is certain. Each one is a question worth asking.

## Keeping it fair and fast

Reading opponents is about public information. Ask questions about what is on the table any time, and answer them for others. Counting a rival's cities or plants out loud is fine. The [WBC table habits](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-tournaments/) also discourage advising other players on their moves, which is a good line for home games too.

## The routine

Before each auction:

1. Cities, capacity, fuel short, and cash for each rival.
2. Who must buy, and who can pay.
3. What will the players after me buy and build?

It takes a minute, and gets faster every game. It is the habit that turns the [endgame count](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-endgame-strategy/) into something you do every round, not just at the end.
