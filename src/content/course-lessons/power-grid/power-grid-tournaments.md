Power Grid is a long game with a lot of arithmetic, which makes it a natural tournament game. Every number is on the table, and the best players are the ones who count it fastest and most carefully. You do not need to enter a tournament to enjoy that. But knowing how the serious tables play changes how you see your own.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is the annual tournament convention of the Boardgame Players Association, held at Seven Springs Mountain Resort in Pennsylvania. Many games crown a champion there each summer, and Power Grid has been one of them since 2004. WBC 2027 is scheduled for July 24 to August 1, 2027.

The event's [history page](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/pgd.html) lists every winner. Steven LeWinter won in 2024 and 2025, and the 2025 event drew 74 players. Jim Savarick has won it five times and leads the event's all-time laurels. The [2026 results](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html) list Edward Povilaitis as the Power Grid champion.

## How the WBC event runs

From the [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/pgd.html):

- **Format:** three heats, a semifinal, and a final. Players are ranked by wins, then by points for each finish, so playing more heats helps.
- **Tables:** five players by preference, four only when needed.
- **Time:** three-hour rounds, with most games expected to finish in under two and a half.
- **Rules:** Recharged rules on every map, without the preset start cities variant.
- **Maps:** each round has its own maps. In 2026 the heats offered South Africa or Bremen, the United Kingdom or the Middle East, and Manhattan or Northern Europe. The semifinal was Brazil and the final was Spain and Portugal. Any map is allowed if the whole table agrees.
- **Who wins a game:** most cities powered, then most money, then most cities, then the highest plant number.

The event is listed for experienced players only, but the preview also offers a demo to learn the game. If you have played the base game a few times, you can get ready from there.

## Table habits worth copying

The WBC preview describes a few table habits. They make home games faster and fairer too.

- **Play your own game.** The event discourages advising other players on bids, fuel, or where to build.
- **Undo only before new information.** You can take back an action until another player reveals something, such as their own purchase.
- **Calculators in the open.** A calculator is fine if everyone can see it is only adding.
- **Show your route.** Before paying for cities, set the houses aside, show the route and the cost, and let others check it. An inefficient route is paid as chosen.

The last habit alone saves arguments at any table. Route math is where most counting mistakes happen.

## Championships beyond WBC

We could not find a publisher-run championship series or national qualifier for Power Grid. The WBC event, held every year since 2004 apart from a gap in 2020 and 2021, is the long-running tournament we could confirm. Conventions near you may run their own Power Grid events, so check their schedules.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights. Ask the staff whether anyone brings Power Grid.
- **Board game meetups.** Libraries and community centers often host them. Our guide to [finding a public game night](/articles/board-game-night-near-me/) shows where to look.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play five-player games.** That is the tournament table, and it is where turn order and fuel fights are sharpest.
- **Rotate maps.** WBC changes maps every round. Each new map teaches you to read fuel and space from scratch.
- **Play on time.** Aim for two and a half hours. It forces faster counting and cleaner decisions.
- **Review the last round.** After each game, check what you could power, what you could afford, and who had more money if you tied.
