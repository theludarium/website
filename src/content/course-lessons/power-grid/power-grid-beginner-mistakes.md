Your first three games of Power Grid are where habits form. Some habits are rules played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that leave you short of money at the worst moment. This lesson covers both, checked against the current Recharged rulebook.

## Rules slips in the markets

**Overfilling plants.** A plant stores at most twice the fuel it burns. A plant that burns 2 oil can hold 4 oil, and no more. You can never buy fuel you have nowhere to store. Hybrid plants take coal, oil, or a mix. Eco plants burn nothing and store nothing.

**Running a plant on part of its fuel.** A plant burns exactly the fuel shown, once per round, or it does not run. You cannot burn half the fuel to power half the cities, and storing double does not let it run twice.

**Picking from the future market.** Only the 4 plants in the current market can go up for auction. The future market is a preview.

**Forgetting the discount token.** At the start of every auction phase, the smallest current plant gets the token and opens at 1 Elektro. If nobody buys it, it leaves the game at the end of the phase. If a newly drawn plant is smaller than the discounted one while the token is still out, that new plant leaves the game at once, the token comes off, and you draw again.

**Refilling the cheap spaces.** In bureaucracy, new fuel fills the most expensive empty spaces first. Burned fuel goes to the supply, not back onto the market. Get this backward and fuel stays cheap all game.

**Bidding in turn order.** Players choose plants in turn order, but bids go around the table clockwise.

## Rules slips on the map

**Building in a full city.** In Step 1, a city holds one house. In Step 2 it holds two, and in Step 3 three. Each company can be in a city only once.

**Paying for the wrong space.** You always take the cheapest open space in a city. If a city is empty in Step 2, you pay 10, not 15.

**Owning a route.** Connections are not yours. Every new city pays the full route from your network, even along lines you paid for before. When you build several cities in one turn, you may route from a city you just built.

**Skipping the reverse order.** Fuel and building both start with the player in last place. Getting this backward hands the leader the cheapest fuel and the best cities.

## Old-edition habits

If someone at the table learned an older edition, watch for these. In Recharged:

- The starting market is a random 8 of plants 03 to 15.
- Every game uses the discount token. The classic edition had none, and Power Grid Deluxe used it only on its North America map.
- A player's city count no longer removes small plants from the market.
- Two players use special rules with a rival company, covered in [lesson 7](/academy/power-grid/power-grid-expansions/).

## The end-of-game misread

The winner is the player who **powers** the most cities in the final bureaucracy, not the one who connects the most. The player who triggers the end does not always win, since they may have built more cities than their plants can run. Ties go to the most money left, so every Elektro counts until the last moment.

## The big strategic mistake: racing to the front

New players see the cities track as a scoreboard and push to lead it early. But player order punishes the leader. The leader opens the auction with the least information, then buys fuel and builds last, after prices rise and the good cities are gone.

You do not need to fall far behind. Just treat the lead as something that costs money, and take it when it pays, not by habit. Our article on [catch-up mechanics](/articles/catch-up-mechanics-board-games/) explains why this rule makes the game so close.

## The three legs of the company

A Power Grid company stands on three legs: plant capacity, fuel, and cities. New players overbuild one.

- **Cities without capacity** earn nothing extra. Powering 5 of 8 cities pays the same as owning 5.
- **Capacity without cities** is a plant you paid for and cannot use.
- **Plants without fuel** sit dark, and fuel can run out.

## Spending every Elektro

The World Boardgaming Championships preview for Power Grid says players constantly come up "one dollar short." New players feel this more. They win a plant in a bidding war, then cannot afford fuel for it, or they build three cities and have nothing left to power them.

Before you bid, add up what you need for fuel this round and at least one city. That number is your floor. Bid above it only for a plant that changes your game.

## Starting in the crowd

In Step 1, every city holds one house. Start next to two rivals and you will pay long routes around their houses for the rest of the game. Look for open space with cheap connections, and remember you do not have to build at all in round 1.

## A checklist for game three

Before you finish each round, ask:

1. Can my plants power every city I own, with fuel on hand?
2. What will I need to spend on fuel next round, at today's prices?
3. Am I leading because I chose to, or by accident?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong in the first three games.
