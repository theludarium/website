Kingdomino ends fast. Twelve rounds with three or four players, six with two, and the last few go by in a blink. Many games are won or lost in those rounds, by players who keep drafting as if the game will go on. This lesson covers what changes at the end and how to squeeze the last points out of your kingdom.

## How the ending works

When the draw pile runs out, the last line has been laid out. Players take their normal turns: place the domino under their king, then move their king onto the last line. Then everyone takes one final turn, placing that last domino, and no new line follows.

So the last pick you make is from the last line, and the domino you take there is the last piece you place.

## The last pick is different

Every pick in Kingdomino buys two things: a domino and a place in the next turn order. The last pick buys only the domino. There is no next round, so the low number buys nothing.

That flips a habit. All game, a low number had hidden value. On the last line, take the domino that scores the most for you, full stop. The number only matters if two dominoes score the same, and even then it does not.

One round earlier, the trade is also lopsided. The pick before the last decides who chooses first from the final line. That is worth something if the final line will have a domino you need, and you cannot see the final line yet. Use what you have counted (lesson 6) to judge how likely that is.

## Fit beats value

By the last three rounds, your kingdom has a shape. Most of the frame is fixed, and the empty squares sit in specific places. A domino is only worth what it scores in those places.

Before each late pick:

1. **List your empty squares.** Count them, and note which pairs of neighbors could take a domino.
2. **List what each pair could take.** Which terrain on each side would join a crowned territory?
3. **Price each domino in the line in your actual spaces,** not in an ideal kingdom.

A three-crown domino that has no legal spot is a discard worth zero. A plain double that drops into your two-square gap beside a 4-crown forest is worth 8.

## The final placement

Your last placement deserves a full look, because you have no future to plan for. Two traps:

- **The forced placement.** If a domino can be placed legally, you must place it, even where it hurts. A domino you would rather discard may still have to go somewhere, perhaps splitting a territory or filling a space you hoped to use differently. Check where it must go before you pick it.
- **The bonus check.** If you are playing Harmony, filling your last gap is worth the domino's points plus 5. If you are playing Middle Kingdom, make sure your frame really leaves the castle centered. Both are easy to miscount at the end.

## Hate drafting at the end

In the last round, reading the table is at its sharpest, because every rival's needs are concrete. If the leader has one gap left beside a big crowned territory, and only one domino in the final line fits it, taking that domino can decide the game. With two players, it often does.

Check the order: you can only take a domino from players who pick after you. If you pick last on the final line, plan for whatever is left.

## Winning the tiebreakers

Kingdomino scores are often close. The rulebook breaks ties by:

1. **The biggest single territory,** counted in squares, crowned or not.
2. **The most crowns** in the whole kingdom.
3. **A shared victory** if both are equal.

In a tight game, these are worth planning for. If you and a rival are within a few points, adding a plain square to your biggest territory can win a tie even when another domino would score one point more. Count both kingdoms before the last pick.

## A last-three-rounds routine

- **Three rounds out:** fix your frame, list your gaps, and note which rich dominoes are still to come.
- **Two rounds out:** pick for fit first, and ask whether first choice of the final line is likely to matter.
- **Last pick:** take the highest-scoring domino for your spaces, or the one that denies the leader the game.
- **Last placement:** check legality, bonuses, and tiebreakers before you put it down.
