Kingdomino is one of the friendliest teaches on any shelf. The danger is not hard rules. It is a host who explains the draft order, the 5 by 5 limit, and every tiebreaker before anyone has touched a domino. Teach the shape of a turn, show one scoring example, and let the first round do the rest.

Here is a script in the order you would say it, then the rules to settle live and a few notes for kids. Print the [Kingdomino teach sheet](/academy/kingdomino/teach-sheet/) for the middle of the table. Our method for [teaching any game in five minutes](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) fits this game well.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up everything except the kings. Give each player a starting tile with their castle on it, shuffle the dominoes number side up into the box, and remove the right number for your player count: keep 24 for two players, 36 for three, all 48 for four. Lay out the first line, lowest number by the box, and flip it face up.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "We are each building a kingdom out of land, in a 5 by 5 square around our castle. Matching land joins up into territories. Crowns make a territory score. Most points at the end wins."

Hold up a domino with a crown on it and point at the crown. That picture does more than a paragraph.

## Minute 2: placing a domino

Say:

1. "Each domino has two squares of land."
2. "A new one has to touch your castle, or touch the same land you already have, side by side. Only one end needs to match."
3. "Everything has to fit in a 5 by 5 square, castle included. If a domino cannot fit, you throw it away."

Place one domino next to a castle to show it. Then place a second one, matching forest to forest, and say "only this end has to match."

## Minute 3: scoring

Build a tiny example in front of everyone: three forest squares in a row with one crown among them. Say: "This forest is 3 squares with 1 crown, so it scores 3. Add a crown and it scores 6. Add a square and it scores 4. Land with no crown scores nothing, however big it is."

That last sentence saves more first games than any other.

## Minute 4: picking dominoes

Point at the line. Say: "On your turn, you place the domino your king is sitting on, then move your king to a domino in the next line. That is what you will place next round."

Then the line that makes the draft click: "The lower the number, the earlier you go next round. Big numbers usually have more crowns. Small numbers get you first pick next time."

## Minute 5: start

Take all the kings in your hand, mix them, and draw them one at a time. Each owner places their king on any free domino in the first line. Lay out the second line beside it, then say "Lowest number goes first" and point at that player.

## Teach the rest live

Explain each of these the first time it comes up. All come from the rulebook.

- **Corners do not count.** A domino touching only at a corner is not connected.
- **The castle is wild.** Anything can touch the starting tile, so it is the safe spot for an awkward domino.
- **You must place a domino if you legally can,** even if you would rather not. Discarding is only for dominoes that cannot fit.
- **The castle does not have to stay in the middle.** Grow in any direction; just keep the whole kingdom inside 5 by 5.
- **The last round.** When the last line comes out, players pick from it as usual, then place those dominoes in one final turn without picking again.
- **Ties.** The biggest single territory wins, then the most crowns, then a shared win.

## Teaching kids

Kingdomino says 8 and up on the box, and it works well with younger players who can match pictures. A few tips:

- **Skip the draft explanation.** Let them pick what they like for a game or two. The turn order idea arrives on its own.
- **Help with the 5 by 5.** Lay a light outline of 25 spaces, or count rows together out loud.
- **Score together,** one territory at a time, and let them do the multiplying they can.
- **Play the Middle Kingdom bonus off at first.** It adds a rule without adding fun for beginners.

## The one bit of strategy worth sharing

New players chase the prettiest domino or the one with the most crowns. Share one idea, as a tip and not an order: "One big territory with a few crowns beats lots of small ones." It is the scoring rule seen from the other side, and it turns a scattered first kingdom into a plan.
