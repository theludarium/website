Kingdomino plays 2 to 4 players, as printed on the box. The Age of Giants expansion raises that to 5, and for two players the rulebook offers the 7 by 7 Mighty Duel.

Kingdomino has grown into a whole family: an expansion, two standalone sequels, a roll-and-write, and a children's game. Before any of them, though, the rulebook in the base box already offers four ways to change the game. Start there.

## How many players

| Players | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Two kings each and 24 dominoes in the original rules, or the Mighty Duel with all 48 |
| 3 | One king each; 36 dominoes and three per line in the original rules, or all 48 with a discard in the 2024 rules |
| 4 | One king each, all 48 dominoes, four per line |
| 5 | Needs Age of Giants, which adds the fifth player's pieces |

Lesson 10 covers two and three players in detail.

## The variants in the base box

The original rulebook lists these as additional rules, and says you can mix them as you like.

### Dynasty

Play three games in a row and add up the scores. Highest total wins. Dynasty takes the sting out of a single bad draw and rewards steady play. It is a good format for an evening, or for a small tournament at home.

### The Middle Kingdom

Score 10 bonus points if your castle is in the center of your kingdom at the end. The 2024 rulebook adds that the bonus still counts if your kingdom is incomplete, as long as the castle is in the center of the 5 by 5 frame. Middle Kingdom forces you to fix your frame from the first domino, so it rewards planning. Lesson 7 covers the layout.

### Harmony

Score 5 bonus points for a complete kingdom with no discarded dominoes. It turns the last rounds into a puzzle: fit becomes worth more, and greed is punished.

### The Mighty Duel

For two players: use all 48 dominoes and build a 7 by 7. Lesson 11 covers it in full.

### Mixing them

A popular combination is Middle Kingdom with Harmony: 15 points for a centered, complete kingdom. It makes the layout matter as much as the crowns, and it is a fine standard once your table knows the base game.

## Age of Giants

[Age of Giants](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07FHRW5QL?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is an expansion for both Kingdomino and Queendomino, released in 2018. It adds giants, which cover crowns in a kingdom and cancel them until they are passed on, along with quest tiles that offer new ways to score. It also adds pieces for a fifth player.

Choose it if your group plays the base game often and wants more interaction and more ways to score.

## Queendomino

[Queendomino](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076N3VQJG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a standalone game from 2017 by the same designer. It keeps the domino draft and the kingdom building and adds coins, buildings to buy, knights that collect taxes, a dragon, and a queen. It is a heavier game, with more decisions per turn. Combined with Kingdomino, it seats 3 or 4 players on 7 by 7 kingdoms, 5 or 6 players on 5 by 5 kingdoms, or 6 or 8 players in teams.

Choose it if your group has outgrown the base game and wants more to manage in the same short frame.

## Kingdomino Origins

[Kingdomino Origins](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09GXXMVYN?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a standalone sequel from 2021, set in prehistoric times. It builds on the same system and comes with three ways to play that add new elements step by step, so a family can grow into it. The first adds fire and volcanoes, which make territories score. The second adds wooden resources. The third adds cavemen, which you bring in with those resources and which score by where they stand.

Choose it if you want a fresh box with the same feel, with room to add more as the table gets comfortable.

## Kingdomino Duel

[Kingdomino Duel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07X3Y4J8W?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a two-player roll-and-write from 2019. Instead of dominoes, you roll dice and draw your kingdom on a sheet. It is small, travels well, and plays quickly.

Choose it for travel, or for a couple who wants a quick game without the full box.

## Dragomino

[Dragomino](https://link.amazon/B09WKyU19) is the family's game for young children, built on the same tile-placing idea. It won the Kinderspiel des Jahres, Germany's children's game of the year award, in 2021. Blue Orange lists it for 2 to 4 players, ages 5 and up.

Choose it if your kids are too young for the base game. Our guide to [board games for 5-year-olds](/articles/board-games-for-5-year-olds/) has more picks for that age.

## The Court

Blue Orange released The Court in 2020 as a free print-and-play expansion for the base game, which adds resources, buildings, and court characters to recruit. It works with Kingdomino or Queendomino. Check Blue Orange's site for how it is offered today.
