Kingdomino plays two to four, and it is a different game at each count. With four players, it is a lively scramble where you control one pick in four. With two, it is a tight duel where you control half of every line. This lesson covers the rules for two and three players in both editions of the rulebook, and what to change in your play.

## Two players: the rules

In the original 2016 rules:

- **Each player takes two kings** of their color.
- **Use 24 dominoes,** chosen at random. Set the other 24 aside unseen.
- **Lay out four dominoes per line,** as in a four-player game.
- **When kings are first placed,** each player places two kings, one for each of their picks.
- **Each round, you place and pick twice,** once for each king, in line order.
- **The game runs 6 rounds,** and each player builds a 5 by 5 from 12 dominoes, just as in a four-player game.

The 2024 Blue Orange Edition rulebook makes the 7 by 7 Mighty Duel its two-player game, with all 48 dominoes. Lesson 11 covers it. Board Game Arena offers both.

## Two players: how it plays

### You control half of every line

With two kings each, every line of four splits two and two. Your two picks interact: your first choice affects what your second king can take, and the order of your kings in the line sets when you pick next round.

A useful way to think about it: each round, you decide how to split your two kings between the front and back of the next line. Two low numbers mean you pick first and second next round, with two weaker tiles now. Two high numbers mean better tiles now and the last two picks next round, when your rival will have taken their pick of the line. A split, one low and one high, is often the balanced play.

### Denial is a full swing

With one rival, every point you deny them is a point of lead. Lesson 8's hate draft math is at its strongest here: a domino worth 4 to you and 12 to your rival is an 8-point swing in your favor.

### Half the box is missing

Only 24 dominoes come out. Each specific domino has a 1 in 2 chance of being in the game at all, so you cannot count on any particular prize. Lesson 6's rule applies: the chance an unseen domino still comes is the pile left divided by everything unseen. Plans that need one specific domino are risky. Plans with several possible pieces are sound.

## Three players: the rules

The two rulebook editions handle three players differently.

- **Original 2016 rules:** use 36 dominoes chosen at random, and lay out three dominoes per line. Each player has one king. The game runs 12 rounds.
- **2024 Blue Orange Edition rules:** use all 48 dominoes and lay out four per line. Each player has one king, and the domino nobody takes is discarded each round. The game also runs 12 rounds.

Board Game Arena uses the four-domino line with a discard. Agree on your rule before you start; the two feel different.

## Three players: how it plays

### With three dominoes a line

The last player each round has no choice, just as in a four-player game. A quarter of the box is missing, so counting is about odds, not certainty. This version plays close to the four-player game, with a bit more room for each player.

### With four dominoes and a discard

Every player has a choice every round, even the last, who picks between two. The discarded domino is the one nobody wanted, and that tells you something about every kingdom at the table. All 48 dominoes show up, so you can count with certainty: everything not yet seen is still to come.

This version rewards counting and reading the table more than any other three-player setup. It is the one to play if your group likes to plan.

## Four players, for comparison

With four players, you control one pick in four, the last player each round takes what is left, and all 48 dominoes come out. It is the most chaotic count, and the friendliest for new players, because there are fewer moments where a single read decides the game.

## What to change at each count

| Count | Main skill | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two | Splitting your two kings, denial | Planning around one domino that may not be in the box |
| Three, 3 per line | Turn order, odds | Treating it like a four-player count |
| Three, 4 with discard | Counting, reading the discard | Ignoring what the discard says about rivals |
| Four | Fast pricing, flexible plans | Overthinking picks you cannot control |
