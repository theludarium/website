Cascadia is a calm game about building your own corner of the Pacific Northwest. Each player grows a landscape of hexagon habitat tiles and fills it with wildlife: bears, elk, salmon, hawks, and foxes. Nobody attacks anybody. The tension lives in the shared display in the middle of the table, where every pick you make changes what the next player can take.

It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2022, and part of its charm is easy to feel: on almost every turn, you can do something useful, even when the offer is not quite what you wanted. This lesson covers the rules in the current English rulebook from AEG and Flatout Games, in the same order the rulebook uses.

## What is in the box?

- **85 habitat tiles**, 25 of them keystone tiles. There are five habitats: mountains, forests, prairies, wetlands, and rivers.
- **5 starter habitat tiles**, each a cluster of three hexes.
- **100 wildlife tokens**, 20 each of bear, elk, salmon, hawk, and fox, plus a cloth bag to draw them from.
- **21 wildlife scoring cards**: 4 for each animal, lettered A to D, and 1 card for the Family and Intermediate variants.
- **25 nature tokens**, shaped like Douglas fir cones, and a scorepad.

## How do you set up?

![Cascadia setup: five scoring cards in a row, face-down habitat tile stacks, a display of four tiles each paired with a wildlife token, the cloth bag, the nature tokens, and a starter habitat tile for each player](/academy/cascadia/setup.svg)

1. Put all the wildlife tokens in the bag and shake it.
2. Count out habitat tiles at random, without looking: 43 for two players, 63 for three, 83 for four. That is 20 per player, plus 3. Shuffle them into face-down stacks and put the rest back in the box.
3. Pick one scoring card at random for each of the five animals and lay them where everyone can read them. For a first game, the rulebook recommends the A cards.
4. Deal each player one starter habitat tile at random, face up in front of them.
5. Reveal 4 habitat tiles in the middle of the table. Draw 4 wildlife tokens from the bag and pair one with each tile, in order. This is the display.
6. Put the nature tokens within reach. The player who most recently saw one of the game's animals goes first, or pick someone at random.

Your starter tile, and everything you add to it, is your **environment**.

## What do you do on your turn?

Two steps, then a refill.

**1. Pick a tile and a token.** Before anything else, check the display for overpopulation:

- If all four tokens show the same animal, they are wiped automatically. Set them aside, draw four new tokens one at a time to pair with the tiles, then return the old ones to the bag. This can happen more than once in a turn.
- If exactly three match, you may choose to wipe those three the same way. You can only do this once per turn.

Then take one pair as it sits: a tile and the token beside it.

**2. Place both in your environment**, in either order.

- The **tile** must touch at least one side of a tile already in your environment. You cannot place it on top of another tile, and you never move tiles once placed. Habitats do not have to match, though matching edges matter for scoring.
- The **token** goes on any tile in your environment that shows that animal and has no token yet. It does not have to be the tile you just took. Each tile holds at most one token, ever.
- If no tile can take the token, or you would rather not place it, return it to the bag.

**Refill.** Replace the tile from the top of any face-down stack and the token from the bag. Do not slide the other pairs; just fill the gap. Play passes clockwise.

## What are nature tokens for?

A keystone tile shows a single habitat and a single animal. When you place that animal's token on it, take a nature token.

Before you pick, you may spend a nature token to do one of two things:

- take **any** one tile and **any** one token from the display, even if they are not paired, or
- wipe **any number** of tokens and replace them from the bag.

There is no limit to how many you spend in a turn. Any you keep are worth 1 point each at the end.

## How does the game end?

If, at the end of a turn, the stacks are empty and the tile just taken cannot be replaced, the game ends right away. Because of the tile count, everyone plays exactly 20 turns.

## How does scoring work?

The scorepad has four parts.

1. **Wildlife.** Score each animal by its card. Bears score in groups, elk in lines or shapes, salmon in runs, hawks spread apart, and foxes by their neighbors. The [openings lesson](/academy/cascadia/cascadia-strategy-openings/) at Level 2 walks through all twenty cards.
2. **Habitat corridors.** For each habitat, find your largest connected group of it. Tiles connect when matching habitat edges touch. Score 1 point per tile in that group.
3. **Corridor bonuses.** Compare each habitat's largest group around the table.
   - Three or four players: the largest gets 3 points and the second largest 1. If two tie for largest, each gets 2 and nobody scores second. If three or four tie for largest, each gets 1. A tie for second scores nothing.
   - Two players: the largest gets 2 points, and a tie gives 1 each.
4. **Nature tokens.** 1 point for each one you did not spend.

Most points wins. If players tie, the one with the most nature tokens wins. If they are still tied, they share the victory.
