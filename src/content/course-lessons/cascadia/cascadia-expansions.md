Cascadia stays fresh longer than most games its size, because the scoring cards change what you are building every game. Before reaching for an expansion, it is worth knowing how much is already in the [base game](https://www.alderac.com/cascadia/): a solo mode, two simpler variants for families, and a whole achievement system with scenarios. This lesson covers all of it, then the products in the wider Cascadia family.

## How does the game change by player count?

At every count, each player takes exactly 20 turns. What changes is the tile count, the bonuses, and how much the display moves between your turns.

- **Two players:** 43 tiles. The largest corridor in each habitat earns 2 points, and a tie earns 1 each. Only one pair leaves the display between your turns, so you can plan a little further, and every pick you leave goes straight to your one opponent.
- **Three or four players:** 63 or 83 tiles. The largest corridor earns 3 and the second largest 1. Three pairs can leave the display before your next turn at four players, so forgiving cards and flexible tiles gain value.

If your group likes a quiet, planning-heavy puzzle, two players suits it. If it likes a busier display and corridor races, play at three or four.

## Solo mode

Solo uses the two-player setup with one starter habitat tile, and the tile stacks sit to the left of the display. Your turn works as usual, with one change before you refill:

1. Discard the tile and the token farthest from the stacks. They leave the game.
2. Slide the remaining two tiles and two tokens away from the stacks.
3. Draw two new tiles and two new tokens to fill the display.

That steady drift means you can see what is about to leave, and you can plan around it. You still play exactly 20 turns. Habitat bonuses work differently: 2 points for each habitat where your largest corridor is 7 or more. The rulebook's rating chart starts at 60 points and tops out at 110 or more.

## The Family and Intermediate variants

Both use the Family and Intermediate scoring card in place of the five wildlife cards.

**Family variant.** Every animal scores the same way: by the size of each group of that animal, in any shape. A single animal scores 2, a group of two scores 5, and a group of three or more scores 9. Groups of the same animal may not touch. You may skip the habitat bonuses if you want an even simpler count.

**Intermediate variant.** The same rules, using the Intermediate side of the card, which scores group sizes differently.

The Family variant is the best way to play with younger kids or very new players. It keeps the drafting and the placement puzzle, and drops the reading.

## Scenarios and achievements

The rulebook ends with an achievement system shaped like a hiking map, where up to five players track progress toward the title of Cascadian biologist. There are three ways to earn achievements:

- **Scenarios:** fifteen set challenges, each with a fixed card set, a target score from 80 to 100, and in many cases up to three more goals. They get harder as you go, and they work solo or with a group.
- **Normal games:** twenty-five achievements, such as reaching a score or winning several habitat majorities. The winner of a game may fill in one.
- **Rule restrictions:** ten twists on the normal rules, such as a display of only three pairs.

The scenarios are the best practice in the box. They force you to play card sets you would not choose.

## Cascadia: Landmarks

[Cascadia: Landmarks](https://www.alderac.com/cascadia-landmarks/) is the expansion for the base game. Per AEG, it adds habitat tiles, keystone tiles, starter habitat tiles, wildlife tokens, and nature tokens, enough to play with five or six. It also adds fifteen new wildlife scoring cards and a Landmarks module, where you place natural landmarks in your habitats for end-game bonuses. It plays 1 to 6 in about 45 to 60 minutes.

## Cascadia Rolling

[Cascadia: Rolling Hills](https://www.alderac.com/cascadia-rolling-hills/) and [Cascadia: Rolling Rivers](https://www.alderac.com/cascadia-rolling-rivers/) are standalone flip-and-roll-and-write games in the Cascadia style. You roll dice, collect wildlife, and complete habitat cards. Each plays 1 to 4 in 15 to 30 minutes, and Flatout Games notes that owning both lets up to eight play. They suit travel and big groups.

## Cascadia Junior

[Cascadia Junior](https://www.alderac.com/cascadia-junior/) is a standalone, simpler version for families with young children. It keeps the spatial puzzle and teaches pattern building, set collection, and simple addition.

## How to choose

- **New to the game:** the base box, A cards first, then random cards.
- **Playing with young kids:** the Family variant first, and Cascadia Junior for the youngest.
- **Playing alone:** solo mode, then the fifteen scenarios in order.
- **Five or six players:** Landmarks.
- **Short on time or table space:** Cascadia Rolling.
