The [tournaments lesson](/academy/cascadia/cascadia-tournaments/) covered where competitive Cascadia happens and how the World Boardgaming Championships event runs. This lesson turns that format into a way of playing, then gives you six weeks of practice to get ready.

The details below come from the [WBC 2026 Cascadia preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/cas.html). Check the current year's preview before any event; formats change.

## Winners only changes everything

The 2026 heats were played "Heats: Winners Only." Only the player who finishes first at a table advances from that heat. All heat winners go on to the quarterfinal, which takes the top 64 players who have won at least one game.

At home, a solid 95 that finishes second feels like a good game. In a winners-only heat, it is the same as finishing last. That changes three things.

**Watch the leader, not the field.** In a four-player game you would normally ignore most of what you leave in the display. In a heat, glance at the leading rival's environment before every pick. The pair they most want is worth denying when it costs you little, the way the [two-player lesson](/academy/cascadia/two-player-cascadia-strategy/) taught.

**Take the bigger risk when you trail.** If you are clearly behind at the midpoint, a safe finish in second gains nothing. Commit to the high-value shape, the Bears B trio or the long run, and spend nature tokens to chase it.

**Protect the lead when you have it.** If you lead late, stop chasing and start denying. Keep your nature tokens, because they are the first tiebreaker.

## The tiebreak order

At the 2026 event, ties were broken in this order:

1. Most nature tokens.
2. Most habitat bonus points.
3. Highest score from a single animal.

The first is the rulebook's own tiebreaker. The other two reward things this course has already taught: winning corridors and building one big shape well. On your last turn of a close game, count nature tokens around the table before you spend one.

## Margin matters for seeding

Winning a heat gets you in. How you win also matters. Heat winners are ranked for byes and seeding by wins, then by placement points, then by a score differential that compares your score with the runner-up's. A bigger winning margin ranks you higher.

So once a heat is safely won, keep scoring. Do not coast through the last three turns.

## Using the two-hour round

A four-player game of Cascadia usually fits well inside two hours, so the clock is rarely the problem. Use the time:

- **Count before the last five turns.** Every player's corridors, every rival's near-finished shapes, every nature token.
- **Score carefully at the end.** Use the [scoring routine](/academy/cascadia/how-to-score-cascadia/) from Level 0, out loud, with the whole table watching.

## The card draw

Wildlife cards are drawn at random for each game, so any of the twenty can come up. At the 2026 event, any player at the table could ask to leave Hawks C out of the random draw. Know before you sit down whether you want to ask.

Random cards are why the [hardest cards lesson](/academy/cascadia/cascadia-hardest-scoring-cards/) matters most here. Your weakest card will come up.

## A six-week practice plan

**Weeks 1 and 2: volume.** Play at least six games with random cards, at four players when you can, or online with [Cascadia Digital](https://www.direwolfdigital.com/cascadia/). Track your score and whether you would have won the table.

**Week 3: the scenarios.** Play through solo scenarios 1 to 4, one card letter at a time. Note which letter costs you the most.

**Week 4: your weak card.** Play three games built around the card you score worst on. Use the [solo lesson](/academy/cascadia/cascadia-solo-strategy/) setup so nothing hides your mistakes.

**Week 5: play to win.** Play four-player games where only first place counts. After each, ask: did I watch the leader? Did I take a risk when I trailed?

**Week 6: rehearsal.** Two or three full games with tournament procedures: random cards, the Hawks C option, careful final scoring, and the tiebreak order. Then rest before the event.

## After the event

Review every game you lost. Which bonus slipped away? Which shape stayed unfinished? Which nature token did you spend for nothing? Those three questions improve your play more than any single win.
