The [midgame lesson](/academy/cascadia/cascadia-midgame-strategy/) gave you a rule of thumb for spending nature tokens. This lesson goes deeper: how to earn more of them, and how to tell a good spend from a wasteful one.

## The rules, exactly

- **Earning:** when you place a wildlife token on a keystone tile showing that animal, take a nature token. A keystone tile shows one habitat and one animal.
- **Spending:** before you pick, you may spend a nature token to take any one of the four tiles and any one of the four tokens, even if they are not paired. Or spend one to wipe any number of tokens and replace them from the bag.
- **No limit:** you may spend as many as you like in one turn.
- **Keeping:** each unspent nature token is worth 1 point at the end, and the player with more nature tokens wins a tie.

## Where keystones come from

The box has 85 habitat tiles, and 25 of them are keystones. That is a little under a third. In a four-player game, 83 of the 85 tiles are in play. In a two-player game, 43 are, so you can expect around a dozen keystones to come out across the whole game.

When four fresh tiles fill the display, the chance that at least one is a keystone is about 76%, from the real tile counts. Keystones are common. The hard part is getting the matching animal.

With a full bag, any one token has a 1 in 5 chance of matching the keystone it is paired with. So a natural keystone with its matching token, sitting in the display, happens less often than you might think: across four fresh pairs, roughly one display in five has one.

## The trick: place keystones early, fill them later

A token can go on any open tile in your environment. That includes a keystone you placed five turns ago.

So the steady way to earn nature tokens is not to wait for a perfect pair. It is to place keystones early, empty, in spots where their animal helps a shape. Each one then turns a future token of that animal into a token plus a nature token.

A few guidelines:

- **Choose keystones whose animal you want.** An elk keystone is a fine landing spot if you are building elk lines and a dead tile if you are not.
- **Fill keystones before flexible tiles.** A keystone can only ever hold one animal. Your two-animal and three-animal tiles should stay open longer.
- **Keystones grow corridors in every direction.** All six edges show the same habitat, so they connect easily.

## What a nature token is worth

On paper, 1 point. In practice, more in the middle of the game, because it is also a choice. A nature token lets you break the pairing in the display exactly when it matters: the turn the elk that closes your line sits next to a tile you cannot use.

That option is worth the most early and mid game, and it shrinks to exactly 1 point plus the tiebreak on your last turn.

## Spends that pay

**The free spend.** Spend one to take a keystone with a token of its animal from elsewhere in the display. Placing them earns a nature token straight back. You traded a token for the exact pair you wanted, at no cost.

**The shape closer.** Spend one when the right token completes a shape worth several points: the third bear on Bears B for 10 points, or the line or run that jumps to the card's next row.

**The rescue.** Spend one when every pair in the display is poor for you. A wasted turn costs a tile, a token, and a step toward your shapes. That is usually more than a point.

**The double.** Spend two in one turn: one to wipe tokens you do not want, then one to take any tile with the best new token. Expensive, so save it for a big swing.

## Spends that do not pay

- Turning a 2-point pick into a 3-point pick. You spent 1 to gain 1.
- Wiping tokens just because they are dull. The next player benefits from the fresh draw, too.
- Spending your last token on the final turn when a rival with fewer tokens is tied with you. That token was going to win the tie.

## Wipes help the table, too

A wipe with a nature token replaces tokens before you pick, but the rest of the display stays for the next players. If you wipe a bear-heavy display because you need salmon, the player after you sees whatever comes out of the bag. Glance at the next player's environment before you wipe. If the fresh draw is likely to help them more than you, take a different line.
