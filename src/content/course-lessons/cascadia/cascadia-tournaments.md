Cascadia looks like a gentle family game, and it is one. It also has a real tournament at the World Boardgaming Championships, and a digital version where you can play people around the world. You do not need to win anything to enjoy them. But knowing they exist changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament Cascadia differs from home play

The best documented tournament is the World Boardgaming Championships event. Its 2026 rules, from the [event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/cas.html), give a good picture of competitive play:

- **The original game and rules.** No promo cards.
- **Random cards.** Wildlife scoring cards are drawn at random for each game. Any player at a table may ask to leave the Hawk C card, the one that scores lines of sight, out of the draw.
- **Four-player tables** where possible, with some three-player games when the numbers require it.
- **Two-hour rounds**, with seats and the first player set at check-in.
- **Tiebreaks.** First, most nature tokens, the same as the rulebook. Then the most habitat bonus points. Then the highest score from a single animal.

That tiebreak list is worth remembering at home, too. Nature tokens and habitat bonuses decide close games.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. Cascadia arrived by popular demand: it topped the WBC's [2024 trial event ballot](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc24/trialvote-2024.html) with 448 votes.

In 2026, Cascadia ran as a Century event, marked as welcoming to beginning players. The format:

- **Three heats.** Heat winners advance to the quarterfinal, which takes the top 64.
- **Quarterfinal and semifinal.** The semifinal is four four-player games.
- **Final.** The four semifinal winners play one game for the title.

Thomas Tu is listed as the 2026 Cascadia champion on the [WBC winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html). The beginner-friendly label makes this one of the friendliest ways to try a real tournament.

## Is there an official championship?

AEG's and Flatout Games' Cascadia pages do not list an official championship, qualifier series, or organized play program. Some stores and cafes may run their own Cascadia tournaments, so ask at the places you already play.

## Online play

[Cascadia Digital](https://www.direwolfdigital.com/cascadia/), made by Dire Wolf, runs on Steam, iOS, and Android. You can play online or alone. It also has the fifteen solo scenarios, fourteen challenges, and a Daily Trek with a new goal each day.

Online play is the fastest way to see many card sets. A season of random cards online teaches more than a year of home games with the same favorites.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights where Cascadia is a regular. Ask the staff.
- **Your library.** Many public libraries host board game nights or lend games, and Cascadia is a popular pick because it teaches fast.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Draw the cards at random, every game.** Tournaments do, so your weakest cards need practice too.
- **Play at four** when you can. The heats favor four-player tables, and the display moves fastest there.
- **Run the scenarios.** The fifteen in the rulebook are drills with a clear pass line.
- **Review the scorepad.** After each game, find the bonus you lost, the shape you left unfinished, and the nature token you spent for nothing.
