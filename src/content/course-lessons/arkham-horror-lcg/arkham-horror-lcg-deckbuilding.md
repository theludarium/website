The starter decks in the 2026 core set are built to teach. Once you know the game, you can build your own, and in a campaign, your deck changes after every scenario. This lesson covers the rules from the core rulebook and the Arkham Grimoire v1.1, then a simple way to build.

## The rules for a legal deck

- **One investigator.** At the same table, everyone plays a different investigator.
- **Deck size.** Include exactly the number of standard player cards on the back of your investigator card. For the five core investigators, that is 30. Weaknesses, signature cards, permanent cards, and story cards do not count toward it.
- **Deckbuilding options.** Every standard card must be allowed by the options on the back of your investigator card. Generally that means your own class, plus some access to another class, often limited by level or trait.
- **Two copies.** No more than 2 copies of a card by title. Cards with the myriad keyword allow 3.
- **Requirements.** Add the signature cards and signature weakness listed on the back of your investigator, in the listed numbers.
- **Level 0 to start.** Most investigators begin a campaign with 0 experience, so only level 0 cards. Card level is the number of pips under the cost.
- **One random basic weakness.** Shuffle the basic weaknesses you own and draw one at random. Add it last, after every other card is chosen.

The starter decks are different: each already includes a specific basic weakness. Do not add a random one to a starter deck.

## How experience works

At the end of each scenario, each investigator earns experience equal to the victory points in the victory display, plus or minus whatever the resolution says. Experience is earned separately and cannot be traded.

Between scenarios, you can spend it:

- **A new card costs its level,** with a minimum of 1. A level 0 card still costs 1 experience.
- **An upgrade** to a higher level version of a card you already have costs the difference in levels, minimum 1. Swap the old version out.
- **Keep your deck size.** For each new card, remove another. You cannot remove weaknesses or required cards.
- **Buy one copy at a time.** Each copy is paid for separately.
- **Save what you do not spend.** Unspent experience is recorded and carries forward.

These steps, plus anything the campaign guide tells you, are the only ways to change a deck during a campaign.

A few keywords bend the rules. A **permanent** card starts in play and does not count toward deck size. An **exile** card leaves the game when used; a non-level-0 exiled card must be bought again before it comes back.

## Building your first custom deck

Start from your starter deck and change a few cards, rather than building from scratch. Then check three things.

**1. Your job.** Count the cards that do your main job. A clue-finder wants tools that raise intellect or find extra clues. A fighter wants weapons and enough damage to finish enemies. If you cannot find your job in your opening hand most games, add more.

**2. Your weak skill.** Every investigator has one or two low skills, and the encounter deck will test them. Add some cards with those icons, or cards that cancel treacheries, so a single bad draw does not knock you out.

**3. Your resources.** Add up the cost of the assets you want in play. If it is far more than you can gain in the first few turns, add an economy card or swap an expensive asset for a cheaper one.

Most experienced players treat these as a starting checklist, not a formula. Play the deck, notice which cards you never wanted, and swap them out after the scenario.

## Spending experience well

Each starter deck section in the rulebook suggests a few first purchases. Joe Diamond's suggests a card that gives him a bigger opening hand. Daniela's suggests an upgraded Bodyguard that deals damage when defeated. Use those as a guide.

A good order for most decks:

1. **Upgrades to cards you already rely on.** Paying the difference makes them cheap.
2. **Cards that make your job reliable,** like a better weapon or clue tool.
3. **Cards for your weak skill,** so treacheries hurt less.
4. **Big swings last.** High level cards are exciting, but saving experience for one 5-level card can leave you weak for several scenarios.

## Card pools: current, legacy, and limited

The Grimoire describes three optional ways to set your card pool:

- **Current:** the 2026 core set, the five investigator decks, the two newest deckbuilding expansions, and the two newest investigator expansions. Fantasy Flight recommends this.
- **Legacy:** every product ever released, Chapter One included.
- **Limited:** the core set, the five investigator decks, and three player card expansions of your choice.

If a modified reprint and its original are both in your collection, a deck may only use one version.

## The Taboo List

Fantasy Flight publishes a Taboo List that adjusts player cards that are too strong or too weak, and playing with it is optional. When it released Grimoire v1.1 in July 2026, Fantasy Flight said the first Chapter Two Taboo List would come in a later update. Check its Arkham Horror page for the current list before you build.

Online deckbuilders such as ArkhamDB, a fan site, can check your deck's legality and experience totals. The [Mastery lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-expert-practice-plan/) covers them.
