Arkham Horror: The Card Game is at its best as a campaign: a series of scenarios where choices, wins, and injuries carry forward. The campaign log is how the game remembers. This lesson covers the campaign rules from the 2026 core rulebook and the Arkham Grimoire v1.1. It is spoiler-free: no story outcomes, no specific resolutions.

## The campaign log

Each campaign guide has a campaign log near the back. Fantasy Flight suggests printing or copying it so you can replay the campaign; downloadable logs are on its website.

After each scenario, record:

- Each investigator's earned and unspent experience.
- Each investigator's physical and mental trauma.
- Story assets or weaknesses someone has become the bearer of.
- Investigators who were killed or driven insane.
- Campaign notes the guide tells you to record.

## "Record" versus "remember"

These two words mean different things.

**Record in your campaign log** means write it under Campaign Notes, **exactly** as written. Later scenarios check for the exact phrase. If the guide says to record that the investigators found three of something, writing "found some" can break a later check. If you are told to cross a note out, ignore it from then on.

**Remember that** means keep it in mind for this scenario only. You do not write it down, because it matters only until the resolution.

## Resolutions

A scenario ends when a card sends you to a resolution, shown as an arrow and "R" with a number. Turn to that resolution in the campaign guide and follow it. Resolutions often add experience, trauma, notes, or cards.

If every investigator is defeated before a resolution is reached, use the guide's "if no resolution was reached" section for that scenario.

**Resigning** is a choice some scenarios offer, through a Resign action on a card. A resigned investigator leaves the scenario safely with no defeat, and their clues go on their location. The per-investigator count stays the same. Resigning is how you bank progress before things fall apart; read the resolutions to see what it means for the story.

## Experience

Each investigator earns experience equal to the total victory points of cards in the victory display, plus or minus what the resolution says. Cards reach the victory display when they have the Victory keyword and you defeat them, or clear all clues from such a location. The [deckbuilding lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-deckbuilding/) covers spending it.

## Trauma

Defeat in a scenario takes you out of that scenario, but not out of the campaign.

- **Defeated by damage:** suffer 1 physical trauma.
- **Defeated by horror:** suffer 1 mental trauma.
- **Both at once:** you choose which.

Each trauma means you start every later scenario with 1 damage or 1 horror already on your investigator. So two trauma of one kind can make a fragile investigator very fragile.

- **Physical trauma equal to your printed health:** you are **killed**.
- **Mental trauma equal to your printed sanity:** you are **driven insane**.
- Some resolutions **devour** an investigator, which counts as killed.

A killed, insane, or devoured investigator cannot be used again in that campaign. The player picks a new investigator and builds a new level 0 deck. If no investigators are left in the collection, the campaign is lost.

## Playing it well

**Treat defeat as expensive.** Being defeated early in a scenario costs your team actions and costs you trauma. Resigning or playing safer is often better for the campaign, even if it gives up some victory points.

**Watch your weak side.** An investigator with low sanity should heal horror and avoid tests that cost it, because one mental trauma takes a bigger share of a small sanity value.

**Do not chase every point.** A resolution that saves trauma may be worth more than an extra experience point, especially early, when one bad scenario can snowball.

## Joining and leaving

Players can drop in and out between scenarios. Leave a departing player's log entries in place, since they can return. A new player must pick an investigator not yet used in the campaign, and starts with no experience and no trauma. For more on keeping a campaign going when someone misses a night, see our article on [what to do when a player misses a campaign session](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/).

## Epic Campaign Mode

After you finish a campaign, the rulebook offers Epic Campaign Mode: carry a surviving investigator into a new campaign. You keep trauma, story rewards, and weaknesses; reset the deck to level 0; reset the chaos bag; and choose an equal or higher difficulty. Your score is the total experience earned before the investigator falls. It is one of the [Mastery](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-expert-practice-plan/) challenges.

## A note on spoilers

Campaign guides are written to be read as you go. Read only the section you are told to read. Resolutions and later scenarios are full of surprises, and this course keeps them that way.
