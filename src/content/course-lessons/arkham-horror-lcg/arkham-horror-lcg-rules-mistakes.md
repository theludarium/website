Arkham Horror: The Card Game is a game where small rules errors add up. Skip one attack of opportunity a round and the scenario is much easier than it should be. Forget that doom on enemies counts, and the agenda never seems to move. This lesson collects the slips that show up most, checked against the 2026 core rulebook, its "Easily Missed Rules" list, and the Arkham Grimoire v1.1.

## Attacks of opportunity

**Mistake: playing a card or moving away while engaged, for free.** If you are engaged with one or more ready enemies and you spend an action on anything other than Fight, Evade, Parley, or Resign, each of those enemies attacks you. That includes draw, resource, play, move, investigate, activate, and engage.

**Mistake: resolving the action first.** The attack comes after you pay the action's costs and before its effect resolves. If you play an asset that would soak damage, the attack lands first.

**Mistake: exhausting the enemy after it attacks.** Enemies do not exhaust from an attack of opportunity. They still attack again in the enemy phase.

**Mistake: thinking fast cards provoke.** An attack of opportunity needs an action to be spent. Fast cards and free triggered abilities cost no action, so they do not provoke. An ability that costs two actions provokes only one attack from each engaged enemy.

**Mistake: exhausted enemies attacking.** Only ready enemies make attacks of opportunity.

## Engagement

**Mistake: putting new enemies on the location.** An enemy drawn from the encounter deck spawns engaged with the investigator who drew it, in their threat area, unless it has spawn instructions or the aloof or massive keyword.

**Mistake: walking past a monster.** Any time a ready, unengaged enemy is at the same location as an investigator, it engages them. That happens when it spawns there, moves there, when you move in, or when it readies there.

**Mistake: forgetting that evaded enemies come back.** Evading exhausts the enemy and moves it out of your threat area onto the location. In the upkeep phase it readies, and if you are still there, it engages you again. Evade, then move away, if you can.

**Mistake: leaving engaged enemies behind.** When you move, enemies engaged with you move with you.

**Mistake: swinging at a friend's monster without care.** If you attack an enemy engaged with another investigator and fail, the damage you would have dealt goes to that investigator instead.

## The mythos phase

**Mistake: playing it in round one.** Skip the mythos phase in the first round of every game.

**Mistake: drawing encounter cards before doom.** The order is fixed. Place 1 doom on the agenda. Then check the threshold. Then each investigator draws an encounter card, in player order.

**Mistake: counting only the doom on the agenda.** The threshold counts all doom in play, including doom on enemies, locations, and other cards. When the agenda advances, all that doom is removed.

**Mistake: advancing the agenda mid-round.** Unless a card says otherwise, the agenda can only advance during that check in the mythos phase. Hitting the threshold during your turn does nothing until the next mythos phase.

**Mistake: forgetting surge and peril.** Surge makes you draw another encounter card after resolving the first. Peril means the drawer cannot talk it over, and nobody else may commit cards or play anything to help.

## Skill tests and cards

**Mistake: paying to commit.** Committing a card to a skill test costs no resources. Only icons matching the tested skill count, plus wild icons.

**Mistake: piling cards into a friend's test.** Another investigator at the same location may commit one card to your test. Investigators elsewhere cannot help.

**Mistake: playing assets on someone else's turn.** You can only play an asset during your own turn, even a fast one, unless a card says otherwise.

**Mistake: treating autofail as a big minus.** The autofail token makes your total skill value 0. For a treachery that hurts you "for each point you fail by," that is a big fail.

**Mistake: dealing 2 damage with a basic fight.** A successful attack deals 1 damage unless a weapon or card adds more.

## Locations and clues

**Mistake: putting clues on every location at setup.** Locations enter play unrevealed, with no shroud or clue value showing. Clues go on a location when it is revealed, usually when someone first enters it.

**Mistake: forgetting the per-investigator icon.** Many clue values and act costs are multiplied by the number of investigators who started the scenario. That number stays the same if someone is defeated or resigns.

## Smaller ones

- **Empty deck.** If you need to draw and your deck is empty, take 1 horror, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck, and draw.
- **Hand size.** In upkeep, discard down to 8 cards, not 5.
- **"Cannot" is absolute.** No card overrides it.
- **The Grim Rule.** If you cannot find an answer or agree, play it the worst way for the group and move on. Look it up after the game.

## A rules habit for the table

Keep the rulebook's back cover and the free Arkham Grimoire handy. Most questions have a written answer. And if your group played an earlier core set, the round is the same, so these fixes apply to Chapter One games too.
