Every player card in Arkham Horror: The Card Game belongs to one of five classes, or is neutral. Each investigator draws mainly from their own class and borrows from others as the back of their investigator card allows. The class tells you what your deck is good at and what it will struggle with.

This lesson uses the class overviews and starter decks in the 2026 core rulebook. Skill values are listed in the order printed on the cards: willpower, intellect, combat, agility.

## The two jobs, and the third

Every scenario asks the group to do two things: **find clues** to advance the act, and **handle enemies** so nobody gets taken out. A third job sits under both: **survive the encounter deck**, which mostly tests willpower and agility.

A good team covers all three. In a two-player game, that usually means one clue-finder and one fighter, with both able to pass a willpower test now and then.

## Guardian

Guardians fight and protect. The rulebook lists their strengths as high combat, healing, team support, heavy weapons, strong allies, enemy management, and extra damage.

**In the core set: Daniela Reyes, the Mechanic (3, 2, 5, 2).** Her ability lets her fight an enemy that attacks any investigator at her location, once per round. Her job is to stand between monsters and the team.

**Plan:** Get a weapon and something that soaks damage into play early. Engage enemies on purpose so your friends can work. Watch your horror, since her sanity is her weak side. For clues, use events that find them without a high intellect test, then let the Seeker do the rest.

## Seeker

Seekers find clues and draw cards. Strengths: high intellect, card draw, deck searching, horror healing, discovering extra clues, powerful tomes, and precise skill tests.

**In the core set: Joe Diamond, the Private Investigator (2, 4, 4, 2).** Each time he investigates successfully, he draws a card, once per round. His starter deck borrows Guardian cards, so he can fight better than most Seekers.

**Plan:** Play an intellect boost like Magnifying Glass early, then investigate every turn. Your card draw funds everything else. His low willpower means treacheries can hurt, so keep a few icons for those tests.

## Rogue

Rogues tilt the odds. Strengths: high agility, evasion, resource generation, succeeding by a lot, extra actions, and movement.

**In the core set: Trish Scarborough, the Former Spy (2, 4, 2, 4).** She gets an extra action each turn that can only be used to evade. Her deck borrows Seeker cards for clues.

**Plan:** Evade, then investigate. Build resources early with economy assets, then spend them to push tests to a margin of 2 or more, which many Rogue cards reward. Like Joe, her willpower is low, so hold resources or icons for treacheries.

## Mystic

Mystics use spells, mostly tested with willpower. Strengths: high willpower, spells and rituals, predicting or ignoring chaos tokens, encounter deck control, and doom manipulation.

**In the core set: Dexter Drake, the Magician (5, 2, 2, 3).** Once per round, after he plays an asset, he can return another asset to hand or play a different one. His deck borrows Rogue economy cards.

**Plan:** Mystics start slow. Spend the first turns on resources and setup, then use spells that fight and investigate with willpower. Once Dexter has his tools, he can do either job. His high willpower also makes him the team's best answer to treacheries.

## Survivor

Survivors scrape by. Strengths: reactive play, cheap cards, recurring discarded cards, discarding for effects, failing forward, and traps.

**In the core set: Isabelle Barnes, the Returned (4, 2, 3, 3).** During her own tests she can take 1 direct horror to commit a skill from her discard pile, once per round. Her deck borrows Mystic cards.

**Plan:** Load up on skill cards and use your ability to commit the best one twice. Isabelle fills gaps: a little fighting, a little investigating, and good willpower. Heal horror when you can, since her ability spends it.

## Building a team

| Players | A balanced lineup from the core set |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Joe, who both investigates and fights |
| 2 | Joe and Daniela |
| 3 | Joe, Daniela, and Dexter |
| 4 | Add Trish or Isabelle |

These are starting points, not the only good choices. A team of Rogue and Mystic can work if both lean into clues and evasion, for example. What matters is that someone owns each job.

## Beyond the core set

Five investigator decks extend each class: Tommy Muldoon (Guardian), Carolyn Fern (Seeker), André Patel (Rogue), Marie Lambeau (Mystic), and Miguel de la Cruz (Survivor). Each comes with its own ready deck. Across nearly ten years of Chapter One products, many more investigators exist, and Fantasy Flight says all of them are compatible with the 2026 cards. The [buying lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-which-box-to-buy/) explains how they fit together.

Your class also shapes how you spend experience, which is where the [deckbuilding lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-deckbuilding/) picks up.
