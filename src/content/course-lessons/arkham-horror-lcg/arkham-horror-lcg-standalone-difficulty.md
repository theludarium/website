Arkham Horror: The Card Game is cooperative, so you play against the game, not a ladder. Its challenge comes from the scenarios themselves, and the game gives you several ways to make them harder or easier. This lesson covers difficulty levels, standalone mode, and the optional rules in the Arkham Grimoire v1.1.

## The four difficulty levels

Every campaign and standalone scenario starts with a choice: Easy, Standard, Hard, or Expert. The 2026 core campaign guide gives each a one-line description, which sums them up well: Easy is for the story, Standard is a challenge, Hard is a nightmare, and Expert is the full Arkham Horror experience.

Two things change with the level.

**The chaos bag.** Each campaign guide lists the tokens for each level. In the 2026 core campaign, Standard has 16 tokens with a worst number token of -4. Expert has 18, running down to -8, and its best number token is 0. The [chaos bag lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-chaos-bag-odds/) shows what that does to your odds: at a margin of 2, worst-case success drops from about 56% on Standard to about 33% on Expert.

**The scenario reference card.** Easy and Standard use one side. Hard and Expert use the other side, where the symbol tokens usually hit harder.

You pick a level for the whole campaign and keep it.

## Standalone mode

Any scenario can be played as a one-off game, outside its campaign. The Grimoire's standalone rules:

- **Build with experience if you like.** You may include higher level cards, as long as your investigator can use them. Count the total experience of those cards.
- **Pay for it with weaknesses.** Add random basic weaknesses by that total: none for 0 to 9 experience, 1 for 10 to 19, 2 for 20 to 29, 3 for 30 to 39, and 4 for 40 to 49. Fifty or more is not allowed.
- **Set up from the campaign guide.** Start at that scenario's setup, and follow any standalone instructions at the end of it.
- **"Record" becomes "remember."** Anything the guide says to record, you only remember for this game.
- **Missing cards.** If a reward or weakness comes from an expansion you do not own, carry on without it.

Killed, insane, and defeated all mean the same thing in a standalone game. As an optional variant, you can write down your resolution and experience as a score and try to beat it next time.

Standalone mode is how scenario packs like Film Fatale and Traces to Nowhere are usually played. It is also the best way to practice a hard scenario without risking a campaign.

## Ultimatums and Boons

The Grimoire lists optional rules any group can add, by unanimous choice, for a whole campaign or scenario. **Ultimatums** make the game harder. **Boons** make it easier. They are not equal in strength, so mix them to land where you want.

A few Ultimatums, in our own words:

- **Dread:** do not skip the mythos phase in round one.
- **Broken Promises:** take the Elder Sign out of the bag.
- **Failure:** add a second autofail token.
- **Finality:** in a campaign, defeat by damage kills you and defeat by horror drives you insane.
- **Orthodoxy:** play with the Taboo List.
- **Malevolence:** on Easy or Standard, use the Hard and Expert side of the reference card.

A few Boons:

- **Hades:** each investigator starts with 2 more resources.
- **Thoth:** each investigator starts with 1 more card in hand.
- **Athena:** the first autofail each game can be cancelled and redrawn.
- **Nodens:** on Hard or Expert, use the Easy and Standard side of the reference card.

Malevolence and Nodens are useful because they split the two dials. You can play the Standard bag with the harsh reference card, or the Expert bag with the gentler one.

The Grimoire also lists **Refractions**, scenario-specific Ultimatums and Boons. They contain light spoilers for their scenarios, so read them only for scenarios you have played.

## Choosing your level

A simple ladder for a group that wants to improve:

1. **Standard** for the first play of any campaign.
2. **Standard with Malevolence** when Standard feels comfortable. It changes symbol effects without changing the bag.
3. **Hard** once you win most scenarios with resources to spare.
4. **Expert** when Hard feels fair. Expect to lose scenarios, and to need the deckbuilding habits from Level 3.

There is no shame in Easy. If your group plays for the story, it is the right setting, and the rulebook says so.

## Reading a scenario before you raise the dial

Before replaying a scenario at a higher level, read its reference card for that level. Note which symbols add damage, horror, or doom. Those are the tokens that end games on Expert, and planning around them is most of the skill.
