Almost everything in Arkham Horror: The Card Game runs through a skill test: finding clues, fighting, evading, and most treacheries the encounter deck throws at you. Learning to play the test well is the first real skill step after learning the rules. This lesson uses the skill test timing from the 2026 core rulebook and the Arkham Grimoire v1.1.

## The eight steps

The Grimoire lays out every test in the same order:

1. **Determine the skill.** Basic actions set it: investigate uses intellect against the location's shroud, fight uses combat against the enemy's fight value, evade uses agility against its evade value. A card ability names its skill in parentheses, such as Fight using willpower. If a card offers a choice, you choose now.
2. **Commit cards.** You may commit any number of cards with a matching icon. Each other investigator at your location may commit one.
3. **Reveal a chaos token.**
4. **Apply its symbol effect**, if it has one.
5. **Total your modified skill.**
6. **Succeed or fail.** Meet or beat the difficulty to succeed.
7. **Apply the results.**
8. **End the test.** Discard every committed card and return the token to the bag.

After you commit, there is a player window to trigger free abilities that boost the test. Then the token comes out. So you commit before you see the token, and there is no going back after the pull.

## What counts as a match

Each skill icon on a card that matches the tested skill adds 1. A wild icon matches any skill. Cards with no matching icon cannot be committed. You pay no resources to commit, even for a card that costs 5 to play.

Skill cards are made for this. They cannot be played at all; they only do their work when committed. Many add a bonus on success, like an extra clue or extra damage. Some limit how many copies go into one test, shown as "Max 1 committed per skill test."

## Helping each other

An investigator at the same location may commit one card to your test. Anyone elsewhere cannot help. This is a reason to move as a pair early in a scenario, before you split up to cover more locations.

Peril encounter cards switch this off. Nobody else may commit or play cards to help while a peril card resolves.

## The margin habit

Before any test, look at the gap between your skill and the difficulty. Call that your margin. A margin of 0 means you need a 0 or better from the bag. A margin of 2 means you can survive a -2.

Many experienced players aim for a margin of about 2 on Standard for tests that matter. The [chaos bag lesson](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-chaos-bag-odds/) shows why: on the Standard bag, going from a margin of 0 to a margin of 2 more than doubles your chance of success at worst. Past a margin of about 4, extra icons barely help, because only symbol tokens and autofail are left to fail you.

That gives you a simple rule:

- **Margin 0 or 1 on a test that matters:** commit, or do something else.
- **Margin 2 or 3:** commit if the test is critical, like the last clue or a killing blow.
- **Margin 4 or more:** stop committing. Keep the cards.

## When to hold a card instead

A committed card is gone. So ask what else it could do.

- **An asset you need in play** is often worth more on the table than as a +1.
- **A card with an effect you will need soon**, like an event that cancels a treachery, should stay in hand.
- **A weak card for this scenario**, or a duplicate of something already in play, is a great commit.
- **Late in the scenario**, cards that will not get played anyway should all become icons.

## Succeeding by more, and failing by less

Some effects care about the size of your result. Rogue cards in particular reward succeeding "by 2 or more." Many treacheries punish you for each point you fail by. So a test you are going to fail anyway can still be worth one card if it cuts the damage.

The autofail token sets your skill to 0, so a "for each point" treachery hits hardest then. Nothing you commit helps against it.

## Tests are also actions

A failed investigate costs an action and gives nothing. On a margin of 0, consider using that action to draw or gain a resource, then test next turn with better cards in hand. A free trigger or a weapon that adds +1 or more combat often makes a fight worth taking, so play it before you start swinging.

## A worked example

Joe Diamond has intellect 4 and is at a location with shroud 3: margin 1. He holds a skill with one intellect icon. Committing it makes the margin 2. If the clue finishes the act, commit. If it is the first of many clues, he might keep it and investigate again next turn with a Magnifying Glass in play.
