Arkham Horror: The Card Game uses a bag of tokens instead of dice. That makes the odds easy to work out, because you can see every token that could come out. This lesson uses the chaos bags listed in the Brethren of Ash campaign guide from the 2026 core set. The numbers below are general and spoiler-free.

## What is in the bag?

Each campaign guide lists a bag for each difficulty. For the 2026 core campaign:

| Difficulty | Number tokens | Symbol tokens | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | +1, +1, 0, 0, 0, -1, -1, -1, -2, -2 | 6 | 16 |
| Standard | +1, 0, 0, -1, -1, -1, -2, -2, -3, -4 | 6 | 16 |
| Hard | 0, 0, 0, -1, -1, -2, -2, -3, -3, -4, -5 | 6 | 17 |
| Expert | 0, -1, -1, -2, -2, -3, -3, -4, -4, -5, -6, -8 | 6 | 18 |

The six symbol tokens are the same at every difficulty: four that point to the scenario reference card, the autofail token, and the Elder Sign.

- **Scenario symbols** do what the scenario reference card says. Usually that is a minus, sometimes with an extra sting. Hard and Expert use the harder side of the card.
- **The autofail token** fails the test, whatever your total.
- **The Elder Sign** triggers your investigator card's Elder Sign effect. Every core investigator's Elder Sign is +0 or better, so for them it passes any test at a margin of 0 or more.

The Easy and Standard numbers match the classic Night of the Zealot bags from Chapter One, so those odds carry over to that campaign too. Other campaigns change the bag, and campaigns often add or remove tokens as you play. Always count what is in your bag now.

## The math

Your **margin** is your skill value, after committed icons and bonuses, minus the test's difficulty. You pass if the token's modifier is no worse than minus your margin.

So the chance to pass is: tokens you can survive, divided by tokens in the bag. The table below counts the number tokens plus the Elder Sign as passes, and treats all four scenario symbols as fails. That is the worst case. Your real odds are a little higher, because some scenario symbols are mild.

| Margin | Easy (of 16) | Standard (of 16) | Hard (of 17) | Expert (of 18) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 6, about 38% | 4, 25% | 4, about 24% | 2, about 11% |
| 1 | 9, about 56% | 7, about 44% | 6, about 35% | 4, about 22% |
| 2 | 11, about 69% | 9, about 56% | 8, about 47% | 6, about 33% |
| 3 | 11, about 69% | 10, about 63% | 10, about 59% | 8, about 44% |
| 4 | 11, about 69% | 11, about 69% | 11, about 65% | 10, about 56% |
| 5 | 11, about 69% | 11, about 69% | 12, about 71% | 11, about 61% |

To see the best case, add the four scenario symbols. On Standard at a margin of 2, that is 13 of 16, about 81%. The truth sits between, and the scenario reference card tells you where.

## What the table teaches

**A margin of 0 is a coin flip you mostly lose.** On Standard, you pass on 4 tokens out of 16. Testing at your skill value is usually a wasted action.

**The first two points matter most.** On Standard, a margin of 2 more than doubles your worst-case odds compared with a margin of 0. This is why committing one or two icons is so strong.

**Then the curve flattens.** On Standard, every number token passes at a margin of 4. After that, only the symbols can fail you, and extra icons do nothing against autofail. Over-committing past that point throws cards away.

**Expert is a different game.** At a margin of 2 you pass about a third of the time at worst.

## Reading the reference card

Before the game, read the scenario reference card for your difficulty. If a symbol is just a small minus, count it as a pass at a high margin. If it is a big minus or adds damage, horror, or doom, count it as a fail and plan around it.

Some symbols scale with the game state, like the current act number. Those get worse as the scenario goes on, so test early when you can.

## Odds for a whole turn

Your three actions are separate tests. If you need two clues and each investigate is 56% at worst, you get both from two tries only about 32% of the time (9/16 times 9/16). Three tries raise your chance of at least two successes to about 59%, but that spends your whole turn. That is why a whole turn of investigating at a low margin often fizzles, and why a +1 intellect tool pays off so fast.
