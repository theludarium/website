Every identity card in Marvel Champions has two sides. The hero side is the costumed character. The alter-ego side is the person behind the mask. You start the game as the alter-ego, and once per round, on your own turn, you may flip.

That flip is the most important decision in the game, and new players often make it by habit. This lesson is about making it on purpose.

## What each form can do

| | Hero form | Alter-ego form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic powers | Attack, thwart, defend | Recover |
| Villain activation | Attacks you | Schemes |
| Card limits | Cards marked "Hero Action" or "Hero form only" | Cards marked "Alter-Ego Action" |
| Hand size | Usually smaller | Usually larger |

The hand sizes are printed on each side. In the core set, every alter-ego has a hand size of 6. The heroes have 5, except She-Hulk at 4 and Iron Man, who starts at 1 and grows with each tech upgrade he controls.

## What the villain does to you

During the villain phase, the villain activates once against each player. The form you are in at that moment decides how:

- **Hero form:** the villain attacks you. You can defend, block with an ally, or take the damage.
- **Alter-ego form:** the villain schemes, placing threat on the main scheme equal to its SCH, plus its boost card.

Minions engaged with you follow the same rule. So the real question each turn is: would the team rather take damage or take threat?

## The cost of each choice

**Ending in hero form** keeps threat off the scheme and puts the villain's attack on you. It also lets you defend, and it lets you play hero actions on your next turn without spending your flip. But every attack chips at your hit points, and your hand refills to the smaller number.

**Ending in alter-ego form** gives you the bigger hand and the recover power, and keeps you out of the fight. But each scheme adds threat, often several points once the boost card flips. In a multiplayer game, one player in alter-ego form can be the difference between holding the scheme and losing it.

## Common flip patterns

**Hero, hero, hero, then recover.** Stay in hero form for several rounds while your hit points hold. When you get low, flip to alter-ego at the start of your turn, play your alter-ego cards, and recover. Then decide whether to flip back next turn.

**Flip at the start of the turn, not the end.** Changing form is free, but it is once per round. A common rhythm for heroes who like to heal is: start the turn in alter-ego, recover and use alter-ego abilities, then flip to hero, act, and end the turn as the hero. You get both forms' value in one turn, and you still face the villain as a hero. The catch: you used your exhausted identity to recover, so your hero cannot also attack or thwart with basic powers that turn unless something readies it.

**The planned rest.** If the main scheme is low and no dangerous minions are out, a turn in alter-ego form is cheap. Take it then, instead of when you are desperate and the scheme is high.

**Watch for "when you change form" cards.** Some heroes reward the flip. She-Hulk deals 2 damage to an enemy after she changes to hero form, so she wants to flip from alter-ego to hero often.

## Obligations and alter-ego cards

Every hero shuffles an obligation into the encounter deck. Each core obligation lets you flip to alter-ego form, then offers a choice: exhaust your alter-ego to remove it, or take a penalty. Having your identity ready, not exhausted, when an obligation shows up is one more reason a well-timed rest pays off.

Many heroes also have alter-ego actions that matter, such as Peter Parker's mental resource or Carol Danvers letting a player draw. These are not consolation prizes. They are part of the engine.

## Reading the table in a team game

In a multiplayer game, flips are a team decision. Before you end your turn, look at:

1. **The main scheme.** How much threat will land this round if you stay in alter-ego form?
2. **Your hit points.** Could the villain plus its boost and your minions knock you out?
3. **Your teammates.** Is someone else already resting this round?

If two players rest the same round, the villain schemes twice. Stagger your rests so the scheme does not jump all at once. For more on keeping threat in check, see the threat lesson in Level 3: [scheme and threat management](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-threat-and-schemes/).
