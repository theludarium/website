Once standard mode feels comfortable, the game has several ways to push back harder. The Rules Reference calls them modes of play, and you can combine them.

## Standard and expert

**Standard mode** is the base game. Follow the scenario's contents and setup, as printed on side 1A of the main scheme.

**Expert mode** uses the listed expert villain stages instead and adds the Expert encounter set to the encounter deck. For the core villains, that means Rhino II and III, Klaw II and III, or Ultron II and III. Two things change at once:

- **The villain is stronger from the first round.** Stage II becomes your opening fight, and stage III is often meaner than anything you have seen. Rhino III enters with a tough status card and stuns each hero when he arrives. Klaw III enters with toughness too. Ultron III cannot take damage while any Drone minion is in play.
- **The encounter deck is harsher.** The core Expert set has three cards. Exhaustion surges and exhausts your identity. Under Fire surges and reveals another card. Masterplan puts 4 threat on each side scheme, or digs out a side scheme if none is in play.

The later stages also have more hit points per player, while the main scheme stays the same. A deck that wins comfortably on standard may lose on expert in the first two rounds.

## Heroic and skirmish

**Heroic mode** adds encounter cards. Choose a heroic level, then deal that many extra encounter cards to each player in every villain phase. Heroic 1 means two encounter cards each per round, before hazard icons. It is a steep increase, so try it on a villain you have already beaten on expert.

**Skirmish mode**, also called rookie mode, shortens the game. Pick one version of the villain and play against only that stage. It is a good way to practice against a single stage, or to fit a game into a short evening.

You can combine expert, heroic, skirmish, and campaign modes as you like.

## Campaigns

Campaign expansions link their scenarios into one story. You play them in order, and a campaign log records what carries from game to game. Each campaign decides what that is, such as cards your heroes earn, choices that change later scenarios, and cards removed from the campaign. Each campaign has its own rules in its own rulebook, so read it before the first game.

Two campaign rules from the Rules Reference are worth knowing:

- **Removed means removed.** If a card is removed from a campaign, it stays out for the rest of that campaign, even if you replay the scenario where it was removed.
- **Expert campaign mode** is its own thing, described in each campaign's rulebook. Because the log carries results forward, a bad game can make the next one harder.

Campaigns are where solo players often find the most story, and where groups find a reason to meet every week.

## A plan to beat expert

**1. Build for both clocks.** On standard, a deck can lean hard on damage. On expert, threat lands faster and side schemes pile up. Make sure your deck, or your team, can thwart 3 or more threat in a turn when it must.

**2. Plan for the arrival of stage III.** Read the stage III villain card before the game. Know what it does when it enters, and have your hit points and allies ready for it. For Rhino, a stun on each hero means your next attacks are canceled.

**3. Cut expensive cards.** Expert punishes slow starts. Lower your deck's average cost and keep cards that help on turn one.

**4. Keep a defender ready.** More boost cards and stronger stages mean bigger attacks. A hero with good DEF, or a cheap ally to block with, saves a game.

**5. Respect the encounter deck.** With Under Fire and Exhaustion in the deck, a single villain phase can reveal several cards. Treachery cancels like Enhanced Spider-Sense and Get Behind Me! are worth more on expert.

**6. Track what beat you.** After each loss, name the clock that ran out, and change one card. Changing one card at a time tells you what worked.

## The difficulty ladder

A simple order for climbing:

1. Standard, recommended modular set.
2. Standard, a harder modular set. The Learn to Play rates the five core modulars from 1 to 5: Bomb Scare, Masters of Evil, Under Attack, Legions of Hydra, and The Doomsday Chair.
3. Expert, recommended modular set.
4. Expert, harder modular set.
5. Expert with heroic 1.
6. Expert campaign.

Each step is a real jump. Stay on one until you win it twice.
