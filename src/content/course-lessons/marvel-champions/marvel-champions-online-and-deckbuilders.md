Marvel Champions is cooperative, so the game is not built around tournaments. The top of the game is not a trophy. It is beating every villain you own on expert, then on heroic, then through a full expert campaign. This lesson covers the tools that help and a plan to get there.

## MarvelCDB: the fan card database

[MarvelCDB](https://marvelcdb.com/) is a fan-run card database and deckbuilder. It says plainly that it is not produced, endorsed, or affiliated with Fantasy Flight Games. It has been running for years and was working when this lesson was written.

What it does well:

- **Card search.** Every card, searchable by name, aspect, type, trait, and text.
- **Deckbuilder.** Pick a hero, and it adds the hero's set for you, then lets you add aspect and Basic cards. It shows your deck size and card counts as you build.
- **Shared decks.** Player decklists, sorted by popularity or date. Looking at three popular decks for your hero is a fast way to see which cards other players value.
- **Rules and FAQs pages**, handy for quick lookups. For anything disputed, check the official Rules Reference.
- **Reviews.** Player write-ups on individual cards, useful when you are deciding between two.

Use shared decks as a starting point, not an answer. Popular decks are often built from large collections and tuned for one playstyle. Build your version, play it, and change it.

## Playing remotely

Fantasy Flight Games' product line for Marvel Champions lists only the physical game. Groups that play at a distance can use a video call with a camera over the villain's area, each player with their own physical deck, or a fan-made module in a general tabletop program.

Video play works better than you might expect, because each player only needs to see the shared villain area and their own cards. One person runs the encounter deck and reads each card aloud.

## Finding other players

- **Your local game store.** Many stores that carry Living Card Games host casual co-op nights. Ask at the counter.
- **BoardGameGeek.** The Marvel Champions forums there have threads for finding players, rules questions, and deck ideas.
- **Fan communities.** Discord servers and subreddit communities for the game share decks, house rules, and challenge runs.
- **Indianapolis.** If you live near Indianapolis, join [the Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## A six-week practice plan

The plan assumes about two games a week, solo or with a steady partner. Change the villains to match what you own.

**Week 1: one hero, one villain.** Pick one hero and play against Rhino on standard twice. After each game, write which clock nearly beat you.

**Week 2: the whole core set.** Same hero, against Klaw and then Ultron on standard. Use each villain's recommended modular set.

**Week 3: build your own deck.** Build a 40-card deck in MarvelCDB using the [deckbuilding rules](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-deckbuilding/). Play it twice against your hardest standard villain. Swap one card after each game.

**Week 4: expert, gently.** Play Rhino on expert. Read stage II and III before you start. If you lose, change one card and play again.

**Week 5: expert, everywhere.** Take your deck against Klaw and Ultron on expert. Swap in a harder modular set once you win.

**Week 6: the next step.** Either start a campaign from an expansion on standard, or try your best expert matchup on heroic 1.

Keep a short log. Date, hero, aspect, villain, mode, modular set, win or loss, and the card you changed. After six weeks, that log will show you what your deck needs better than any guide.

## Where to go from here

When your log fills up with expert wins, the open-ended challenges are yours: an expert campaign, a higher heroic level, or one hero against every villain you own. Groups often set their own goals, such as beating each villain with each aspect.
