The core set has three scenarios, and the Learn to Play suggests playing them in order: Rhino, then Klaw, then Ultron. Each scenario uses its villain's encounter set, the Standard encounter set, and one modular set. The recommended modulars are Bomb Scare for Rhino, Masters of Evil for Klaw, and Under Attack for Ultron, but you can swap in any modular set to change the game.

In standard mode, each villain has two stages. Hit point values below are per player, from the printed cards.

## Rhino: learn to defend

**Stages:** Rhino I (14 hit points per player), then Rhino II (15 per player). **Main scheme:** The Break-In!, with a target of 7 threat per player and 1 threat per player each round.

Rhino is simple. He hits hard, he schemes for 1, and his deck is mostly about making him hit harder. Charge attachments give his attacks overkill, which pushes extra damage from a defending ally through to its hero. The Armored Rhino Suit soaks up damage before it reaches him. Stampede makes him attack you in hero form. When Rhino II enters play, he brings the Breakin' & Takin' side scheme, whose hazard icon deals an extra encounter card each round.

His minions are a good first lesson in keywords: Hydra Mercenary has guard, so you cannot attack Rhino while it is engaged with you, and Sandman enters with a tough status card.

**Plan:** defend his attacks with your hero rather than chump-blocking with allies once Charge is out. Clear Crowd Control quickly when it appears: its crisis icon stops you from removing threat on the main scheme. Push damage hard; a 7 per player scheme gives you time.

## Klaw: learn to manage threat

**Stages:** Klaw I (12 per player), then Klaw II (18 per player). **Main scheme:** two stages, Underground Distribution and then Secret Rendezvous, and each one brings out a minion when it is revealed.

Klaw starts the game with the Defense Network side scheme in play, which has a crisis icon. Klaw gets an extra boost card whenever he attacks, so his attacks swing more than his low ATK suggests. When Klaw II enters play, he reveals The "Immortal" Klaw, a side scheme that gives him 10 more hit points until you clear it.

His minions are the real problem. Armored Guards have guard and toughness, and Weapons Runners surge. Klaw's own treacheries heal him or punish you differently in each form.

**Plan:** clear Defense Network early, because nobody can thwart the main scheme while it stands. Kill minions as they arrive rather than letting them stack. When Klaw II arrives, clear the "Immortal" side scheme before you commit damage to him: those 10 extra hit points vanish when it goes.

## Ultron: learn to protect your deck

**Stages:** Ultron I (17 per player), then Ultron II (22 per player). **Main scheme:** three stages, ending with Countdown to Oblivion, where threat cannot be removed.

Ultron turns your own deck against you. Many of his cards put the top card of your player deck into play facedown as a Drone minion, engaged with you. The Ultron Drones environment makes each Drone a 1 SCH, 1 ATK, 1 hit point minion. When a Drone is defeated, the card goes to your discard pile, so every Drone also costs you a card from your deck.

After Ultron I attacks you, you choose: place 1 threat on the main scheme, or take a Drone. Ultron II puts a Drone into play when he attacks you and gets +1 ATK for each Drone engaged with you. Advanced Ultron Drones have guard.

**Plan:** treat Drones as a priority. Cards that deal 1 damage to each enemy, like She-Hulk's Ground Stomp or War Machine, clear several at once. Do not let Drones pile up before Ultron II, or his attacks grow fast. Move through the first two main scheme stages carefully, because the last stage cannot be thwarted, only outraced.

## Expert mode, in short

Expert mode swaps in the later villain stages, Rhino II and III, Klaw II and III, or Ultron II and III, and adds the Expert encounter set. Rhino III enters with a tough status card and stuns each hero. Ultron III cannot take damage while any Drone is in play. These are the Level 5 challenges in this course; see [expert mode and campaigns](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-expert-mode-and-campaigns/).

## Your nemesis is in there too

Every hero's nemesis set waits off to the side. The Standard set's Shadow of the Past treachery brings your nemesis minion and its side scheme into play. Each core nemesis side scheme carries an icon, so plan for one more problem in a long game.
