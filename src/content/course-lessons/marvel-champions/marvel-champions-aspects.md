Every Marvel Champions deck has two halves. One half is your hero's own cards, the identity-specific set that always comes along. The other half is yours to choose, and it comes from one aspect plus the Basic cards.

The four aspects are Aggression, Justice, Leadership, and Protection. Each one is a style of play with its own color: red for Aggression, yellow for Justice, blue for Leadership, and green for Protection. Basic cards are not part of any aspect and fit in any deck.

The card examples below are from the core set. Every later box adds more cards to each aspect.

## Aggression: hit things

Aggression wants to deal damage, fast. Its cards are attack events, ways to raise ATK, and tools for clearing minions.

**Core examples:** Uppercut deals 5 damage to an enemy. Relentless Assault deals 5 to a minion and gains overkill if you paid with a physical resource. Combat Training gives a hero +1 ATK. Chase Them Down removes 2 threat after your hero defeats an enemy, so Aggression has a small answer to threat. The Hulk and Tigra allies hit hard.

**Best with:** heroes who already have a strong attack, or heroes who need a faster clock. The weakness is threat: an Aggression deck needs teammates or its own hero cards to hold the main scheme.

## Justice: stop the schemes

Justice is about threat. Its cards remove threat, punish schemes, and turn thwarting into extra value.

**Core examples:** For Justice! removes 3 threat, or 4 if you paid with a mental resource. Heroic Intuition gives a hero +1 THW. Great Responsibility lets your hero take threat as damage instead of placing it on a scheme. The Daredevil ally deals 1 damage after he thwarts, and Jessica Jones gets stronger with each side scheme in play.

**Best with:** heroes with weak THW, like Spider-Man, and any team that keeps losing to the scheme. In multiplayer, one Justice player can free the rest of the table to focus on damage.

## Leadership: build a team

Leadership is allies. Its cards put allies into play, make them stronger, and use them more than once.

**Core examples:** Inspired gives an ally +1 THW and +1 ATK. Get Ready readies an ally, so it can act twice. Make the Call puts an ally from any player's discard pile into play under your control for its printed cost. The Triskelion raises your ally limit by 1. Maria Hill draws a card for each player, and Vision can boost his own THW or ATK.

**Best with:** heroes who like to stay in alter-ego form or who have strong resources, because allies act in either form. The weakness is consequential damage: allies wear down, so a Leadership deck needs a steady stream of them.

## Protection: stay standing

Protection is defense. Its cards prevent damage, punish attackers, cancel encounter cards, and heal.

**Core examples:** Armored Vest gives a hero +1 DEF. Counter-Punch deals your hero's ATK in damage to an enemy after your hero defends. Indomitable readies your hero after it defends, so you can defend and still act. Get Behind Me! cancels a treachery's "When Revealed" effects and makes the villain attack you instead. The Black Widow ally can cancel a revealed encounter card, though another one is revealed in its place, and Luke Cage enters with a tough status card.

**Best with:** heroes with good DEF or retaliate, like Black Panther, and solo players who face every attack alone. The weakness is speed: Protection decks are hard to kill but can be slow to win.

## Basic cards: the glue

Basic cards go in any deck, whatever its aspect. They are the general tools: card draw, resources, and flexible effects.

**Core examples:** Nick Fury enters play and then lets you remove 2 threat, draw 3 cards, or deal 4 damage, and he leaves at the end of the round. Avengers Mansion lets a player draw a card each round. Helicarrier makes a card cheaper. Energy, Genius, and Strength are resource cards limited to one per deck. Haymaker, First Aid, and Emergency cover small gaps.

Each aspect also has a resource card, such as The Power of Justice, that doubles the resources it makes when paying for a card of that aspect. These are limited to two per deck.

## How to choose

Ask one question: which clock does my hero not cover?

- A hero with high ATK and low THW wants Justice, or doubles down with Aggression if a teammate is on threat duty.
- A hero with good THW and low ATK wants Aggression.
- A fragile hero, or anyone playing solo, should look at Protection.
- A hero with lots of resources and alter-ego value pairs well with Leadership.

In a team, spread the aspects out. Two Justice players will rarely lose to threat, but they may never kill the villain.

A few later heroes change these rules with deckbuilding requirements printed on their identity card. Follow the card. The [deckbuilding lesson](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-deckbuilding/) covers the full rules.
