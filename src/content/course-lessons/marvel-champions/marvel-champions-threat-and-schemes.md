New teams lose more games to the main scheme than to damage. The villain's hit points are big and obvious. The threat clock moves a little each round, and by the time it looks dangerous it is often too late.

The fix is counting. Threat comes from a few known sources, and you can add them up before the villain phase starts.

## Where threat comes from

Every round, threat lands on the main scheme from these sources, in this order:

1. **Acceleration.** In step one of the villain phase, the main scheme gains its acceleration value, plus 1 for each acceleration icon on cards in play and each acceleration token. Most main schemes print acceleration per player.
2. **Schemes.** The villain schemes once for each player in alter-ego form, and each engaged minion schemes against a player in alter-ego form. A villain's scheme adds its SCH plus its boost card's icons.
3. **Encounter cards.** Some treacheries make the villain scheme or place threat directly. Cards with incite place threat on the main scheme when revealed.

Side schemes are separate. They enter play with their own threat, and they do not complete the main scheme. But they carry icons that make the main scheme harder, and some of them, if left alone, end the game or hurt badly.

## Count it a round ahead

Use the Rhino scenario as a worked example, with two players. The Break-In! has a target of 7 per player, so 14, and an acceleration of 1 per player, so 2 per round.

- **Both heroes in hero form, no side schemes:** 2 threat per round. From 0, you have about seven rounds before the scheme completes, with no thwarting at all.
- **One player rests in alter-ego form:** Rhino schemes once, for his SCH of 1 plus a boost card. The Rhino, Standard, and Bomb Scare sets together average close to one boost icon per card, so expect about 2 more. That round costs about 4.
- **Bomb Scare in play:** its acceleration icon adds 1 more every round until you clear it.

Now you can plan. If the scheme is at 8 of 14 and you expect 4 this round, you are at 12 with one round left. Somebody thwarts now.

## Which scheme to clear first

Side schemes carry three icons, and the icon tells you how urgent each one is:

- **Crisis:** while any crisis icon is in play, player cards cannot remove threat from the main scheme. Clear crisis schemes first whenever the main scheme is getting close.
- **Acceleration:** adds 1 threat to the main scheme every round. The longer it stays, the more it costs. Clear it early.
- **Hazard:** deals one more encounter card each round. That means more minions, more treacheries, more trouble. Clear it when the table is getting buried.

Some side schemes have their own effects, too. Klaw's "Immortal" side scheme gives him 10 hit points until it is gone, so clearing it is also damage.

A rule of thumb that holds up: a side scheme with a crisis icon is a lock on the main scheme, so it outranks almost everything else when the main scheme is high.

## How to thwart efficiently

Thwarting is spending. Every point of THW you put into a scheme is a point not going into the villain. Be efficient:

- **Use the cheapest tools first.** Allies thwart and then take consequential damage, which is often a good trade for a cheap ally.
- **Thwart side schemes to zero.** A side scheme with 1 threat left still has its icon. Finish it.
- **Thwart the main scheme only when needed.** Threat on the main scheme does nothing until it reaches the target. If you are safe for three rounds, use those three rounds to hit the villain.
- **Know which stage is the last.** In a scenario with several main scheme stages, completing an early stage advances the deck instead of ending the game. Excess threat does not carry over, but acceleration tokens do, and the new stage often has a When Revealed effect. In the core set, only the final stage loses the game.

## When the encounter deck runs out

Each time the encounter deck is empty, it reshuffles and you add an acceleration token. In a long four-player game this can happen more than once, and each token is permanent. If your team is playing slowly, those tokens add up, which is one more reason to win the damage race.

## Hero form keeps the clock slow

The biggest threat lever is form. Each player in alter-ego form during the villain phase gives the villain a scheme instead of an attack. Pair this lesson with [when to flip](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-hero-vs-alter-ego/): stagger your rests, and rest on rounds when the scheme can afford it.
