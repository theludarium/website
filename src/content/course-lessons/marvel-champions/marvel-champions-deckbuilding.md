The pre-built decks in the Learn to Play are a fine start, but sooner or later you will want to build your own. The rules are short. They live in Appendix I of the Rules Reference, and here they are in plain words.

## The deckbuilding rules

1. **One identity.** Choose exactly one hero.
2. **40 to 50 cards.** Your deck has at least 40 and at most 50 cards. The identity card does not count, and neither do cards with the permanent keyword.
3. **Your hero's whole set.** Include every identity-specific card that comes with your hero, in the exact quantity it comes with. In the core set, that is 15 cards per hero. Since version 1.8, if one of those cards matches a teammate's hero, you may swap it for a team-up card that names both heroes.
4. **One aspect.** You may choose one aspect: Aggression, Justice, Leadership, or Protection. The rest of the deck comes from that aspect and from Basic cards.
5. **Three copies.** No more than three copies of any card, counted by title.
6. **Unique cards.** No more than one copy of each unique card, counting your identity too. Two unique cards with the same title but different subtitles or alter-egos can both go in.
7. **Card limits.** Some cards print their own limit, such as "Max 1 per deck."
8. **Follow the identity card.** If your hero prints deckbuilding requirements, follow them.

Your nemesis set and obligation are not part of your deck. They go into the encounter deck setup, as covered in the [first lesson](/academy/marvel-champions/how-to-play-marvel-champions/).

## Why 40 cards

You can go up to 50, but most builders stay at 40, and the math explains why. Your hero's 15 cards are the strongest in the deck, so a smaller deck draws them more often. A 40-card deck has 15 of 40 hero cards, or 37.5 percent. A 50-card deck has 15 of 50, or 30 percent. Unless your hero needs a specific large package, extra cards dilute it.

## A first deck: Spider-Man, Justice

This deck uses only one core set. Spider-Man has a weak THW of 1, and Justice covers it.

**Spider-Man's 15 cards:** Black Cat, Aunt May, Swinging Web Kick (3), Backflip (2), Enhanced Spider-Sense (2), Spider-Tracer (2), Web-Shooter (2), and Webbed Up (2).

**Justice, 15 cards:**
- For Justice! (3)
- Great Responsibility (3)
- Heroic Intuition (2)
- Surveillance Team (2)
- The Power of Justice (2)
- Daredevil
- Jessica Jones
- Interrogation Room

**Basic, 10 cards:**
- Nick Fury
- Mockingbird
- Avengers Mansion
- Helicarrier
- Energy, Genius, and Strength (one each)
- Emergency (2)
- Haymaker

That is 40. Spider-Man's attacks come from his own set, so the Justice cards handle threat, and Basic adds draw, cheaper cards, and two strong allies. Heroic Intuition is limited to one in play per player, so two copies give you a good chance to find it without dead draws.

## Count your resources

Every card is also a resource, so a deck is a budget. A few checks:

- **Average cost.** If many cards cost 3 or more, you will spend whole turns discarding to pay. Keep plenty of cards at 0 to 2.
- **Resource types.** Look at what your best cards need. Swinging Web Kick has no type requirement, but For Justice! is better paid with a mental resource. Peter Parker makes a mental resource once per round, which helps.
- **Dedicated resource cards.** Energy, Genius, and Strength make two resources of their type each. The Power of Justice makes double when paying for a Justice card.

## Game environments

The Rules Reference also offers game environments, still marked as a beta. They limit which products you build from:

- **Current:** the core set plus the most recent products. In version 1.8 that means the Age of Apocalypse, Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D., Civil War, and Fear No Evil boxes, ten recent hero packs, and anything released after July 2026. The list moves forward with each update.
- **Legacy:** every product.
- **Limited:** the core set plus up to 3 campaign or custom scenario expansions and up to 4 hero packs of your choice. Version 1.8 cut the hero pack allowance from 10 to 4.

None of this is required. It is a way to keep deckbuilding fresh, and a fair way to compare decks with friends.

## Tools that help

Fan deckbuilders do the counting for you. MarvelCDB, an unofficial card database, adds your hero's set for you, shows your deck size as you build, and shares decks other players have built. The [online play and deckbuilders lesson](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-online-and-deckbuilders/) covers it in more depth.
