Marvel Champions: The Card Game is a cooperative card game for 1 to 4 players from Fantasy Flight Games. Each player is one hero with their own deck, and the whole table works together against one villain run by the game itself. It is a Living Card Game: no random packs, just fixed boxes.

This lesson follows the Rules Reference, version 1.8 (2026), and the core set's Learn to Play booklet. When they disagree, the Rules Reference wins. Fantasy Flight Games posts the current Rules Reference at [fantasyflightgames.com/marvel-champions-rules](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/marvel-champions-rules).

## How do you win, and how do you lose?

You win together when you defeat the villain's final stage. You lose together when threat on the final main scheme stage reaches its target, or when every hero is defeated.

That gives every game two clocks. Damage on the villain is your clock. Threat on the main scheme is theirs.

## How do you set up?

![A schematic two-player Marvel Champions setup: the villain, main scheme, encounter deck, and token pool at the top, and each player's identity card, deck, discard pile, and hand below](/academy/marvel-champions/setup.svg)

The Rules Reference lists 16 setup steps. In short:

1. **Pick heroes.** Each player takes an identity card and puts it alter-ego side up, then sets their hit point dial to the number on that card.
2. **Pick a first player** and give them the first player token.
3. **Set aside** each hero's obligation card and nemesis set.
4. **Shuffle each player deck** and make a pool of damage, threat, and acceleration tokens, plus the stunned, confused, and tough status cards.
5. **Choose a scenario.** Put the villain deck and main scheme deck in the middle. Set the villain's hit point dial: the printed number is per player, so double it for two players.
6. **Build the encounter deck.** Shuffle the encounter sets listed on side 1A of the main scheme with the obligations you set aside.
7. **Resolve the scenario setup.** Do what side 1A says, flip it to 1B, and resolve anything on the villain card.
8. **Draw your hand** up to your hand size, shown near the bottom of your identity card. Each player may then mulligan once: discard any cards and draw back up.

The Learn to Play suggests Rhino for your first game. The core set also has Klaw and Ultron, each harder than the last.

## What do you do on your turn?

Each round has a player phase and a villain phase. In the player phase, each player takes a full turn in player order. On your turn you may do any of these, in any order, as often as you can pay:

- **Change form** once, flipping between hero and alter-ego.
- **Play cards** from your hand: allies, upgrades, supports, and events.
- **Use a basic power.** In hero form, exhaust your hero to attack (deal damage equal to ATK) or thwart (remove threat equal to THW). In alter-ego form, exhaust to recover (heal hit points equal to REC).
- **Use your allies** to attack or thwart. Each one exhausts to do it.
- **Trigger action abilities** on cards you control.

You can hold up to three allies. After an ally attacks or thwarts, it takes consequential damage shown under its ATK or THW.

## How do you pay for cards?

Cards cost resources, and you make resources by discarding other cards from your hand. Each card shows its resource icons in the bottom-left corner: energy, mental, physical, or wild, which counts as any type. Extra resources beyond the cost are lost.

That is the central tension of the game. Every card is either something to play or fuel to play something else.

When everyone has taken a turn, each player discards down to or draws up to their hand size, then readies every exhausted card.

## What happens in the villain phase?

The villain phase has a fixed order:

1. **Place threat** on the main scheme equal to its acceleration value, plus 1 for each acceleration icon and acceleration token in play.
2. **Enemies activate.** In player order, the villain activates once against each player, then each minion engaged with that player activates. If you are in hero form, they attack you. If you are in alter-ego form, they scheme, placing threat on the main scheme.
3. **Deal encounter cards.** Each player gets one facedown encounter card. Each hazard icon in play adds one more card for the table, dealt in player order.
4. **Reveal encounter cards** one at a time, in player order. Minions engage the player who revealed them. Treacheries resolve and are discarded. Side schemes enter play with threat.
5. **Pass the first player token** clockwise.

## What are boost cards?

Each time the villain activates, it gets one facedown card from the encounter deck as a boost card. After any defender is declared, flip it and add its boost icons to the villain's ATK or SCH. Some boost cards also have a star icon, which means a boost ability you resolve. Minions get a boost card only if they have the villainous keyword.

When an enemy attacks, one character may defend. A hero exhausts and reduces the damage by their DEF. An ally exhausts and takes the whole attack.
