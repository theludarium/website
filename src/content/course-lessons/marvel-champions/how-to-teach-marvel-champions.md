Marvel Champions teaches well because each player only has to learn one hero. The shared rules are short. The trouble spots are the villain phase, which new players have never seen, and paying for cards by discarding other cards, which feels wrong the first time.

This script takes about ten minutes. Print the [Marvel Champions teach sheet](/academy/marvel-champions/teach-sheet/) and put it in the middle of the table. For general teaching habits, see [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/).

## Before anyone sits down

Set up the game yourself. Use the Rhino scenario with its recommended modular set, Bomb Scare, and give each player a pre-built core set deck. The Learn to Play lists those decks, and each one pairs a hero with one aspect.

Good first heroes are the ones with simple plans. Spider-Man draws a card each time the villain attacks him. Captain Marvel turns energy into card draw. She-Hulk hits hard and is tough to knock down. Save Iron Man for a later game: his hand starts small and grows as he plays tech upgrades, which takes some planning.

Set each villain dial to its per player number before you start talking.

## The script, in order

**1. The goal (30 seconds).** "We all win or lose together. We win if we knock out Rhino's last stage. We lose if threat on this card reaches this number, or if all of us go down."

Point at the main scheme and its target. Then say: "So there are two clocks. Our damage on Rhino, and his threat on the scheme."

**2. Your hero (2 minutes).** "Your identity card has two sides. Right now you are the alter-ego, the person. On your turn you can flip once to become the hero."

Then the trade-off: "As a hero you attack, thwart, and defend, and Rhino attacks you. As the alter-ego you heal, and Rhino schemes instead of attacking. Both sides have a hand size printed near the bottom."

**3. Paying for cards (2 minutes).** "Cards cost resources. To pay, you discard other cards from your hand. The icon in the bottom-left corner is what each card pays. Wild pays for anything. Extra is lost."

Then let them try it with one real card in hand. This lands faster by doing than by hearing.

**4. Your turn (1 minute).** "On your turn, do anything in any order: play cards, attack, thwart, use allies, flip once. Attacking or thwarting with your hero exhausts it. At the end of the round we all draw back up to hand size and ready everything."

**5. The villain phase (3 minutes).** Walk it on the table:

1. "Threat goes on the main scheme."
2. "Rhino activates once against each of us, in order. If you are the hero, he attacks you. If you are the alter-ego, he schemes. He gets one facedown boost card each time, flipped after you choose whether to defend."
3. "Each of us gets one encounter card. We flip them in order. Minions engage whoever revealed them, and they activate against that player every round too."
4. "The first player token moves left."

**6. Defense (1 minute).** "When something attacks, one person can defend. A hero exhausts and blocks damage equal to their DEF. An ally exhausts and takes the whole hit. Or you take it."

**7. Start (30 seconds).** "We talk freely. Show your hand or not, it's up to you. Let's go."

## What to leave for later

Hold these until they come up: obligations, nemesis cards, side scheme icons like crisis and hazard, keywords like guard and surge, and status cards. Each card explains itself when it arrives, and you can read the keyword together. Explain stunned, confused, and tough the first time one is placed, in one line each: stunned cancels the next attack, confused cancels the next thwart or scheme, tough prevents the next damage.

## Run the first round

Make the first player phase slow and public. Ask each player what they plan before they play, and point out when a card is better as fuel than as a play. In the villain phase, flip everything face up and narrate.

The co-op trap is one experienced player running everyone's turn. Each player has their own deck and their own hand, which helps, but a host can still slide into steering. Ask questions instead of giving orders: "What do you want to do about that minion?" For more on this, read [the quarterback problem in cooperative games](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/).

## After the game

Ask two questions. Which clock nearly beat us? And when did someone flip at the wrong moment? Those two answers set up the next three lessons in this course.
