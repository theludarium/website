The Learn to Play booklet in the core set is a good teacher, but it is not the full rulebook. The Rules Reference is, and when the two disagree, the Rules Reference wins. Fantasy Flight Games updates it every so often. The current version is 1.8, from 2026, and it changed a few rules that older videos and guides still teach the old way.

Every point below was checked against the Rules Reference. Get a copy from [fantasyflightgames.com/marvel-champions-rules](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/marvel-champions-rules).

## Forms

**Mistake: flipping more than once a turn, or on someone else's turn.** You can change form once per round, during your own turn. A card that flips you, such as an obligation, does not use up that one change.

**Mistake: expecting healing in hero form.** The basic recover power belongs to the alter-ego. Your hero can still heal with card abilities, like Captain Marvel's.

**Mistake: thinking a flip resets your hero.** When you change form, only the form changes. Damage, status cards, attachments, and whether you are exhausted all stay.

## The villain phase

**Mistake: one villain activation per round.** The villain activates once per player, in player order. In a three-player game, it activates three times.

**Mistake: flipping the boost card too early.** The villain gets its facedown boost card when it activates, but you turn it face up only after a defender is declared. Defend or not first, then find out how bad it is.

**Mistake: giving minions boost cards.** Only the villain gets a boost card by default. A minion gets one only if it has the villainous keyword.

**Mistake: one encounter card for the whole table.** Each player is dealt one encounter card every villain phase, plus one more for each hazard icon in play. Hazard extras are dealt one per icon in player order, not one per player per icon.

## Defense

**Mistake: two defenders on one attack.** Only one character defends an attack. A hero reduces the damage by their DEF and takes the rest. An ally takes all of it.

**Mistake: refusing to defend for a friend.** You can defend an attack that targets another player, with your hero or one of your allies. The Rules Reference allows it.

**Mistake: losing tough to a fully blocked attack.** When a hero with a tough status card defends, DEF reduces the damage first. If that brings it to 0, the hero keeps the tough card.

## Status cards

**Mistake: skipping the cost of a stunned attack.** If a stunned character tries to attack, you discard the stunned card instead, but you still pay the costs, including exhausting. Confused works the same way for thwarting, and for a villain or minion it cancels the next scheme.

## Decks and hands

**Mistake: ignoring an empty player deck.** When your deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck, then deal yourself one facedown encounter card. Big card-draw turns have a price.

**Mistake: ignoring an empty encounter deck.** When the encounter deck runs out, shuffle its discard pile into a new deck and put an acceleration token next to the main scheme. That token adds 1 threat every round for the rest of the game, and it carries over when the main scheme advances.

**Mistake: hoarding resources.** Excess resources are lost. If you discard a card with two resource icons to pay a cost of 1, the second resource is gone.

**Mistake: a second mulligan.** You mulligan once, at setup. After that, the end of each player phase is when you discard down to, or draw up to, your hand size.

## What changed in version 1.8

Fantasy Flight Games announced Rules Reference 1.8 in July 2026. Three changes matter at every table:

- **Surge.** A card with surge now deals you one more facedown encounter card, which goes to the end of the cards waiting to be revealed. You still resolve every dealt card before the villain phase ends. Older guides say to reveal the extra card right away.
- **Overkill.** Excess damage from an overkill attack is no longer copied. It moves on: from an ally to its controller's hero, or from a minion to the villain. If tough prevents the damage, there is no overkill.
- **Retaliate.** Retaliate is now a forced response that deals damage to the attacker after the character is attacked. Because it is a forced response, you choose its order alongside your other forced responses.

The update also added a "calculate damage" step to enemy attacks, after boost cards resolve and before damage is dealt, and changed some card text through errata. Fantasy Flight's [announcement post](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2026/7/23/mission-updates/) lists all of it.

## A rules habit for the table

Keep the Rules Reference on a phone. It is organized like a glossary, so look up the keyword or term rather than searching the Learn to Play. The fan site MarvelCDB also has rules and FAQ pages, which help with one-card questions. Settling a rule in thirty seconds beats arguing about it for five minutes.
