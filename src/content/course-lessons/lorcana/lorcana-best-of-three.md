Store nights often play single games. Competitive events play matches. Premier Play events are always best of three, and Set Championships run 60-minute, best-of-three Swiss rounds. A match changes how you play, from the first choice to the last turn.

## Match points

In Swiss rounds, a match win is worth 3 match points, a draw 1, and a loss 0. A match is recorded by games won, lost, and drawn. Winning 2 to 0 and 2 to 1 count the same. A match where one player wins a game and the other game is unfinished at time is recorded as 1-0-1, a win for the player with the game.

So a draw is far worse than it looks: it gives each player a third of a win. Finishing your matches is a skill.

## Play or draw

Before the first game, the two players decide at random who chooses. That player picks whether to play first or draw first. After each game, the loser of that game makes the choice for the next. The choice must be made before either player looks at an opening hand. A player who cannot decide is treated as playing first. In a single-elimination top cut, the higher seed from the Swiss rounds makes the first choice instead.

The player going first skips the draw on their first turn. So the choice is a trade: one extra card for the second player, against one turn's head start for the first.

Lorcana is a race, and you know from the lore clock lesson that a tied race goes to whoever moves first. That is why going first is usually the stronger choice, especially for decks that want to quest early. Some slower decks, built to trade and outlast, may value the extra card more against an aggressive opponent. Decide before the event what your deck prefers in each matchup you expect, so you are not guessing at the table.

## Round time

Swiss rounds run at least 50 minutes, with 60 the recommended maximum. Set Championship Swiss rounds are 60 minutes. Single-elimination rounds are recommended to be untimed; if a time limit is needed, it should be at least 70 minutes and announced before the cut begins.

That means a best-of-three has about 20 minutes per game. Lorcana games can run longer than that between careful players. Pace matters.

## When time is called

If a game is still going when time is called, the rules give it a short ending:

1. The active player finishes the current turn. This is turn 0.
2. Then five more turns are played in total, counting both players' turns.
3. If someone reaches 20 lore or the game otherwise ends, report it normally.
4. If the game is unfinished after turn five, the player with more game wins in the match wins it. If game wins are equal, the match is a draw in Swiss.

In single elimination, a tied match plays five more turns, starting a new game if needed. If game wins are still level, the player with more lore in the current game wins, and if lore is also tied, play continues until one player has more.

## Pacing a match

- **Shuffle well, but promptly.** Rounds start the moment pairings go up.
- **Decide your mulligan rule in advance.** The odds lesson gives you a method; use it without agonizing.
- **Think on your opponent's turn.** Plan your next turn while they take theirs.
- **Do not concede too late or too early.** If a game is lost, conceding early leaves time to win the next two. But if the clock is close, playing it out may be your best chance.
- **Watch the time between games.** If game one ran long, the next game needs to be decisive. Lean toward the faster line.

## Between games

Use the minute between games well:

- Remind yourself what beat you, or what won.
- Remember who chooses play or draw.
- Shuffle thoroughly, and offer your deck for a cut.

The Tournament Rules (July 14, 2026) describe no sideboard: you register one deck list, deck checks compare your deck against it, and that is the deck you play every game. Your adjustments between games are in how you play, not what you play.

## Practice the whole match

At home or at weekly play, set a timer and play matches, not games. You will learn how long your deck takes, where you lose minutes, and what the end-of-round procedure feels like before it ever decides a real match.
