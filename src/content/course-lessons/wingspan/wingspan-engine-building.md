By round 2, every player has a few birds on their mat and a rough shape to their game. Some have a forest that pours out food. Some have a wetland that draws three cards a turn. The opening was about getting your engine started. The midgame is about running it, and about deciding when to stop adding parts.

## Count your turns

The turn count is fixed: 8, 7, 6, and 5. At the start of round 2 you have 18 turns left. At the start of round 3, 11. At the start of round 4, just 5.

That count is your most useful midgame number. A brown power is worth roughly what it gives you, times the number of times you will still use its row. A power that gains 1 food is a strong bird in round 1 and a weak one in round 4. Counting turns turns that hunch into arithmetic.

## What a working engine looks like

In Wingspan, an engine is a row where one action does several things. Take a forest with three birds in it. Your cube lands on the fourth slot: 2 food from the birdfeeder, with the option to discard a card for a third. Then the cube slides back across three birds, and any brown powers among them fire.

You do not need all three rows to work like that. Many strong boards have one main engine row, a second row that supports it, and a third that mostly holds birds for points and eggs.

## Depth costs eggs

Going deep in one row makes its action bigger and fires more powers each use. It also costs more eggs: a fourth or fifth bird in a row costs 2 eggs to play.

That is the central midgame tension. Eggs spent on bird costs are points you will not have at the end. A bird in column 5 has to be worth its own points plus the 2 points of eggs it ate, or it has to fire enough times to make up the difference.

## Powers that score while they run

Some brown powers do more than feed your engine: they put points on the board each time they fire. Birds that tuck cards or cache food add 1 point per tuck or cached token. In the midgame, these birds turn every use of their row into points as well as resources.

If you have one of these in a row, that row becomes a better choice every turn. Build toward it, and put the next useful bird in the same row so the action grows too.

## Pink powers and the other players

Pink powers are the main way other players' turns reach your mat. Read your opponents' pink powers once, early. If a rival has a bird that gains food whenever another player plays a bird in a certain habitat, your next bird in that habitat feeds them a little.

That does not mean avoid those actions. It means choose the action that is best for you after counting what it hands to someone else. And keep your own pink powers in mind when you pick birds: a pink power that fires off a common action can pay out every round.

## Reading the tray and the feeder

Wingspan has more interaction than its reputation suggests. Stonemaier's own FAQ lists it: the dice you take from the birdfeeder and when you reroll them, the face-up birds you take, the end-of-round goals on the green side, and powers that give other players things.

- **The tray.** Taking a face-up bird means the next player cannot. If a rival clearly needs a wetland bird for the round goal, the one on the tray is worth a look.
- **The feeder.** Dice come out one at a time. Which die you leave behind shapes what the next player can take, and whether every remaining die shows one face and allows a reroll.

Our piece on [luck and skill](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/) calls the birdfeeder reset a release valve on luck. In the midgame, it is also a small lever you hold over the next player.

## Goals in rounds 3 and 4

On the green side, round 3 pays 6, 3, and 2, and round 4 pays 7, 4, and 3. Those two goals are worth more than rounds 1 and 2 combined, so check each rival's count on them during round 2. If someone already has five wetland birds and the round 3 goal is wetland birds, second place may be cheaper for you than a fight for first.

## When to switch from building to scoring

There is no single turn to switch, but a common rhythm:

- **Round 2:** keep building. Play the birds that make your main row stronger.
- **Round 3:** start blending. Play your last strong birds, lay eggs where they also help a goal, and look at your bonus card count.
- **Round 4:** score. The [endgame scoring lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-scoring/) later in this level does that math.

A useful test for any midgame bird: will it fire at least three more times, or is its printed value higher than the eggs it costs? If neither, laying eggs or drawing toward a better bird is usually the stronger turn.
