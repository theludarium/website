Wingspan has no fixed opening. Your five birds are random, the tray is random, and the birdfeeder rolls fresh every few turns. But strong openings still look alike. They start with a hand that can be played, they spend round one growing, and they keep an eye on goals that will not score for an hour.

## The five-card choice

At setup you hold 5 birds and 5 food, and you keep 5 things in total: each bird you keep costs 1 food. That makes the very first decision a trade.

A few guidelines:

- **Keep birds you can play in round one.** A bird that needs three fish you do not have is a card you hold, not a card you use.
- **Keep the food your birds eat.** If two of your birds eat invertebrates, the invertebrate token is worth keeping.
- **Prefer cheap birds early.** A bird with a cost of one food can go down on turn one.
- **Look for brown powers.** A brown power that helps you get food or cards early keeps paying every time you use its row.

Keeping two or three birds with matching food is a common, sturdy start. Keeping five birds and no food can work, but only if the birdfeeder cooperates.

## Choose the bonus card with your birds

You choose your bonus card at the same time as your birds, and you may look at both. Bonus cards print the share of birds in the deck that qualify. A higher share means a steadier card.

The best pick is often the card your kept birds already help. If two of your five birds fit a bonus card, that card is half built before your first turn.

## Read all four goals before turn one

All four end-of-round goals are on the board from setup. On the green side they also grow in value: round 1 pays 4, 1, and 0 for 1st to 3rd, while round 4 pays 7, 4, and 3.

Two takeaways:

- **Round 1's goal is the smallest prize.** Take it if it is cheap, but do not bend your whole opening around it.
- **Plan for rounds 3 and 4 now.** If round 4 counts eggs in a certain nest type, a bird with that nest played in round 1 will be sitting there, ready to hold eggs, when it matters.

## Which row to grow first

Look at your mat. Without any birds, gain food gives 1 die, draw cards gives 1 card, and lay eggs gives 2 eggs. Every bird you add to a row covers a slot and makes that action bigger.

Food is usually the first bottleneck, because every bird needs it. That is why many players grow the forest early. Cards are the second bottleneck: a stronger wetland lets you dig for birds that fit your bonus card and the goals. The grassland already starts at 2 eggs, so it often grows a little later.

Treat this as a default, not a law. Your hand decides. If your best early birds are grassland birds with useful powers, play them.

## Column one is free

The first column of every habitat costs no eggs. So your first bird in the forest, your first in the grassland, and your first in the wetland are all egg-free. Spreading your first few birds across habitats saves eggs; stacking them in one row means paying an egg by the second bird.

There is a counterweight. A deeper row makes its action bigger and fires more powers each time. Most openings balance the two: one or two rows growing, every first bird egg-free.

## Brown powers are worth more early

A brown power fires each time you use its row. Round 1 has 8 turns, and the whole game has 26. A brown-power bird played early in round 1 may fire many times before the game ends. The same bird played in round 4 might fire once.

So in the opening, a modest bird with a good brown power often beats a high-point bird with no power. Points on a card are fixed. A power multiplies with time.

## A rough plan for round one

There is no script, but a sound round one usually mixes:

- **Two to four turns playing birds,** mostly in column 1 or 2, so egg costs stay low.
- **One or two food turns** to afford the next bird.
- **A card turn** if your hand runs thin.
- **Maybe one egg turn,** mainly to pay for a column 2 or 3 bird.

If you end round one with three birds on your mat, your engine is on schedule.

## Watch the first-player token

The first-player token passes clockwise each round, and the bird tray is replaced with 3 fresh birds at the end of each round. So the player who starts the next round gets the first look at a fresh tray. If you are next in line for the token, a card turn can wait until then.
