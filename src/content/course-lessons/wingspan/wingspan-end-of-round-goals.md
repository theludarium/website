Every game of Wingspan shows you four goals before anyone keeps a card. Most beginners read each one when its round ends. That is like reading the exam questions after handing in the paper. This lesson closes out Level 1 with the goals: what they count, how they pay, and how to plan for them without bending your whole game around them.

## What the goals count

The base game has 8 goal tiles, each with a goal on both sides, so 16 goals in all. At setup each tile lands random side up. They come in a few families:

- **Birds in a habitat:** birds in your forest, grassland, or wetland.
- **Eggs in a habitat:** eggs on birds in your forest, grassland, or wetland.
- **Eggs in a nest type:** eggs on birds with bowl, cavity, ground, or platform nests.
- **Birds of a nest type with eggs:** how many of your bowl, cavity, ground, or platform birds hold at least 1 egg.
- **Total birds:** every bird on your mat.
- **Egg sets:** complete sets of one egg in each of your three habitats.

Star nests are wild, so a star-nest bird counts toward every nest goal.

Notice how many goals count eggs. Twelve of the sixteen care about eggs in some way. That is one more reason eggs are never only a cost.

## How they pay

On the green side, players rank on each goal:

| Round | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 5 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 6 | 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 7 | 4 | 3 |

You need at least 1 of the counted item to score. Ties share the points for the places they cover, rounded down; the [scoring lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-scoring/) at Level 2 works through real examples.

On the blue side you score 1 point per item, up to 5, and nobody is ranked.

Two things jump out of the table. First, rounds 3 and 4 together pay 13 for first place, while rounds 1 and 2 pay 9. Second, look at the gaps. The difference between 1st and 2nd is always 3 points. The difference between 2nd and 3rd is just 1. So the big fight is for first. Second and third are close, and a cheap second often beats an expensive first.

## Reading the goals at setup

Before you choose your five birds, read all four tiles and look for overlap.

- **Habitat pairs.** If round 2 counts birds in the wetland and round 4 counts eggs in the wetland, every wetland bird with a decent egg limit serves both.
- **Nest pairs.** If two goals care about the same nest type, a bird with that nest, or a star nest, pulls double duty.
- **Total birds.** This goal rewards cheap birds. It pairs well with a plan to play many small birds early.

One bird that helps two goals is worth a point or two more than its card says. That can decide which of two similar birds you keep.

## When to chase, when to settle

Ask three questions each round:

1. **Where do I stand now?** Count yours and every rival's. It is all face up.
2. **What would first cost me?** A bird I would play anyway costs nothing. A detour into the wrong habitat costs real tempo.
3. **What would second cost?** Often nothing at all.

In round 1 the prize is small: first is worth 4. Take it if it falls out of your plan, but do not spend your opening chasing it. By round 4, first is worth 7, and one extra egg in the right place can be the best point per turn on the board.

## Turn order matters at the end of a round

The goal is scored after everyone places their last cube. The player who acts last in the round sees every final count before deciding. If that is you, you can wait and spend your last turn exactly where it changes a place. If you act early, aim to finish a little ahead, since others can still catch you.

The first-player token passes clockwise each round, so who acts last changes too. Check it at the start of each round.

## The blue side as practice

If your group still plays the blue side, use it to build the habit anyway. Count your items each round and try to reach 5 by the end. When you switch to the green side, the counting will already be automatic, and only the ranking will be new.
