The [tournaments lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-tournaments/) showed where competitive Wingspan happens. This last lesson is about getting ready for it: a plan you can follow over about six weeks, built on the skills from every level of this course.

Rules and format details below come from the World Boardgaming Championships (WBC) [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/wsn.html). Always check the current event page before you go.

## Know what the format rewards

The 2026 WBC event ran three heats, then a quarterfinal, semifinal, and four-player final, with two-hour rounds and four players per table. Heat standings count **wins first**, then place points: 1,000 for a first, 100 for a second, 10 for a third, and 1 for a fourth.

That scoring says something important. A single heat win outranks any number of seconds. So in a heat, play to win:

- **Late in a game you are losing,** take the line with a chance to win, even if it risks third place. A safe second is worth little.
- **Early, play your normal game.** Strong fundamentals still produce the most wins.

Know the in-game tiebreakers too: most unused food, then most eggs, then most birds. Food ended the 2026 final, as the [scoring lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-scoring/) describes.

## Practice under event rules

Set up your practice games the way the event will:

- **Four players.** The tray and feeder move far more between your turns than at two or three.
- **Base game only,** since expansions need every player's agreement.
- **Remove the four set-aside birds:** Chihuahuan Raven, Common Raven, Franklin's Gull, and Killdeer.
- **Green side of the goal board,** since ranked goals and tie math decide close games.
- **A clock.** Aim to finish setup, play, and scoring in well under two hours.

## A six-week plan

**Week 1: baseline.** Play three games under event rules. Fill every scorepad row: birds, bonus card, goals, eggs, cached food, tucked cards. Note your total and your weakest row.

**Week 2: openings.** Deal yourself 5 birds, 2 bonus cards, and 4 random goal tiles. Give yourself two minutes to choose what to keep and sketch round 1. Do it ten times. Then play two games and see whether your keeps held up. The [openings lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-opening-strategy/) and the [bonus card lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-bonus-cards/) are your references.

**Week 3: volume online.** [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=wingspan) runs Wingspan in your browser and has hosted millions of games. It is a Premium game there, so starting a table needs a paid membership, though a free account can join one. Play many four-player games quickly. Focus on one skill per session, such as pricing birds as in the [evaluation lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-bird-evaluation/).

**Week 4: the finish.** In every game, stop at the start of round 4 and write down your five turns before taking them. Afterward, compare the plan to what happened. Most players find several points here.

**Week 5: the table.** Play with people stronger than you if you can. Before each of your turns, name what the next player needs, as in the [reading the table lesson](/academy/wingspan/wingspan-reading-opponents/). Solo games against the Automa on its hardest setting fill gaps between sessions.

**Week 6: rehearsal.** Play two or three full games on a strict two-hour limit with players you do not know well, if possible. Practice the habits that keep a table running: announce your action clearly, call out pink power triggers, and count goals out loud at round end.

## Measure progress

Keep a simple log of every practice game: player count, your score, the winner's score, and each scorepad row. Two benchmarks help:

- **The WBC yardstick.** The 2026 event report put the average winning score at around 95, with a high of 126. If your four-player average climbs toward the 90s, you are in the conversation.
- **Your weakest row.** If eggs or goals keep trailing, that is where the next week's practice goes.

Our article on [tracking plays](/articles/tracking-plays-changes-what-you-buy/) has a simple way to keep that log.

## Table manners count

Tournament tables run on trust. Keep your food visible, since it is public. Take dice one at a time. Ask before touching another player's pieces. If you spot a rival missing their pink power, say so. Good sportsmanship is part of why the WBC event welcomes beginners, and part of why people come back.

## Where to play next

- **The WBC.** Wingspan has been on the program since 2022. Check the event list for dates and the current rules.
- **BPA online tournaments.** The Boardgame Players Association lists them on its [online tournaments page](https://www.boardgamers.org/pbem.html).
- **Your local store and Stonemaier's [demo store map](https://stonemaiergames.com/retailer-locator/demo-stores/).** Four-player practice tables are easier to find than you think.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

That is the end of the course. You can play, teach, read every card, run the feeder, time an engine, count every point, read the table, beat the Automa, and walk into a heat with a plan. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
