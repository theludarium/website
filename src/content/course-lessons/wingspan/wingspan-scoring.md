Wingspan's final scoring rarely holds a surprise for the player who counted. Everything is face up: birds, eggs, goals, even most of the food. In the last round you have only five turns, and each one has a price you can work out. This lesson is that arithmetic.

## Where the points come from

| Source | How it scores |
| --- | --- |
| Birds | The points printed on each bird you played |
| Bonus cards | As printed on each card you hold |
| End-of-round goals | As marked on the goal board |
| Eggs | 1 point each |
| Cached food | 1 point each |
| Tucked cards | 1 point each |

Two things score nothing on their own: food tokens in your supply and bird cards in your hand. One bonus card does count the birds left in your hand, and unused food breaks ties, as you will see below.

## What one round-four action is worth

With five turns left, ask what each action scores now.

**Lay eggs.** Your grassland slot gives 2 to 4 eggs, and some slots let you pay 1 food for one more. Each egg is 1 point, as long as a bird has room for it. Add any grassland brown powers that lay eggs or tuck cards.

**Play a bird.** Count the bird's printed points, minus the eggs it costs. A 6-point bird in column 4 costs 2 eggs, so it nets 4 points, plus whatever its "when played" power adds and any goal or bonus card it helps. Food spent has no point value, so spending food is free in round 4, except for the tiebreaker.

**Gain food.** Worth nothing directly, unless forest powers cache food. It earns its place only if it pays for a bird you will play this round.

**Draw cards.** Also worth nothing directly, unless wetland powers tuck cards, or the bird you draw can be played before the game ends.

That is why the classic round-four pattern is eggs, eggs, and one more bird. It is a pattern, not a rule. A strong tuck or cache row can beat it.

## Check your egg room

Eggs only score if they fit. Before round 4, add up the empty egg spaces on your birds. If you have room for 6 eggs and your grassland lays 4 per turn, only one full egg turn and part of another will pay. After that, the action is worth less than it looks.

Birds with high egg limits are worth more than their printed points for exactly this reason. Some goals also count eggs on birds with a certain nest type, so eggs can score twice: once at the end, once for the goal.

## Goal math, with ties

On the green side, the goals pay 4, 1, 0 in round 1; 5, 2, 1 in round 2; 6, 3, 2 in round 3; and 7, 4, 3 in round 4. You need at least 1 of the counted item to score.

Ties work like this: add the points for the tied place and the next place or places, divide by the number of tied players, and round down. The next place is not awarded to anyone else, and 4th place scores 0.

Here is a real example. At the 2026 World Boardgaming Championships Wingspan final, the [event report](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbook26/WSN.html) lists every goal result:

- **Round 1:** three players tied for 1st. Each scored (4 + 1 + 0) / 3, which is 1 point. The fourth player scored 0.
- **Round 3:** three players tied for 2nd. Each scored (3 + 2 + 0) / 3, which is 1 point.
- **Round 4:** two players tied for 1st. Each scored (7 + 4) / 2, rounded down to 5. The next player took 3rd place, worth 3.

Notice how little a crowded tie pays. Three-way ties for first in round 1 were worth just 1 point each. Breaking a tie by a single bird or egg is often worth more than any other move in the round.

On the blue side, you score 1 point per item, up to 5. No ties to worry about.

## Bonus cards at the finish

Read your bonus card again at the start of round 3. Some cards pay a set amount for each qualifying bird. Others pay in steps, with more points once you reach a larger count. If you are one bird short of the next step, that bird may be worth several points more than its printed value.

Birds that let you draw extra bonus cards are worth checking for too. A second bonus card you can actually score is free points at the end.

## The tiebreaker that decided a championship

At that same WBC final, the top two players both finished on 101 points. The tiebreaker in the rulebook is the most unused food tokens. The winner had 7. The runner-up had 1.

The lesson for your own games: on your last turn, if a food token is not buying you a point, keep it. And when choosing between two actions worth the same points, pick the one that leaves you more food.

## A last-round checklist

1. How many turns do I have left, and how much egg room?
2. What does each of my four actions score right now?
3. Can one bird or egg break a goal tie in my favor?
4. Am I one bird short of my bonus card's next step?
5. Is there spare food I can keep for the tiebreaker?
