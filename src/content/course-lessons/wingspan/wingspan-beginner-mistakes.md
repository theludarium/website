Your first three games of Wingspan are where habits form. Some of those habits are rules played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that quietly cost ten or fifteen points. This lesson covers both, checked against the rulebook, its appendix, and the official Rules and FAQ page.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Reading a bird's eggs as its price.** The egg icons on a bird card show its egg limit: how many eggs it can hold. The egg cost to play it comes from the column it lands in: free in column 1, 1 egg in columns 2 and 3, 2 eggs in columns 4 and 5.

**Using the 2-for-1 food trade everywhere.** Any 2 food can stand in for 1 food only when you play a bird. The rulebook is clear that the trade cannot be used during other parts of the game.

**Grabbing all the dice at once.** Take dice from the birdfeeder one at a time. The FAQ notes this matters: if the dice left all show one face, you may reroll between picks. And the face with two foods gives you one food, not both.

**Refilling the tray mid-turn.** If you draw a face-up bird, its space stays empty until the end of your turn. The next card you draw that turn comes from the two left or the deck.

**Activating a row when you play a bird.** Playing a bird triggers only that bird's own "when played" power. It does not slide a cube through a row or fire brown powers.

**Missing pink powers.** Pink powers fire on other players' turns, once between each of your turns. They are easy to forget because the owner is not the one acting. The FAQ adds one more: your pink power can still fire once after your last turn of the game.

**Thinking benefits are required.** You can always decline a benefit, such as laying fewer eggs or taking less food. You can never decline a cost. And since every power is optional, you can skip a power to skip its cost.

**Spending cached food.** Food cached on a bird is a point at the end. It never goes back to your supply.

**Scoring a goal with none of the item.** On the green side, you need at least 1 of the counted item to score. Ties have their own math, which the endgame lesson walks through.

## The big strategic mistake: building too late

A bird with a brown power played on turn two can fire every time you use that row for the rest of the game. The same bird played in round 4 fires once or twice. Players new to engine builders often spend round 1 laying eggs and grabbing food "to get ready," and their first real engine bird shows up in round 2.

The fix is simple to say and hard to feel the first time: play birds early, even small ones. A bird in column 1 costs no eggs. Your first bird in each habitat is free of eggs, so three cheap birds spread across the forest, grassland, and wetland can all be played without laying a single egg.

## Hoarding cards with no food, or food with no cards

Watch for two opposite habits:

- **The full hand.** Ten birds in hand and two food tokens means you cannot play any of them. Cards are not points until they are on your mat.
- **The full pantry.** Seven food tokens and two birds you cannot afford is the same problem in reverse.

A healthy rhythm alternates: get the food, play the bird, find the next bird.

## Forgetting eggs are points

Every egg on your mat is 1 point at the end. New players treat eggs only as a cost for birds. By round 4, a lay eggs action with a few grassland birds can be worth more points than playing one more bird. The endgame lesson does that math in detail.

## Ignoring the round goal until the round ends

All four goals are face up from the start. New players read the round 1 goal when round 1 ends. Read all four before your first turn, and glance at the current one before every turn. A small detour, such as playing a bird into the forest instead of the wetland, can turn a 0 into a 1st place.

## Picking a bonus card that needs rare birds

Bonus cards print the share of birds in the deck that qualify. A card that counts a common trait is a steadier bet than one that needs a rare trait, unless your opening hand already holds two or three birds that fit.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before each turn, ask:

1. Which row gets stronger if I add a bird to it?
2. What does this round's goal count, and where do I stand?
3. Did my last action trigger anyone's pink power?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong in the first three games. When you are ready for more, our [Wingspan strategy guide](/articles/wingspan-strategy/) picks up where this lesson stops.
