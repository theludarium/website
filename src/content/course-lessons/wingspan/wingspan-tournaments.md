Wingspan has a gentle reputation, and it earns it. It is also a game people compete at seriously, with a championship event that draws a couple of hundred players, online tournaments between conventions, and plenty of ways to play strangers from your couch. You do not need to enter any of them to enjoy them. But knowing how they work sharpens how you play at home.

## How tournament Wingspan differs from home play

Tournament rules vary by event, so always read the event's own page. The World Boardgaming Championships (WBC) is a good model, and its [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/wsn.html) spells out its rules:

- **Four players per table** as much as possible in the heats, and always four in the later rounds.
- **Base game only,** unless every player at the table agrees to add expansions.
- **Four birds set aside.** The Chihuahuan Raven, Common Raven, Franklin's Gull, and Killdeer are removed before play.
- **Tiebreakers written in advance:** most unused food tokens, then most eggs, then most birds played.

Stonemaier also lists an official variant for competitive play: choose the first player before anyone decides which birds and food to keep, so everyone knows the turn order when choosing.

## Why those four birds?

The appendix explains what they do. Both ravens let you discard an egg from another of your birds to gain 2 food. Both the gull and the killdeer let you discard an egg to draw 2 cards. Each is a brown power, so it can fire every time you use its row, and an early one keeps paying all game. The WBC rules call them the Power 4 Birds.

You may not need to remove them at home. But if one player always seems to win with an early raven, now you know why tournaments set them aside. The Oceania rules make a similar suggestion: if nectar makes the two ravens too strong, remove them.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The WBC is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. Wingspan joined in 2022, and its [event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/WSN.html) shows steady growth: 156 players that first year and 217 in 2026.

The 2026 format:

- **Three heats,** then a quarterfinal, semifinal, and a four-player final.
- **Two-hour rounds.**
- **Advancement:** 64 players to the quarterfinal, 16 to the semifinal, 4 to the final.
- **Heat standings** count wins first, then place points: 1,000 for a 1st, 100 for a 2nd, 10 for a 3rd, and 1 for a 4th.
- **Class B, "beginning players welcome."** That makes it one of the friendliest ways to try a real tournament.

The event asks players to bring a copy of the game so there are enough to go around.

In the 2026 final, the top two players tied on 101 points, and the title went to Chad Martin on the unused food tiebreaker. The [event report](https://www.boardgamers.org/yearbook26/WSN.html) puts the average winning score at around 95, with a high of 126. Those numbers make a useful yardstick for your own four-player games.

## Online tournaments

The Boardgame Players Association, which runs the WBC, also holds online tournaments between conventions. Its Wingspan event history lists online champions for 2023, 2024, and 2025. Check the BPA's [online tournaments page](https://www.boardgamers.org/pbem.html) for the next one.

## Playing online

- **[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=wingspan)** has Wingspan in your browser, free to play against other people. It is the quickest way to get many games in.
- **The digital edition by Monster Couch** runs on computers, consoles, and phones, with an AI opponent and the European, Oceania, and Asia expansions available. Stonemaier lists every platform on its [digital versions page](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/wingspan/digital-versions/).
- **Stonemaier's [high score leaderboard](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/wingspan/leaderboard/)** posts top scores by month and player count. It is just for fun, and scores from the digital edition have appeared on it.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Stonemaier's demo stores.** The publisher keeps a [demo store map](https://stonemaiergames.com/retailer-locator/demo-stores/) of shops where you can try its games before you buy.
- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights where Wingspan is a regular. Ask the staff.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups, and some lend games.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play at four.** That is the tournament count, and it changes how fast the tray and feeder move.
- **Use the green goal side.** Ranked goals and tie math are where close games are won.
- **Fill in every scorepad row.** Birds, bonus cards, goals, eggs, food, and tucked cards. Your weakest row is your next lesson.
- **Keep spare food in round 4.** A championship was decided by it.

That is the end of the course. You now know how to play, teach, open, run an engine, count the finish, choose expansions, and find a tournament. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
