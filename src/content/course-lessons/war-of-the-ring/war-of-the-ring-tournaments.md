Welcome to Level 5. War of the Ring has one of the steadiest competitive scenes of any big strategy game, built around a yearly championship and an online community that has played for many years. The good news for newcomers is that the main event is coached: you can learn there. This lesson covers how it runs and where else to play.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) is a yearly tournament convention run by the Boardgame Players Association at Seven Springs, Pennsylvania. War of the Ring has a Century event there, coded WOR.

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/wor.html) lays out the format:

- **Class C, coached.** The GM and assistants answer rules questions, and there is a demo before the event for anyone who wants to learn.
- **Single elimination with a mulligan round.** A mulligan round first, then four rounds, a semifinal, and a final.
- **Four-hour rounds.** Warnings come at 90 and 45 minutes left. Games still running near the limit can be adjudicated by the GM, so slow play is a real risk.
- **Second Edition, no expansions.** Players are asked to bring a copy if they own one, since every two-player table needs a game.

## Bidding for sides

Players do not agree on which side is stronger, and it can depend on who is playing. So tournaments let players bid. In 2026 the WBC used **muster points**. Players bid in half-point steps, and the bid is a number of extra units the opponent gets to place before the game. Each point equals one Muster die's worth of recruits: a Regular or a Leader (or Nazgûl) costs half a point, and an Elite costs one. The preview also limits how those units can be spread across Nations.

In 2025 the event used a different system, **Dwarven Rings**, in which the bid was a number of re-rolls of starting action dice. The [2025 preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc25/previews/wor.html) links to the full rules for it. The system changes, so always read the current preview.

The skill here is real. If you think the Shadow is favored, you bid to play it, and each half point you offer is a small gift to the other side. Practice games with a bid teach you what an extra Elite in Minas Tirith is worth.

## Other events

Ares Games has promoted War of the Ring events at the World Series of Board Gaming, held in Las Vegas. The lineup changes from year to year, so check that year's schedule for whether War of the Ring is on it and how it runs. Local conventions and game stores also run tournaments; the BoardGameGeek forums for the game are the best place to find them.

## Online play

The War of the Ring community has long played online through a fan-made Java client and a Tabletop Simulator module, organized through a community Discord and the War of the Ring guild on BoardGameGeek. The guild page explains how to set up the Java client and links to the online ladder and past online tournaments. Online games are the fastest way to get the volume of play a tournament asks for, and the Java client saves full game logs you can study afterward. The Second Edition rules are also on Dized, where you can search them by topic.

## Finding a game near you

War of the Ring is a two-player game that takes an evening, so finding a regular opponent matters more than finding an event. Try the BoardGameGeek forums and your local game store's bulletin board. For Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## Before your first event

- Read the current preview twice, including the bidding and adjudication rules.
- Play at least two full games under the four-hour limit.
- Know the [rules new players get wrong](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-rules-mistakes/) cold. Rules questions eat clock time.
- Bring your own copy if you can.
