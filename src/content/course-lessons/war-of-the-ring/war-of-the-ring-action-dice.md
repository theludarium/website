In War of the Ring you never choose freely what to do. You roll, and the faces tell you which kinds of action you have this turn. The two sides roll different dice, which is how the game makes Sauron feel like a war machine and the Free Peoples feel like a desperate alliance. Knowing what each face is worth to your side is the first real skill above the basics.

## The two kinds of dice

Each die has six faces. The rulebook's reference chart lists them, and the sides share four kinds of result.

| Face | Shadow die | Free Peoples die |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character | 1 face | 2 faces |
| Army | 1 face | none on its own |
| Muster | 1 face | 1 face |
| Muster/Army | 1 face | 1 face |
| Event | 1 face | 1 face |
| Special | Eye | Will of the West |

The Free Peoples have more Character faces, so their heroes and the Fellowship move more. They have no plain Army face, so their armies move and fight less. The Shadow's Eye goes straight to the Hunt Box. The Free Peoples' Will of the West can become any other result.

## What each face can do

- **Character:** move one army that has a Leader or Character, or attack with it; play a card that shows the Character icon. The Free Peoples can also move or hide the Fellowship, separate Companions, or move every Companion on the map. The Shadow can move all its Nazgûl and Minions.
- **Army:** move up to two different armies, or attack with one; play a card that shows the Army icon.
- **Muster:** move one Nation a step on the Political Track; play a card that shows the Muster icon; recruit troops for a Nation At War. The Shadow also uses Muster to bring a Minion into play.
- **Muster/Army:** either of the above.
- **Event:** draw one card from either of your decks, or play any card from your hand.

A single Muster recruits one Elite, two Regulars, two Leaders (or Nazgûl), or one Regular and one Leader. Two figures always go to two different Settlements.

## The odds of a roll

Simple math helps you plan.

- **Shadow Eyes.** Each Shadow die is an Eye one time in six. With seven dice, you expect a little over one Eye per turn. About 28 percent of rolls have no Eyes, and fewer than 10 percent have three or more.
- **Free Peoples movement.** Half of each Free Peoples die can act as a Character: two Character faces plus Will of the West. With four dice, about 94 percent of rolls give at least one die that can move the Fellowship.

So the Free Peoples can plan on moving the Ring most turns.

## Will of the West

Before taking an action, the Free Peoples may change a Will of the West result into any other result and use it as that. It is also the only face that can bring Aragorn or Gandalf the White into play, under the conditions on their cards. Because it can be anything, it is usually the last die to spend.

## The Elven Rings

The Free Peoples start with three Elven Rings. When it is your action, you can use one to change one of your unused dice to any other result, and then take a normal action. A player can use only one Elven Ring per turn, and the Free Peoples cannot make a Will of the West with one.

After the Free Peoples use a Ring, it flips and goes to the Shadow, who can use it once and then discards it. The Shadow can even turn a die into an Eye, which moves it to the Hunt Box without using an action. Every Ring the Free Peoples spend is a gift to the other side, so spend them for turns that matter.

## Passing and skipping

Players alternate one die each, Free Peoples first. When you have fewer unused dice than your opponent, you can pass instead of acting. The Free Peoples usually have fewer dice, so passing lets them wait and see what the Shadow does before committing. 

## Growing the pool

The Shadow starts with seven dice and can reach ten: one each for Saruman, the Witch-king, and the Mouth of Sauron. The Free Peoples start with four and can reach six with Aragorn and Gandalf the White. A new die joins at the start of the next turn, and a die is lost if its Character is eliminated.

## Cards: the second economy

Each turn both players draw one card from each of their two decks: the Character deck and the Strategy deck. A card can be played with an Event die or with the die face shown on the card. Each card can also be played as a combat card during a battle, which costs no die. The hand limit is six, and a deck that runs out is never reshuffled. Spending an Event die to draw is often right for the Shadow late in the game, when the right card ends a siege.
