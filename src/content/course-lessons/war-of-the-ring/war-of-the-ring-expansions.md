Most War of the Ring players learn and keep playing the two-player base game, and for good reason. It is built as a duel between two very different sides. But the box also supports three and four players, and Ares Games has added several expansions. This lesson covers what each option changes so you can choose with care.

## The base game and the Anniversary Release

The base game is [War of the Ring Second Edition](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1903385113?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), for 2 to 4 players. The Anniversary Release is a deluxe printing of the same game with painted figures and a larger board. It uses the same rules and cards, and the expansions work with both. If you already own the regular Second Edition, you are not missing any rules.

## Two players

Two players is the standard game, and every rule in the rulebook is written for it. Each player runs a whole side. The game's balance and its tournament scene are built around it. Most players rate it best at two, and the [article on player counts](/articles/board-game-player-counts/) explains why some games simply have one count they are built for.

## Three and four players

With more players, the sides become teams, and each player controls certain Nations.

**Four players** split each side in two:

| Side | Player | Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free Peoples | Gondor player | Gondor and the Elves |
| Free Peoples | Rohan player | Rohan, the North, and the Dwarves |
| Shadow | Witch-king player | Sauron, the Witch-king, and the Mouth of Sauron |
| Shadow | Saruman player | Isengard, the Southrons and Easterlings, and Saruman |

**Three players** use the same Shadow split, with one Free Peoples player who plays almost as in the two-player game. The exception: the Free Peoples cannot take two actions in a row with the same Nation.

Some rules change with more players:

- **Leading Player.** Each team has a Leading Player token, which passes between teammates each turn after the first. The Leading Player decides the Fellowship phase and Hunt allocation and rolls the team's dice.
- **Turn order.** Actions go around the table: Free Peoples non-leader, Shadow non-leader, Free Peoples leader, Shadow leader.
- **Cards.** After turn 1, each player draws from one deck of their choice. In the four-player game the hand limit is four. Teammates may agree to swap one card without showing or discussing it.
- **Mixed armies.** When teammates' units share a region, the player with more units controls the combined army.
- **Shadow Elites as Leaders.** While Saruman is in play, Isengard and Southron and Easterling Elite units also count as Leaders.

Team play is a social way to bring more people to the table, and it suits groups where two players know the game and two are learning. It is slower, and the team table talk is part of the fun.

## The expansions

Each expansion adds a layer to the base game and can be combined with the others.

- **[Lords of Middle-earth](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A0Q79RQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** was the first expansion. It brings in characters who appear only on Event cards in the base game, including Elrond, Galadriel, Sméagol, Gothmog, and the Balrog of Moria, along with other versions of Gandalf, the Witch-king, and the Mouth of Sauron. It adds new action dice too.
- **[Warriors of Middle-earth](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01I5GPG7W?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds allied factions for both sides, such as the Ents, the Eagles, the Corsairs, and the Dunlendings, each with its own figures and rules.
- **[Kings of Middle-earth](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CGLZVGTD?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds Sovereigns and Dark Chieftains: the rulers of the Free Peoples, such as Théoden, Denethor, and Thranduil, and Shadow leaders to oppose them. Each side gets a special Ruler action die. Ares later released a limited edition box combining Kings and Lords.

Ares has also announced a fourth expansion, Sieges of Middle-earth, built around the great sieges of the Free Peoples' Strongholds, such as Helm's Deep and Minas Tirith. It gives the Shadow siege weapons and the Free Peoples new Heroic Feats. ICv2 reported in September 2026 that it is due at retail in December 2026. <!-- AFFILIATE: War of the Ring: Sieges of Middle-earth -->

## Which one first?

A defensible order is to match the box to what you want more of:

- **More characters and story:** Lords of Middle-earth.
- **More armies and battlefield variety:** Warriors of Middle-earth.
- **More politics and leaders:** Kings of Middle-earth.

All of them add rules and time to an already long game. The article on [whether expansions are worth it](/articles/expansions-worth-it/) has a general test that applies here: buy one when you have played the base game enough to know what you want changed.

## Expansions and tournaments

Competitive play sticks to the base game. The 2026 World Boardgaming Championships preview states that every game uses the Second Edition with no expansions. If tournament play is your goal, put your practice time into the base box first.

Also note that War of the Ring: The Card Game is a separate, shorter game from Ares, not an expansion. It shares the theme and the two sides but has its own rules.
