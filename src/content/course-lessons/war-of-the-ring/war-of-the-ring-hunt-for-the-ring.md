The Hunt for the Ring decides many War of the Ring games, and it runs on simple dice math. This lesson works through the numbers so that both players know what each Fellowship step risks. All rules here come from the Second Edition rulebook, version 2.3.

## How a Hunt roll works

Each time the Free Peoples move the Fellowship outside Mordor, the Shadow rolls one combat die for each Shadow die in the Hunt Box, up to five. Each 6 is a success. One success is enough for the Hunt to work.

The die the Free Peoples used to move then goes into the Hunt Box. Each Free Peoples die there adds 1 to every Hunt die for the rest of the turn. A 1 always fails.

## The odds table

These are the chances of at least one success, before any re-rolls.

| Hunt dice | First move (6s) | Second move (5+) | Third move (4+) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 17% | 33% | 50% |
| 2 | 31% | 56% | 75% |
| 3 | 42% | 70% | 88% |
| 4 | 52% | 80% | 94% |
| 5 | 60% | 87% | 97% |

Read across a row and the lesson is plain. With two Hunt dice, a first move is found about one time in three. A second move that turn is found more than half the time. A third is found three times in four.

## Re-rolls

The Shadow gets one re-roll of a failed Hunt die for each of these in the region with the Ring-bearers figure: a Shadow-controlled Stronghold, Shadow army units, and Nazgûl. That is up to three re-rolls.

Here is a handy rule of thumb. If every die fails, the Shadow re-rolls one per condition, so a re-roll works like an extra Hunt die when the Shadow has at least that many dice. Two Hunt dice and one re-roll give the same odds as three dice: 42 percent on a first move. That is why the Free Peoples avoid moving while the figure sits next to a Shadow army or a Nazgûl, and why Shadow players fly Nazgûl to the Fellowship's last known region.

## What a success costs

On a success, the Shadow draws a tile from the Hunt Pool. The standard tiles show a damage of 0 to 3, or an Eye. An Eye's damage equals the number of successes rolled, so a big roll hurts more. Some tiles also show a Reveal icon.

The Free Peoples then resolve the damage in order. First they may use a Play on the Table card that reduces it. Next they may use the Guide's ability. Then they may take a casualty: the Guide or a random Companion dies, and his Level comes off the damage. Whatever is left is added to Corruption. Finally, if the tile had a Reveal icon, the Fellowship is revealed.

A casualty is a trade. Losing Gandalf to a 3 saves 3 Corruption but removes the Free Peoples' best Leader from the war. Losing a hobbit to a 1 is cheap. Damage of 0 does nothing, though a Reveal still costs a die later to hide.

## The pool changes as you draw

Outside Mordor, a drawn tile stays out of the pool. Only when the pool is empty do the standard tiles all go back. When the Fellowship enters Mordor, the Eye tiles already drawn go back in, along with any special tiles that cards have put in play.

That means the tiles drawn early shape the Mordor draws. If several 3s came out on the road, the Mordor pool is gentler. If they did not, it is harsher. Keep track of what has been drawn.

## Healing Corruption

Corruption goes down in a few ways. The main one is declaring the Fellowship in a Free Peoples City or Stronghold not under enemy control during the Fellowship phase. That heals 1 point, once per Fellowship phase, and the Fellowship can stay and heal again next turn. Some cards and abilities heal too. Healing costs time, and time is what the Shadow armies want.

## Mordor is different

On the Mordor Track there is no Hunt roll. Every move automatically draws a tile. An Eye tile's damage equals the number of dice in the Hunt Box, Free Peoples dice included. Special tiles with a Stop icon keep the Ring-bearers on the same step. The Fellowship still has to be hidden to move, and Companions there can no longer separate. If a turn passes without the Free Peoples trying to move or hide, the Ring-bearers gain 1 Corruption. Five successful steps reach the Crack of Doom.

So the Free Peoples want to arrive with as little Corruption as possible, and the Shadow wants dice in the Hunt Box every turn the Fellowship is there. Every die in that box raises the cost of each Eye tile.

## The Shadow's budget

For the Shadow, every die put in the Hunt Box is a die not spent on the war. Eyes that roll naturally are free Hunt dice. Many players allocate one or two dice early and adjust: more when the Free Peoples are moving fast, fewer when they are healing or stalled.
