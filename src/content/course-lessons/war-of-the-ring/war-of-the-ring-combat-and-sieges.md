Battles in War of the Ring look like piles of plastic, but the math is learnable. This lesson covers the battle sequence from the Second Edition rulebook, version 2.3, then the numbers that should guide your attacks and defenses.

## The battle round

A battle starts when an army attacks an adjacent region using an Army die, or a Character die if the army has a Leader or Character. Only units of a Nation At War can attack. Each round has five steps, which both players do together.

1. **Combat cards.** Each player may play one Event card as a combat card. The attacker chooses first, then the defender, in secret, and both reveal together. If timing is unclear, the lower initiative number on the card goes first.
2. **Combat roll.** Roll one die per unit, up to five. Each 5 or 6 hits.
3. **Leader re-roll.** Re-roll misses, one per point of Leadership, up to five.
4. **Casualties.** Each hit removes a Regular or turns an Elite into a Regular. Two hits can instead remove one Elite. The attacker removes first.
5. **Cease or retreat.** The attacker may stop. If not, the defender may retreat to an adjacent free region. If neither does, start another round.

Leadership is the number of Leaders or Nazgûl plus each Character's printed Leadership. Leaders never count as casualties, but if every unit in an army dies, its Leaders and Characters die with it.

## Expected hits

Each die hits one time in three. Here is the average number of hits for five combat dice, by Leadership.

| Leadership | Hits on 5+ | Hits on 6 only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1.7 | 0.8 |
| 1 | 2.0 | 1.0 |
| 2 | 2.3 | 1.2 |
| 3 | 2.6 | 1.3 |
| 5 | 2.8 | 1.5 |

Two lessons come out of this. First, the jump from no Leader to two Leaders is worth more than a sixth or seventh unit, because units above five add no dice. Second, hitting only on 6 halves your output, which is what Strongholds do to attackers.

Extra units still matter: they keep your army at five dice while it takes losses. An army of ten keeps rolling five dice through five casualties.

## Elites are armor

An Elite takes one hit by stepping down to a Regular, and two hits to remove outright. So an army of five Elites can absorb ten hits while still rolling five dice for the first five. For the Free Peoples, whose dead never return, that durability is worth more than it first appears.

## Cities, Fortifications, and Strongholds

- **City or Fortification.** In the first round only, the attacker hits on 6. After that, both sides hit on 5 or 6. Osgiliath and the Fords of Isen are the two Fortifications.
- **Stronghold.** Before each round, the defender chooses a field battle or a retreat into the siege. In a siege, the defenders move inside, up to five units plus any number of Leaders, and the attacker may advance into the region around it.

## How a siege works

Once a Stronghold is under siege, attacking it takes an action and is a siege battle. The attacker hits only on 6. The defender hits on 5 or 6 as usual. A siege battle lasts one round, but the attacker can buy each extra round by turning one of their Elites into a Regular.

Put the numbers together. Five attacking dice with two Leaders average about 1.2 hits a round in a siege. Five defenders with a Leader average about 2.0. A Stronghold held by five units, some of them Elite, can take many turns of dice to crack, and each turn spent there is a turn the Fellowship keeps walking.

That is why a Shadow siege usually comes with cards. Some combat cards raise the attacker's dice or let them strike before the roll. Reading your own hand before you commit to a siege is part of the math.

## Sorties and relief

A besieged army can attack out in a sortie. It fights a field battle, so both sides hit on 5 or 6, and if it stops, it goes back inside. Another friendly army in an adjacent region can also attack the besiegers to relieve the siege; the defenders inside do not join that battle. The besieger can bring more troops into the region freely, since it counts as a move, not an attack.

## When to retreat into the walls

Retreating into the siege is not automatic. It caps your army at five units and gives up the region. It is right when the attacker is much bigger, when the field battle odds are bad, or when time matters more than units. A field battle can be right when your army is large with strong Leadership and the attacker is thin. The defender can make this choice again before every round of the attack.

## The political side of battle

Every battle against a Nation's army moves that Nation one step toward At War and activates it if it was passive. Capturing one of its Settlements moves it again. A Shadow attack on a sleeping Nation can wake it before the Shadow is ready, so count that step before you roll.
