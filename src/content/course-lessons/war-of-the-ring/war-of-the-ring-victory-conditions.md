Every War of the Ring game ends one of four ways. Each side has a Ring victory and a military victory, and they pull against each other. A player who only watches one of them usually loses to the other. This lesson lays out all four, then turns them into a plan for your first games.

## The four conditions

The rulebook numbers them, and the numbers matter.

1. **Corruption (Shadow).** The Ring-bearers reach 12 Corruption. The Shadow wins at once, at any moment in the turn.
2. **The Ring is destroyed (Free Peoples).** The Ring-bearers reach the Crack of Doom at the end of the Mordor Track with fewer than 12 Corruption. The Free Peoples win at once.
3. **The Shadow conquers Middle-earth.** At the end of a turn, the Shadow holds Free Peoples Cities and Strongholds worth 10 or more points.
4. **Sauron is banished.** At the end of a turn, the Free Peoples hold Shadow Cities and Strongholds worth 4 or more points.

If two conditions are met in the same turn, the lower number wins. So if the last step into the Crack of Doom also pushes Corruption to 12, the Shadow wins. And if both sides meet a military condition at the same Victory Check, the Shadow's comes first.

## How victory points work

Points come from enemy Settlements you control, shown by your Settlement Control marker. An enemy City is worth 1 point. An enemy Stronghold is worth 2. Towns are worth nothing for victory, though they still matter for mustering.

Points can be lost. If the original owner takes a Settlement back, the marker comes off and the points go with it. That is why the Victory Point Track can move backward, and why a Shadow player at 8 points is not yet safe.

A Stronghold under siege still belongs to the defender. The attacker only scores it once every defending unit inside is gone and the attacker still has a unit in the region.

## Why the Shadow usually wins on the map

Ten points is a lot, but the Shadow has the armies for it, and its losses go back to its reinforcements. The Hunt matters as a tool even when Corruption is not the main goal. Every Companion it kills and every point of Corruption it adds forces the Free Peoples to slow down, heal, or spend dice hiding. That buys time for the armies.

So a common Shadow approach is to treat the military win as the plan and the Hunt as pressure. A Shadow player who puts everything into the Hunt can watch a lucky Fellowship walk into Mordor. A Shadow player who ignores the Hunt can watch an unlucky one do the same. [Shadow strategy](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-shadow-strategy/) in Level 3 goes deeper.

## Why the Free Peoples usually win with the Ring

Four points sounds easy next to ten. But Shadow Strongholds sit deep in enemy land, and Free Peoples units that die never come back. Most Free Peoples wins come from the Ring, with the armies there to buy time. The military threat still matters: a Free Peoples army near a weakly held Shadow Stronghold forces the Shadow to keep units home.

## A first-game plan for the Shadow

- **Turn 1:** Use Muster dice to move Sauron and Isengard to At War. Both start one step away.
- **Hunt:** Allocate one or two dice early, and let Eyes add the rest.
- **Choose a front.** Pick one target region, such as Rohan or Gondor, and send most armies there. Spreading out evenly is the most common way to stall.
- **Count to 10.** Before each turn, list which Settlements would get you there. Strongholds are worth twice as much, but they are much harder to take.

## A first-game plan for the Free Peoples

- **Move the Ring most turns.** One or two moves a turn is a steady pace. A third move invites a Hunt that hits on 4s.
- **Weigh each Companion.** A Companion in the Fellowship can be spent as a Hunt casualty, but he also lets the Shadow allocate one more Hunt die. Separate a Companion when he can do a job, such as waking a Nation or leading an army.
- **Defend Strongholds, not borders.** You cannot hold everything. Keep enough in your key Strongholds that a siege takes many turns.
- **Watch Corruption.** Declaring the Fellowship in a friendly City or Stronghold during the Fellowship phase heals 1 Corruption and activates that Nation.

## Reading the race

The game is a race between the Fellowship's steps and the Shadow's points. Ask two questions every turn. How many turns until the Shadow reaches 10? How many turns until the Ring-bearers finish the Mordor Track? Whichever number is smaller tells you who is winning, and the losing side needs to change something now.
