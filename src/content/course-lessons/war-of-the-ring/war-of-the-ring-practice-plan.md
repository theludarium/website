A War of the Ring game is long, so you will not play a hundred of them before your first event. That makes how you practice matter more than how much. This plan assumes about one full game a week, plus short drills you can do alone. Adjust it to your calendar, but keep the order: rules, both sides, then the clock.

## Week 1: the rules, cold

Reread the Second Edition rulebook from the Hunt chapter to the end. Those chapters hold most of the rules that decide games: the Hunt sequence, revealing, the Mordor Track, and the victory conditions. Then reread the [rules new players get wrong](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-rules-mistakes/).

Play one game as the side you like less. Stop whenever a rules question comes up and settle it from the book, not from memory.

**Solo drill:** set up the board and say aloud every starting army, Nation by Nation, then check against the setup chart.

## Week 2: the Free Peoples

Play a full game as the Free Peoples. Before each turn, write down how many times you plan to move the Fellowship and why. After the game, compare your plans with what happened.

**Solo drill:** with the [Hunt odds table](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-hunt-for-the-ring/) in front of you, roll the Hunt yourself for different situations. Two dice on a second move, three dice with a re-roll, and so on. Feeling the odds in your hands helps you trust them at the table.

## Week 3: the Shadow

Play a full game as the Shadow. Before the first turn, write the exact Settlements that would give you 10 points. Track how many turns each one takes. Most players learn here that one front taken fast beats three fronts taken slowly, as the [Shadow strategy](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-shadow-strategy/) lesson argues.

**Solo drill:** set up a siege of Minas Tirith with your usual attacking army and roll it out round by round. Count how many Shadow dice and Elites it costs to take.

## Week 4: the bid

Play one game with the tournament bid. Use the current World Boardgaming Championships system; in 2026 that was muster points, where the winning bidder's opponent places extra units before the game. Bid for the side you think is stronger, then play it. Afterward, ask whether the units you gave away mattered and where.

Try a second game with the bid reversed if you can find the time. The point is to learn your own price for each side.

## Week 5: the clock

Play a full game under a four-hour limit, with warnings at 90 and 45 minutes left, as the WBC event does. Notice where your time goes. Common time sinks are big decisions about which die to use and long battle sequences. Two habits help: decide the die before the action, and roll battles quickly once the cards are chosen. For more on long turns, see [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/).

**Solo drill:** take one turn's roll from a past game and time how long it takes you to plan all your dice. Then do it again faster.

## Week 6: review and rest

Go back over your notes from weeks 2 to 5. Find the turn in each game where it slipped away. Look for patterns: are you moving the Fellowship too fast, spreading Shadow armies, spending Elven Rings early, or losing Strongholds you could have held longer?

Play one last relaxed game as whichever side you plan to bid for. Then stop. A tired player makes more mistakes over four hours than a rusty one.

## At the event

- Arrive early and help set up. Bring your own copy if you have one.
- Ask the GM about anything in the preview you are unsure of before the first round.
- Track Corruption, points, and the Hunt Pool openly. Mistakes at a tournament table cost both players time.
- Win or lose, write your one-sentence review that night.

## After the first event

The War of the Ring community plays all year, mostly online. Keep up one game a week and alternate sides, and you will arrive at your second event a much stronger player. The [tournament lesson](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-tournaments/) lists where to find games, and for local tables in Indianapolis, the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) gives first word on learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

You have finished the course. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point, so thank your opponent, win or lose.
