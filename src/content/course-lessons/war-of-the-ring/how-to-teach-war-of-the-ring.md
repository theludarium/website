War of the Ring has a reputation as a game you cannot teach in one evening. It is long, but it is not hard to start. The rules fall into two threads that match the books: a hidden race to destroy the Ring, and a war across the map. If every rule you explain hangs on one of those threads, a new player can follow it.

Print the [War of the Ring teach sheet](/academy/war-of-the-ring/teach-sheet/) for the table, set up before your guest arrives, and use the script below. Budget about 20 minutes for the teach, and expect the first game to run long. The [general guide to teaching a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) covers the habits that help with any game.

## Which side should a new player take?

Give a new player the **Shadow**. It has seven dice, big armies, and clear goals: hunt the Ring, and take Cities and Strongholds. The Free Peoples ask for more juggling, since four dice must cover both the Fellowship and the armies, and a slip can end the game in one bad Hunt draw. If your guest knows the books and wants to carry the Ring, let them, but say plainly that it is the harder seat for a first game.

## The host script

**1. The story (one minute).** "You are Sauron. I am everyone trying to stop you. You win by taking enough of my Cities and Strongholds, or by corrupting the Ring-bearers until they give in. I win by walking the Ring into Mount Doom, or, much less often, by taking your fortresses."

**2. The map (three minutes).** Point to Rivendell, Minas Tirith, Helm's Deep, Lórien, Erebor, and then Mordor. Show the difference between a Town, a City (1 point), and a Stronghold (2 points). Say the targets out loud: "you need 10 points, I need 4."

**3. The Ring thread (five minutes).** Show the Ring-bearers in Rivendell and the Progress counter. "When I move the Fellowship, this counter goes up, but the figure stays put. You know roughly where I am, not exactly." Then the Hunt: "Dice in the Hunt Box are your searchers. Every time I move, you roll them. A 6 finds me, and you draw a tile that kills a Companion or adds Corruption. If I move twice in one turn, you hit on 5 or 6." Hold up the Corruption track: 12 is a Shadow win.

**4. The war thread (five minutes).** "An army attacks a neighboring region. Roll a die per unit, up to five, and hit on 5 or 6. Leaders and Nazgûl let you re-roll misses." Then politics: "Most of my Nations are asleep. They cannot muster or attack until they reach At War on this track. Some of yours are close already." Point at Sauron and Isengard, one step from At War.

**5. The dice (four minutes).** Now the action dice make sense. Hold up one die of each color and name each face. "Every turn, we take turns spending one die. Your Eyes go straight to the Hunt Box, so a roll full of Eyes is a turn full of searching." Show the Will of the West face on a blue die.

**6. Cards (one minute).** "Each turn you draw one card from each deck. A card can be played with an Event die or the die face shown on it, or used in a battle as a combat card. Read them, but do not worry about them yet."

**7. What to skip.** Leave out the Mordor Track, the Elven Rings, Minions, Aragorn, and Gandalf the White. Explain each one the first time it matters. Leave the multiplayer rules in the box.

## Coaching the first three turns

Play the first two turns with your cards and plans in the open. Say what you are doing and why: "I am moving the Fellowship once and stopping, because a second move makes your Hunt easier." Point out when one of the guest's dice could do something big, such as a Muster that brings Isengard to At War.

A good first-game rhythm for the Shadow is simple: put one or two dice in the Hunt, use the rest to bring Nations to war and push armies toward Rohan or Gondor. A good rhythm for the Free Peoples is to move the Fellowship once or twice each turn and spend the rest holding the line. The [first-game plan](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-victory-conditions/) in Level 1 builds on that.

## Keeping it moving

The game slows when a player stares at seven dice. Two habits help. First, ask the active player to pick the die before deciding where to use it. Second, remind both players that passing is allowed when they have fewer unused dice than the opponent, which is often the Free Peoples. For more on long turns, see [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/).

At the end, ask your guest which thread felt closer to winning. That answer is a better guide to their next game than any rule you could add.
