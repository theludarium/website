The Free Peoples start with four dice against seven, armies that never come back, and most of their Nations asleep. They also have the game's best weapon: a Fellowship that only needs to take a few lucky steps. Good Free Peoples play is about tempo. You decide how fast the Ring moves, and you make every Shadow attack cost more time than it is worth.

## The Ring is the plan

Most Free Peoples wins come from destroying the Ring. That means most turns should include at least one Fellowship move. With four dice, about 94 percent of rolls give at least one die that can do it, since two faces show a Character and Will of the West can become one.

The question is how many moves. One move a turn is quiet but slow. Two is a steady pace. Three is a gamble: with two Shadow dice in the Hunt, the third move is found 75 percent of the time. The [Hunt odds lesson](/academy/war-of-the-ring/war-of-the-ring-hunt-for-the-ring/) has the full table. A sound habit is to move twice when the Hunt Box is light and once when it is heavy, then use your other dice on the war.

## Choosing the Guide

The Guide is the highest-Level Companion still in the Fellowship, chosen by you when there is a tie. Gandalf the Grey and Strider are both Level 3, so you can choose between them at the start.

- **Gandalf the Grey** as Guide: after you play a card with an Event die, you draw a card of the same type.
- **Strider** as Guide: any of your dice can hide a revealed Fellowship, not just a Character die.

Strider's ability saves your Character dice for moving. Gandalf's helps a card-heavy plan. Either way, read every Companion's card; the hobbits' Guide abilities matter late, when they may be the only ones left.

## When to separate Companions

Companions in the Fellowship do nothing but absorb Hunt damage and offer a Guide ability. They also raise the Shadow's limit on Hunt dice by one each. A separated Companion moves up to the Progress counter plus his Level, so separating after a few hidden steps can put him far down the road.

Separate a Companion when he has a job:

- **Waking a Nation.** Each Companion can activate certain Nations by ending a move in one of their Cities or Strongholds. Boromir, Legolas, and Gimli each wake their own people. The rulebook names Gandalf, Aragorn, Meriadoc, and Peregrin as able to wake any Free Peoples Nation.
- **Leading an army.** A Companion with Leadership gives re-rolls, and Gandalf and Strider each add 1 to the Combat Strength of their army.
- **Becoming someone bigger.** Outside the Fellowship, Strider can become Aragorn and Gandalf can return as Gandalf the White, each adding a die.

Keep a cheap Companion or two in the Fellowship for the road to Mordor. Once the Fellowship is on the Mordor Track, Companions can no longer separate.

## Aragorn and Gandalf the White

Aragorn needs Strider out of the Fellowship, standing in one of the Gondor cities named on the Aragorn card, and a Will of the West die. Gandalf the White needs Gandalf the Grey eliminated or out of the Fellowship, and at least one Minion in play now or before. Then a Will of the West die places him in Fangorn or in a Free Peoples Elven Stronghold, or replaces Gandalf the Grey on the map. Each brings a fifth or sixth die from the next turn on. That is a big deal for a side that starts with four.

## Waking the Nations

Gondor starts two steps from At War, and Rohan, the North, and the Dwarves start three steps away. All of them start passive and must be activated before their last step. A Nation that is not At War cannot attack or recruit with Muster dice, so a sleeping Gondor cannot rebuild when the siege comes.

You do not need every Nation At War. You need the ones the Shadow is about to attack to be ready to muster. The Shadow will often wake them for you by attacking, since each battle moves a Nation one step. Spend your own Muster dice on the Nation that is next in line.

## Defending in time

You cannot hold every border. Hold Strongholds. Five units with Elites and a Leader inside a Stronghold force a siege where the Shadow hits only on 6, and each extra round costs them an Elite. That can take many Shadow dice and several turns. Retreating into the siege early can be right, because what you are buying is time, not territory.

Watch the Victory Point Track. If the Shadow is at 8 and you can recapture a City, the points go back and the clock resets.

## Threaten a little

The Free Peoples need only 4 points to win on the map. You rarely get there, but a Free Peoples army near a lightly held Shadow Stronghold makes the Shadow keep units home. That pressure costs little.

## The Elven Rings and passing

Use an Elven Ring to fix the roll on a turn that decides something: a Fellowship move into Mordor, a siege that must hold. Remember that each Ring you use goes to the Shadow. When you have fewer unused dice than the Shadow, pass instead of acting. Let them commit first, then answer.
