A Cube night is part game, part dinner party. Someone opened their home or reserved a table, and someone else built the box of cards that everyone is about to shuffle. Most of the etiquette comes down to one idea: you are borrowing something that took a lot of care to make.

None of this is complicated. It is just easier to know before you walk in.

## How do you find a Cube draft?

Cubes live wherever Magic players gather. Ask at your local game store; regulars often know who owns a Cube. The [Wizards Store and Event Locator](https://locator.wizards.com/) lists stores near you.

Know the difference between a Cube night and a store's booster draft. At **Friday Night Magic**, Wizards' Friday night event at local stores, Booster Draft is one of the formats, and there you open new packs and keep what you draft. A Cube draft uses the owner's cards, and they all go back in the box.

In Indianapolis, our guide to [where to play Magic](/articles/where-to-play-magic-indianapolis/) covers stores and how to find a local Cube.

## What should you bring?

For most Cube nights, almost nothing:

- **Yourself, on time.** A draft cannot start until every seat is filled. Being late holds up seven people.
- **A pen**, if the group tracks results on paper.
- **Dice** for counters and life totals, if you have them.
- **A deck box or a rubber band**, to keep your 40 cards together between rounds.

You do not need cards, sleeves, or lands. The owner usually provides them. Ask before you bring snacks to the table itself; many hosts keep food and drinks off the play area to protect the cards.

## How long will it take?

Plan on about three hours for a night with two rounds:

| Part | About how long |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | 40 to 45 minutes |
| Deckbuilding | 20 to 25 minutes |
| Each round | 50 minutes for best of three |
| Cleanup | 10 minutes |

A third round adds about 50 minutes. If you have to leave early, tell the host before the draft starts so they can plan pairings. Our host's guide to [running a Cube draft](/articles/how-to-run-a-cube-draft/) shows the full schedule from the other side of the table.

## Etiquette during the draft

- **Keep picks private.** Do not show your picks or your pack, and do not talk about what is in the packs.
- **Keep the pace.** Take your time on the first picks, then speed up as the packs shrink. Nobody minds a slow pick in your first draft; just do not let a pack sit while you chat.
- **Pass gently.** Pass face down, in the direction for the pack you are on: left, right, then left. If a pack comes to you with the wrong number of cards, say so right away.
- **Ask questions.** "What does this keyword do?" is always welcome. Ask the host or a neighbor between packs, so you do not slow the table mid-pack.

## Etiquette during the games

- **Handle cards with care.** Shuffle the way the owner's sleeves allow, gently, without bending.
- **Say what you are doing.** Announce your spells and attacks out loud, so nothing is missed.
- **Keep the board clear.** Drinks and food stay away from the play area.
- **Be a good opponent.** Shake hands, win quietly, lose kindly. Our piece on [how to be a good loser](/articles/how-to-be-a-good-loser/) applies to winning too.
- **Use the owner's rules.** Some Cubes have house rules about sideboarding, mulligans, or special cards. When in doubt, the owner decides. Our guide to [Cube house rules](/articles/cube-house-rules/) shows the common ones.

## Giving the cards back

This is the part that decides whether you are invited again.

1. **Count your cards.** Leaving out basic lands, your deck plus your sideboard should equal every card you drafted, 45 in a classic draft.
2. **Separate the basics.** Lands go back on the land pile, not into the Cube.
3. **Check for strays.** Look under the table, in your deck box, and between your sleeves. A single missing card can mean a hole in the Cube for months.
4. **Return tokens and dice** that belong to the host.
5. **Sort the way the owner asks.** Some owners want cards by color, some want one pile. Ask.

Never take a Cube card home, even by accident. If you find one in your bag later, message the owner that night.

## Say thank you

Cube owners spend money, hours, and care on a box they share for free. A thank you, a hand with cleanup, and a word about your favorite card in their Cube mean more than you would guess. The best way to say thanks is to come back next time.
