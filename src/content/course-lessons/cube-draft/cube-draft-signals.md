Welcome to Level 2. At Level 1 you learned to build a working deck and pick a speed. Now you learn to draft the deck the table is willing to give you. That skill is called reading signals, and it is the difference between fighting your neighbors for every card and having a color flow to you for a whole pack.

Our explainer on [card drafting](/articles/card-drafting-explained/) introduces the idea. This lesson puts numbers on it.

## Who feeds you

In an eight-player draft, packs move in a fixed direction:

- **Packs 1 and 3** pass left, so you receive cards from the players on your **right**.
- **Pack 2** passes right, so you receive from the players on your **left**.

That means your right-hand neighbor shapes two of your three packs. The players upstream from you take first; you get what they leave. Signals are what you learn about their colors from what they leave.

## What a late card means

When a pack reaches you at pick 5, four drafters have each taken one card from it. If it still holds the best red removal spell in the Cube, all four of them passed it. One drafter passing it means little; they may have taken a bomb. Four in a row suggests red is not being taken upstream.

A single late card is a hint, not proof. Look for a pattern: strong cards of one color arriving at picks four, five, six, and seven, across different packs.

## The wheel

With eight drafters and packs of 15, every pack you see comes back to you eight picks later:

| You first see it at | Cards then | It wheels at | Cards then |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pick 1 | 15 | Pick 9 | 7 |
| Pick 2 | 14 | Pick 10 | 6 |
| Pick 3 | 13 | Pick 11 | 5 |
| Pick 7 | 9 | Pick 15 | 1 |

The wheel is the most reliable signal you get, because you saw the pack before. When your first pack returns at pick 9, you know exactly which seven cards the other seven drafters took. If two good blue cards from your opening pack are still there, the whole table passed them twice.

The wheel also lets you plan. At pick 1, if a pack has two good cards for an archetype nobody seems to draft, you can take the stronger card and hope the other one wheels. It works more often in Cubes where some decks are niche.

## Signals you send

Signals run both ways. In pack 1, the players to your left receive what you pass. If you take every good red card, your left neighbor sees little red and is likely to choose other colors.

That pays off in pack 2, when the direction flips and your left neighbor feeds you. A neighbor you kept out of red will pass you red. This is called cutting a color, and it is one reason committing to a color in the middle of pack one, and staying in it, tends to be rewarded.

The reverse also holds: if you pass a lot of a color in pack 1, expect your left neighbor to be in it, and expect less of it in pack 2.

## When to move in

Reading signals is useful only if you act on them. A simple approach:

1. **Picks 1 to 4:** take the strongest cards. Note colors, do not commit.
2. **Picks 5 to 9:** watch for the color that keeps arriving late. Check the wheel at pick 9.
3. **By the end of pack 1:** settle on one color you are sure of and one you lean toward.
4. **Pack 2:** a new set of feeders. Your second color can still change if the left side is open to something else.
5. **Pack 3:** you are feeding the same players as in pack 1. Stay the course.

Our [Cube draft strategy guide](/articles/cube-draft-strategy/) covers the same timing with a pick order alongside.

## Where signals mislead

**Cube is not a set.** In a booster draft, every pack comes from one set, with most of its cards at lower rarities. In a Cube, every card is strong, and the owner may have more cards in some colors. A late card can mean the Cube is deep in that color, not that it is open. Knowing the Cube helps.

**Players take cards for other reasons.** Drafters hate draft, take cards they love, or chase an archetype that does not care about colors. Read the pattern, not each pick.

**Small tables bend the numbers.** With fewer drafters, packs wheel more often and signals arrive faster. Level 3 covers [drafting at small tables](/academy/cube-draft/cube-draft-small-tables/).
