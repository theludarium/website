[Codenames: Duet](https://www.czechgames.com/games/codenames-duet) is the cooperative member of the family, built for two and playable by more. The [versions and editions lesson](/academy/codenames/codenames-variants-and-editions/) introduced it. This lesson is for when you have beaten it a few times and want to beat it reliably, or on a tighter clock.

<!-- AFFILIATE: Codenames: Duet -->

The core numbers come from Czech Games Edition: a 5 by 5 grid, a shared key with two different sides, 9 agents and 3 assassins on each side, 15 agents to find in all, and 9 turns for the standard game, or 11 for a gentler one. A new edition came out in 2025, so check your rulebook against anything below marked as edition-specific.

## How the key overlaps

Each side shows 9 agents, and together the team needs 15. That means 3 words are agents on both sides. The other 12 agents are visible on only one side: 6 that only you can clue, and 6 that only your partner can.

That simple sum has a big consequence. **When you give a clue, you see only your half.** A word that looks like a bystander to you may be one of your partner's agents. A word that looks like an assassin to you may be safe, or even an agent, on the other side.

<!-- VERIFY: the third assassin's mapping in the 2025 Duet rulebook. The rules say one of your three assassins is an assassin on both sides and one is an agent on the other side; the 2017 rules made the third a bystander on the other side. -->

So two rules of thumb:

- **As clue giver, avoid your assassins** exactly as in the base game. You cannot know which of them are safe for your partner.
- **As guesser, trust the clue over your own key.** Your guesses are checked against your partner's key, not yours. If their clue clearly points at a word you see as a bystander or even an assassin, it may be their agent. But be careful: one of the three words you see as an assassin is an assassin on their side too.

## The pace: 15 in 9

Fifteen agents over nine turns is an average of 1.67 words a turn. At a steady two a turn, nine turns find 18, which leaves a slack of only 3 words for every miss, early stop, and clue for one.

That changes clue giving from the base game. In the base game, clues for two are the safe default. In Duet, a run of twos with a couple of misses runs out of time. You need a few clues for three, which the [clues for three lesson](/academy/codenames/codenames-clues-for-three/) showed how to build safely.

The three words shared by both sides help here. Either player can clue them, so they are flexible fillers: whoever has the better link takes them.

## Stopping costs a turn, too

In Duet, every turn uses one timer token, whether it ends with a bystander or with the guesser choosing to stop. There is no plus one rule: the guesser may keep guessing as long as each guess is right.

That makes the idea from the [race math lesson](/academy/codenames/codenames-odds-and-tempo/) even stronger in Duet. If you were going to stop anyway, a last guess that hits a bystander costs you nothing extra. The token is spent either way. The only guesses to refuse are the ones that could be an assassin on your partner's side.

One more detail matters: a bystander on your partner's side is not necessarily a bystander on yours. When you guess a bystander, the word stays in play for the other player, who may still need it. Keep track of which words have been ruled out for whom.

## Clue for the board you both see

Your partner reads your clues with the same grid in front of them, plus their own half of the key. Use that, but remember what they cannot see. They know which words are green on their own key, not whether those words are also green on yours, so do not count on them to rule a word out for you. And when you see words already covered, you know those links are free to use again: once a word is covered, its form is fair game for a clue.

## Sudden death

If the timer runs out with agents still hidden, the game goes to sudden death. No more clues. Players guess in any order, without taking turns, and any wrong guess, even a bystander, ends the game in a loss.

<!-- VERIFY: sudden death wording against the printed 2025 Duet rulebook. -->

Sudden death is where good notes pay. Every guess is still checked against your partner's key: the green words on your own key are the ones your partner has to find. Before the last token goes, both players should be clear on which leftovers from earlier clues are still out there. Then guess only the words you are sure of, surest first.

## Harder challenges

Once you win at nine turns most of the time, Duet has room to grow:

- **Tighter clocks.** Play with fewer timer tokens or allow fewer mistakes. The free Codenames Companion app has a mission map for Duet, with harder missions that open up after the standard game.
- **More players.** Duet works with two groups, each seeing one side of the key. It plays like a team game, with all the table talk that brings.
- **Mixed decks.** Shuffle Duet's 400 words with the base game's for a deck neither of you knows by heart.
