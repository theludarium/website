Codenames plays 4 to 8 or more players, split into two teams, each with a spymaster and at least one operative. For two players, Czech Games Edition's answer is Codenames: Duet, a cooperative version built for two that also takes more.

Codenames has grown into a family of games. Some are new ways to play, some are the same game in a new box, and some are expansion packs for the one you already own. This lesson sorts them out, starting with the base game at different player counts.

## How many players does Codenames need?

| Players | How it plays |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Play Codenames: Duet. Partners share one key and work together. |
| 3 | No mode in the current rulebook. Duet works, since the publisher says it plays with more than two. |
| 4 | One spymaster and one operative per team. A lone operative may think out loud, which the rulebook allows. Quick and personal. |
| 5 to 8 | Two or three operatives a side: enough voices to catch a bad guess, few enough that quiet players still speak. |
| 9 or more | It works, but debates get long. Two copies side by side often beat one huge game. |

Our guide to [party games for large groups](/articles/party-games-for-large-groups/) has more options for a crowd.

Earlier printings of the rulebook included variants for two and three players: one team plays together against a team with no players, and the spymaster covers one enemy word on each of the absent team's turns. The current rulebook drops these and points two-player tables to Duet.

## Codenames: Duet

[Codenames: Duet](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FJMNPGXM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the cooperative version, built for two and playable by more. Each player sees one side of a shared key, and partners take turns giving clues. Together you try to find 15 agents within 9 turns, extendable to 11 for newer teams, without touching one of the assassins. Each side of the key has 9 agents and 3 assassins.

Its 400 words can be mixed with the original's. A new edition came out in 2025. If you play mostly as a couple or with one friend, this is the box to buy, and Level 3 has a [Duet strategy lesson](/academy/codenames/codenames-duet-strategy/) for when it gets hard. Our list of [two-player cooperative games](/articles/two-player-cooperative-board-games/) has more in that vein.

## Codenames: Pictures

[Codenames: Pictures](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01HT9DERU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) swaps the words for picture cards, each packed with small details. The grid is 20 cards instead of 25, and the clues are still one word and a number. It plays 4 to 8 or more. It suits younger players, mixed language groups, and anyone who thinks in images.

## Codenames XXL

[Codenames XXL](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FQJ7XV89?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the same game with larger word cards and bigger print. A refreshed edition came out in late 2025 with a revised word list. It helps at a wide table, in a classroom, or with players whose eyes prefer bigger type.

## Expansion packs

In 2026, Czech Games Edition added small expansion packs. [Sci-Fi](https://www.czechgames.com/games/codenames-sci-fi-expansion-pack) and [Fairy Tales](https://www.czechgames.com/games/codenames-fairy-tales-expansion-pack) each add 50 themed words, plus a few new agent tiles, for Codenames or Duet. Mix them into your deck, or play a whole game from one pack. [Cute Critters](https://www.czechgames.com/games/codenames-cute-critters-expansion-pack) adds 40 pictures for Codenames: Pictures.

## Themed editions

[Codenames: Back to Hogwarts](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G246H544?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a competitive, house against house version set in the Wizarding World, for 4 to 8 or more. It adds new obstacles on the grid, special house abilities, and a House Cup you can track across sessions.

[Codenames: Critical Role Adventures](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H7NQWVPL?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a fully cooperative take with heroes who level up. It was announced for the third quarter of 2026.

## House rules worth agreeing on

The rulebook leaves several choices to each group. Settle them before the first clue:

- **Names and titles.** One-word names are always valid. Your group can allow full names and titles too.
- **Abbreviations.** Your group can allow common ones, like NASA.
- **Soundalikes.** Traditional rules allow them only for meaning. The Codenames app allows all soundalikes, and your group can choose to play that way.
- **Expert clues.** Unlimited and zero clues, covered in the [endgame lesson](/academy/codenames/codenames-endgame/) and in [expert clues](/academy/codenames/codenames-expert-clues/).
- **A timer.** The rulebook points to a free phone app, Codenames Companion, with timers and randomized keys.

A good house rule makes the game more fun for this table. If it starts arguments, drop it.

## How to choose

- **Two players:** Duet.
- **Kids or mixed languages:** Pictures.
- **A big room or bigger print:** XXL.
- **A group that has played the base game to death:** an expansion pack, or Duet's 400 words mixed in.
- **A fan table:** a themed edition.
