Welcome to Level 1. Your first three games of King of Tokyo are loud, quick, and full of habits forming. Some of those habits are rules played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that quietly hand the game to someone else. This lesson covers both, checked against the current IELLO rulebook and its card clarifications.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Healing in Tokyo.** Heal results do nothing while you are in Tokyo, City or Bay. Only Power cards can give you Life there. This is the slip new tables make most, because it feels like it should work.

**Yielding to the wrong damage.** You may Yield only after losing Life to Smash results rolled by another Monster. A Power card that costs you Life does not open the door. And when you Yield, you still lose the Life.

**Skipping the move in.** If Tokyo is empty during your enter Tokyo step, you must enter. It is not a choice. That includes the first player of the game, whose Smash results hit nobody because Tokyo starts empty.

**Leaving dice unresolved.** You must resolve every die after your last roll, in any order you like. You cannot ignore a Smash result because you would rather not wound the Monster in Tokyo.

**Forgetting the third roll.** On your third roll you may pick up dice you set aside earlier and roll them again. Many players think kept dice are locked. They are not.

**Scoring pairs, or miscounting sets.** Numbers score only in sets of three or more. Four 3s score 4 points: 3 for the set, 1 for the extra die. Two 3s score nothing.

**Healing past 10.** Life tops out at 10 unless a Keep card says otherwise.

**Forgetting the 2 points.** A Monster that starts its turn in Tokyo gains 2 points before rolling. Make it a ritual: the player in Tokyo announces it as they pick up the dice.

**Tokyo Bay at the wrong time.** With 5 or 6 players, Tokyo Bay is open. Once 4 or fewer Monsters remain, whoever is in the Bay leaves at once, or moves to Tokyo City if it is empty. Monsters in City and Bay never wound each other.

## Card rules that trip people up

The rulebook ends with clarifications for a few Power cards. These come up often enough to know in advance.

- **Poison and Shrink tokens** stay even if the card that gave them is discarded, and you cannot remove them while you are in Tokyo. Outside, you spend a Heal result to remove one token instead of gaining 1 Life.
- **Fire Breathing** hurts your neighbors at the table, even if they are in the same place as you.
- **Mimic** copies another Keep card in play. You may move its token at the start of your turn by paying 1 Energy.
- **Metamorph** lets you sell your Keep cards back for their full printed cost at the end of your turn.

When a card does something odd, read it aloud and check the rulebook's clarifications page before arguing.

## The big strategic mistake: staying too long

Tokyo pays 2 points for every turn you start there. That is tempting, and new players stay for one more turn, then one more. Meanwhile every Monster outside is aiming every Smash result at them, and they cannot heal.

The fix is to decide your exit number before the game gets loud. A common rule of thumb: once your Life drops to around the number of Smash results the table can roll at you before your next turn, Yield. The [endgame lesson](/academy/king-of-tokyo/king-of-tokyo-endgame/) does the math in detail. For now, think of it this way: with four Monsters outside, each one swinging, 5 or 6 Life can disappear in a single round.

## Ignoring Energy

New players see claws and numbers as the real game and treat lightning as leftovers. But Energy buys Power cards, and Power cards are how a Monster pulls ahead: extra dice, extra rerolls, armor, points. A player who never buys a card is playing the base dice against Monsters with upgrades.

## Hitting Tokyo when you cannot afford to enter

If you Smash the Monster in Tokyo and it Yields, Tokyo is empty, so you must move in. New players swing at Tokyo with 3 Life left and suddenly find themselves the target of the whole table. Before you keep Smash results from outside, ask: am I ready to be in Tokyo if they leave?

## Rerolling everything, every time

The dice reward focus. A player who keeps a little of everything ends with one Energy, one heart, one claw, and a pair that scores nothing. Pick one or two goals per turn and chase them. The [openings lesson](/academy/king-of-tokyo/king-of-tokyo-strategy/), next, covers how.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before your last roll, ask:

1. Where am I, in Tokyo or out? That decides what Smash and Heal results do.
2. If I hit Tokyo and they leave, do I want to go in?
3. What will I buy with this Energy, and is anything in the row worth it?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong early. If your table includes players who freeze on decisions, our piece on [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) explains why dice games like this one keep turns moving.
