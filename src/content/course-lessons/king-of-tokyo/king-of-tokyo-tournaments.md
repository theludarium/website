King of Tokyo is a party at the table, and that is reason enough to play it. It also has a quiet competitive side: an annual championship event, publisher tournaments at conventions, and a large online community. You do not need any of them to enjoy the game. Knowing they exist can still change how you play at home.

## Online play

The rulebook points players to [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=kingoftokyo), which runs King of Tokyo in your browser with no download. Its game page lists the game as free to play for 2 to 6 players, and its rules section covers Power Up!, Halloween, the four Monster Packs, the Even More Wicked! micro expansion, the Mutant Evolutions variant, and the Origins and Dark versions. The rulebook also says Board Game Arena runs regular tournaments.

Online play is the fastest way to see many games in a short time. Because the site handles every rule, it is also a good way to settle what a tricky Power card does.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention at Seven Springs Mountain Resort in Pennsylvania, where many games each crown a champion. It crowned its first King of Tokyo champion in 2014. The [event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/kot.html) lists a champion every year from 2014 to 2019 and again in 2024 and 2025, with fields of 90 to 127 players, and the [WBC 2026 results](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html) name a 2026 King of Tokyo winner too.

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/kot.html) describes how it runs:

- **The base game.** Expansion Monsters are allowed, but no variant rules. Tokyo Bay is in, because every table has 5 or 6 players.
- **One-hour rounds, single elimination.** Each table's winner moves on until six or fewer players meet in a final.
- **Coached.** The event is listed in WBC's Coached class, so it is open to players without tournament experience. <!-- VERIFY: WBC's definition of a Coached (Class C) event on its Event Description page. -->

The tiebreakers are worth studying even if you never go. When a table needs a second place, Monsters still alive outrank eliminated ones, and Monsters eliminated later outrank those eliminated earlier. Only then do points decide, then Life. In other words, the tournament rewards the same thing the [endgame lesson](/academy/king-of-tokyo/king-of-tokyo-endgame/) does: survival first.

WBC 2027 is scheduled for July 24 to August 1, 2027, according to the [WBC page](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbcindex.html).

## Run a tournament at home

You can borrow the WBC format for a game night. With twelve players, run two tables of six. Each table's winner goes to a final, along with the runner-up from each table by the survival-first tiebreakers. Thirty-minute games make it fit in an evening, and eliminated players can cheer on the final.

## Finding local play

King of Tokyo is one of the easiest games to bring to an open game night, because it teaches in minutes and plays up to six.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights. Ask the staff whether King of Tokyo comes out, or bring it.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guild pages list groups near you.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it. For a family or kids program, our [kids game afternoon](/articles/kids-game-afternoon-program/) plan puts King of Tokyo in the 10 to 12 age group.

## How to keep getting better

- **Play at five and six.** That is the tournament count, and it is where the Yield decision is hardest.
- **Name your reason** every time you stay in Tokyo or leave it. If you cannot, you are guessing.
- **Count before you roll** in the endgame. The kill math from the endgame lesson becomes automatic with practice.
- **Teach it often.** Explaining Tokyo to new players sharpens your own sense of when to hold it.
