Flip 7 is one of the fastest teaches on any shelf. The risk is not that the rules are hard. It is that a host explains Flip Three edge cases to people who have not flipped a card yet. Teach the question, deal, and let the deck bring up the rest.

Here is a script in the order you would say it, then the rules to handle live, one bit of math, and where to play next. Print the [Flip 7 teach sheet](/academy/flip-7/teach-sheet/) for the middle of the table. Our method for [teaching any game in five minutes](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) fits this game well.

## Before anyone sits down

Shuffle thoroughly; the rulebook prints that word in capital letters. Have a pen and paper ready, or open the scoring web app from the publisher's [Flip 7 page](https://theop.games/pages/flip-7). Pick a dealer and a separate scorekeeper. Splitting those jobs keeps the game moving.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "Each round, you flip cards into a row and add up the numbers. Stop whenever you like and keep your points. But flip a number you already have, and you bust and get nothing for the round. Once someone reaches 200 at the end of a round, the highest score wins."

## Minutes 2 and 3: hit, stay, bust

Deal the first card to everyone, then say:

1. "On your turn, say hit or stay. Hit, and I deal you a card."
2. "Numbers go in one row. If the new one matches a number you already have, you bust."
3. "Stay, and you're out of the round, but you keep what's in front of you."

Then the line that makes the game click: "There are twelve 12s in this deck and only one 1. Big cards score more, and they bust you more."

## Minute 4: the seven

"Flip seven different numbers and the round ends for everyone, right then. You get 15 bonus points." Point out that the 0 counts as a different number, and that modifier and action cards do not count.

## Minute 5: the special cards, in one breath

Point at the teach sheet and name each one once:

- "Plus cards add points. The x2 doubles your numbers. You can't bust on these."
- "Freeze: someone banks their points and is out of the round."
- "Flip Three: someone takes three cards in a row."
- "Second Chance: keep it. It saves you from one bust."
- "When you get an action card, you choose who gets it, yourself included."

Then stop. Say "Hit or stay?" and look at the first player.

## Teach the rest live

Explain each of these the first time it happens. All come from the rulebook, the publisher's [official FAQ](https://theop.games/pages/flip-7-faqs), or the [Flip 7 rules FAQ on Dized](https://rules.dized.com/game/dPDRM857TU-BFRF7LzGE0g/faq).

- **An action card during the deal.** Pause the deal and resolve it right away. A Freeze can even go to someone who has not been dealt a card yet.
- **Something turns up during a Flip Three.** A Second Chance can be kept and used straight away. A Freeze or another Flip Three waits until the three cards are done, and is used only if that player did not bust.
- **Whose turn after a Flip Three?** Play picks up with the player after the one who was dealt the Flip Three, not the one who took the cards.
- **A Second Chance saves someone.** Discard it with the duplicate. That player's turn is over, but they are still in the round.
- **One player left.** The last active player must play any Freeze or Flip Three on themself.
- **A tie at 200 or more.** Everyone keeps playing rounds until there is one winner.

## The one bit of strategy worth sharing

New players ask the same question every round: when should I stop? There is no fixed answer, but there is a handy shortcut.

Every number from 1 to 12 has as many copies as its value. If you hold a 9, eight more 9s are out there. Add that up across your row, and the number of cards that can bust you is your number total minus one for each number card you hold (leave the 0 out of that count, since it has no twin).

Two rows, early in a fresh deck:

- **12, 9, and 7.** Worth 28 points. About 25 of the 91 cards left would bust you, a bit more than 1 in 4.
- **1, 2, and 3.** Worth 6 points. Only 3 cards would bust you. Keep flipping.

Cards face up on the table, and cards set aside from earlier rounds, are not in the deck, which is why the rulebook tells players to keep the card count in mind. Share all this as a tip, not an order. Pushing your luck anyway is the fun of it, and our piece on [luck and skill in board games](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/) explains why that mix lasts.

## Keep the action cards friendly

Freeze and Flip Three let players aim at each other. With kids or a new group, set the tone early:

- **Aiming at the leader is normal, not mean.** Say so before the first Freeze lands.
- **Freeze cuts both ways.** It stops a row from growing, but it also saves that player from busting.
- **Flip Three is the real threat.** Three forced cards on a long row often means a bust, unless that player is one number from Flip 7.

## Big tables and small ones

The box says 3 or more, and past 18 players the rulebook recommends a second deck. With a big group, a quick dealer matters more than any rule. With two or fewer, play the rulebook's challenge: count the rounds it takes you to reach 200. For more crowd picks, see [party games for large groups](/articles/party-games-for-large-groups/) and [quick board games under 30 minutes](/articles/quick-board-games-under-30-minutes/).

## Where to play next

**Online.** [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=flipseven) has Flip 7 in your browser, linked from the publisher's product page. It is a quick way to play many rounds and get a feel for the odds.

**Tournaments.** The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) ran Flip 7 as a trial event in 2026, marked as welcoming to beginning players. Per the [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/FL7.html), it was single elimination with one-hour rounds: an open first round at tables of five or six, then a semifinal and a final. Matt Reidy won, per the [WBC results](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html). WBC 2027 runs July 24 to August 1, 2027, and members vote on its trial events, so check the event list before you plan a trip.
