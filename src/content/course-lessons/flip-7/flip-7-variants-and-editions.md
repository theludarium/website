Flip 7 has no expansions in the usual sense. One deck plays the whole game, and the box says 3 or more. But the game feels different at three than at twelve, there is an official way to play with fewer, there are themed editions, and there is a meaner sequel. This lesson covers all of it.

## How the game changes by player count

**Three or four players.** You get many turns each, and the deck lasts several rounds before it runs out. That makes the set-aside pile worth watching. If last round burned through the 12s, this round is safer for anyone holding one. Action cards have few targets, so they often come back to you.

**Five to eight.** The classic party size. Rounds move quickly, there is always someone worth aiming a Flip Three at, and the deck runs out every few rounds.

**Big tables.** The rulebook recommends a second deck past 18 players. With a crowd, each round uses many cards, so reshuffles come often, sometimes mid-round. When that happens, cards in front of players stay put, even busted ones, and the rest are shuffled into a new deck. Counting matters less here, and table energy matters more. A quick dealer and a separate scorekeeper keep the game moving. Our guide to a [large group game night](/articles/large-group-game-night/) has more on running a crowd.

## The official two-player challenge

The rulebook is clear that Flip 7 is a game for three or more. For two or fewer, it offers a challenge instead: play rounds on your own and count how many it takes you to reach 200. Without opponents, there is no one to aim a Freeze or Flip Three at, so every action card goes to you. That makes it a good way to practice the odds from lesson 4, and lesson 11 turns it into a full practice plan with benchmarks.

## House rules worth trying

These are not official. They are easy changes some groups make, and none of them breaks the game.

- **A shorter race.** Play to 100 instead of 200 for kids, a quick opener, or the last game of a long night.
- **Count the round, not the game.** For a very casual table, skip the running total and just applaud whoever scores most each round.
- **Open scoring.** Keep a scoresheet in the middle of the table so everyone can see the race. The rules do not hide scores, but making them visible helps newer players read the endgame.

Agree on any house rule before the first card is dealt.

## Flip 7: With a Vengeance

If your group finds Flip 7 too polite, [Flip 7: With a Vengeance](https://theop.games/products/flip-7-with-a-vengeance) is a standalone sequel, not an expansion. The publisher's store lists it as a Walmart exclusive. The race to 200, busting, and the Flip 7 bonus all work the same way. Almost everything else gets sharper:

- **108 cards, with numbers up to 13.** There are thirteen 13s, so the top of the deck is even riskier.
- **Special number cards.** A Zero that scores you nothing unless you flip 7, an Unlucky 7 that wipes the rest of your row, and a Lucky 13 that lets you hold two 13s.
- **Modifiers that hurt.** Minus cards and a divide-by-two that you can play on any player.
- **Take-that action cards.** Steal a card, swap cards, force a discard, force one more card, and a Flip Four.
- **No safe harbor.** Staying does not protect your row. Until the round ends, other players can still target you.
- **Brutal Mode.** An optional rule where round scores can go below zero and a Flip 7 can take 15 points from someone else instead.
