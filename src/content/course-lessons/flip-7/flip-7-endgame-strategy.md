For most of a Flip 7 game, the math from lesson 4 is your guide: stay when your row is big and risky, hit when it is small and safe. Near the end, that math changes. The game is not about the best average round anymore. It is a race, and races are won on the gap between you and the leader.

## How the game really ends

Two rules from the rulebook and the publisher's [official FAQ](https://theop.games/pages/flip-7-faqs) shape every endgame:

- **The game ends only at the end of a round.** When a round ends with at least one player at 200 or more, the highest total wins. Crossing 200 does not win by itself. Another player can pass you in the same round.
- **A tie at the top means everyone plays on.** Not just the tied players. Everyone gets another round, and the highest total after that wins.

So the real goal is to finish the last round ahead. 200 is just the signal that the last round has come.

## How long is the race?

In our simulation of the real deck, a single player who stays around 20 to 25 points averages about 20 points a round, busts included. That puts a game at roughly ten rounds, more or fewer depending on how bold the table is. It also means that once a leader passes about 180, the next round is very likely the last.

## Know the number you need

Late in the game, every player has a target, and you can work it out.

Say the leader has 182 and you have 160. The leader stays with 20, for 202. To win this round, you need 43 or more, because 202 minus 160 is 42, and 42 only ties, which sends everyone into another round. Now you know exactly when to stop: not at a comfortable 25, but at 43.

If the leader has not stayed yet, estimate. A leader at 182 needs only 18 more points to cross 200, which is about two average number cards.

## Playing from the front

When you lead, you want a quiet, small round.

- **Bank early.** If you are at 185, a safe 15 or 20 points is worth more than a big row. A bust leaves you stuck and gives everyone another round to catch up.
- **Use a Freeze on yourself.** If you are dealt one with enough points to cross 200, freezing yourself banks it now, before a Flip Three can reach you.
- **Watch the second-place player, not the whole table.** If one chaser is close, your action cards go to them. A Flip Three on their long, risky row is your best defense.
- **Remember the Flip 7 ends the round for everyone.** Players still in the round score what they have, so it does not wipe them out, but it stops their rows from growing.

## Playing from behind

When you trail, a safe round is a losing round. If the leader is going to cross 200 this round and you bank 20 when you needed 43, the result is the same as busting.

- **Push to your number.** Hit until your round score covers the gap, then stay. The odds from lesson 4 still tell you how risky each flip is; the score gap tells you whether the risk is worth it.
- **Love the x2.** A doubled row is the fastest catch-up in the game. With an x2 in front of you, a modest row of numbers can cover a big gap.
- **Go for seven when it is close.** The 15-point bonus that was a bad bet in the middle of the game can be the only way to cover a large gap.
- **Aim everything at the leader.** Freeze the leader while their row is small, ideally during the deal, when a Freeze can land before they have any card at all. Flip Three the leader when their row is long and risky.

## The middle of the pack

With five or more players, several people may be chasing at once. Here the leader is everyone's target, and that helps you: let others spend their Freezes and Flip Threes on the leader, and save yours for whoever is about to pass you. If two players are fighting for first, a quiet strong round can slip you past both.

## When two players cross 200

It happens often at big tables. Both crossed, but only the higher total wins. If you are one of them and still active, the question is not "Did I cross?" but "Am I ahead of the other one?" Count before you stay.

## Make the last rounds a show

The last rounds of Flip 7 are its best moments, so make them visible. Read totals aloud. Let the table call out each player's target. A rough scoresheet in the middle of the table, or the publisher's scoring web app from its [Flip 7 page](https://theop.games/pages/flip-7), keeps everyone in the race.
