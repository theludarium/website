Flip 7 teaches in five minutes, which means most tables start playing before the odd cases come up. Your first three games are where habits form. Some are rules slips that nobody catches because the game keeps moving. Others are legal choices that quietly cost points. This lesson covers both, checked against the current rulebook and the publisher's [official FAQ](https://theop.games/pages/flip-7-faqs).

## Rules slips to catch early

**Counting modifiers toward seven.** Only number cards count toward Flip 7. A row of six numbers with a +4 and a Freeze above it is still one short. The 0 does count, because it is a number card.

**Letting the x2 double everything.** The x2 doubles only the total of your number cards. Add the plus cards after doubling, then the 15-point bonus. The FAQ is clear: the x2 never touches the bonus or the other modifiers.

**The dealer choosing where action cards go.** The player who is dealt the action card decides who gets it, any active player, themself included. The dealer just deals.

**Taking another card right after a Second Chance saves you.** Discard the Second Chance with the duplicate, and your turn is over. You get your next card on your next turn.

**Using Second Chance against a Freeze.** It does not work. Second Chance only stops a bust from a duplicate number.

**Holding two Second Chances.** You may have only one in front of you. If you are dealt another, give it to another active player, or discard it if nobody can take it.

**Resolving a Freeze in the middle of a Flip Three.** A Freeze or another Flip Three that turns up during a Flip Three waits until all three cards are flipped. If the player busts, it is discarded. A Second Chance is the exception: keep it and use it straight away.

**Shuffling the used cards back in.** After each round, set the cards aside. Only when the deck runs out do you shuffle them into a new deck. This matters more than it looks, because it makes the deck change from round to round.

**Thinking the first to 200 wins.** Nobody wins mid-round. At the end of a round where anyone has 200 or more, the highest total wins. Two players can cross 200 in the same round, and the one with more points takes it.

## The big strategy mistake: chasing seven

New players love the Flip 7 bonus, and they should. It is the loudest moment in the game. But a player who hits every turn hoping for seven different numbers busts far more often than they make it. In a fresh deck, a player who never stays reaches seven fewer than 1 time in 10, by our simulation of the real 94-card deck.

The bonus is 15 points. A solid round of 20 to 25 points, banked, is worth more on average than a long shot at seven. Go for it when the row makes it cheap: lots of low numbers, a Second Chance in hand, or six numbers already down.

## Staying too early with a safe row

The opposite mistake is just as common. A player with a 1, a 2, and a 3 stays because three cards feel like enough. But only 3 cards in the whole deck can bust that row. Low numbers are safe numbers. When your row is small and your bust count is tiny, hit.

## Ignoring the cards on the table

Every card face up in front of another player is a card that is not in the deck. So is every card set aside from earlier rounds. If four 12s are already out and you hold a 12, your 12 is far safer than it was at the start. New players look only at their own row. Look at everyone's.

## Freezing the wrong player

The first Freeze a new player gets usually goes to whoever is winning. That feels right, but a Freeze banks points. Freeze a leader who has a long, risky row and you just saved them from busting. A Freeze hurts most when it lands on a short row that was about to grow. Lesson 6 covers aiming action cards in detail.

## Forgetting you can freeze yourself

A Freeze can go to yourself. If you are dealt one with a big row in front of you, freezing yourself banks it right now. Waiting to stay on your next turn leaves a gap, and in that gap someone else may draw a Flip Three and aim it at your row. Banking now takes you out of range.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before every hit, ask:

1. How many cards in the deck can bust me right now?
2. What is already showing on the table?
3. If I bust, how much do I lose?

Those three questions fix most of what goes wrong early. For a broader take on why a little luck keeps games fresh, see our piece on [luck and skill in board games](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/).
