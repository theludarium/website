Flip 7 feels like pure nerve, but there is real math under every flip, and it is friendly math. You do not need a calculator. You need one counting trick, a sense of how the odds climb as your row grows, and one rough rule for when to stop. Every number in this lesson comes from the real 94-card deck: 79 number cards, 6 modifiers, and 9 action cards.

## Count the cards that can bust you

Every number from 1 to 12 has as many copies as its value, and there is a single 0. So when a number is in your row, the copies still out there are its value minus one.

- Hold a 12: eleven more 12s can bust you.
- Hold a 7: six more 7s.
- Hold a 1 or the 0: nothing can bust you on that card, because there is no other copy.

Add those up across your row and you have your **bust count**. A quick way to say it: your number total, minus one for each number card from 1 to 12 you hold. A row of 12, 9, and 7 totals 28, minus 3, so 25 cards can bust you.

Divide by the cards left in the deck to get your bust chance. In a fresh deck with only your three cards out, that row faces 25 out of 91, about 27 percent.

## Bust chance by how many numbers you hold

The table below uses a fresh deck and counts only your own cards as gone. "Safest row" is the lowest numbers you could hold, "riskiest row" is the highest, and "typical" is the average across rows as they are actually dealt, from our simulation of the real deck.

| Numbers in your row | Safest row | Typical | Riskiest row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0% (a 0 or a 1) | about 8% | 12% (a 12) |
| 2 | 0% (0 and 1) | about 15% | 23% (12, 11) |
| 3 | 1% (0, 1, 2) | about 23% | 33% (12, 11, 10) |
| 4 | 3% (0 to 3) | about 30% | 42% (12 down to 9) |
| 5 | 7% (0 to 4) | about 38% | 51% (12 down to 8) |
| 6 | 11% (0 to 5) | about 44% | 58% (12 down to 7) |

Two lessons hide in that table. First, each number you add raises the typical bust chance by about 7 percentage points. Second, the gap between safe and risky rows is huge. Four low cards are safer than one 12.

## Worked examples

**8, 5, and 11.** Bust count 7 + 4 + 10 = 21. Fresh deck: 21 out of 91, about 23 percent.

**7, 4, 9, and 2.** Bust count 6 + 3 + 8 + 1 = 18. Fresh deck: 18 out of 90, exactly 20 percent. Four numbers, but a gentle bust chance, because the 2 and 4 barely add risk.

**0, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12.** One away from Flip 7. Bust count 2 + 4 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 33, out of 88: about 38 percent. The cards that finish the seven are every 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, and 11 left: 1 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 40, about 45 percent. Close to a coin flip either way.

## Adjust for what you can see

The table assumes a fresh deck. Real decks are not fresh. Cards in other rows and cards set aside from earlier rounds are gone.

Say you hold 12 and 11, worth 23 points. Fresh, your bust chance is 21 out of 92, about 23 percent. Now say last round's set-aside pile of 22 cards holds six 12s and four 11s, and the other players show 7 cards with neither number. The deck has 94 minus 22 minus 2 minus 7, or 63 cards. Your bust count drops to 5 + 6 = 11. That is 11 out of 63, about 17 percent.

Flip it around: if that pile held no 12s or 11s at all, all 21 bust cards are still in a deck of 63, about 33 percent. Same row, double the risk. This is why the rulebook tells players to keep the card count in mind.

## When does the average say stay?

Each hit risks your whole row to gain one card. So compare two numbers:

- **What you could lose:** your points times your bust chance.
- **What you could gain:** on average, one more flip adds about 4 to 6 points, less when your row already holds many big numbers.

Take 12, 9, and 7: 28 points at about 27 percent risks about 7.7 points to gain about 4. Stay. Take 7, 4, 9, and 2: 22 points at 20 percent risks about 4.4 to gain about 5.6. Hit.

Our simulation of a single player in a fresh deck, ignoring action cards, found the best average round came from staying once the row reached about 20 to 25 points. Stopping at 25 averaged about 20 points a round. Pushing to 40 averaged about 13, because the busts swallowed the big rows.

## What changes the math

- **Second Chance:** while you hold one, your next duplicate does not bust you. Hit more freely.
- **The x2:** it doubles what you could lose, so stay a little sooner.
- **The race:** averages are for the middle of the game. Near 200, the score gap matters more than the average, which is lesson 9.

The math is a guide, not a rule. Pushing past the numbers now and then is half the fun.
