Hit and stay are your own business. Action cards are where Flip 7 becomes a table game. Three Freezes, three Flip Threes, and three Second Chances sit in the 94-card deck, and each time one turns up, someone has to choose a target. The same card can rescue one player and wreck another. This lesson is about telling which is which.

## Read the rows before you aim

Before you give anyone an action card, look at every active player and ask two questions:

1. **How big is their row?** That is what they bank if they stop now.
2. **How risky is their row?** That is their bust count from lesson 4: each number's value minus one, added up.

Those two numbers sort the table into four kinds of rows. Small and safe, like a lone 2. Small and risky, like a lone 12. Big and safe, which is rare. Big and risky, like 12, 11, and 10. Each action card has a best home among them.

## Freeze: stop a row before it grows

A Freeze banks whatever the player has and takes them out of the round. That is a penalty only if their row was going to grow.

- **Best target:** a small, safe row. A player holding a 1 and a 3 would happily hit five more times. Freezing them now locks in 4 points.
- **Worst target:** a big, risky row. Freezing 12, 11, and 10 hands that player 33 points and spares them a third-of-the-deck bust chance on every flip.
- **The sharpest Freeze comes during the deal.** If a Freeze turns up while the first cards are going out, it must be used right away, and it can go to a player who has not been dealt a card yet, per the [Dized rules FAQ](https://rules.dized.com/game/dPDRM857TU-BFRF7LzGE0g/faq). That player banks nothing for the round.

And remember yourself. If your own row is big and risky, the Freeze is a clean way to bank it before anyone can aim a Flip Three at you.

## Flip Three: break a long, risky row

Flip Three forces three cards, one at a time. It stops early only on a bust or a Flip 7. The longer and higher the row, the more those three cards hurt. Our simulation of the real deck, counting only the target's own cards as gone, shows how wide the range is:

| Target's row | Busts during the Flip Three | Flips 7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A lone 2 | about 21% | none |
| 3 and 5 | about 35% | none |
| 8, 5, and 11 | about 63% | none |
| 12, 11, and 10 | about 73% | none |
| 12, 11, and 10, holding a Second Chance | about 28% | none |
| Six numbers, 0 to 5 | about 13% | about 86% |
| Six numbers, 7 to 12 | about 68% | about 31% |

So:

- **Best target:** a long row of big numbers with no Second Chance. Three forced flips at a third of the deck is usually the end of that round.
- **Worst target:** a short, low row. You just gave them three free cards.
- **Careful near seven.** A player with six numbers is not always a good target. If their missing numbers are the common ones, three flips likely finish their Flip 7, which ends the round for everyone and hands them 15 bonus points.
- **Check for a Second Chance first.** It cuts the damage by more than half.

If you keep a Flip Three for yourself, do it when your row is small and safe, so three cards are mostly upside.

## Second Chance: a shield, and sometimes a gift

Keep your first Second Chance. While you hold it, your next duplicate number does not bust you, so you can hit more freely. When it saves you, discard it with the duplicate, and your turn ends.

You may hold only one. If you are dealt a second, you must give it to another active player, or discard it if nobody can take one. Every option helps someone, so pick the player it helps least:

- **A player with a small, safe row** gains little from a shield they barely need.
- **Avoid the leader** and anyone with a long, high row, where a Second Chance is worth the most.

One limit worth repeating from the FAQ: a Second Chance does not protect against a Freeze. It only stops busts from duplicate numbers.

## When you are the last one in

If every other player has busted or stayed, any Freeze or Flip Three you are dealt must go to you. That changes the math late in a round. With a big row, drawing a Freeze is good news: it banks you. Drawing a Flip Three is the danger, because three forced cards land on your own long row. So each hit risks a little more than your bust count shows.

## Keep it friendly

Aiming at someone is the game, not an insult. Say so early, especially with kids or a new group.
