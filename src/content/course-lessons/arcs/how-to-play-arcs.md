[Arcs](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D856FJ1J?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a space opera for two to four players, designed by Cole Wehrle, who also designed Root. You move ships, build cities, fight, and send agents to sway the powers of the Reach. Underneath it all sits a small trick-taking card game, and that card game is the key to everything.

This lesson covers the base game, from the Leder Games rulebook dated April 11, 2024. Leder also hosts an official how-to-play video on its [How to Play Arcs page](https://ledergames.com/pages/how-to-play-arcs).

## How do you set up?

![An abstract Arcs map at setup: six clusters in a ring, one closed with out-of-play markers, a player's starting ships, city, and starport, and side panels for the Court, the action deck, and the ambition boxes](/academy/arcs/setup.svg)

1. **Build the action deck.** Shuffle the 20 action cards numbered 2 to 6. With four players, add the eight cards numbered 1 and 7.
2. **Set the markers.** Put the three ambition markers on their blue, lower sides in the Available Markers spaces, the chapter marker on 1, and the zero marker in its slot.
3. **Deal the Court.** Shuffle the 25 Guild cards and 6 Vox cards into the Court deck. Deal three face up with two players, four with three or four.
4. **Draw a setup card** for your player count. It closes two clusters (two or three players) or one cluster (four players). Cover that cluster's gate with a path marker and its three planets with out-of-play markers.
5. **Place starting pieces.** Give the initiative marker to a random player. Following the setup card, each player puts 3 ships and a city in their A system, 3 ships and a starport in B, and 2 ships in C.
6. **Take two resources** matching the planets in your A and B systems, then draw six action cards.

Two-player games add a few rules, covered in [lesson 9](/academy/arcs/arcs-player-counts-expansions/).

## The map

The Reach is six clusters, each with three planets and a gate. Gates link to their own planets and to neighboring gates. Each planet has a type (Material, Fuel, Weapon, Relic, or Psionic) and one or two building slots.

You **control** a system if you have more fresh ships there than each Rival. Pieces start fresh. One hit damages them (tip a ship, flip a building). A second hit destroys them.

## How does a round work?

A chapter is a series of rounds. In each round, every player plays one action card.

**The player with initiative leads.** Play any card face up. You get one action for each pip on it, chosen from its suit:

| Suit | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| Administration | Tax, Repair, Influence |
| Aggression | Battle, Move, Secure |
| Construction | Build, Repair |
| Mobilization | Move, Influence |

**Everyone else follows** in clockwise order, in one of three ways:

- **Surpass:** play a higher card of the lead suit. Take actions up to its pips.
- **Copy:** play any card face down. Take one action from the lead card.
- **Pivot:** play a card of a different suit. Take one action from your own card.

**Seize the initiative** by playing an extra card face down with yours, which gives no actions. In a four-player game, Surpassing with a 7 also seizes. Only one player can seize per round.

At the end of the round, a player who seized keeps the initiative. Otherwise it goes to whoever Surpassed with the highest number. If nobody did, it stays put.

## The seven actions

- **Tax:** gain a resource from one of your cities, or from a Rival city you control, which also captures one of their agents.
- **Build:** a city or starport in a system where you have a piece, or a ship at your starport.
- **Move:** any number of ships to an adjacent system. Leaving from your own starport lets them keep flying through gates.
- **Repair:** fix one damaged piece anywhere.
- **Influence:** put an agent on a Court card.
- **Secure:** take a Court card where you have the most agents.
- **Battle:** roll dice against a Rival in a system with your ships. Lesson 7 covers the dice.

Before you use any pips, you can spend resources in your **Prelude** for extra actions.

## Ambitions and Power

You score Power only through **ambitions**. When you lead, you may declare the ambition shown on your card. Take the highest available ambition marker, place it on that ambition's box, and cover your card's number with the zero marker. Your card now counts as a 0.

The five ambitions are Tycoon (Material and Fuel), Tyrant (Captives), Warlord (Trophies), Keeper (Relics), and Empath (Psionics). When everyone is out of cards, the chapter ends and each declared ambition scores: first place takes the higher number on its markers, second takes the lower. Anyone can win any declared ambition, whoever declared it.

## How do you win?

After scoring, the game ends if anyone has 27 Power with four players, 30 with three, or 33 with two, or if Chapter 5 just ended. Most Power wins. Otherwise everyone draws six new cards and the next chapter begins.
