The Blighted Reach is the campaign expansion for Arcs. It needs the base game, and its rules are firm about one thing: play at least one single-session game first, and make sure everyone is comfortable with the base rules. Otherwise, it warns, expect a long and tedious first game. With that done, the campaign is the biggest version of Arcs there is.

## Three Acts

A campaign is three games, called **Acts**. They end differently from a base game:

- Acts I and II last exactly **three chapters**. Act III lasts **four**.
- An Act never ends early because someone reached a Power target. Ignore those thresholds.
- At the end of Act III, the player with the most Power across all three Acts wins, unless someone completes a final objective.

Campaign setup is very different from the base game. There are no setup cards and no closed clusters. Two Imperial clusters get Imperial ships, Free cities appear elsewhere, and damaged Blight starts in every system outside the Imperial clusters. The campaign uses its own Court of Guild and Vox cards, marked CC, plus one lore card per player shuffled into the Court deck, and the Imperial Council card sits at the left end of the Court. Event cards are shuffled into the action deck: two with two or three players, three with four.

## Fates

Each player draws two **A Fates** and secretly picks one. There are 24 Fates in all, eight each of A, B, and C, and each comes with its own set of cards and pieces. Your Fate's objective card shows how you set up and how you advance your **objective marker** down the Power track toward 0.

Fates change the game for everyone. Many add cards to the shared **rules booklet** as you play, and the rules ask you to explain each one aloud, since they affect the whole table.

## Regents, Outlaws, and the Empire

Everyone starts as a **Regent** of the crumbling Empire. One Regent is the **First Regent**, who governs the Empire and holds the Imperial Trust. Regents can command Imperial ships in systems where they have their own pieces, and they follow the Empire's laws: they cannot harm Imperial ships except when taking hits as attackers, and they cannot harm or tax Rival Regents in systems with Imperial ships.

A Regent can become an **Outlaw** by calling a Summit and leaving the Empire. Outlaws can battle Regents freely and do not pay into the Imperial Trust, but the Empire can attack them.

## The Blight and the Free States

The **Blight** spreads through the Reach. It is Tough: one hit does nothing, two hits damage or destroy it. It hits ships during Crises, and in the Intermission it destroys buildings in systems with no ships. You can battle it, and destroyed Blight counts as Trophies.

The **Free States** are independent cities and starports. You can tax, build, and Catapult from Free buildings you control, and you can battle and raid them.

## Events

There are three kinds of events. **Summits** are freeform deal-making, with Favors you collect and return. **Crises** are when the Blight attacks and some cards cause chaos. **Edicts** are when the First Regent governs. Events happen when someone plays an Event card, or when someone secures the Imperial Council from the Court. An Event card lets you take actions as if you had played the lead card, but you cannot lead with it, Copy with it, or use it to seize the initiative.

## The Intermission

After Acts I and II comes the Intermission. In order, the key steps are:

1. Resolve your Fate. If you failed your objective, lose Power equal to where your marker sits.
2. Clear the Court, and return all Trophies and Captives.
3. Destroy all damaged ships and buildings, and buildings in Blighted systems with no ships.
4. The player with the most Power takes the initiative, then everyone with more than 1 Power **loses half**, rounding in their favor.
5. Choose a Fate. Completing your objective lets you keep your Fate or draw one new one. Failing it means drawing two new ones.

Halving Power keeps the campaign close, so an early lead is a head start, not a win. Repair damaged pieces before an Act ends, because the Intermission destroys them.

## Act III

In Act III, A and B Fates have two **grand ambitions** that are always active and score only for you, worth more if you fulfill both. C Fates have a **final objective** instead. A C Fate with its marker at 0 and at least 1 Power wins outright at the end of a chapter, which beats winning on Power.

## Running a campaign

- **Book three sessions.** You can pack up between Acts using the log sheet, map trays, and player trays, but you cannot play a single-session game with the box until the campaign ends or you abandon it.
- **Keep the same group.** Our article on [missed sessions in campaign games](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/) has options if someone cannot come back.
- **Talk deals out loud.** Summits reward negotiation. Our guide to [negotiation games](/articles/negotiation-board-games/) covers good table manners for it.
