Arcs has a reputation as a hard teach, and the reputation is half earned. The rules are short. The trouble is that the card play feels strange until you have done it, and everything else hangs on it. So teach the card play first, in your hands, before the map makes anyone nervous.

This script assumes a base game with no leaders or lore, as the rulebook asks for a first game. Print the [Arcs teach sheet](/academy/arcs/teach-sheet/) for the table, and set up before people sit down so the teach starts with cards, not cardboard.

## Before you start

Set up the whole board from the [how to play lesson](/academy/arcs/how-to-play-arcs/), deal six action cards to each player, and leave the hands face down. Put the Court row where everyone can read it.

## The script

**1. The goal (30 seconds).** "We are rival powers at the edge of a dying empire. We score Power by winning ambitions, which are like objectives we pick during play. Most Power at the end wins."

**2. Pick up your cards (2 minutes).** "Each card has a suit, a number, some pips, and an ambition. The suit says which actions you can take. The pips say how many. The number matters when we follow. The symbol at the bottom left is the ambition you can declare with it." Have everyone hold up one card and read it aloud.

**3. Leading and following (4 minutes).** This is the heart of the teach. Say it slowly.

"Whoever has initiative leads a card and takes one action per pip. Then each of us follows in one of three ways. Surpass: same suit, higher number, and you get your card's full pips. Copy: any card face down, and you get one action from the leader's card. Pivot: any other suit, and you get one action from your own card."

Then the hook: "Whoever Surpasses highest takes the initiative for the next round. Or you can seize it now by throwing away an extra card. That card gives you nothing, and you have one fewer turn this chapter."

Play one mock round face up, with each player saying which of the three they would choose and why.

**4. The actions (3 minutes).** Walk the four suits from the aid sheet. Spend the most time on Move, because Catapult moves from a starport are the first thing people miss, and on Tax, because it is how most resources arrive. Mention that Influence puts an agent on a Court card and Secure takes the card once you have the most agents there.

**5. Control (1 minute).** "You control a system if you have more fresh ships there than anyone else. A damaged ship does not count. Control lets you tax Rival cities and stops their Catapult moves."

**6. Ambitions (3 minutes).** "When you lead, you may declare the ambition on your card. The marker goes on that ambition's box, and your card becomes a zero, so anyone can Surpass it. That is the price." Then show the five ambitions on the map and say what each one counts. Point at the markers' numbers: first place takes the big one, second the small one.

**7. The end (1 minute).** "When everyone is out of cards, we score declared ambitions, draw six new cards, and do it again. The game ends when someone passes the Power target or after Chapter 5."

**8. What we skip.** "Battle dice, raiding, and Outrage I will teach the first time someone attacks. Resources in the Prelude I will show on your first turn with one."

## Running the first round

Let the player with initiative lead with an easy card, ideally Construction or Mobilization. Have them say "I lead, three pips, I build twice and repair." Then each player names their choice before they act. This slows round one and speeds up every round after it.

Encourage at least one ambition in the first two rounds. A chapter with nothing declared scores nothing, and new players learn more from one scoring than from five minutes of explanation.

## When the first battle comes

Stop the game for two minutes. Show the three dice and say what each does in one line each: skirmish hits safely, assault hits hard but hurts you, raid steals and hits buildings. Then walk the five resolution steps on the aid sheet as the attacker rolls. The [battle dice lesson](/academy/arcs/arcs-battle-dice-odds/) has the full odds for later.

## Teaching tips

- **Teach in hands, not on the map.** Cards first, pieces second.
- **Name every play.** "Pivot, one action" said aloud catches most mistakes.
- **Keep hands private.** The rules say you cannot show your action cards to anyone, so help through questions, not by looking.
