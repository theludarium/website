Welcome to Level 4. You can play a full game well. Now you learn to set up the right game for the people at the table, and what to add when you want more. Arcs adjusts several rules by player count, and each one changes how the game feels.

## What changes by player count

| Rule | 2 players | 3 players | 4 players |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Action cards | 2 to 6 (20 cards) | 2 to 6 (20 cards) | 1 to 7 (28 cards) |
| Clusters closed | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| Court row | 3 cards | 4 cards | 4 cards |
| Power target | 33 | 30 | 27 |
| Extra rules | Phantom resources, mulligan | None | 1s and 7s |

Every count has four setup cards of its own, and each places starting systems and closed clusters differently. A setup card can be marked for experienced players, so start with the unmarked ones.

## Four players

Four is the full game. The 1 cards have no ambition, but tend to carry many pips, so they make strong leads for pure actions. The 7s let the leader declare any ambition, and a 7 that Surpasses seizes the initiative without costing an extra card. The map has five clusters in play, so it is crowded and fights come early.

## Three players

Three players use the 20-card deck and a map with only four clusters open. Each player holds six of twenty cards, so most of the deck is in hands each chapter, which makes counting cards easier. Three-player games are prone to two players fighting while the third builds, so watch the quiet player's ambitions closely.

## Two players

Two-player Arcs adds two rules.

**The phantom third player.** At setup, take the six resources matching the six planets covered by out-of-play markers and put them on the ambition boxes: Material and Fuel on Tycoon, Weapons on Warlord, Relics on Keeper, and Psionics on Empath. At scoring, these count as if a third player had them, and Weapons on Warlord count as Trophies. Otherwise they are out of play. So a declared Keeper with one Relic on its box needs two Relics to win outright.

**The mulligan.** After drawing, the player without initiative may discard their six cards and draw six new ones, once, and must keep the new hand. This happens at setup and at each new chapter.

Two-player games often turn decisive early, and the rulebook notes that conceding is fine when the winner is clear.

## Variants in the rulebook

- **Split discard piles.** Normally every played card goes into one face-down pile. For a more strategic, slower game, or a tournament, keep one pile for cards played face down and one for cards played face up.
- **Extra lore.** In Leaders and Lore games, each player can draft a second lore card. See the [leaders and lore lesson](/academy/arcs/arcs-leaders-and-lore/).

## The expansions

**[Arcs](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D856FJ1J?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** is the base game for two to four players: the map, four players' pieces, 28 action cards, the base Court of Guild and Vox cards, 18 dice, and 8 leaders and 14 lore cards for Leaders and Lore mode.

**[Arcs: Leaders and Lore Pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D8S71F6Q?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds 8 new leader cards and 14 new lore cards. It is the simplest way to add variety to the base game, and its lore also works in the campaign. It was known as the More to Explore pack during the original crowdfunding.

**[Arcs: The Blighted Reach](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D8RH22Q8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** turns Arcs into a three-game campaign with 24 Fates, the Empire, the Blight, and a campaign Court. It needs the base game. The [next lesson](/academy/arcs/arcs-blighted-reach-campaign/) covers how it plays.

**Coming later: Beyond the Reach.** In spring 2026, Buried Giant Studios, the new studio of designer Cole Wehrle and artist Kyle Ferrin, crowdfunded three new expansions. Lost Vaults and Fated Leaders expands the base game, with pieces for a fifth player, a new 31-card Court deck, Fated Leaders with simpler powers, and randomized map elements. Beyond the Reach and Halls of Power expand the campaign. Check the campaign page for when they reach stores.

## Which to buy first

If your group plays the base game and likes it, the base box's own Leaders and Lore is the first step, and it costs nothing extra. The Leaders and Lore Pack comes next for groups who want more leaders. The Blighted Reach is for a group that will meet for three long sessions in a row. Our article on [whether expansions are worth it](/articles/expansions-worth-it/) has a general test, and our guide to [player counts](/articles/board-game-player-counts/) explains why some games shine at one number.
