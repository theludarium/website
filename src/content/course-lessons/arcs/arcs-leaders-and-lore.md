After a game or two, the rulebook says you are ready for Leaders and Lore. It also warns, plainly, not to play your first game with them. They give every player wild, unique powers, which is the fun of Arcs at its best and a lot to track while you are still learning the card play.

The base game box includes 8 leader cards and 14 lore cards. The separate Leaders and Lore Pack adds 8 more leaders and 14 more lore. Leaders and Lore is also the standard competitive format: the WBC Arcs event uses it in every game.

## What changes

**Your leader** replaces the standard starting pieces and resources. Its card shows A, B, and C boxes with the pieces to place in your systems from the setup card, with pips under the ship icon showing how many ships. It also names the two resources you start with, and it gives you special powers.

**Your lore card** gives a persistent power. Lore cards have no Guild suit and no raid cost, so they never count toward ambitions and can never be stolen.

## How the draft works

Leaders and Lore replaces the step where players place starting pieces and take resources.

1. Lay out a row of leader cards and a row of lore cards, each with one more card than there are players.
2. In **reverse turn order**, starting with the player to the right of whoever has initiative and going counterclockwise, each player takes one leader or one lore card.
3. Keep going until everyone has one leader and one lore. You cannot take a second of either.
4. Place starting pieces and take starting resources as your leader card shows.
5. Leaders with extra setup steps do them last, in turn order.

The setup card is drawn before this, so you can see which clusters are closed and where your A, B, and C systems are while you draft.

## Two leaders from the rulebook

The rulebook shows two leaders, and they make good examples of how leaders are built.

**The Mystic** starts with a Psionic and a Relic. Its Attuned ability gives an extra Psionic when it Copies or Pivots in order to tax. Its Cryptic ability places agents on its Material and Fuel Outrage slots during setup, so those resources start Outraged. That is a clear push toward Empath and Keeper and away from Tycoon.

**The Elder** has Beloved, which lets it Influence after defending in a battle where the attacker took Trophies, and stops Rivals from Ransacking the Court when they battle it. Its Just ability changes Tyrant: the Elder gains nothing for second place, and only second-place Power when it takes first. That steers the Elder away from Tyrant.

The pattern is common: a leader's powers point you at some ambitions and away from others. Read your leader as a plan, not just a power.

## How to draft

**Take the contested pick first.** If one leader is clearly strongest for this map, take it, since you will not see it again. If the leaders are even but one lore card is excellent, take the lore first. The second pick usually comes back to you with fewer choices but rarely with nothing useful.

**Know where you pick.** The player to the right of the initiative picks first. The player with initiative picks last, and also acts first in the game. That balances a little, but it means the initiative player should expect the leftovers.

**Fit the map.** Check your setup card systems. A leader that wants Relics is weaker if your A and B systems sit far from Relic planets. A leader that starts with more ships suits a crowded map.

**Pair for one plan.** A lore card that helps your leader's best ambition is worth more than a stronger lore card that pulls you elsewhere. A lore card that adds actions, like Tool-Priests (a Build that makes a ship at any city you control), fits almost any plan.

## Lore and new actions

Many lore cards give a **new action** tied to a standard one, like Tool-Priests' Summon (Build). When you would take that standard action with a pip or a resource, you may take the new action instead. Modifiers to the standard action do not change the new action, but they do apply to any standard action inside it. The rulebook's example is Living Structures, whose Nurture (Build) is a Tax: build modifiers do not apply, but tax modifiers do.

## The extra lore variant

For more chaos, deal one extra lore card per player into the row and draft until everyone has one leader and two lore. The rulebook says you can use as many lore cards as you like, with a warning that it can overwhelm the table. Try it once your group knows several leaders.
