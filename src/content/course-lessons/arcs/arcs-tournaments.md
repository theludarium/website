Welcome to Level 5. Arcs is young, but it already has a tournament path, and the best-known event is open to beginners. This lesson covers where Arcs is played for a title, how the main event runs, and how to find games to practice.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) is an annual tournament convention run by the Boardgame Players Association. Arcs joined its schedule in 2025, the year after the game's release, with GM Joel Tamburo running the event both years.

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/ARC.html), updated June 17, 2026, set the format:

| Rule | 2026 event |
| --- | --- |
| Class | B: beginning players welcome |
| Rounds | 3 heats, a semifinal, and a final |
| Round length | 4 hours |
| Advancement | 16 to the semifinal, 4 to the final |
| Games | 4 players, unless smaller games are needed to balance a round |
| Rules | Leaders and Lore in every game |

The preview notes that Leaders and Lore is included in the base game, so no extra purchase is needed to play.

The [2025 preview](https://boardgamers.org/wbc25/previews/ARC.html) used a shorter, three-hour round and advanced players on heat wins alone. It also asked players to bring their own copies.

## How heats are scored

The 2026 event advanced players by **Heats: Most Wins, then Points**. Wins come first. Ties on wins are broken by points from every heat you played:

| Finish | Points |
| --- | --- |
| 1st | 1,000 |
| 2nd | 100 |
| 3rd | 10 |
| 4th | 1 |

If players are still tied, the GM's own tiebreak gives one extra point per heat played, and a high dice roll settles anything left. So a player with one win and two second places (1,200 points) ranks above a player with one win, a third, and a fourth (1,011 points).

That math matches what the [ambitions lesson](/academy/arcs/arcs-ambitions-scoring/) teaches about play itself: when you cannot win a game, finishing second still matters. Play every heat to the end.

Format, round length, and advancement can change from year to year, so read the current WBC Arcs preview before you go.

## Past winners

The [WBC results page](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html) lists Bailey Burdett as the 2026 Arcs winner. The BPA's [Arcs event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/ARC.html) lists the first WBC Arcs event in 2025, won by Dennis Mishler in a field of 38 players.

## Conventions and store events

Arcs tournaments also appear at other conventions. Breakout in Toronto, for example, ran Arcs qualifier rounds and a final on March 14, 2025, with each qualifier game's winner advancing. Check a convention's event schedule for an Arcs listing and its rules, since some may use the base game without leaders.

For store play, Leder Games keeps a [store locator](https://ledergames.com/pages/store-locator) for shops that carry its games. A store that stocks Arcs is a good place to ask about a regular game night. The [Arcs forums on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/359871/arcs) are the largest public place to find players, rules discussion, and local groups.

## Online play

Dire Wolf, the studio behind the digital edition of Root, [announced a digital version of Arcs](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/announcing-arcs-digital/), planned to start in Steam early access with mobile versions later. Check Dire Wolf's news page for where it stands.

A digital edition is the fastest way to practice the card play, because it enforces the rules and keeps the pace up. Once it is available, it is the natural home for the volume of games a tournament asks for.

## What tournament play changes

- **Leaders and Lore always.** Practice the draft until you can read a leader in a minute.
- **Four players.** Plan for 1s and 7s, five open clusters, and a 27 Power target.
- **The clock.** A four-hour round is roomy for experienced players and tight for slow ones. Practice at the pace of 30 minutes per player that the rulebook gives for experienced tables.
- **Table manners.** Deals are not binding, hands stay private, and the rules ask tables to keep the game flowing rather than argue technicalities.

Tournament groups can also use the rulebook's split discard piles, which reward careful card counting. The [Root tournaments lesson](/academy/root/root-tournaments/) covers the WBC experience for Leder's other big game.
