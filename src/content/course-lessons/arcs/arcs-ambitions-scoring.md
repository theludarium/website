Every point of Power in a base game of Arcs comes from ambitions. There are no points for ships, cities, or cards on their own. They matter only because they help you place in a declared ambition. Once that clicks, every choice in the game has a measuring stick.

## The five ambitions

| Ambition | Counts | Comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tycoon | Material and Fuel | Taxing, raiding, Guild card suits |
| Tyrant | Captives | Taxing Rival cities, securing cards with Rival agents |
| Warlord | Trophies | Destroying Rival pieces |
| Keeper | Relics | Taxing, raiding, Guild card suits |
| Empath | Psionics | Taxing, raiding, Guild card suits |

Resources and Guild cards both count, by their icons. Weapons count for none of these. Note that both players can gain Trophies in a battle: the defender takes any attacking ships it destroys.

## How the markers pay

There are three ambition markers, each with a first-place and a second-place number. They start on their blue, lower sides. When you declare, you take the **highest** available marker.

| Marker | Blue side | Flipped side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest | 5 / 3 | 9 / 4 |
| Middle | 3 / 2 | 6 / 3 |
| Lowest | 2 / 0 | 4 / 2 |

At the end of each chapter, after scoring, the lowest-value marker that has not been flipped turns to its higher side. So ambitions grow more valuable as the game goes on, and later chapters swing the Power race harder.

Three things follow from the table:

- **The first declaration each chapter is worth the most.** Later declarations take smaller markers.
- **Only three declarations fit in a chapter.** Once all three markers are placed, nobody can declare.
- **Stacking adds up.** You can declare an ambition that is already declared. First place takes the higher number on every marker there, and second takes the lower. In the rulebook's example, Tycoon holds two markers worth 7 for first.

## Ties and qualifying

On a tie for first, every tied player takes second place Power instead, and nobody takes first. On a tie for second, the tied players get nothing. And you need at least one of the thing counted to score at all.

Ties hurt the leader more than anyone. If you are one Relic ahead in Keeper, a single raid that steals one Relic turns your first place into a shared second.

## City bonuses

Building cities uncovers slots on your player board. Two of them add Power when you win an ambition outright: +2 for the first, and +5 total once both are uncovered. You get the bonus once per ambition, not per marker, and never on a tie. A player with both bonuses who wins two ambitions in one chapter gains 10 extra Power, which is a strong reason to keep building.

## When to declare

Declaring costs something: your lead card becomes a 0, so anyone holding that suit can Surpass it and take the initiative.

**Declare early** when your lead is safe. A wide lead in Captives or Trophies is hard to erase, because Rivals need actions to catch up and you can keep adding. Early declarations also take the biggest marker.

**Declare late** when your lead is fragile. A one-resource edge in Keeper or Empath can be raided away. Declared in the last rounds, it gives Rivals fewer turns to respond.

**Declare to deny** when a Rival is waiting to declare something they would win. Each declaration you make takes a marker off the table. Once your ambition holds the biggest marker, theirs can only get a smaller one, and once all three are out, it cannot be declared at all this chapter. In four-player games, a 7 lets you declare any ambition, which makes it the best denial card in the deck.

## Resources are spent, ambitions are counted

Here is the core tension of Arcs. A Relic in your board is a point in Keeper. Spent in your Prelude, it is a Secure action, and it is gone. Every time you spend a resource, check whether it is holding up an ambition first. A good habit is to spend resources of types nobody has declared.

## Trophies and Captives build up

Trophies return only when Warlord is scored, and Captives only when Tyrant is. Until then they pile up across chapters. A player who fought all game with no Warlord declared may be sitting on a large lead that a single declaration cashes in. Check every Trophies and Captives box before you declare either ambition.

## Reading the end

After a chapter's scoring, the game ends if anyone has reached 27 Power with four players, 30 with three, or 33 with two, or after Chapter 5. When a leader is close, everyone else should declare ambitions that leader cannot place in, and avoid stacking ones they would win.
