Arcs gives you a lot of freedom from the first turn, which is exactly why first games stall. Everyone has six cards, a few ships, and no idea what matters. This lesson gives you a plan that works in a base game without leaders or lore. It will not win every game, but it will keep you in the Power race and teach you what the game is asking for.

## Read your hand before turn one

Sort your six cards by suit and look at three things.

- **Pips.** Lower-numbered cards tend to carry more pips. On the official aid sheet, the Construction 2 carries four pips. Many pips make a card a good lead.
- **Numbers.** High cards win the initiative when they Surpass. A 6 is a strong follow. A 2 is easy to beat when you lead it.
- **Ambitions.** Each card from 2 to 6 shows one ambition: 2 is Tycoon, 3 Tyrant, 4 Warlord, 5 Keeper, and 6 Empath. In four-player games, 1s show none and 7s let you pick any.

A hand with two or three cards in one suit gives you control of rounds: you can lead that suit and often Surpass in it too.

## Build the second city early

Your cities sit on your player board covering resource slots. Each city you build uncovers more room to hold resources, and some uncover the +2 and +3 bonuses for ambitions you win outright. More cities also mean more to tax.

So a solid opening is to use Construction pips to put a city on a second planet near your starting systems, ideally a planet type that feeds the ambition you want. Material and Fuel feed Tycoon, Relics feed Keeper, and Psionics feed Empath.

Remember the cost: every city is also a target. A city a Rival can reach and destroy is a gift of Trophies, so build where your ships already are.

## Aim at one ambition

Before you declare anything, look at the map and the player boards and ask: which ambition am I already winning, or one tax away from winning? Declare that one. Declaring an ambition you are behind in hands Power to someone else.

Second place matters too. In a three- or four-player game, second place on a declared ambition is often worth a few Power, and a player who quietly takes second in every ambition can stay close to the lead. Watch who is in second as closely as who is first.

## Declare with the right card

Declaring turns your lead card into a 0, so anyone can Surpass it and take the initiative from you. That cost is lowest when the card's number did not matter much anyway. Declaring with a 2 gives up little, since a 2 was easy to beat already. Declaring with a 6 throws away a card that might have kept you leading.

Declaring early in the chapter also gives everyone time to catch up, while declaring late gives them less time to respond. [The ambitions lesson](/academy/arcs/arcs-ambitions-scoring/) covers this timing in depth.

## Use the Court

Guild cards from the Court give new actions and modifiers, and their suit counts toward ambitions like a resource. A Material Guild card counts for Tycoon, a Relic card for Keeper, and so on. Weapon cards add to no ambition, but their rules can be strong.

A simple first-game habit: when a Court card fits your plan, Influence it with spare Mobilization or Administration actions, then Secure it with an Aggression pip or a Relic. Watch for Rivals placing agents on the same card, since the most agents takes it.

## Fight only for a reason

Battle is risky in your first game. Assault dice hurt you, intercepts hurt you more, and a lost fleet leaves your cities open. Good early reasons to fight:

- Warlord is declared and you can take Trophies.
- A Rival city sits alone where your ships control the system.
- A Rival's ships threaten your only starport.

Otherwise, build, tax, and keep your ships fresh. Fresh ships hold control.

## Keep count of the chapter

With six cards each and no seizes, a chapter runs about six rounds. Each seize costs the seizer a card, so they will run out first. Near the end of a chapter, check who still holds cards. The last player with cards plays unopposed rounds and can grab a final tax or secure before scoring.
