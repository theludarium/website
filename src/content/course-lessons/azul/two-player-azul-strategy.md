Welcome to Level 3. Two-player Azul was the format of the 2024 world championship's online qualifiers, and it plays very differently from the four-player game you may have learned on. With five displays and one opponent, you can see and count almost everything. The skill is in what you leave behind.

## Judge every pick by the gap

At three or four players, a point you deny one opponent helps the others. At two, there are no others. A point you stop is exactly as good as a point you score.

That changes the math on denial. If a pick costs you 2 on the floor and stops a placement worth 6, you gained 4 on the only number that matters, the gap between you. Before each pick, ask two questions: what does this score me, and what does it cost my opponent? Add them.

Our [two-player games for date night](/articles/two-player-board-games-date-night/) guide explains why Azul stays so tense at two. This is the reason: every tile on the table is a choice between you and one other person.

## The round is a countable sequence

A two-player round deals 20 tiles onto 5 displays. You and your opponent alternate picks until the displays and the center are empty. Late in a round, you can count exactly how many picks are left:

- **Each display still full** needs one more pick to empty it. Its leftovers go to the center.
- **Each color in the center** needs one pick, because a center pick takes every tile of one color.

When the displays are gone, the count is just the number of colors in the center. If it is your turn and that number is odd, you take the last pick. If it is even, your opponent does.

Why it matters: late in a round, the center often holds a big pile of a color nobody can place. If both players avoid it, it goes to whoever takes the last pick, and it lands on their floor.

## Changing the count

You change the count with the leftovers you push into the center. Leftovers that match a color already there add no pick. Leftovers in a new color add one.

A worked example. One display is left: 2 red, 1 blue, 1 black. The center holds 5 yellow and 1 blue. Neither of you can use yellow. It is your turn.

- **Take the red.** Blue and black go to the center. The center now has yellow, blue, and black: 3 picks, starting with your opponent. They take one, you take one, and they are left with the 5 yellow.
- **Take the blue from the display.** Red, red, and black go to the center. Now it has yellow, blue, red, and black: 4 picks, starting with your opponent. You end up with the yellow.

Same tiles, same table, a five-tile swing on the floor decided by which color you took. The skill is noticing it two or three picks ahead, while you still have choices.

A few tools for shifting the count:

- **Take a color that is already in the center** from a display, so its leftovers merge with piles already there.
- **Split a pile by color.** A display with three colors adds up to two new center picks; one with two colors adds at most one.
- **Take a small pile early,** so the center has fewer colors later.

Your opponent can do all of this too. When both players count, the round becomes a short puzzle at the end, and the player who started counting first usually wins it.

## The marker at two players

At two players, the starting player marker decides who takes the first pick from 5 fresh displays. That first pick has the widest choice of the round, and the second player has to answer it. Take the marker when the next round's first pick could fill a big line or complete a column. Leave it when your opponent would pay for it with a cluttered floor.

Remember that the first center pick also changes the count. Taking the marker is a pick like any other.

## Reading one board

With one opponent, you can follow every line they have. Before each pick, know:

1. Which of their lines are one pick from complete, and in what color.
2. Which wall rows already hold that color, so which lines they cannot use.
3. Whether any of their rows has 4 tiles, which means they could end the game.

If you are ahead, push for the end. If you are behind, keep their fourth-tile rows starved.
