Azul looks like a gentle family game, and it is one. It is also a serious competitive game, with a long-running event at the World Boardgaming Championships, a world championship run with its publisher, and a busy online scene. You do not need to win any of them to enjoy them. But knowing they exist changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament Azul differs from home play

Most competitive play shares a few features:

- **The original game, colored side.** No sequels and no add-ons. The gray wall stays face down.
- **A time limit.** Rounds are scheduled, so slow play is not an option.
- **Scores written down.** Players often record every score on a sheet each round, not just on the track.
- **Clear rulings on the gray areas.** Tournaments settle questions the rulebook leaves open, like how many tiles you must place.

Scoring is the same as your home rules: adjacency for every tile, the floor line each round, and 2, 7, and 10 for the end bonuses.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. Azul is one of them, and it is on the [2026 winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/azl.html) lays out the format:

- **Beginners welcome.** The event is marked as open to beginning players, which makes it one of the friendliest ways to try a real tournament.
- **Three heats, then a quarterfinal, a semifinal, and a final.** Each round runs one hour.
- **Four-player tables first.** The heats seat four players where possible, with some three-player games as needed. Players with at least one heat win fill the quarterfinal, ranked by wins and then finishes, and close second places can help fill the semifinal.
- **The original game on the colored side.**

The preview also lists a few special procedures:

- You must place as many of your picked tiles as fit in a line, or put them all on the floor.
- Players score their walls one at a time at the end of each wall-tiling phase, and write scores on a sheet each round.
- Tied games go to the player with more complete horizontal rows, then fewer tiles on the wall, then the starting player marker on the floor, then seat order.

The event is popular. Its [event history](https://www.boardgamers.org/eventhistory/azl.html) shows 190 players in 2022, 205 in 2023, 229 in 2024, and 220 in 2025.

## The Azul World Championship

In early 2024, Plan B Games, Asmodee, and Board Game Arena ran an Azul World Championship. The [announcement on the Board Game Arena forum](https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?t=34573) set out the path:

- **Eight online qualifiers** on Board Game Arena in January and February 2024, split across European, North American, and Southeast Asian time zones.
- **Two-player knockout games** in the qualifiers, with up to 512 players in each.
- **A live final in Lisbon, Portugal,** on April 24, 2024, played with four players at the table.

Each qualifier winner earned a trip to the final. It is a fitting home for the game: Azul's theme comes from the painted tiles of Portugal.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=azul) has Azul in your browser for 2 to 4 players, free to play, with the gray wall variant as an option. It is also where the world championship qualifiers ran. Online play is the fastest way to get many games in, and the site keeps a rating so you can watch yourself improve.

If you want team play, the fan-run [Azul Cup](https://azul-cup.carrd.co/) organizes national teams of at least three players for two-player games on Board Game Arena. It is unofficial and not run by the publisher.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Next Move's own site links to Asmodee's [store locator](https://storelocator.asmodee.com/). Many stores run open game nights where Azul is a regular. Ask the staff.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups, and Azul is a common choice because it teaches fast.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it. If you want a small event of your own, our guide to [running a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers formats that suit a four-player game like Azul.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play four-player games.** That is what the WBC heats seat first.
- **Play inside an hour.** A timer keeps you from stalling over every pick.
- **Score one player at a time.** It is how the WBC does it, and it catches scoring slips.
- **Review your losses.** How many points did you lose on the floor? Which round did someone else end the game before you were ready?
