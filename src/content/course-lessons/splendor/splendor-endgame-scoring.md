Splendor ends faster than people expect. One turn, everyone is building. The next, someone is at 12 and two cards from the finish. The players who win close games are the ones who counted before that moment arrived.

## How the end works

The end is triggered when a player finishes a turn with 15 or more prestige points. Play continues until everyone has had the same number of turns. The player to the right of the First Player takes the last turn.

So where you sit matters:

- **If you are the First Player and reach 15,** every other player gets one more turn.
- **If you sit to the First Player's right,** your turn is always the last of the round. Reach 15 there and the game ends at once.

Before you trigger the end, count what each player after you can score on their last turn.

## What cards are worth per gem

Card values before any discounts, from player-written [strategy notes on Board Game Arena](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_splendor):

| Level | Gem cost | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 to 5 | 0 |
| 1 | 4 | 1 |
| 2 | 5 | 2 |
| 2 | 6 | 3 |
| 2 | 7 or 8 | 2 |
| 2 | 8 | 1 |
| 3 | 7 | 4 |
| 3 | 10 | 5 |
| 3 | 12 | 4 |
| 3 | 14 | 3 |

Two lessons stand out. First, the best points per gem sit in level 3 and the cheaper level 2 cards. Second, the printed cost is not what you pay late in the game. Your bonuses cut it, so the real question is how many pieces a card costs you now. A 14-gem card can cost you four pieces if your engine covers most of it.

## Counting your path to 15

Once you reach about 10 points, sketch the finish:

- Which cards can I buy next turn, and for how many points?
- Which noble am I closest to?
- How many turns does that add up to?

A common finish is one or two level 3 cards plus a noble, on top of the points you already have. A 5-point card that also completes a noble is 8 points in one turn.

## Noble timing

Nobles arrive at the end of your turn, after your action. If a card both scores and completes a noble, you gain both together. Plan the card that closes your noble as a scoring turn, not a setup turn.

If two nobles are within reach, remember you can take only one per turn. Take the one an opponent is also chasing first.

## The tiebreaker

Ties go to the player who bought the fewest development cards. Nobles are tiles, not cards, so they do not count. In a close race, that rewards big, efficient purchases. If two players finish level on points and everything else is equal, the one who got there with one 4-point card instead of two 2-point cards wins.

## Holding back on purpose

Sometimes the winning move is not to trigger the end. If reaching 15 now hands a rival one last turn to reach 16, and you could instead set up a bigger finish, waiting can be right. Count both lines before you choose.

It works the other way too. If a rival is about to trigger the end, you need points now. Spend your Gold, take the card that scores most this turn, and skip the long build.

## Reserved cards at the end

Reserved cards still in your hand at the end cost you nothing. But they score nothing either. In the last few turns, a reserve is worth it only if you can buy that card before the game ends, or it takes away someone else's winning card.

## A last-turn checklist

1. How many turns does each player have left, given who sits where?
2. What is the most I can score this turn, nobles included?
3. If I reach 15, can anyone pass me on their last turn?
4. Am I holding pieces or Gold I should spend now?
