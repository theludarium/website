The previous lesson showed where competitive Splendor happens and how events are scored. This one turns that into a plan. Four weeks is enough to walk into a first event, a store tournament, or a convention heat, and play your real game instead of a nervous one.

## Know what you are training for

The official [tournament guide](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-spacecowboys/uploads/2025/10/Tournament_Guide_A4.pdf) from Space Cowboys sets the frame most events borrow. Four details shape how you should practice:

- **A 45-minute limit.** When time runs out, the game ends at once and points are counted. No final round.
- **Points by place.** At a table of X players, 1st scores X+3, 2nd X+1, 3rd X-1, 4th X-3. Tied players share the points.
- **A score ratio for ties.** Each player's score is divided by the table winner's score. Lose 14 to 16 and you earn 0.875. Lose 10 to 16 and you earn 0.625. The ratios add up across rounds.
- **One sheet of notes.** You may bring a rules summary or notes, up to one sheet, both sides, and your opponents may not look at it.

The guide also makes players responsible for checking the setup: the shuffle, the number of pieces, everything. Once a game starts, there is no appeal over a setup mistake. Check before the first turn.

Other events, like the World Boardgaming Championships heats covered in the previous lesson, set their own clocks and advancement rules. Read the event page before you go.

## Week 1: find your baseline

Play as many three- and four-player games as you can, at home or on [Board Game Arena](https://en.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=splendor). After each one, write down how many turns you took to reach 15, or your score when someone else ended it.

Player-written [strategy notes on Board Game Arena](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_splendor) say nearly every game takes 17 to 28 turns, and suggest aiming for fewer than 22. That works out to about 2 points every 3 turns. Use 22 as your first target.

Turn on Board Game Arena's competitor mode for these games. It switches off helpers like highlighting the cards you can afford, the way a real table does.

## Week 2: cut wasted turns

Pick your three slowest games from week 1 and replay them in your head. Look for the turns that did not move you forward:

- a reserve you never bought,
- pieces returned to the ten-piece limit,
- a noble race you lost by one turn,
- a level 1 card bought after turn ten that added nothing.

Each one is a turn you can win back. The [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/) has a full list of what costs tempo. Play this week's games with one rule: say out loud what each action does for you before you take it.

## Week 3: play on the clock

Now play every game under the 45-minute limit. At four players, a game of around 22 turns each is about 88 turns in 45 minutes. That is roughly 30 seconds per turn, per player. Use your opponents' turns to plan, so your own turn is mostly confirming.

Practice the clock ending, too. In the last ten minutes, a tournament game can end before anyone reaches 15. When time is short, points on the board beat a bigger build that finishes after the buzzer. Ask: if the game stopped after this round, where would I place?

## Week 4: play every game to the end

The score ratio changes how you play a game you are losing. A loss at 14 earns a better tiebreak than a loss at 10, and second place is worth two more points than third at a four-player table.

So when you cannot win, play for the next place and the highest score you can reach. Buy the points. Take the noble. Make the winner score as few as you can, because their score is the number everyone else is divided by.

Spend this week on mock rounds. Three games in a row, clocked, scored with the guide's points and ratios. Keep your own standings.

## Build your one sheet

Your notes sheet is legal, so make it count. A good one fits on one side:

- **The color wheel:** White, Blue, Green, Red, Black. Each noble asks for neighbors on it, and each level 3 card leans on the color before it. The [deck lesson](/academy/splendor/splendor-card-deck-odds/) has the details.
- **The five 5-point cards,** with a box to tick as each one appears.
- **Your endgame checklist** from the [endgame lesson](/academy/splendor/splendor-endgame-scoring/).
- **Your target pace:** turns to 15 and seconds per turn.

## After the event

Write down every game: your seat, your place, your score, the winner's score, and the turn you fell behind. That record is the start of your next four weeks.

For every other city, the [store locator](https://storelocator.asmodee.com/) and our guide to [running a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) will get you to a table.

That is the whole ladder. You can play, teach, open, read the table, win a noble race, time the finish, read the decks, and sit down at a tournament with a plan. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
