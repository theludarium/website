Your first three games of Splendor are where habits form. Some are rules slips, played wrong for months because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that quietly cost you the race. This lesson covers both, checked against the current rulebook.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Taking two of a color from a short stack.** You can take two of the same color only if at least 4 of that color are in the supply before you take them. With three left, it is three different colors or nothing from that stack. At two players, with only 4 of each color, that means every piece of the color must be in the supply.

**Taking Gold with a gem action.** Gold comes only from reserving a card. Neither gem action can take it.

**Doing two things in one turn.** One action per turn. No taking gems and then buying, and no buying two cards. A noble arriving is not an action, so it does not count.

**Counting pieces toward nobles.** A noble wants bonuses, which means cards. Five green pieces in front of you count for nothing. Five green cards count for five.

**Taking two nobles at once.** One noble per turn, at the end of the turn. If you qualify for two, pick one and hope the other waits.

**Expecting change.** If your bonuses cover more than a card's cost, you pay nothing and get nothing back.

**Discarding reserved cards.** You cannot. A reserved card leaves your hand only when you buy it, and you can hold at most 3.

**Checking the ten-piece limit at the wrong time.** You can go over 10 during your turn. The limit applies at the end of your turn, Gold included, and you choose which pieces go back.

**Ending the game at 15.** Reaching 15 starts the end. The round finishes so everyone has the same number of turns, and the player to the right of the First Player goes last.

**Breaking ties the wrong way.** The fewest development cards wins a tie, not the most.

**Forgetting to refill.** A card bought or reserved from the table is replaced right away from its deck.

## The big strategic mistake: hoarding gems

New players love the gems. They take three a turn, every turn, and sit behind a glittering pile waiting for the perfect card. Then they hit the ten-piece limit and hand pieces back.

Pieces do nothing until you spend them. A card does something every turn for the rest of the game. A good rule for your first games: if you can buy a useful card, buy it.

## Spreading your colors too thin

A player with one card of every color has a small discount everywhere and a real discount nowhere. Many of the best cards ask for a lot of one or two colors, and so do the nobles. Pick two or three colors and lean into them. The [openings lesson](/academy/splendor/splendor-opening-strategy/) shows how to choose.

## Reserving out of worry

Reserving feels safe. You get a Gold and a card nobody else can take. But it uses your whole turn, fills one of only three slots, and a card you cannot afford for six turns is dead weight. Reserve when you have a reason: a card you will buy soon, a Gold you need now, or a card an opponent is about to take.

## Rushing points too early

The level 2 and 3 cards look like the way to win, and a 1-point card on turn three feels like progress. It usually is not. Early points cost more gems per point, while a cheap level 1 card makes every later purchase cheaper. Points come later, and faster, once the engine runs.

## Ignoring everyone else

Splendor can feel like solitaire. It is not. The card you are saving for can vanish on the turn before yours, and the noble you are three cards from may be one card away for someone else. Even in game one, glance at what the next player can buy.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before each turn, ask:

1. Can I buy something useful right now?
2. Which two or three colors am I building?
3. Is anyone about to take the card or noble I want?

That habit fixes most of what goes wrong in the first three games. For more on tempo and reserving, our [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/) picks up where this lesson stops.
