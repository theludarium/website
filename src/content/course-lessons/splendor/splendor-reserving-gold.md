Reserving is the action new players either never touch or touch too often. At Level 0 you learned the rule. In the openings lesson you saw two good reasons to reserve early. This lesson is about the cost, so you can tell a strong reserve from a nervous one.

## The rule, exactly

On a reserve, you take one face-up card from the table, or the top card of any of the three decks without showing it. Then you take 1 Gold, if any are left. The card goes into your hand.

- You can hold at most 3 reserved cards. With 3 in hand, you cannot reserve at all.
- You cannot discard a reserved card. The only way out of your hand is to buy it.
- There is no penalty for reserved cards left in hand at the end. They just score nothing.
- If the Gold is gone, you can still reserve. You get the card and nothing else.

Gold is wild. It stands in for any gem when you buy, even a color you already hold. Spent Gold goes back to the supply, so it can come out again for the next player who reserves. And Gold counts toward your ten-piece limit like any other piece.

## What a reserve really costs

Compare a reserve with the usual alternative. Taking three different gems puts 3 pieces in front of you. A reserve puts 1 piece in front of you, the Gold, plus a card you still have to pay for.

So a reserve is about two pieces behind a gem turn. That is the price. It is worth paying when one of these is true:

- **The card would be gone by your next turn.** Someone else can buy it, and you need it.
- **The Gold is worth more than two ordinary gems.** That happens when a color you need is empty or nearly empty, and only a wild piece can cover it.
- **Taking the card hurts a rival more than it costs you.** They were one turn from buying it, and now their plan has to change.

A reserve that does none of these is two pieces thrown away and a hand slot filled.

## Which cards to reserve

The best reserve is a card you will buy within two or three turns. Your bonuses already cover part of it, and the Gold you take covers part of the rest.

Level 2 cards in your colors are the classic choice. They score, they add a bonus, and they are often the card an opponent is also eyeing. Player-written [strategy notes on Board Game Arena](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_splendor) suggest just that pattern: reserve a key level 2 card, keep building with level 1 cards, then buy the reserved card when your bonuses catch up.

Be careful with level 3 cards. A 5-point card that costs 10 or more gems can sit in your hand for half the game. Reserve one early only if your engine is clearly heading toward its colors.

## Blind reserves from the deck

Taking the top card of a deck hides your plan and gives you the Gold. It also hands you a card you did not choose.

A blind reserve makes sense when:

- you need the Gold now, and nothing face up is worth taking, or
- you do not want the table to see which card you are chasing.

From the level 1 deck, a blind card is usually cheap enough to buy later. From the level 3 deck, it is a gamble on points and colors. Level 3 of this course looks at what is actually in each deck, so you can judge that gamble better.

## Spending Gold well

Gold is your only flexible piece, and only 5 exist in any game. A few habits help:

- **Spend Gold on the scarce color.** If red is gone from the supply and blue is plentiful, use Gold for red and take blue.
- **Save it for a big turn when you can.** The same notes recommend holding Gold toward the end, when colors run short and one wild piece can finish a level 3 card.
- **Do not hoard it.** Gold in front of you counts toward 10. Two Gold you never spend are two pieces you could not take.

## Your hand is information

A face-up reserve tells everyone which card you want and roughly which colors you are building. Sometimes that is fine. Sometimes it tells a rival to drain that color before you can buy.

Watch other players' reserves the same way. A face-up reserve is the clearest signal in the game. The midgame lesson at Level 2 shows how to use it.

## Keep a slot open

Late in the game, a reserve can steal the card a rival needs to reach 15. With 3 cards already in your hand, you cannot make that move. Try to keep at least one slot free from the midgame on.
