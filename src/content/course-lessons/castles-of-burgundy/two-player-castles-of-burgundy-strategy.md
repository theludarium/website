The Castles of Burgundy plays well at two, and some players like it best that way. The game is shorter, turns come faster, and the puzzle stays the same. But the scoring numbers change, and the depots shrink, and those changes shift what wins. This lesson covers what is different and how to play the duel.

## What changes at two

From the rulebook:

- **The board's back side,** using only the inner 12 depot spaces and 4 black depot spaces. That is 16 tiles each phase instead of 32.
- **Goods score 2 points per tile** sold, instead of 4 at four players. The 1 silver per sale stays the same.
- **Bonus tiles are 5 and 2,** instead of 7 and 4.
- **Starting workers:** the start player takes 1 worker and the other player 2.

Everything else, including region scoring and the phase bonus, is unchanged.

## Regions matter more

Region points do not scale with player count. A three-space region closed in phase A is still 16 points. Goods, on the other hand, are worth half what they are at four. Three goods tiles sold at four players score 12 points; at two, they score 6.

So the balance shifts toward your duchy. Ships still matter, but more for their turn order effect than for their goods. When a die could place a ship for a small sale or close a region, the region usually wins at two.

## Every tile is a swing

At four players, a tile you take might have gone to any of three rivals, or to nobody. At two, there is only one other player, and half as many tiles. Most tiles in a depot are wanted by someone, and if not you, then them.

That makes the swing test the core habit of the duel. For any move, ask:

**What does this gain me, plus what does it cost my opponent?**

A castle worth 6 points to you that your opponent also needs to close a region worth 13 is a 19-point swing. A monastery worth 8 to you that your opponent does not want is just 8. Often the second-best tile for you is the best move, because it is the best tile for them.

You will not always find a move that does both. But in the duel, a move that helps you and blocks them is worth looking for every turn.

## Reading one opponent

You have one board to watch, so watch it closely. Your opponent's dice are on the table the whole round, so you can see what they can reach.

1. **Their storage:** which tiles are waiting, and which numbers they need to place them.
2. **Their dice:** which depots they can reach this turn, with the workers they hold.
3. **Their small regions:** which ones are one tile from closing.

If their dice can reach a tile that closes a region for them, and you go first, consider taking it.

## Turn order in the duel

The bridge decides who picks first each round. With two players, going first means taking the best tile from a shared pool of 16. Going second means taking what is left.

Every ship moves you one space forward, on top of any piece already there. A ship placed when your pieces share a space puts you ahead next round. Time your ships for rounds where the depots are richest, usually early in a phase, after the refill.

## Bonus races at two

With two players, both can earn a bonus tile in every color: the first gets 5, the second gets 2. So the race is worth 3 points, the difference between the tiles. That is smaller than at four, where coming third earns nothing. Race when it is cheap, and do not overspend.

The exception is monastery 26, which scores 3 more points per bonus tile. With it in your duchy, even the small tile is worth chasing.

## Watch the clock of the phase

At two players, each numbered depot holds just two tiles, so the good ones go early. By round 4 or 5 of a phase, there may be nothing useful left in reach. Plan your bigger moves for the first rounds after a refill, and use the late rounds of each phase for workers, sales, and placing what you already stored.
