At home, a long game of The Castles of Burgundy is fine. Ravensburger lists 90 to 120 minutes, and a thoughtful table can run past that. At a tournament, time is part of the game. A table that runs out of time does not get to finish the way it wants, and a slow player can be penalized. This lesson is about playing well and on time.

## The time limit at the WBC

The [2026 World Boardgaming Championships event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/cob.html) sets every round at three hours. If a game is not done in time, the game master adjudicates: players finish the current round and do final scoring. If one player is mainly responsible for slow play and does not speed up after repeated reminders, the game master may penalize them.

The message is plain: keep the game moving, and do not be the reason it runs out.

## The budget

Do the math once and it sticks.

- Three hours is 180 minutes.
- The game is 25 rounds, so 180 minutes is about 7.2 minutes per round.
- With four players, that is about 1.8 minutes per turn, and less once you take out setup, the phase refills, and final scoring.

So a good target is about a minute and a half per turn, on average. Some turns will take ten seconds. A few will take three minutes. That is fine as long as the average holds.

## Think on other players' turns

The single biggest time saver is built into the rules. Everyone rolls at once, so you see your dice at the start of the round, often several turns before you act. Use that time.

By the time your turn comes, you should have:

1. **A plan:** what each die does, and any worker or silver you will spend.
2. **A backup:** what you will do if a player before you takes your tile.

When your turn comes, check that your plan still works, then act. The [reading the table lesson](/academy/castles-of-burgundy/castles-of-burgundy-reading-the-table/) gives you a routine for those minutes.

## Where to spend your long thinks

Not every turn deserves the same time. Spend it where the game changes:

- **The start of each phase.** The depots are full and fresh, and the choices are richest. Take an extra minute here to plan the whole phase: which regions you will close and which tiles you want.
- **Big chains.** A town hall into a castle into a church is worth a careful minute to get the order right.
- **The last two rounds.** Final scoring rewards exact counting.

And save time on the rest. The middle rounds of a phase, when the depots are thin, often have one clear move.

## Habits that keep turns short

- **Speak your plan once, then act.** Saying "4 takes the ship, 2 places it" helps you and the table.
- **Keep your board tidy.** Workers, silver, goods stacks, and storage where everyone can see them. Hunting for a worker costs time.
- **Score as you go.** Move your score marker the moment you score. Recounting later costs more than it saves.
- **Default moves.** When nothing good fits, take 2 workers. It is never a wasted action, and it is a fast one.

## Practicing with a clock

At home, put a timer on a phase, not a turn. Five rounds of a four-player game in about 30 minutes is a good pace. Online, timed games on [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=castlesofburgundy) give you a clock without having to set one up.

## Be kind about time

A clock can make a table tense. Gentle reminders work better than sighs. If a new player is slow, help them see their options rather than pressing them. Our guide to [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) has kind ways to do that, and they work at tournaments too.
