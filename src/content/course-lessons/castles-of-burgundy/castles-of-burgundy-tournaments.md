The Castles of Burgundy looks like a quiet game of building your own board, and it is one. It is also a game people travel to compete in, with a long-running championship at the World Boardgaming Championships, a ring event at the World Series of Board Gaming, and a busy ranked scene online. You do not need to enter any of them. But knowing how competitive play works sharpens how you play at home.

## How tournament play differs from home play

The event at the World Boardgaming Championships shows what changes:

- **Four-player tables** where possible, with three-player tables to fill out the numbers.
- **A time limit.** Long games are the enemy of a tournament schedule, so rounds have a set length, and slow play can be penalized.
- **Set duchies.** The organizer chooses the duchy boards for each round, so nobody gets to pick a favorite.
- **Limited expansions.** Some expansion tiles stay out, and so do the Trade Routes.

Rules mistakes cost more there, too. A second building of the same type in one town, for example, is the kind of slip that tournament rules spell out how to correct.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. The Castles of Burgundy is one of them, and Mike Munson won it in 2026, according to the [winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/cob.html) lays out the format:

- **Three heats,** then a semifinal and a final. Each round has a three-hour limit, and the game master decides any game not finished in time.
- **Random four-player tables** in the heats, with three-player tables when the numbers do not divide evenly.
- **Wins count most.** Heat standings rank players by wins first, then by second places, and so on down. The top 16 reach the semifinal, and only semifinal winners reach the four-player final.
- **Chosen boards.** The heats use a set of base duchies plus special monastery boards from an older expansion, and the semifinal and final use two championship boards. Some expansion tiles and the Trade Routes are not used.
- **Beginning players welcome.** The event is marked that way, which makes it a friendly first tournament.

The preview also states the event's first rule: the main goal is to have fun.

## The World Series of Board Gaming

The [World Series of Board Gaming](https://wsbgvegas.com/) (WSBG) is a five-day tournament in Las Vegas that aims to crown the best all-around board gamer. It runs 16 ring tournaments, one per game. Each ring winner moves on to the semifinals, and the overall champion wins $25,000. Its competitive events are for players 18 and up.

The Castles of Burgundy had a ring event in 2025: the Dice Tower broadcast the [ring championship final](https://dicetower.com/video/castles-burgundy-ring-championship-world-series-board-gaming-2025). The 2027 event is set for September 24 to 28, 2027.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=castlesofburgundy) has The Castles of Burgundy in your browser for 2 to 4 players, free to play, with more than 4.5 million games played since it arrived in 2021. It also has ranked play.

Check the settings before you play ranked games. Tables can use the first edition rules, which differ from your box on a few monastery tiles, or the 2019 edition rules. One setup option gives every player the same randomly chosen duchy. Identical duchies are good practice: everyone faces the same puzzle, so the scores compare cleanly.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights, and a heavier euro like this one often has a regular group. Ask the staff.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play at four.** That is what most tournament tables are.
- **Use random duchies,** and the same one for everyone when you can.
- **Play on a clock** sometimes. A time limit forces you to plan during other players' turns.
- **Review your losses.** Which region did you fail to close? Which tile did a rival take first, and could a ship have stopped it?

That is the end of the course. You now know how to play, teach, open, run a midgame engine, count the endgame, choose expansions, and find a tournament. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
