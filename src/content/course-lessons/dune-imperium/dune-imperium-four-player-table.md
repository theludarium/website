Welcome to Level 4. Tournaments seat full tables: the World Boardgaming Championships runs three and four player Heats, as its [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/DNI.html) shows. Four players is also the hardest count to read. This lesson is about seeing the whole table at once.

## What changes at four

The [rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_Rules_2020_10_26.pdf) makes three changes, and the crowd makes a fourth.

- **Everyone starts at 1 Victory Point.** The race to 10 is a little shorter.
- **The third Conflict reward is in play.** Third place pays, so cheap deployments earn more.
- **Ties work differently.** If exactly two players tie for first, both take the second reward, and the other two still compete for the third.
- **The board fills up.** The original board has 22 spaces. Four players with two or three Agents each send 8 to 12 Agents a round, plus the Mentat.

That last point changes everything about Agent order. Spaces you could leave for later at three players are gone at four.

## Seat order and the first player

The First Player marker passes clockwise each round, so in a ten-round game everyone goes first two or three times. Know which rounds are yours. Going first means first pick of the board, but also first to commit and, often, first to reveal.

Your seat matters every round too. The player on your left acts right after you, so a space you take is often a space you took from them. The player on your right acts just before you. Watch what they need, because they will take it first.

## Track the leader, not your neighbor

With four players, a fight between two of them helps the other two. The question each round is not "who annoys me" but "who is closest to winning."

Each round, find the leader, then ask:

1. **Which space do they need?** A Faction space for an Alliance step, the Swordmaster, a Maker space with a big pile.
2. **Which Conflict reward would push them toward 10?**
3. **Can I take one of those from them, and does it cost me much?**

A block that also helps your plan is free. A block that costs you a point should be used only against a real threat. Our article on [kingmaking](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) explains why a block that hands the game to a third player is worse than no block at all.

## Conflicts with three prizes

The third reward changes the math from the [Conflict lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-conflict-strategy/). One troop and a few swords can be enough for a prize at four players. That makes the Conflict area busier, and makes the strongest player's ceiling harder to read, because more players have a reason to commit.

Watch the ties, too. If two strong players are likely to tie for first, both get second, and the third reward is suddenly a real prize for everyone else.

## Alliance races with four

Four players on four tracks means most tracks have at least two players racing. The free Faction spaces become the most contested on the board. When one rival is a step from an Alliance and you cannot stop them, the player on their left might. Sometimes letting someone else spend the Agent is the right answer.

## Reading hands and garrisons

At three players you can track everyone closely. At four, focus:

- **Garrisons:** who can add troops at the last moment.
- **Agents left:** who can still reach a Combat space.
- **Intrigue cards held:** the [Intrigue lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-intrigue-cards/) explains why to add a cushion for each.
- **Reveal order:** who has already set their strength.

Those four counts are enough to estimate every player's ceiling.

## Pace

A four-player game takes longer, and tournament rounds run on a clock. WBC Heats are three hours. Play your Agent turns quickly when the choice is easy, and save your thinking for the round that decides a Conflict or an Alliance. Our piece on [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) has habits that help.

## A round-start routine

Before your first Agent each round:

1. Who leads, and by how much?
2. What do they need this round?
3. What does the player on my left need?
4. Is this Conflict worth fighting at four, and for which place?

With that routine, a crowded table stops being chaos and becomes a puzzle you can read.
