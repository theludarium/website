You know the rules, the strategy, the odds, and how events are scored. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: full tables, the event's boxes, a clock, and a scoring system that rewards wins. This lesson is a six-week plan to get there. It assumes three to five hours a week. Scale it, but keep every part.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date and a box list. The [tournaments lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-tournaments/) covers the main options:

- **The WBC Heats:** in 2026, the original game with Rise of Ix, three and four player tables, three-hour rounds, and Epic mode in the Final, per the [2026 preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/DNI.html).
- **The North American Championship qualifiers at Gen Con:** in 2026, Uprising for the qualifiers and Uprising with Bloodlines for the Top 64, per [Dire Wolf's announcement](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/join-the-conspiracy-in-the-dune-imperium-championship-tournament-at-gen-con/).

WBC 2027 runs July 24 to August 1, 2027, according to the [WBC page](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbcindex.html).

The two events use different games. Pick one and train for it. Practicing Uprising for a Rise of Ix event wastes half your weeks.

## The five parts of every week

**1. Volume.** Play many games. [Dune: Imperium Digital](https://www.direwolfdigital.com/dune-imperium-digital/) runs online games against people and AI, and Dire Wolf announced on June 9, 2026 that [Uprising was available](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/uprising-digital-is-now-available/) on Steam and mobile. Digital play handles the bookkeeping, so you see more decisions per hour. Aim for four to six games a week. <!-- AFFILIATE: Dune: Imperium Digital -->

**2. The right boxes.** At least once a week, play on the table with exactly your event's setup: the original with Rise of Ix for WBC, Uprising with the right expansions for Gen Con. Use four players when you can.

**3. The clock.** Time every table game. A four-player game that runs well over three hours would not finish in a WBC round. Track how long your group takes and where the time goes.

**4. Drills.** Ten minutes, two or three times a week:

- **Hand drill.** Deal yourself five random cards from your deck. Split them into Agent and Reveal piles in under thirty seconds, as in the [hand planning lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-hand-planning/).
- **Ceiling drill.** At any point in a digital game, pause and work out each opponent's maximum strength: troops in, garrison, Agents left, Intrigue cards held. The [midgame lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-midgame-strategy/) has the method.
- **Conflict III drill.** Name every Conflict III card in your event's game from memory, with its rewards. In the original and Uprising, every one appears in a full game, as the [odds lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-probability/) shows.
- **Deck count.** In each game, say before you draw which cards are left in your deck.

**5. Review.** After every loss, write short answers to three questions:

1. Which Conflict did I overpay for, or skip when I should have fought?
2. Which Alliance did I lose or fail to take, and when could I have changed it?
3. When did I get my third Agent, and could it have been sooner?

A mistake you see three times is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: foundations.** Volume and review. Focus on openings: track your Swordmaster round and High Council round in every game, and compare them with the winner's.

**Weeks 3 and 4: the table.** Add timed games with your event's exact boxes. If your event is WBC, play at least one Epic mode game to 12, since the Final uses it. Practice the round-start routine from the [four-player lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-four-player-table/).

**Week 5: event scoring.** Play every game for the win only, the way both events reward. When you are in third with two rounds left, take the risky line that could win, not the safe one that holds third. At WBC, keep scoring even when you cannot win, because total Victory Points break ties.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. A couple of timed games early in the week, then ease off. Arrive rested; a four-hour Final is long.

## What to measure

Keep a few numbers from week to week:

- **Win rate** in four-player games.
- **Average Victory Points,** win or lose.
- **The round you got your third Agent.**
- **Points from Influence and Alliances** versus points from Conflicts.
- **Game length** in timed games.

If your win rate and average score rise while your games get shorter, the plan is working.

## The table across from you

A tournament can turn a game into work. Keep one thing in view. Every opponent across the table chose to spend their weekend on the same game you love. Learn their names. Talk through the close Conflicts afterward. If you are near Indianapolis, the Gen Con championship has been coming to your city, and you can join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

That is the end of the course. You can play, teach, and read the board; plan a hand and run an economy; build a deck, fight the right Conflicts, and race the tracks; time Intrigue, play Uprising's spies and worms, count your odds, and beat the Rivals; read a four-player table under event rules; and walk into a tournament with a plan. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
