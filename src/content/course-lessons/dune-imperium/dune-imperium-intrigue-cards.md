Welcome to Level 3. You can build a deck, fight for the right Conflicts, and race the tracks. Advanced play is about what the table cannot see. Intrigue cards are the biggest source of hidden information in Dune: Imperium, and handling them well is a mark of a strong player.

## The three timings

The [rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_Rules_2020_10_26.pdf) splits Intrigue cards by when you may play them:

- **Plot:** any time during one of your own Agent or Reveal turns.
- **Combat:** only in the Combat phase. Cards that trigger "when you win a Conflict" wait until you have won, and the [FAQ](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_FAQ_25-1-13.pdf) says a tie for first is not a win.
- **Endgame:** only at the end, after the last round, before the score is final.

You keep them face down and separate from your deck, and you must meet a card's conditions and pay its costs to play it. Unlike an arrow on an Imperium card, an Intrigue card's cost is not optional.

## Where they come from

In the original game, the rulebook names three main sources: **Carthag** (with a troop), **Conspire** (with Solari, troops, and Emperor Influence), and **Secrets** (with Bene Gesserit Influence). Cards and Conflict rewards add more. In Uprising, the main sources are Secrets, Sardaukar, and Assembly Hall.

Secrets has a second edge: every opponent holding four or more Intrigue cards gives you one at random. So four is a soft limit. Above three, you are a target.

## Combat Intrigue: the order of play

In the Combat phase, everyone with at least one troop in the Conflict, starting with the first player, may play Combat Intrigue cards or pass. Passing once does not lock you out; the phase ends only when everyone passes in a row.

That structure rewards patience:

- **If you are ahead, pass.** Make the others act first. If nobody plays anything, you win without spending a card.
- **If you are behind, play only what wins.** A card that brings you level but not ahead just invites the leader to answer.
- **Play your strongest last.** A small card early can draw out a rival's answer, then a big one takes the round.

You need a troop in the Conflict to take part. A player who deployed even one troop, with 2 strength, can still turn a fight with a Combat card. The rulebook's example card, Ambush, adds 4 strength.

## The threat is worth something

Karlis Povisils, the 2022 North American Champion, told Dire Wolf in his [interview](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/an-interview-with-karlis-povisils-2022-dune-imperium-na-champion) that holding Intrigue cards matters even when you do not play them, because it keeps opponents unsure. A rival deciding whether to commit four troops against you has to price in whatever you might be holding. Sometimes that doubt makes them overpay. Sometimes it makes them sit out.

So an unplayed Intrigue card is not wasted, as long as it does that job. One or two in hand is a threat. Five is a gift to whoever visits Secrets next.

## Counting the table

You cannot see opponents' Intrigue cards, but you can count them. Keep a quiet tally:

- Who drew one this round, and from where?
- Who has played one, and which type?
- Who is holding three or more?

A player who just drew from Conspire and kept the card through a close Conflict may be holding something for later, or holding a Plot or Endgame card that does nothing in Combat. You learn more each time they decline a chance to play.

## Plot cards and tempo

Plot cards are flexible: most give resources, Influence, or cards on your own turn. The best moment is usually when they complete something: the step that reaches 2 or 4 Influence, the Solari that buys the Swordmaster this turn, the spice that opens Heighliner. A Plot card that fills a gap in a chain is worth more than the same card played on its own.

## Endgame cards

Endgame cards do nothing until the game ends, so they are pure points or tiebreakers held in reserve. Read them as soon as you draw them. Some reward a condition you can set up over several rounds. The FAQ's note on Corner the Market, for example, counts The Spice Must Flow cards in your deck and discard pile against each opponent's.

## Uprising notes

The [Uprising rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_UPRISING_Main_Rulebook_23-10-12.pdf) keeps the three timings. Its examples show how much a Combat card can do: Contingency Plan adds 3 strength, and Unexpected Allies costs 2 water, removes the Shield Wall, and summons a sandworm, which doubles that player's rewards. With worms in the game, a single Intrigue card can swing a Conflict by much more than its printed strength.

## A short checklist

1. How many Intrigue cards does each rival hold?
2. Am I above three, and is anyone heading to Secrets?
3. In Combat, am I ahead? Then pass first.
4. Which Plot card completes a chain this round?
