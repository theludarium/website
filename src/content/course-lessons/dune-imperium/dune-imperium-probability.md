Dune: Imperium has two decks you can reason about: your own, and the Conflict deck. Neither is as random as it feels. This lesson puts numbers on both, using the real component counts from the rulebooks and checked arithmetic for the odds. You do not need a calculator at the table; you need a feel for a few numbers.

## Your deck between shuffles

The [rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_Rules_2020_10_26.pdf) sends acquired cards to your discard pile and reshuffles only when you need to draw from an empty deck. So once a deck is shuffled, you draw through it in order, five cards a round, with nothing new added until the next shuffle.

That has a big consequence. **You can know what is coming.**

- **Rounds 1 and 2:** your ten starting cards split into two hands of five. Everything in your first hand is not in your second. Nothing you buy in rounds 1 or 2 can show up until round 3, unless a card makes you draw extra.
- **Later shuffles:** if your deck has 15 cards, after two rounds 5 are left, and those 5 are your next hand. If your best card has not appeared, it is coming next round, for certain.

Draw effects muddy this a little, and so does a shuffle that happens partway through drawing a hand. But the habit of counting what is left pays off every game.

## The chance a card is in your hand

Right after a shuffle, the chance a given card lands in your hand of five is 5 divided by your deck size. The chance of at least one of several useful cards is higher. Here is the full table, worked out from the hand size and deck sizes:

| Deck size | 1 useful card | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 50% | 78% | 92% | 98% |
| 12 | 42% | 68% | 84% | 93% |
| 14 | 36% | 60% | 77% | 87% |
| 16 | 31% | 54% | 71% | 82% |
| 18 | 28% | 49% | 65% | 77% |

"Useful card" can mean anything: a specific card, any card with a Fremen icon, any card with two or more swords. Count how many your deck has, find your deck size, and read across.

Two readings worth remembering:

- **One copy of anything is unreliable.** In a 14-card deck, a single card shows up about a third of the time. If your plan needs it this round, you need a backup.
- **Three or four is reliable.** Four Fremen-icon cards in a 14-card deck give you at least one about 87% of the time.

## Needing two cards together

Combos are harder. The chance that two specific cards are both in the same hand of five is 22% in a 10-card deck, 15% in 12, and 11% in 14. A plan that needs two particular cards at once will fail most rounds. Build for "at least one of these" instead.

## What trashing buys

Trashing raises every number in the table. Cut a 14-card deck to 12 and a single card's chance rises from 36% to 42%. Cut it to 10 and it is 50%. That is the math behind the [deck-building lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-deck-building/), and behind its warning: the useful cards have to stay in.

## The Conflict deck

Setup always builds ten Conflict cards: one Conflict I, five Conflict II, and four Conflict III. Since the box holds a known number of each, you can work out how likely any one card is to appear.

| Version | Conflict I used | Conflict II used | Conflict III used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original | 1 of 4 (25%) | 5 of 10 (50%) | 4 of 4 (always) |
| Uprising | 1 of 3 (33%) | 5 of 9 (56%) | 4 of 4 (always) |
| Original with Rise of Ix | 1 of 6 (17%) | 5 of 11 (45%) | 4 of 5 (80%) |

The counts come from each [rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_UPRISING_Main_Rulebook_23-10-12.pdf) and the [Rise of Ix rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_RISE_OF_IX_Rulebook_22-2-11.pdf), which adds two Conflict I, one Conflict II, and one Conflict III card.

What that means:

- **In the original and Uprising, every Conflict III card comes up, if the game lasts ten rounds.** Learn them. You know the last four fights before the game starts; you just do not know the order.
- **Any one Conflict II card is roughly a coin flip.** Do not plan around a particular one.
- **Rise of Ix's Epic mode** drops the Conflict I card and uses five Conflict II cards on five Conflict III cards, so with Rise of Ix every Conflict III card comes up.

## Reading the late rounds

In the original game, rounds 7 to 10 are Conflict III. If two Conflict III cards are already done, the other two are what remain, in some order. Each is a 50% chance to come next round. That is enough to decide whether to stock your garrison now or wait.
