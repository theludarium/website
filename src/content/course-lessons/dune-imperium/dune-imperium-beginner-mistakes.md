Your first three games of Dune: Imperium are where habits form. Some are rules slips, played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal plays that quietly cost points. This lesson covers both, checked against the rulebook and Dire Wolf's [official FAQ](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_FAQ_25-1-13.pdf), with an Uprising section at the end.

## Troop slips

**Deploying from a space without crossed swords.** You may move troops into the Conflict only on a turn when you send an Agent to a Combat space. Recruit four troops at Rally Troops and all four go to your garrison.

**Pulling more than two from the garrison.** On a Combat space you may deploy every troop recruited that turn, from any source, plus up to two from your garrison. The FAQ is clear that you cannot get around the limit by "re-recruiting" troops already sitting in the garrison.

**Counting swords with no troops.** Swords only add strength if you have at least one troop in the Conflict. No troops, no strength, whatever your Reveal turn showed.

**Sending troops home to the garrison.** After Combat, troops in the Conflict return to your supply. The garrison is for troops that never fought.

## Card slips

**Using both halves of a card.** On an Agent turn you use the Agent box; on your Reveal turn, the Reveal box. A card played as an Agent gives nothing when you reveal. A card with no Agent icons can only be revealed.

**Buying into your hand.** Acquired cards go to your discard pile. You see them after your next reshuffle.

**Saving Persuasion.** There are no Persuasion tokens. What you do not spend on your Reveal turn is gone.

**Paying a cost twice.** When a card offers "pay this, get that," you may pay once. The FAQ uses Duncan Idaho as the example: one water, one troop, one card, not two of each.

## Combat and Intrigue slips

**Handing out a third reward in a three-player game.** The third reward goes only to third place in a four-player game. With fewer players, it is given only when two or more players tie for second.

**Playing Intrigue cards at the wrong time.** Plot cards on your own turns. Combat cards only in the Combat phase. Endgame cards at the end. Cards that trigger "when you win a Conflict" are played after you win, and the FAQ says a tie for first is not a win.

**Hoarding Intrigue.** When someone sends an Agent to Secrets, every opponent holding four or more Intrigue cards gives them one at random.

## Influence slips

**Taking an Alliance on a tie.** The first player to reach 4 keeps the Alliance token until someone climbs to a **higher** space. Matching them is not enough.

**Forgetting to lose things.** Drop below 2 Influence and you lose that track's Victory Point. Drop to 3 or lower while holding the Alliance and the FAQ says it moves to whoever else has 4 or more, or back to the board if nobody does. And if you were tied with someone at the top and step down, they take it. The bonus for reaching 4 stays with you either way.

**Ignoring board requirements.** Sietch Tabr needs 2 Influence with the Fremen before you can send an Agent there.

## The big strategic mistake: buying Persuasion and nothing else

New players see big Persuasion numbers and buy cards for their Reveal boxes. Two games later their deck can buy anything and go almost nowhere, because every card has the same one or two Agent icons.

The rulebook says it plainly in its strategy tips: part of what you pay for is access. Before you buy, look at the icons in the card's top left corner and ask which spaces it opens that your deck cannot reach now.

## Fighting every Conflict

The rulebook also warns that a smaller win at a lower cost can beat a big win bought dearly. Throwing six troops at a Conflict I card leaves you thin for the Conflict II cards that follow. Some rounds the right play is to send in two troops for second place, or nothing at all.

And deploy late when you can. The last player to commit sees what everyone else has done.

## Uprising slips

**Recalling a Spy without playing a card.** Infiltrate and Gather Intelligence both happen as part of an Agent turn, so you still play a card. You may recall two Spies in one turn only if each does a different job.

**Skipping a contract.** With the CHOAM module, completing a contract is mandatory once you meet its condition. Several contracts for the same space all complete on one visit.

**Worms where they cannot go.** While the Shield Wall stands, sandworms cannot join Conflicts at Arrakeen, Spice Refinery, or Imperial Basin. Sandworms never wait in a garrison, and summoning them does not use up your Maker Hooks.

**Paying full price for the Swordmaster.** It costs 8 Solari until any player has one, then 6.

## A checklist for game three

Before every Agent turn, ask:

1. Is this a Combat space, and how many troops can I deploy from here?
2. Which half of this card am I using?
3. Who is one step from passing me on a Faction track?
