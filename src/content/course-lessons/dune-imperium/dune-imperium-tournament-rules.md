A tournament game of Dune: Imperium has the same rules as a game at home. What changes is the frame around it: how results are scored, how ties between players across games are broken, and how long you have. This lesson covers the two main events in the United States as they ran in 2026. Our guide to [running a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) explains the formats from the organizer's side.

Formats change from year to year. Always read the current rules for your event.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

From the [WBC 2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/previews/DNI.html):

- **Format:** Heats, then a Semifinal and a Final. Heats are three and four player games in three-hour rounds. The Semifinal is three hours; the Final runs four.
- **Game:** the original Dune: Imperium with Rise of Ix. A Heat table may play the base game only if everyone at it agrees, or if Rise of Ix is not available. A Family Atomics rule applies throughout, and the Final uses Epic mode.
- **Class:** beginning players welcome.

### How Heats are scored

Each Heat finish earns points: 1,000 for first, 100 for second, 10 for third, and 1 for fourth. Add them across the Heats you play. The top 16 move on to the Semifinal.

Players are ranked for those 16 places by, in order:

1. Most wins across all Heats.
2. Total points from the scale above.
3. Total Victory Points scored across all Heats.
4. A high dice roll.

### What that scoring means

- **A win is worth ten second places.** One first place, 1,000 points, beats any number of second places you could play in three Heats. Play every Heat to win your table.
- **Second still matters,** but only to separate players with the same number of wins.
- **Your Victory Points count even when you lose.** The third tiebreaker adds up your in-game score in every Heat, so a loss at 9 points is better than a loss at 5. Keep scoring in a game you cannot win.
- **Play more Heats if you can.** Your score is a total, so a Heat you skip is a chance at a win you gave up.

## The North American Championship at Gen Con

Dire Wolf's [2026 announcement](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/join-the-conspiracy-in-the-dune-imperium-championship-tournament-at-gen-con/) described a different structure:

- **Open qualifiers** on Thursday and Friday of Gen Con, played in Uprising, booked through the Gen Con event catalog.
- **The winner of each qualifier game** moves on to the Top 64 on Saturday.
- **Top 64:** Uprising with Bloodlines. **Top 16 and the final four:** Uprising, Bloodlines, and a preview of the upcoming Conspiracy expansion.
- **More than one try.** You could book several qualifier slots. If you lost one, you could play a later one. If you won and then won again, the second-place player at that later table advanced instead.

### What that structure means

Every qualifier is one game, and only first place goes through. There is no credit for second. That makes the qualifier the purest "play to win" format in competitive Dune: Imperium. A risky line that wins one game in three is better than a safe line that finishes second every time.

The posted announcement does not list time limits. Ask at the event.

## Playing under a clock

A three-hour round is plenty for a four-player game at a steady pace, and not enough for a slow one. Habits that help:

- **Plan your hand at round start,** as in the [hand planning lesson](/academy/dune-imperium/dune-imperium-hand-planning/). Then easy Agent turns take seconds.
- **Think on others' turns.** Watch what they take and adjust your plan before your turn comes.
- **Spend time where it matters:** the Conflict commitment, the Alliance step, the last round.

Our piece on [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) has more ways to keep a heavy game moving.

## Clean table habits

The rulebooks already set the habits that keep a tournament table fair:

- **Keep your supply in clear view.** Resources, troops, and components are public, as setup in the [rulebook](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_Rules_2020_10_26.pdf) says.
- **Keep Intrigue cards face down and separate** from your deck, and reveal them only when played.
- **Announce your strength** when you set it, and move your Combat marker.
- **Say what you are doing.** Name the space, the cost you are paying, and how many troops you deploy and from where. It prevents disputes and speeds the table up.
- **Call the GM or a judge** for a rules question rather than arguing it. The current [FAQ](https://d19y2ttatozxjp.cloudfront.net/pdfs/DUNE_IMPERIUM_FAQ_25-1-13.pdf) is worth having on your phone.
