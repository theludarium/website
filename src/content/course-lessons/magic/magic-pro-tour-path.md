At the top of competitive Magic sits the Pro Tour, a few events a year where the best players meet for prize money and the World Championship race. It can look out of reach. It is not closed. The ladder is published, and its first rung is at a local game store.

This lesson maps that ladder from Wizards' [Pro Tour page](https://magic.gg/pro-tour) and its 2026 to 2027 announcements. The details change from season to season, so check [magic.gg](https://magic.gg) before you plan around a date.

## The main ladder

**1. Regional Championship Qualifier (RCQ).** The first rung, covered in the [first competitive event lesson](/academy/magic/magic-store-championship-rcq/). RCQs run at stores and larger venues at Competitive REL, and top finishers earn a seat at their Regional Championship.

**2. Regional Championship (RC).** Each region holds its own championship. In the current round, which began September 11, 2026, Regional Championships play Modern, and the United States has two, run by Star City Games: Baltimore in September and Los Angeles in October. Top finishers earn invitations to the first Pro Tour of 2027. You can also reach an RC through Magic Online qualifiers, or by already holding an invitation to the matching Pro Tour.

**3. The Pro Tour.** Professional REL, held at MagicCon events. Wizards announced the 2027 schedule:

| Event | Dates | Formats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro Tour Nauctis, MagicCon: Detroit | February 26 to 28, 2027 | Nauctis: The Sunken Realm Draft and Modern |
| Pro Tour at MagicCon: Tokyo | May 14 to 16, 2027 | Draft and Standard |
| Pro Tour at MagicCon: Las Vegas | August 27 to 29, 2027 | Draft and Standard |
| World Championship 33, MagicCon: Amsterdam | December 3 to 5, 2027 | Draft and Standard |

Notice the formats. Every 2027 Pro Tour includes a draft. A constructed specialist who cannot draft gives away a big share of every event. Your Level 2 [draft work](/academy/magic/magic-draft-strategy/) matters all the way up.

## The side doors

Wizards lists several other routes onto the Pro Tour:

- **Spotlight Series.** Large open tournaments where the top 8 earn Pro Tour invitations.
- **Pro Tour Qualifiers held at MagicCon events.** Top finishers qualify.
- **MTG Arena.** Top 16 finishers at each Arena Championship qualify. You reach an Arena Championship through Arena's qualifier events.
- **Magic Online.** The top 8 at each Magic Online Champions Showcase qualify.
- **Staying qualified.** Players who earn 30 or more match points at a Pro Tour qualify for the next one, and a points system rewards strong results across the last three.
- **The Hall of Fame**, for a few legends of the game.

For players who prefer playing from home, Arena and Magic Online are real paths. They also offer the volume of practice that store events cannot.

## What changes at the top

- **Professional REL.** Players are held to a higher standard of technically correct play.
- **Open decklists.** At Regional Championships and the Pro Tour, the Tournament Rules say opponents' decklists are given to players. You prepare knowing exactly what you might face. So does everyone else.
- **Travel.** RCs and Pro Tours are destination events. Budget for a weekend away, and look for a teammate to share costs and testing.
- **Teams.** Many players prepare with a testing team: a few friends who share decks, practice games, and sideboard plans. You can start one at your local store.

## Is this path for you?

The competitive path rewards practice, patience, and a love of hard games. It does not require being a professional. Most people who play RCQs never reach a Pro Tour, and that is fine. A qualifier still gives you a day of focused games, a few new friends, and a clear measure of how much you have improved.
