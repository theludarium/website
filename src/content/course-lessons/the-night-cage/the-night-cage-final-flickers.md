No candle burns forever. Every tile you draw, discard, or lose to a Wax Eater comes out of the same Draw Stack, and one day it runs dry. That starts Final Flickers, the endgame. The board stops growing and starts shrinking, and the group has a short window to escape. This lesson covers the rules and how to plan the last stretch.

## When Final Flickers starts

Final Flickers starts when the Draw Stack is empty. It begins immediately at the end of the turn of the Prisoner who drew the last tile.

From then on, no new tiles can be added to the board. Play goes on with whatever tiles are already there. When you move, there is nothing to draw for the open Passages ahead of you, so you can only walk onto tiles that already exist.

## The extra tile each turn

At the end of every Prisoner's turn during Final Flickers, after removing the tiles that are no longer Illuminated, you must also remove one more Illuminated tile from anywhere on the board. A Monster can be the tile you remove. The official FAQ adds that a tile with a Prisoner on it can be removed too, if there are no other options, which it calls a particularly tragic way to lose.

Two timing details matter:

- **Extra Moves come first.** The extra tile is removed only after all Move Again steps bought with Nerve are done.
- **Nerve from Staying comes last.** Nerve gained by Staying arrives at the very end of your turn, even after the Final Flickers removal. So you cannot spend it on the same turn you earn it.

## Sustain

Sustain is the Nerve spend made for this phase. Spend 1 Nerve during Final Flickers to skip removing that extra Illuminated tile after your turn. Each Sustain keeps one more tile on the board for one more turn.

Nerve is capped at 2, so no Prisoner can bank a big pile. That is why [When to Stay and Spend Nerve](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-nerve/) suggests holding Nerve as the Draw Stack runs low. A table that arrives at Final Flickers with full Nerve has room to breathe.

## How you lose in the endgame

The rules name the ways the dark wins at the end:

- **A Prisoner is isolated.** The darkness cuts them off, and they cannot bring their Key to the Gate with the others.
- **A Prisoner falls with nothing to land on.** If you are falling and the Draw Stack is empty, you fall forever, and the game ends in a loss.
- **Too many Keys or Gates are gone.** If all four Gates are lost, there is no escape.

The second point changes how you treat Crumbling tiles near the end. During Final Flickers, any Fall is a loss, because there is no tile to land on. Leaping into a Pit to cross the board is off the table. And Staying on a Crumbling tile you stepped onto last turn means you Fall. Check every tile under every Prisoner before anyone decides to Stay.

## Plan the convergence early

To win, every Prisoner must stand on the same Gate at the same time, each holding a Key. The Gate is the only tile that more than one Prisoner can share, so it is also the only place the group can fully gather.

Most of that work belongs before Final Flickers starts:

- **Choose one Gate.** Splitting attention between two Gates spreads your light thin. Once a Gate is lit and reachable, aim everyone at it.
- **Anchor the Gate.** A Prisoner standing on the Gate keeps it lit while the others travel. A Gate that drops out of everyone's light is discarded like any other tile.
- **Finish the Keys first.** One Key each, and a Prisoner can carry only one. Passing a Key to an adjacent, connected Prisoner is a free action, so a Prisoner near a spare Key can pick it up and hand it off. [Keys and Gates](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-keys-and-gates/) covers the counting.
- **Shorten the routes.** Remember the edges wrap. The shortest path to the Gate may run off one side of the board and back on the other.
- **Pull the far Prisoner in.** The Prisoner furthest from the Gate is the one most likely to be isolated. Spend the group's moves and Nerve on them first.

## Choose the removed tile together

The extra tile each turn is removed "from anywhere on the board", so the group decides which one. Treat that choice as part of the plan. Good candidates are tiles behind everyone, dead ends, and Monsters whose line of sight crosses your route. Bad candidates are the Gate, any tile on the path a Prisoner still needs, and any tile that would leave a Prisoner cut off.

Talk the choice through each turn. Our article on [cooperative games and teamwork](/articles/cooperative-games-teamwork/) has more on making those calls as a group.

## The last turn

You win the moment every Prisoner stands on one Gate with a Key and they turn the Keys together. Count the moves each Prisoner needs and the Nerve you have for Move Again and Sustain, and you will often know a few turns ahead whether the escape is on. If it is close, spend everything. Leftover Nerve does nothing.
