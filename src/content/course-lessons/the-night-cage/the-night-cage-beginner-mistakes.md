The Night Cage is hard on purpose. The official rules say it is intended to be difficult to win, so a loss in your first game is normal. Still, a loss is more useful when the rules were right. A short list of rules trips up many new groups. Here they are, with the official answer for each.

## Crumbling tiles give way on the next turn

Start tiles, Straight Passages, Key tiles, and two of the T tiles are Crumbling. You can spot them by the thin crack lines around them. A Crumbling tile does not collapse the moment you step onto it. It becomes a Pit on your next turn after you step onto it.

On that next turn you choose. If you Move off, the tile flips into a Pit as you scramble away, and you are safe. If you Stay, the floor gives way and you Fall. New groups often flip the tile as soon as someone arrives, or forget to flip it at all. Your Start tile is Crumbling too, so the timing matters from the very first round.

## Staying burns wax, and a Monster does not go away

Staying gains you a Nerve, but it also burns wax: you discard the top tile of the Draw Stack face up onto the Discard Board. The common slip is what happens when that tile is a Monster. You do not discard it. You must replace one of the tiles connected to you with the Monster tile. Staying does not trigger Monster attacks, so it sits quietly for now, but it is right beside you, and the next Move in its line of sight will set it off.

A second slip: spending the Nerve you just gained. The official FAQ says Nerve from Staying arrives at the very end of your turn, even after any Final Flickers discard, so you cannot use it on the turn you earn it. And no Prisoner can hold more than 2.

## Do not draw for Passages a friend already lights

When you Move, you draw one tile for each open Passage leading from your new space. But no tile is drawn for a Passage that another Prisoner already Illuminates. Groups that miss this burn through the Draw Stack fast.

The same idea works in reverse. A tile is discarded only when no Prisoner lights it anymore. If you walk away from a tile your neighbor still lights, it stays.

## Keys crumble, and each Prisoner carries one

Key tiles are Crumbling. Walk across a Key tile you cannot use, and it becomes a Pit with the Key lost inside it. And a Prisoner can carry only one Key, because the other hand holds the candle. So a Prisoner who already has a Key and finds another faces a real problem. Walk away and the Key falls out of the light. Walk over it and the tile crumbles. Block the only Passage to it and nobody else can reach it.

What many groups miss is that Keys can be passed. When two Prisoners are adjacent and connected by a Passage, one can hand a Key to the other during either one's turn. It is a free action. Lesson 7, on [Keys and Gates](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-keys-and-gates/), shows how to use that.

## Only a Gate holds more than one Prisoner

The Passages are too narrow to share. You cannot Move onto a tile where another Prisoner stands, and you cannot slip past one. The only exception is a Gate, which holds any number of Prisoners at once. This matters most at the end, when everyone needs a route to the same Gate. A single corridor with a friend standing in the middle of it is not a route.

## Falling is not death

New players often treat a Pit like the end. It is not. When you Fall, your turn ends and your Prisoner token waits at the edge of the board, beside the row or column that runs through the Pit. At the start of your next turn you choose an empty, unlit space along that line, draw a tile to land on, and carry on. The rules even let you leap into an adjacent Pit you are connected to, on purpose.

Falling has two real dangers. You might land on a Monster, which attacks at once. And if the Draw Stack is empty when you need a tile to land on, you fall forever and the group loses. Outside those cases, a Fall is a way across the board. Lesson 6, on [Crumbling tiles, Pits, and Falling](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-crumbling-tiles-and-falling/), covers how to use one well.

## A quick check before the first turn

Most of these slips vanish if someone points at the pieces during setup. Hold up a cracked tile, a Wax Eater, a Key tile, and a Gate, and say one sentence about each. The [teach sheet](/academy/the-night-cage/teach-sheet/) puts the turn order on one page so the table can check a rule without stopping the game.

In a cooperative game, a rules check is not an argument; it is the group looking after itself. For more on keeping a co-op table friendly, see our piece on [co-op games without the quarterback problem](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/).
