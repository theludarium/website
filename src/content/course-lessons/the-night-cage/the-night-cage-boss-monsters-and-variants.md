The Night Cage is meant to be hard to win, and the rules hand you ways to make it easier or harder still. This lesson covers the two Boss Monsters, The Pathless and The Dirge, and the simple difficulty tweaks in the Variants section, including a communication rule that changes how a co-op table talks.

## Boss Monsters

Boss Monsters can join either the Standard Game or the [Advanced Game](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-advanced-game/). You can add one of them or both. The rules describe them as overpowered on purpose, built to create dread and push you toward new plans while you wait for them to appear.

The official FAQ says you may use any combination of advanced and Boss Monsters at once, though it strongly advises against using them all.

## The Pathless

There are 3 Pathless tiles in the box. To add The Pathless, shuffle 2 of them into the Draw Stack during setup. Add the third for even more of a challenge.

The Pathless is placed like any tile and is triggered just like a Wax Eater. Two things set it apart:

- **Nothing stops its attack.** Walls, gaps between tiles, and Pits do not block it. Its attack reaches every space along the row and the column it sits in.
- **It hits hard.** A hit from The Pathless discards 5 tiles from the Draw Stack, and this attack cannot be Blocked with Nerve.

### Playing around The Pathless

Because its reach ignores the maze, the safe question is simple: which Prisoners share its row or its column? Every one of them is exposed to a 5-tile hit that Nerve cannot soften. When you draw one, its placement is a group decision. Look for an open space whose row and column hold as few Prisoners as you can manage, and remember that Prisoners will move into those lines later.

## The Dirge

The Dirge is a huge Monster that covers 9 spaces when it arrives. Omens warn you that it is coming.

**Setup.** Shuffle 2 Omen tiles into the Draw Stack. The other 3 Omens stay in the box; they are for other content. The FAQ is clear that Omens are not Monsters. They are only a countdown.

**The countdown.**

- When the first Omen is revealed, set it beside the board and draw a new tile to replace it.
- When the second Omen is revealed, place it on the board like any tile. This marks The Dirge's arrival and anchors where it lands.

**Placing The Dirge.** Place the oversized Dirge token so part of it overlaps the Omen you just placed. You choose its position and its orientation, and you may try placements before you commit. The whole shape must fit on the board; no part can hang off an edge.

**What it does.** Every Prisoner beneath The Dirge goes Lights Out, discards 3 tiles from the Draw Stack, and then falls into a Pit. That can include the Prisoner who placed the Omen, if they lay The Dirge over themselves. This attack can be Blocked with Nerve, dropping the discard to 2. Then every tile beneath The Dirge, the Omen included, is discarded.

Finally, flip The Dirge to its Pit side, over the same spaces. That leaves a chasm-sized Pit 9 spaces large. It stays until no Prisoner's candle lights it any longer.

### Playing around The Dirge

- **The first Omen is a warning.** Once it appears, the next Omen will place The Dirge. Keep Prisoners from bunching where a 9-space shape could cover several of them.
- **Placement is yours.** The official example shows how a careful placement can hit no Prisoners at all. Try the options before you commit, as the rules allow, and look for one that spares both your Prisoners and the tiles you need, like a Gate or a Key.
- **The chasm can help.** It is a Pit, and Pits interrupt a Wax Eater's line of sight, so a big one can cut several dangerous lines at once. For how Pits work as a tool, see [Crumbling Tiles, Pits, and Falling](/academy/the-night-cage/the-night-cage-crumbling-tiles-and-falling/).

## Turning the difficulty dial

The Variants section offers small tile tweaks:

- **Easier:** add a Key tile or two to the tile count.
- **Harder:** remove a single Key or Gate tile from the tile count, or both.
- **Harder, another way:** swap two T Passages for Crumbling versions.

Each tweak pulls on a different part of the game: extra Keys make the "one Key each" goal less fragile. Fewer Gates make every lost Gate scarier. More Crumbling tiles mean more Pits and more chances to Fall. If your group keeps losing to the same thing, the easier tweak that targets it is a good place to start.

## Eerie Silence

The Eerie Silence variant limits talk: Prisoners cannot speak to each other unless they are connected by a Passage.

This one changes the feel more than the numbers. Players who wander apart lose their voice in the plan, and regrouping becomes a way to talk again, not just a way to relight a candle. It is also a natural cure for one player running the whole game.
