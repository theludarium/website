There is no game master in Jaws of the Lion. The monsters run on a short set of rules, and once you can run those rules in your head, the game changes. You stop reacting to monsters and start placing them. This lesson walks through the rules in the order a monster uses them, citing the Learn to Play Guide by page.

## Step 1: find the focus

Before a monster does anything else on its turn, it picks a **focus**: the one character it will try to attack (p. 11).

The focus is the character the monster can attack **using the least movement**. For a melee monster, that means the shortest path to a hex adjacent to a character. For a ranged monster, it means the shortest path to a hex within range of a character (p. 11 and p. 18).

Some details that settle most arguments:

- **Movement, not distance.** A character two hexes away behind a wall can be farther, in movement, than a character five hexes away in the open (p. 11).
- **No line of sight needed.** Focus only needs a path (p. 11).
- **It does not need to arrive this turn.** If a path exists at all, even a long one, the monster focuses on that character (p. 11).
- **Traps count as obstacles.** Unless a trap is the only way to reach anyone, the monster plans around traps. If it must cross them, it picks the path through the fewest (p. 17).
- **Difficult terrain costs 2** when counting, just as it does for movement (p. 20).
- **Ties.** If two characters take the same movement, the guide breaks the tie by initiative order: the one who acts earlier is the focus (p. 11). The Rules Glossary says the same, with no extra step. The full Gloomhaven rules add a step first: the character closer in range wins the tie, and only then does initiative decide. If your group moves on to Gloomhaven, play it that way.
- **No path, no focus.** If there is no hex the monster can reach to attack anyone, it does not move or attack (p. 11).

## Step 2: move

Once it has a focus, a monster moves toward the hex it found, up to its movement for the turn (p. 11). Three rules shape the move:

- **No Move on the card, no move.** A monster only does what its ability card says (p. 17).
- **Ranged monsters move only as far as needed** to get in range. If they start adjacent to their focus, they step away to avoid the Disadvantage of a ranged attack on an adjacent target, if they can. A monster that already has Disadvantage from another source, like Muddle, will not bother to step away (p. 18).
- **Melee and disarmed monsters** move to get adjacent, as normal (p. 18).

If more than one hex is equally good, the players choose where it goes (p. 11).

## Step 3: attack

If the monster ends its move able to attack its focus, it attacks with its base attack, changed by the ability card, then flips a card from the shared monster modifier deck (p. 11 and p. 17). Active bonuses on the stat card, like a monster that always wounds, apply to every attack it makes (p. 23).

**Several targets.** When a monster can attack more than one target, it first finds its main focus, then looks for the closest hex from which it can attack that focus and as many other targets as its attack allows (p. 22).

## The elements

Monster ability cards can infuse and consume elements too. Monsters always consume an element if they can, and every monster of that set that acts gets the benefit, not just the first (p. 26). Watch the element board before you leave fire or light waning for an ally: the monsters may take it first.

## Using focus on purpose

Because focus is predictable, you can steer it:

- **Stand the tank closer, in movement.** If the Red Guard is the cheapest character to reach, the monster goes there, even if the Hatchet is right behind.
- **Use initiative for ties.** When two characters are equally close, the guide's tiebreak favors the one acting earlier. A tank who goes early pulls attention.
- **Block paths with obstacles and allies.** Monsters cannot move through characters, so a body in a corridor can force a long path to the person behind.
- **Use traps as walls.** Monsters avoid them, so a trap between you and a monster can redirect its whole route.
- **Pull ranged monsters forward.** A ranged monster that is already in range will not move. A ranged monster next to your tank will step away, sometimes into a worse spot.

## When it is not clear

The guide's own advice is good: have the monster act sensibly and do not get stuck on specifics (p. 22). When a question is truly open, the Rules Glossary has a monster behavior appendix full of illustrated examples, and the guide points to it (p. 30). If the group still disagrees, choose one reading, write it on the campaign sheet, and play it that way every time.
