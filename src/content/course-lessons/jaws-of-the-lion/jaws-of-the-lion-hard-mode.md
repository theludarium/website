Cooperative campaign games do not have championships, and Jaws of the Lion is no exception. There is no official tournament or organized play for it that we could find. So this course ends where the game's own challenge does: at the top of the difficulty dial. This lesson covers what Hard and Very Hard change, the habits that win there, and a practice plan.

## What changes on Hard and Very Hard

From Lesson 9, the Learn to Play Guide sets Hard at the normal scenario level plus 1, and Very Hard at plus 2 (p. 29). The monster level always matches the scenario level, so every monster uses a stronger stat line on its card. Traps hit harder. In return, money tokens can be worth more gold and the scenario bonus experience rises, by 2 for each level (p. 29).

What does not change: your cards, your hit points, and your hand size. Everything you gain on Hard comes from playing better, not from a bigger character.

## The habits that win at the top

**Predict every focus, every round.** On Normal, a surprise monster attack costs a few hit points. On Very Hard, it can exhaust a character. Before card selection, say who each monster will focus on, using the rules in Lesson 7.

**Kill or disable before they act.** Higher-level monsters hit harder, so damage you prevent is worth more than damage you heal. Use low initiative, Stun, Immobilize, Disarm, and Muddle to keep the biggest threat from acting at all.

**Treat the clock as the real health bar.** Plan the scenario in rounds from the start, as in Lesson 5. Know which round each character will rest, and agree on when the big lost actions go off.

**Spend lost cards to save cards.** A lost area attack that clears a room early saves every player several rounds. On Hard, that trade gets better, not worse.

**Rest on a schedule.** Long rest when no monster can reach you, and never let two characters long rest in the same round if monsters are near.

**Use the elements every round.** Infuse for an ally who acts later, and consume before the monsters can (p. 26).

**Build for the hard part.** Pick items and perks that make the worst rounds survivable, not the easy rounds faster. Removing negative modifier cards with perks makes every attack more reliable (p. 24).

## A six-session practice plan

1. **Session 1, Normal, with a focus log.** Each round, write who each monster will focus on, then check. Aim for zero misses.
2. **Session 2, Normal, with a clock.** Each player writes their maximum rounds and crosses them off. Compare your finish to your prediction.
3. **Session 3, Hard.** Expect it to be tight. After the scenario, name the round where the party lost the most cards and what would have saved them.
4. **Session 4, Hard, a new pair.** Swap characters if your group allows it, so each player learns another job. The guide lets you start a new character at level 1 (p. 30).
5. **Session 5, Hard, with initiative swings.** Plan at least two double turns (late, then early) for each character.
6. **Session 6, Very Hard.** Play your next new scenario at plus 2, or save Very Hard for a second campaign. Win or lose, you will know exactly which habit failed.

If the campaign runs out before you are done, the guide explains how to reset the box: decks go back in number order, and each character gets a new sheet (p. 30). The city map board is hard to reverse, which is where a removable sticker set helps (Lesson 9).

## What mastery looks like

You will know you have mastered Jaws of the Lion when a hard scenario feels like a puzzle you can read, not a fight you hope to survive. You will know who every monster will hit, how many rounds each character has left, and which turn the party will spend its best cards. That skill carries into every Haven game, and Lesson 11 has the map of where to take it next. If the group is newer, our article on [the quarterback problem in cooperative games](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/) helps keep a hard game fun for everyone at the table.
