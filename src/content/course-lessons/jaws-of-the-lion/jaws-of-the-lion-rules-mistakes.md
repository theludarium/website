Gloomhaven players argue about rules more than most groups, and Jaws of the Lion tables are no different. The good news is that the Learn to Play Guide answers most of the arguments in one sentence. This lesson collects the common slips, each checked against the guide. Page numbers refer to the guide.

A slip in either direction matters. Some make the game far harder, and some quietly make it easy.

## Card play

**Mistake: two top actions in one turn.** You use the top of one card and the bottom of the other, in either order. Never two tops or two bottoms (p. 7).

**Mistake: sharing numbers during card selection.** You can discuss plans, but you cannot show cards or give specific numbers or card titles (p. 6). "I am going early" is fine. "I am going at 14" is not.

**Mistake: forgetting basic actions.** Any card can be played as "Attack 2" on top or "Move 2" on the bottom, and is then discarded, even if the printed action would have been lost (p. 15). Basic actions never earn the card's experience (p. 20).

**Mistake: getting experience for kills.** Experience comes only from the experience icons on actions you perform, plus the bonus for completing a scenario. Killing a monster gives none by itself (p. 20).

## Monster behavior

**Mistake: focusing on the closest character.** A monster focuses on the character it can attack using the least movement, not the one physically nearest. Walls and obstacles change the count. If two characters tie on movement, the guide breaks the tie by initiative order (p. 11); full Gloomhaven checks which one is closer in range first. <!-- VERIFY: no official Rules Glossary PDF is online; confirm in the printed glossary whether proximity is checked before initiative. -->

**Mistake: thinking focus needs line of sight.** It does not. A monster focuses on any character it has a path to, even if it cannot reach them this turn (p. 11).

**Mistake: monsters walking through traps.** Monsters treat traps as obstacles when they pick a focus and move, unless a trap is the only way to their target (p. 17).

**Mistake: monsters moving when the card does not say Move.** A monster only performs the abilities written on its card (p. 17).

**Mistake: ranged monsters walking up to you.** A monster with a ranged attack moves only as far as it needs to get in range, and it steps away from an adjacent target to avoid Disadvantage if its movement allows (p. 18).

## Attacks and damage

**Mistake: ranged attacks on adjacent enemies at full strength.** A ranged attack against an adjacent enemy gains Disadvantage (p. 8).

**Mistake: shield applied too early.** Shield reduces damage after the attack modifier is applied, and it only works against attacks, not traps or other damage (p. 21).

**Mistake: losing a card to cut damage in half.** Losing one card from your hand, or two from your discard pile, negates the whole of one source of damage. It is all or nothing (p. 16).

**Mistake: forgetting the shuffle.** Only the 2x and null cards (and other cards with the shuffle symbol) trigger a shuffle, and the shuffle happens at the end of the round, not right away (p. 8).

## Resting

**Mistake: short resting with one card.** You need at least two cards in your discard pile to short rest, and you do it at the end of the round (p. 12).

**Mistake: long resting for free.** A long rest is your whole turn at initiative 99. You choose the card you lose, heal 2, and refresh spent items, but monsters still act before you get your cards back (p. 22). <!-- VERIFY: the guide is silent; confirm in the printed Rules Glossary whether a long rest needs at least two cards in the discard pile, as in original Gloomhaven. -->

## Loot, elements, and the end

**Mistake: forgetting end-of-turn loot.** At the end of each of your turns, you automatically loot your own hex (p. 15). Money left on the map when the scenario ends is gone.

**Mistake: infusing and using an element on the same turn.** An element enters the board at the end of the turn that made it. You cannot create and consume it in the same turn (p. 26).

**Mistake: stopping the moment the goal is met.** The scenario ends at the end of that round, so players still to act can grab last-minute loot (p. 18).

**Mistake: replaying a won scenario for loot.** The guide says you cannot replay scenarios you have completed (p. 13). A failed scenario is set up again, and you keep the money, experience, and treasure from the failed attempt (p. 16).

## When the guide does not answer

The guide's last page points to the Rules Glossary, which covers every rule and has a monster behavior appendix full of examples. The guide also gives a good attitude for unclear monster moves: have the monster act sensibly and do not get stuck on the details (p. 22). For the bigger debates, Lesson 7 walks through focus step by step.
