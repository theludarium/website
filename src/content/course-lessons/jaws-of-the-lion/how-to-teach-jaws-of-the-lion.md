Most heavy games ask the host to explain everything before the first turn. Jaws of the Lion does not. Its Learn to Play Guide is built to be read during play: it gives you the rules for Scenario 1, sends you off to play, and then adds new rules before Scenario 2, 3, 4, and 5. By the end of the fifth scenario, the guide says, you have the full system.

That changes the host's job. You do not teach Gloomhaven. You teach one round, and then you read.

## Before anyone arrives

Read the guide's Scenario 1 rules once, pages 3 to 12. Pull the four large character boxes and the Vermling Raider monsters. Set up the scenario book, the stat card in its envelope, and the monster modifier deck, as in [How to Play](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/how-to-play-jaws-of-the-lion/). Leave the character pieces in their boxes so each player can open their own.

Have the [teach sheet](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/teach-sheet/) printed. For general teaching habits, our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) applies here too.

## The pitch (30 seconds)

"This is a cooperative campaign. We are a mercenary crew, and we play one scenario a night, then save. There are no dice. Every turn comes from two cards you pick, and the monsters run themselves by a few simple rules. If all of us run out of cards or health, we lose the scenario and try it again."

## Pick characters (2 minutes)

Read the one-line descriptions from the guide's character panel: the Demolitionist for melee damage and breaking obstacles, the Hatchet for ranged damage and looting, the Red Guard for protection and moving monsters around, and the Voidwarden for healing and support. Let people choose on feel. Lesson 4 has a plan for each, and the guide lets anyone switch characters after Scenario 1.

With fewer than four players, leave characters out. The guide recommends one character per player when two to four people play.

## Teach the round (5 minutes)

Hold up two cards and say it in this order:

1. **"Pick two cards in secret."** Talk strategy, but no numbers and no card names.
2. **"One of them is your initiative card."** Point at the number in the middle. "Lower goes first. Tonight the monsters always go at 50."
3. **"On your turn, top of one card, bottom of the other."** Either order. Point at the line between halves.
4. **"Any card can be Attack 2 on top or Move 2 on the bottom."** Basic actions are introduced in Scenario 2, but a nervous new player relaxes when they know a safe option exists. If you want to stay with the guide's order, save this line for the second scenario.
5. **"Monsters chase whoever they can reach soonest."** That is enough for Scenario 1, where they only move and attack.

Then stop. Do not teach resting yet. The guide tells everyone to play three rounds and then explains the short rest right when hands run out. That timing makes the rule land.

## During the first scenario

Keep the guide open and read the boxed prompts aloud when you hit them. The guide marks every "do this now" step with an exclamation icon in the margin. When a question comes up that the guide has not reached yet, the answer is usually "the guide covers that in the next scenario."

Watch for three slips: someone using two top actions, someone forgetting that a ranged attack on an adjacent enemy has Disadvantage, and someone forgetting to shuffle a modifier deck after a 2x or null appears.

## What the tutorial adds, scenario by scenario

Knowing the plan helps you keep promises like "we will get to that":

- **Scenario 2:** money and treasure, traps, doors, basic and lost actions, looting, area attacks, push and pull, monster ability decks, and losing cards to avoid damage. Afterward, gold and buying items.
- **Scenario 3:** difficult terrain, experience, active bonuses, shield, jump, new conditions, and the long rest. Afterward, perks and city events.
- **Scenario 4:** full monster decks, spawning, the element board, battle goals, and objectives.
- **Scenario 5:** a boss, then leveling up and the scenario level.

## After the scenario

Read the conclusion and rewards together, then let each player name their character on the sheet. That small ritual turns a rules lesson into the start of a campaign. If someone cannot make the next session, the article on [a campaign game with a missed session](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/) has options, and the guide itself says the game adapts to two, three, or four characters.
