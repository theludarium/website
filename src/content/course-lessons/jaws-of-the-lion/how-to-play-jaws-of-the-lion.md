Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion is a cooperative campaign game from Cephalofair Games for 1 to 4 players. You play mercenaries fighting through a story of 25 scenarios, and you save the game between them. There are no dice. Every action comes from cards you choose, and the monsters follow a set of rules instead of a game master.

This lesson covers the core rules as the Learn to Play Guide teaches them in the first scenario. It is spoiler-free: nothing here goes past the starting characters and the tutorial.

## What is in the box, and what you need tonight

Jaws of the Lion comes with two books that matter on night one: the **Learn to Play Guide** and the **Scenario Book**. The guide walks you through the first five scenarios step by step, and the scenario book is the map. You open it to the scenario and play on its pages. There is also a **Rules Glossary**, the full reference, which the guide suggests you save for later questions.

The guide tells you which pieces to take out and when.

## Setup for the first scenario

![An abstract first scenario setup: the scenario book open as a hex map, monster pieces and tokens around it, and each player's mat, dial, and cards in front of them.](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/setup.svg)

Each player picks one of the four characters: the Demolitionist, Hatchet, Red Guard, or Voidwarden. Take that character's figure, mat, initiative order token, a hit point dial, the small attack modifier deck, and the six level "A" ability cards the guide names, plus the reference card.

Set the red side of the dial to the number under the white "1" on your mat. That is your hit point total at level 1. Put your initiative token in the middle of the table.

Open the scenario book to Scenario 1 in the middle of the table. Place your figure on any starting hex, one figure per hex. Monster icons on the map have three rows: the top row is for two characters, the middle for three, the bottom for four. A white icon means a normal monster, gold means an elite, and black means none at your count. Put the monster stat card in its envelope with level 1 showing, and shuffle the 20-card monster attack modifier deck next to it.

## The round in four steps

**1. Card selection.** Everyone secretly picks two cards from their hand. You can talk about plans ("I will deal with the one on the left"), but you cannot show cards or name numbers or card titles. Pick one of the two as your initiative card.

**2. Ordering of initiative.** Flip your cards. The number in the middle of your initiative card is your speed, and lower goes first. Line up the initiative tokens from low to high. In Scenario 1 the monsters always act at 50; after that, they draw an ability card each round. A character wins a tie with a monster.

**3. Turns.** On your turn you use the top action of one card and the bottom action of the other, in whichever order you like. Never two tops or two bottoms. Any card can instead be a basic "Attack 2" on top or "Move 2" on the bottom.

**4. End of round.** If an attack modifier deck drew a card with the shuffle symbol (the 2x or the null), shuffle its discards back in. Then start again.

## Attacks and damage

To attack, flip one card from your attack modifier deck for each target and add it to your attack value. A 2x doubles the attack, and the null deals no damage. Melee attacks hit adjacent enemies; ranged attacks list a range. A ranged attack against an adjacent enemy has Disadvantage: draw two modifiers and use the worse. Advantage draws two and uses the better. Only walls block line of sight.

When a monster's damage tokens reach its hit points, it is killed.

## How monsters act

Monsters act on their initiative, elites first, then normals, each in standee-number order. Each one first picks a **focus**: the character it can attack using the least movement. Then it moves toward that character and attacks if it can. Lesson 7 covers focus in depth, because most monster questions come down to it.

## Resting and exhaustion

Played cards go to your discard pile. When you need them back, you rest. A **short rest** happens at the end of a round: shuffle your discards, lose one at random, and take the rest back. (Once per rest, you can suffer 1 damage to lose a different random card instead.) From Scenario 3 you can also **long rest**, which uses your whole turn at initiative 99 but lets you choose the card you lose and heal 2.

If your hit points reach 0, or you cannot play two cards at the start of a round, you are **exhausted** and out of the scenario. If every character is exhausted, the scenario is lost. Characters fully refresh after every scenario, win or lose.

## Winning a scenario

Each scenario has a goal, such as killing all enemies. The scenario ends at the end of the round when the goal is met. Read the conclusion, take the rewards, and you are one step further into the campaign.
