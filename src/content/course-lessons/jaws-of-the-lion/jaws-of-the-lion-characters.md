Jaws of the Lion comes with four characters, and the campaign is built around them. That makes the choice feel big, but the guide lets anyone switch after Scenario 1 (p. 13), and the game plays well with any combination.

This lesson gives each character's job, the facts that shape how it plays, and a plan for the first few scenarios. Card names come from the Learn to Play Guide and Cephalofair's own character descriptions. Nothing here goes beyond what the box shows you at the start.

## The Demolitionist: hit hard, break things

**Job:** melee damage and obstacle destruction. The guide's character panel says exactly that.

**What shapes it:** the smallest hand of the four, 9 cards, according to Cephalofair. That means fewer rounds per scenario than anyone else, but its cards hit hard, and Cephalofair calls its attack modifier deck one of the best in any Haven game. Some abilities destroy obstacles and reward it for doing so, which opens new paths for the whole party.

**First-games plan:** be at the front, and make every round count. Because the hand is small, avoid rounds where you only move. Pair a movement bottom with a top that attacks. Save the big lost actions for rooms where they kill several monsters or finish a dangerous one. The guide's suggested first pair is One-Two Punch and Knock Out the Support (p. 6).

## The Hatchet: ranged damage and loot

**Job:** ranged damage and looting, per the guide's panel.

**What shapes it:** a core card called The Favorite. Cephalofair describes the loop: you throw your favorite hatchet for big attack bonuses, then need to get it back before you can use it again. Your first level 1 cards are The Favorite and Retrieval, which tells you how central that loop is (p. 19).

**First-games plan:** stay out of melee. Every ranged attack you make against an adjacent enemy has Disadvantage, so position first, then throw. Let the Red Guard or Demolitionist hold the front. Because you are often away from the fighting, you are also well placed to loot money tokens, which become gold at the end of the scenario. The guide suggests Center Mass and Double Throw for the first round (p. 6).

## The Red Guard: the wall

**Job:** protection and monster manipulation.

**What shapes it:** Cephalofair calls it the tankiest of the four, and the guide's example shows 10 hit points at level 1 (p. 4). Cephalofair describes chains and hooks that pull enemies where the Red Guard wants them, damage that triggers when enemies come close or attack, and heavy use of the fire and light elements.

**First-games plan:** stand where monsters will focus on you instead of your fragile allies. Lesson 7 shows how to steer focus with your position and initiative. Pull enemies into bad spots, use shield and retaliate-style effects to punish attackers, and create fire and light for your own later turns. The guide's suggested first pair is Blinding Sickle and Shocking Advance (p. 6).

## The Voidwarden: the support

**Job:** healing and support.

**What shapes it:** abilities that grant actions to other figures. The guide explains that a Voidwarden can give an action to an ally or an enemy: the granted figure performs it on the Voidwarden's turn, using its own attack modifier deck (p. 10). Cephalofair names Master Influence as the core card, which strengthens those effects, and calls the Voidwarden by far the best support class in the box. Keep it behind the front line.

**First-games plan:** your best turn often makes someone else's turn better. Give an extra attack to the character who can finish a monster, heal before damage piles up, and use the big hand to outlast everyone else. When you make a monster attack another monster, you are dealing damage without risking your own cards. The guide suggests Suggestion and Wicked Scratch for the first round (p. 6).

## Which characters at which player count?

With four players, everyone plays one character. With fewer, any mix works, but a few combinations are easy to run well:

- **Two players:** Red Guard with Hatchet gives you a front line and a back line. Demolitionist with Voidwarden gives you a damage engine with a healer behind it.
- **Three players:** leave out whichever role the group cares least about. Without a Red Guard, the Demolitionist takes the front.

Solo players run two characters at once. Lesson 10 has a plan for that.

## A note on hand size

Hand size sets how long you last. Your hand limit is printed in the top right corner of your character mat (p. 24). With only short rests and no lost cards, a 9-card hand gives at most 20 rounds and an 11-card hand gives at most 30. Lesson 5 shows that math, and why the Demolitionist's 9 cards make every lost action a bigger decision.
