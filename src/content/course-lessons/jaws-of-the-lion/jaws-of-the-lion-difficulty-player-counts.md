Jaws of the Lion plays 1 to 4, and every scenario scales to your group in two ways: how many monsters appear, and how strong they are. The first comes from the map. The second is the scenario level, and you control it. This lesson covers both, with page references to the Learn to Play Guide.

## Player count: what the map changes

Every monster icon in the scenario book has three rows: two characters, three characters, and four characters (p. 4). A white icon means a normal monster, gold an elite, and black means no monster at that count. So the map itself adds and upgrades monsters as the party grows.

- **Four players:** the guide's default. Everyone runs one character, and every role is covered.
- **Two or three players:** the guide recommends one character per player (p. 3). Fewer characters face fewer monsters, but you also miss a role. Lesson 4 suggests pairs that cover each other.
- **Solo:** you control two characters, which the guide calls a more complex way to play (p. 3). Lesson 10 covers it.

The guide also says you can add or drop players between scenarios, and the scenario level adjusts to the average character level (p. 30). Someone who misses a night does not break the campaign. For the general idea of why some games feel different at each count, see our article on [board game player counts](/articles/board-game-player-counts/).

## Scenario level: the difficulty dial

The scenario level sets the monster level and several rewards (p. 29). The guide's formula for **Normal** is the average level of the characters, divided by two, rounded up. Four level 2 characters give an average of 2, divided by 2 is 1, so scenario level 1.

Then pick a difficulty:

| Difficulty | Scenario level |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Normal minus 1 |
| Normal | Average level divided by 2, rounded up |
| Hard | Normal plus 1 |
| Very Hard | Normal plus 2 |

The monster level always equals the scenario level. The scenario level also raises trap damage, the gold each money token is worth, and the bonus experience for completing the scenario (p. 29). At level 0, traps deal 2 and the bonus is 4 experience; at level 1, traps deal 3 and the bonus is 6. Harder play pays more experience and, from level 2, more gold.

The guide's own advice for a new group: stay on scenario level 1 until at least one character reaches level 3, then move to 2 (p. 29).

## When a scenario beats you

First, check the rules. The guide says so directly: if you are having real trouble, make sure you are not overlooking a rule (p. 16). Lesson 3 has the usual suspects, and monster focus (Lesson 7) is the big one.

If you are playing correctly and still losing, the guide allows you to start the scenario with all monster stat cards turned to level 0 (p. 16). You keep the gold, experience, and treasure from the failed attempt either way.

## Which difficulty for which group?

- **New to the hobby or to cooperative games:** Normal, and drop to Easy without guilt if a scenario stalls the campaign. Finishing the story matters more than a perfect record.
- **Experienced gamers new to Gloomhaven:** Normal for the tutorial, then try Hard once everyone has played a few scenarios.
- **Veterans of Gloomhaven or Frosthaven:** many start on Hard. Very Hard is a real challenge best saved for groups that know their characters well. Lesson 12 is a plan for it.

## What to buy, and what you do not need

Everything in this course uses the base box: [Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B088QSDB7S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). You need nothing else to play the whole campaign. The stickers on the city map board are permanent, so groups who want to replay the campaign or pass the box on often buy the [Jaws of the Lion Removable Sticker Set and Map](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08VJLC2LX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). It is optional. The guide also says you can skip the stickers and track scenarios another way (p. 3).
