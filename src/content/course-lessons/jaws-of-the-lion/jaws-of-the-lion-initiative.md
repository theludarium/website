Every Jaws of the Lion card has a number in the middle. Pick it as your initiative card, and that number decides when you act this round. Lower goes first. That sounds like "faster is better," but it is not. Initiative is a choice about timing, and good players swing between low and high on purpose.

## How initiative works

After everyone picks two cards, each player chooses one as their initiative card and places it on top. Cards are revealed, the monster sets reveal their ability cards, and everyone acts from lowest to highest (p. 6 and p. 17).

Ties, per the guide (p. 7):

- **Character and monster tie:** the character goes first.
- **Two characters tie:** compare their second cards. Lower goes first.

From Scenario 2 on, each monster set flips one ability card per round, and that card's number is the initiative for every monster of that set (p. 17). Monsters in a newly opened room act this round: if their initiative is lower than the character who opened the door, they act right after that character's turn (p. 17).

A long rest always acts at 99 (p. 22).

## Before the monsters: when going first wins

Pick a low number when acting first changes what the monsters can do:

- **You can kill it before it acts.** A dead monster never attacks. This is the strongest reason to go first.
- **You can get out of reach.** Step out of a melee monster's path, or behind a wall from a ranged one.
- **You can disable it.** Stun stops all its abilities, Immobilize stops movement, Disarm stops attacks, and Muddle gives its attacks Disadvantage (p. 9 and p. 16). A condition applied before the monster acts lasts through its turn.
- **You need to open a door on your terms.** Going early lets the whole party react to the new room in the same round.

## After the monsters: when going last wins

Pick a high number when you want the monsters to come to you:

- **Let them walk into range.** A melee character with a short move can wait for the monster to step adjacent, then hit it without spending movement.
- **Let them use up their attack on the tank.** If the Red Guard goes before the monsters and stands in front, monsters spend their turn on the Red Guard, and the rest of the party acts after with a clear picture.
- **Clean up.** Late initiative lets you see what the round did and finish whatever is left.

Monster ability decks have their own initiative spread. After a few scenarios against the same monster type, you learn roughly when it acts, which makes the "before or after" choice much easier.

## The double turn

Because the order resets every round, you can act very late one round and very early the next. That gives you two turns back to back with no monster turn in between. Every character can do it with the right pair: look for one card with a very high number and one with a very low number.

Use the double turn to set up and finish: move into position and attack late, then attack again and step away early before the monsters respond.

## Initiative and the team

Initiative order is also how your team combines its abilities.

- **Elements.** A figure infuses an element at the end of its turn, and it can be consumed on later turns while it is Strong or Waning (p. 26). If the Red Guard infuses fire at initiative 20, an ally at 40 can consume it the same round. Elements wane at the end of each round, so the order matters.
- **Granted actions.** A Voidwarden who grants an ally an attack does it on the Voidwarden's turn (p. 10). Grant it before the monsters act, and the target may die before it can hit anyone.
- **Pushes and pulls.** Moving a monster away or into a better spot before an ally's turn can set up that ally's area attack.

## Talking about initiative without breaking the rules

During card selection you can share plans, but no specific numbers or card names (p. 6). Use words that mean something: "early," "before the monsters," "late," "after the boss," "I am resting." That is enough to plan a round, and it keeps one player from running everyone's cards.

If someone is frozen by the choice each round, the article on [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) has gentle ways to keep the table moving. A simple house habit: decide "early or late" first, and only then look for the cards that fit.
