Jaws of the Lion is a campaign, not a stack of one-off games. Each scenario changes the city map, your characters, and which scenarios come next. This lesson explains how that structure works, using only what the Learn to Play Guide shows in its first five scenarios. **No spoilers:** it names no later scenarios, enemies, or story events.

## The shape of the campaign

Cephalofair lists 25 scenarios, five of them the tutorial. The campaign branches: your decisions shape which scenarios open, and some rewards close other scenarios for good. The guide says plainly that you will not play every scenario in one campaign (p. 30).

Two rules hold the structure together:

- **You cannot replay a completed scenario** (p. 13).
- **A failed scenario is set up again** from the start. You keep the money, experience, and treasure you earned in the failed attempt (p. 16).

## The city map board

The city map board tracks which scenarios are open. When rewards list a "New Location," you put that scenario's sticker on the matching square, and you mark a scenario's box when you complete it (p. 3 and p. 13). The stickers are permanent. If you would rather not mark the board, the guide says to track scenarios another way (p. 3).

## Gold and items

During a scenario, money tokens are looted from the map. Each monster drops one when it dies. Money left on the map at the end is lost (p. 14). After the scenario, each token converts to gold: 2 gold each at scenario levels 0 and 1, rising at higher levels (p. 18 and p. 29). Gold belongs to one character and cannot be shared or traded (p. 18 and p. 19).

Between scenarios you can buy items from the shop, sell them back for half their value rounded up, and trade items between characters (p. 19). Some rules to remember:

- One copy of each item per character.
- Bring at most one head, one body, one feet, two hand, and a number of small items equal to half your level, rounded up (p. 19 and p. 30).
- Items are consumed (once per scenario), spent (once per long rest), or usable whenever the situation allows. All refresh after the scenario (p. 19).

**A spending plan many groups use:** buy items that save cards first, such as ones that let you avoid a lost card or recover cards, then items that make attacks more reliable. Cards are your clock, as Lesson 5 shows.

## Experience and levels

Experience comes from the icons on the actions you use, plus a bonus for completing each scenario, which is 6 at scenario level 1 (p. 20, p. 23, and p. 29). Level 2 takes 45 experience, and the sheet shows the rest of the thresholds up to level 9 (p. 13 and p. 29).

When you level up (p. 29):

1. Add one new ability card of your new level or lower to your pool.
2. Gain one perk.
3. Raise your maximum hit points to the number under your new level on your mat.

Your pool grows past your hand limit, so before each scenario you choose which cards to bring, up to your hand limit (p. 27). You can look at the next scenario's information before you choose. At level 5, the guide tells you to open the other pack of ability cards in your box (p. 30).

## Perks and battle goals

**Perks** change your attack modifier deck: remove bad cards, add good ones, or add cards with effects. Each choice is permanent (p. 24). You earn perks by leveling up, through battle goals, and from some scenario rewards (p. 24).

**Battle goals** are secret side goals. At the start of each scenario, each player gets two and keeps one. If the scenario succeeds and you met your goal, you gain the check mark shown on the card. Every three check marks earn a perk, up to 18 check marks in all. A failed scenario gives nothing for battle goals (p. 25).

Battle goals reward a little selfishness. Chase them when they are cheap, but never at the cost of the scenario: no check marks come from a loss.

## City events

After every successful scenario, you draw a city event, read it, and choose one of two options as a group. Some options require a certain character. Each event is used once and then set aside for good (p. 24).

## Saving between sessions

At the end of a session, each player bags their character sheet, mat, modifier deck, current cards, and tokens and puts them in the character's box (p. 13). With the city map board updated, the next session starts in minutes. If a player misses a night, the guide says you can add or remove characters, and the scenario level adjusts to the group (p. 30). Our article on [a campaign game with a missed session](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/) has more ways to keep a group going.
