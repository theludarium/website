Dinosaur Island plays 1 to 4. The core rules stay the same at every count, but a handful of setup numbers change, and they change how crowded the shared phases feel. This lesson covers those differences, the plot twists that make each game feel new, and the two official variants.

## What changes with player count

Setup scales with the number of players:

| Players | Dice in the pool | Objectives revealed | Scientists placed | Market tiles removed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 5 | 3 | 6 | "3+" and "4+" |
| 3 | 7 | 4 | 9 | "4+" |
| 4 | 9 | 5 | 12 | none |

Some things do not scale:

- **Six cold storage slots** at the research center, at every count.
- **Three face-up recipes**, one per stack.
- **Four market rows** with one attraction, lab upgrade, and specialist each.
- **4 copies** each of Dino Research II and DNA Refinement II.
- **80 visitors** in the bag.

Solo uses its own setup, covered in [the solo lesson](/academy/dinosaur-island/dinosaur-island-solo-mode/).

## Two players: room to plan

With 6 scientists for 5 dice, 6 storage slots, and 3 recipes, there is usually space for everyone. The research center is less of a fight, and you can plan two rounds ahead with more confidence. The market turns over the same way, but each player sees more of it, with only two players taking two actions each.

With fewer scientists competing for the dice, it is more likely that some die goes unclaimed and lands in the holding area. Watch your security.

The game ends when two of three objectives are claimed, so a two player game can end quickly if both players chase the same short objectives.

## Three players: the middle

Three players place 9 scientists across 7 dice, 6 storage slots, and 3 recipes. Most rounds, someone misses the die they wanted. Turn order, set by score, starts to matter a great deal, because picking first at the research center is a real advantage.

## Four players: a crowded center

Four players place 12 scientists across 9 dice, 6 storage slots, and 3 recipes, so the research center fills fast. Late picks get what is left. With 12 scientists for 9 dice, it is more common for every die to be claimed, which leaves nothing in the holding area.

The market also moves faster. With eight market actions a round, good items rarely slide far. Buy what you need when you see it.

At four, reading rivals, from [the reading opponents lesson](/academy/dinosaur-island/dinosaur-island-reading-opponents/), is worth the most. The bag still has 80 visitors, and if all four players reach 20 excitement, they draw the bag dry.

## Plot twists: two rules that change everything

At setup, shuffle the 11 plot twist cards and reveal 2. Their rules apply all game. If the second contradicts the first, discard it and draw again.

A few examples from the rulebook:

- Each player starts with 5 workers instead of 4.
- Each player may hire 1 additional specialist during the game.
- Each player may raise cold storage limits by 1 for one DNA type at the end of each round.
- Once per round, each player may raise excitement by 1 when assigning a scientist to increase cold storage.
- The bag starts with no hooligans; add 5 at the end of round 1 and 5 more at the end of round 2. This is the recommended first game twist.

Read the plot twists before you plan your opening. A fifth starting worker makes an aggressive first round possible. A fourth specialist slot makes specialists stronger all game. The cold storage twists make big dice claims and DNA objectives easier.

## Variant: Predictable Systems

Play without plot twist cards. The rulebook offers this to keep the most long-term strategies viable. It is a good choice for a group learning how the core engine works, and for comparing your scores across games.

## Variant: Chaos Threat Theory

In the Park Phase, reroll the die in the holding area and use the new result instead of the original. Normally you know the holding die before the Worker Phase and can buy exactly the security you need. With Chaos Threat Theory, you cannot. You must choose a margin: base threat plus 3 is always safe; less is a gamble. [The visitor odds lesson](/academy/dinosaur-island/dinosaur-island-visitor-odds/) covers what each point of shortfall costs.

## Choosing a setup for your group

- **Learning:** short objectives, the recommended first game, no extra plot twist.
- **Regular nights:** medium objectives, 2 random plot twists.
- **Planners:** long objectives, Predictable Systems.
- **Gamblers:** Chaos Threat Theory with any objectives.

Our piece on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) has ideas for keeping a regular table together long enough to try every setup.
