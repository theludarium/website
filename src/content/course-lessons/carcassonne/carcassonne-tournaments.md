Carcassonne looks like a family game, and it is one. It is also a serious competitive game, with a world championship, national qualifiers, a long-running event at the World Boardgaming Championships, and a busy online scene. You do not need to win any of them to enjoy them. But knowing they exist changes how you look at the game at home.

## How tournament Carcassonne differs from home play

Most competitive play shares a few features:

- **Base game only.** No expansions, and usually no River or Abbot.
- **Two players** at the world championship level, where the game is most strategic and luck evens out over many rounds.
- **A clock.** At the world championship, each player has 15 minutes for the whole game on a chess clock. Run out and you lose.
- **Open information.** Under the world championship rules, you may choose and secretly look at your next tile during your opponent's turn, but it must stay above the tabletop, and you show it to your opponent before you place it. Your supply of meeples stays visible at all times.

Farmers are scored the same way as in your home rules: for each field, the player with the most farmers scores 3 points per completed city touching it.

## The Carcassonne World Championship

The world championship final is held each autumn at the Spielezentrum in Herne, Germany. Its [official site](https://carcassonne-meisterschaft.de/en/tournament.htm) shows Hans im Glück among its partners. The 2025 final, the 19th, drew 52 players from 45 countries and was won by Xiangyu Qin of China. The 2026 final, the 20th, was held on Saturday, October 24, 2026, at the same venue.

The format, from the [tournament rules](https://carcassonne-meisterschaft.de/en/tournamentrules.htm):

- Six Swiss rounds of two-player games. Each round pairs you with someone on a similar record.
- The top eight advance to quarterfinals, then semifinals, a final, and a game for third place.
- Ties in the standings are broken by the strength of the opponents you faced.

Spots in the final go to the reigning world champion, the national champion of each participating country, and the winners of several recognized online events.

## National championships

Each participating country sends its national champion. The world championship's [country list](https://carcassonne-meisterschaft.de/en/countries.htm) shows which countries take part each year.

- **United States:** the US National Carcassonne Championship has run on Board Game Arena since 2021, and its winner represents the United States at the world championship. Details are posted by its [Board Game Arena group](https://en.boardgamearena.com/group?id=8728315).
- **United Kingdom:** the [UK Carcassonne Championship](https://www.ukgamesexpo.co.uk/events/3670-carcassonne-uk-championship/) runs at UK Games Expo, and its winner qualifies for the world championship.

If your country is not on the list, the championship site also has a registration route for players from countries without a national championship.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC) is an annual tournament convention in Pennsylvania where many games each crown a champion. Carcassonne is one of them, and it was on the [2026 winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

The WBC Carcassonne event has used a heats format. In 2025 there were three heats, and heat winners moved on to a quarterfinal, semifinal, and final. Rounds ran one hour, the base tiles only, and multiplayer tables. The event was marked as welcoming to beginning players, which makes it one of the friendliest ways to try a real tournament. See the [2025 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc25/Previews/car.html) for the full rules.

For 2026 the event changed shape. The [2026 event preview](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc26/Previews/car.html) lists two-player games only, single elimination over six rounds, a semifinal, and a final, still with one-hour rounds and still marked as welcoming to beginners. That makes it much closer to the world championship format.

## Online play

[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=carcassonne) has Carcassonne in your browser, with ranked play against people around the world. It is also where the US national championship is played. Online play is the fastest way to get many two-player games in, and two-player games are where tournament skill grows.

## Finding local play

You do not need a tournament to find good opponents.

- **Your local game store.** Many run open game nights where Carcassonne is a regular. Ask the staff.
- **BoardGameGeek.** Its forums and local guilds list game groups near you.
- **Your library or community center.** Many host board game meetups, and Carcassonne is a common choice because it teaches fast.
- **Start your own.** Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## How to practice like a competitor

- **Play two-player games, base tiles only.** That is what tournaments play.
- **Play on a clock** sometimes. Fifteen minutes each forces you to decide fast and trust your reads.
- **Review your losses.** Where did your meeples get stuck? Which field did you lose, and when could you have defended it?

That is the map. The next two lessons get you ready to walk into one of these events: first the official rules and the clock, then a practice plan to get your two-player game where it needs to be.
