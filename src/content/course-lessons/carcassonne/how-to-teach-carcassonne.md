Carcassonne is one of the easiest games to teach badly. The rules are short, so hosts read them all out, farmers and final scoring included, before anyone has touched a tile. The table's eyes glaze over by the second coat of arms.

The fix is to teach the turn, play the turn, and let the game do the rest. Here is a script in the order you would say it, with the moments to stop talking. Print the [Carcassonne teach sheet](/academy/carcassonne/teach-sheet/) and set it where everyone can see it.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up first, so the teach starts with the game ready. Start tile in the middle. Face-down stacks around it. Seven meeples in front of each seat, the 8th on 0 on the scoreboard. River tiles and abbots back in the box.

Decide now whether to teach farmers. The rulebook suggests adding them after two or three games. With new players or kids, follow that. With a table of experienced gamers, teach them, but teach them last.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "We are building a medieval map together, one tile at a time. You score by putting your people on roads, cities, and monasteries, and finishing them. Most points at the end wins."

Hold up a meeple. "These are your people. You have seven. That number matters more than it looks."

## Minutes 2 to 4: one turn, out loud

Draw a tile and do a real turn while you narrate.

1. "I draw one tile. I place it so every edge matches. Road to road, city to city, grass to grass."
2. "Now I may put one meeple on the tile I just placed. On a road it is a traveler, on a city a knight, on a monastery a monk."
3. "If my tile finished something, it scores now."

Then name the one rule that shapes everything: "You can't put a meeple on a road or city that someone already claimed. Even if their meeple is three tiles away."

Stop here. Ask: "Questions on the turn?"

## Minutes 5 to 7: what things are worth

Point at the teach sheet.

- "A finished road: 1 point per tile."
- "A finished city: 2 per tile, plus 2 for each of these shields." Point at a coat of arms.
- "A monastery: finished when all 8 spaces around it are filled. That's 9 points."
- "When something scores, your meeple comes home. That's how you get your people back."

Then the meeple warning, the one tip that helps new players most: "If all seven of your people are stuck on things that never finish, you can't play meeples anymore. Finish small things early."

## Minutes 8 to 9: sharing and the end

Save sharing for a quick sentence: "Two separate cities can join into one. Then whoever has more knights in it scores. Ties both score the full points."

Then the end: "When the last tile goes down, unfinished stuff still scores, but less. Roads and monasteries the same, cities half."

If you are teaching farmers, now is the time, and keep it to four sentences:

- "Lay a meeple down on the grass. That's a farmer."
- "Farmers never come back. They score only at the very end."
- "At the end, a field scores 3 points for every finished city it touches."
- "Most farmers on a field takes it, ties all score."

## Minute 10: start, and teach the rest live

Say: "That's it. The youngest goes first." Then play.

The rest you teach when it happens:

- **The tile that fits nowhere.** It goes back in the box, draw again. Explain it the first time it comes up, which may be never.
- **Placing a meeple on the tile that finishes a feature.** When someone does it, point out that they scored and got the meeple back in one turn.
- **A city merge.** When two cities first join, walk the table through who has the most knights.

## What to leave out

- **Final scoring details** until the last few tiles. Remind everyone then.
- **Strategy.** Let people find their own first.
- **The river and the abbot.** Both are good, and both are for game three or later.

## Coaching during the first game

Help with the puzzle, not the decisions. "That tile fits there, or there" is helpful. "You should take that city" is playing for them.

Watch for players with no meeples left. One gentle question helps more than a lecture: "What could you finish this turn?"
