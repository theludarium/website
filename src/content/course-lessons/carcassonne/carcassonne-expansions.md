Carcassonne has more expansions than almost any game on the shelf. That is a gift and a trap. Add too much at once and the clean three-step turn disappears under special pieces. Add one thing at a time and the game can grow with your group for years.

This lesson starts with what changes at different player counts, then the two small expansions already in the base box, then the expansion boxes worth knowing.

## How does the game change by player count?

The base game plays 2 to 5.

- **Two players:** the most strategic count. You get many turns, so long plans work, and every point you take is a point your one opponent does not. Blocking and joining matter most here.
- **Three or four:** the classic family game. A good mix of planning and surprise.
- **Five:** the most chaotic. You take fewer turns each, the board changes a lot between them, and fast, small features gain value.

If your group likes a calmer, puzzle-like game, two or three players suits it. If it likes table talk and swings, go to four or five.

## The River

The River is the first mini-expansion, and it is in the current base box: 12 tiles with dark backs that replace the start tile.

- Set aside the source tile and the lake tile. Shuffle the other river tiles face down, put the lake at the bottom, and place the source as the start tile.
- Players draw river tiles first, and each must continue the river.
- The river cannot loop back on itself, so two bends in a row cannot turn the same direction.
- Meeples follow the normal rules, but none can go on the river itself.
- Once the lake is placed, play continues with normal land tiles.

The River does not change scoring. It spreads the opening out, so the first few turns feel less crowded around one start tile.

## The Abbot

The Abbot is the second mini-expansion, also in the base box. Each player takes the abbot in their color. Some land tiles show gardens, which matter only with this expansion.

- When you place a tile with a monastery or a garden, you may place your abbot there instead of a normal meeple. The abbot can go only on a monastery or a garden, and normal meeples cannot go on gardens.
- Gardens score like monasteries: 9 when surrounded.
- The abbot's special ability: on a turn when you do not place a meeple, you may take your abbot back and score its monastery or garden right away, 1 point for it plus 1 per surrounding tile.
- At final scoring, the abbot scores like a monk.

The Abbot adds a small, satisfying decision every few turns, without new rules for anything else. It is a good first step once the base game feels easy.

## The first expansion boxes

The usual starting points are the first two numbered expansions. Both need the base game.

**[Inns & Cathedrals](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-inns-cathedrals/)** adds 18 land tiles with inns on roads and cathedrals in cities, a large meeple worth two normal ones, and pieces for a sixth player. It raises the stakes on big roads and cities and makes merges more tense. It is the natural first expansion. <!-- AFFILIATE: Carcassonne: Inns & Cathedrals -->

**[Traders & Builders](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-traders-builders/)** adds 24 land tiles, goods tokens, builders, and pigs. A builder can give you an extra tile placement on a road or city it shares, and a pig makes your farmers' field worth more. It suits groups who like more planning per turn. <!-- AFFILIATE: Carcassonne: Traders & Builders -->

**[The Princess & The Dragon](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-the-princess-the-dragon/)** adds 30 land tiles, a dragon that eats meeples, a fairy that protects them, a princess that can remove a knight from a city, and magic portals. It is the most chaotic of the early expansions, and a good fit for groups who enjoy [take-that games](/articles/take-that-games/). <!-- AFFILIATE: Carcassonne: The Princess & The Dragon -->

## The Big Box

If you know you will want several expansions, the **[Carcassonne Big Box 2022](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-big-box-2022/)** collects the base game and 11 expansions, including Inns & Cathedrals, Traders & Builders, the River, the Abbot, and smaller ones such as The Flying Machines and The Ferries. It plays 2 to 6. Do not add them all at once; it works best as a library you draw from. <!-- AFFILIATE: Carcassonne Big Box 2022 -->

## Spin-offs and family versions

Several standalone games use the Carcassonne idea with their own twist, including **[Hunters & Gatherers](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-hunters-gatherers/)**, **[Amazonas](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-amazonas/)**, **[Safari](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-safari/)**, and **[Gold Rush](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne-gold-rush/)**. Each plays on its own; none needs the base game. <!-- AFFILIATE: Carcassonne spin-offs (Hunters & Gatherers, Amazonas, Safari, Gold Rush) -->

For younger kids, **[My First Carcassonne](https://www.zmangames.com/game/my-first-carcassonne/)** is for ages 4 and up and drops point counting: players race to place all their pieces instead. <!-- AFFILIATE: My First Carcassonne -->

## How to choose

- **Your group loves the base game as is:** add the River, then the Abbot, then stop and enjoy it.
- **You want more of the same with higher stakes:** Inns & Cathedrals.
- **You want more depth:** Traders & Builders.
- **You want laughter and chaos:** The Princess & The Dragon.
