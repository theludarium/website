Your first three games of Carcassonne are where habits form. Some are rules mistakes, quietly played wrong because nobody checked. Others are legal moves that cost a lot of points. This lesson covers both, checked against the current rulebook and supplemental rulesheet.

## Rules slips to catch early

**Placing a meeple on an older tile.** You may place a meeple only on the tile you just placed. Not the tile next to it, not one you placed last turn. If you missed the chance, it is gone.

**Joining a claimed feature directly.** If a road or city has a meeple anywhere on it, you cannot add one to it, even on a distant tile. You can only share a feature by starting a separate one and connecting them later.

**Moving meeples.** Meeples stay put until their feature scores. There is no picking one up because you changed your mind.

**Scoring only your own features.** When your tile completes a feature, everyone with meeples on it scores, not just you. The player who places the tile does not need a meeple there.

**Splitting points on a tie.** Tied players each score the full points. Nobody halves anything.

**Forgetting the half value of cities.** At final scoring, an unfinished city scores 1 per tile and 1 per coat of arms, not 2. Roads and monasteries score the same as when complete.

**Counting unfinished cities for farmers.** A field scores 3 points only for **completed** cities it touches. An open city next to a field adds nothing to it.

**Missing a field boundary.** Roads and cities split fields. Two patches of green on either side of a road are usually two separate fields, each with its own farmer count. Trace the field with a finger before you place a farmer.

**Arguing over take-backs.** The rulebook does not cover undoing a placement. Tournaments treat a tile as placed once you let go of it. At home, agree on your table's rule before the game so nobody feels singled out.

## The big strategic mistake: running out of meeples

New players treat meeples like they are free. They put one on every tile. By the midpoint, all seven sit on half-built cities and long roads, and every new tile is a turn with no choice.

The fix has three parts:

- **Prefer features that can finish soon.** A two-tile city with one open edge comes back fast. A sprawling city with five open edges may never close.
- **Keep two in reserve.** Having a meeple ready when a great tile appears is worth more than claiming a mediocre road.
- **Use the free-meeple trick.** If your tile completes a feature nobody owns, place a meeple on it. It scores and returns the same turn.

## Monasteries in bad spots

A monastery needs all 8 neighboring spaces filled. Placed on the edge of the map late in the game, it may sit at 4 or 5 points with your monk stuck on it. Placed early, near where the map is growing, other players fill it in for you.

## Farmers too early, or too many

Farmers never come back. A first-time farmer placed on turn three leaves you with six meeples for the rest of the game, and a second leaves five. Most beginners do best holding farmers until the second half, then placing them on fields touching several cities that are complete or nearly so.

The opposite mistake shows up at the very end: meeples still in your supply when the last tile goes down. They score nothing. In the final few turns, a farmer on even a small field is points you would not otherwise get.

## Building cities that cannot close

Some open city edges need a shape that is rare, or that may already be used up. A city with three awkward openings can wait all game. When a city starts looking like a puzzle with missing pieces, stop adding to it and spend meeples elsewhere.

## Being too polite about sharing

New tables often feel that joining someone's city is rude. It is not. It is the main way players interact. If an opponent's big city has an open side next to empty space, starting your own small city there and connecting it later is a normal, friendly play.

The defense is just as normal: finish your city before someone joins, or add a second knight through your own merge.

## A quick checklist for game three

Before you place each meeple, ask:

1. Is this feature already claimed anywhere?
2. Can it realistically finish?
3. How many meeples will I have left?
