Carcassonne is a game about building a map together and arguing, politely, over who gets credit for it. Each turn you add one square tile to a growing landscape of roads, cities, monasteries, and fields. Then you decide whether to put one of your wooden followers, the meeples, on what you just placed. Finish a feature with your meeple on it and you score.

This lesson covers the rules in the current Z-Man Games edition. The rulebook splits them in two: the core rules, then a supplemental sheet for farmers and two small extras. We follow the same order.

## What is in the box?

You get a start tile with a dark back, a large set of land tiles with normal backs, a scoreboard, and meeples in five colors, 8 per color. There are also 12 river tiles (dark backs) and 5 abbots. Those are the two mini-expansions included in the box. Leave both out for now; [lesson 7](/academy/carcassonne/carcassonne-expansions/) covers them.

## How do you set up?

![The start tile in the middle of the table, face-down tile stacks around it, the scoreboard with every score meeple on 0, and each player's 7 meeples](/academy/carcassonne/setup.svg)

1. Put the start tile face up in the middle of the table.
2. Shuffle the land tiles and make several face-down stacks, so everyone can reach at least one.
3. Each player picks a color and takes 7 meeples as their supply.
4. Put your 8th meeple on the 0 space of the scoreboard. It tracks your score.
5. The youngest player goes first. Play passes clockwise.

## What do you do on your turn?

Three steps, always in this order.

**1. Place a land tile.** Draw exactly one tile and place it face up next to a tile already on the table. Every edge must continue the landscape: road meets road, city meets city, field meets field. In the rare case your tile fits nowhere, return it to the box and draw another.

**2. Place a meeple (optional).** You may put one meeple from your supply on the tile you just placed. Where you put it decides its job:

- On a road, it is a **traveler**.
- On a city, it is a **knight**.
- On a monastery, it is a **monk**.

The one big limit: you cannot place a meeple on a feature that already has a meeple anywhere on it, even far away on another tile.

**3. Score points.** If your tile completed a road, city, or monastery, score it now. That includes features with other players' meeples on them. After scoring, the meeples on that feature go back to their owners' supplies.

## When is a feature complete, and what is it worth?

| Feature | Complete when | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road | Both ends are closed, by a village, a city, a monastery, or a loop | 1 per tile |
| City | Fully surrounded by walls, with no gaps inside | 2 per tile, plus 2 per coat of arms |
| Monastery | All 8 surrounding spaces have tiles | 9 (itself plus the 8 around it) |

Move your score meeple one space per point.

Because you place a meeple before you score, you can put a meeple on a tile that finishes a feature, score it, and take the meeple straight back.

## What if two players share a feature?

You cannot join a feature that is already claimed. But two separate features can grow into one. If you start your own city next door and a later tile connects the two, both knights are now in the same city.

When a shared feature scores, the player with the most meeples on it scores the full points. If players tie for most, every tied player scores the full points. Nobody splits anything.

## How do farmers work?

Once your group has a game or two behind it, add farmers from the supplemental rules. The green land on the tiles is fields. Roads, cities, and other features divide fields from each other.

- Place a meeple **lying down** on a field. It is now a **farmer**.
- The same claim rule applies: no farmer on a field that already has one.
- Farmers score only at the end of the game. They never come back to your supply.
- At the end, each field is worth **3 points for every completed city it touches**.
- The player with the most farmers on a field scores it. Ties all score in full.

One city can touch several fields, so it can pay out to several farmers.

## How does the game end?

The game ends when the last land tile has been placed. Then everyone scores the meeples still on the board:

- **Road:** 1 point per tile, same as during the game.
- **City:** 1 point per tile and 1 per coat of arms, half its completed value.
- **Monastery:** 1 point, plus 1 per surrounding tile.
- **Field:** 3 points per completed city it touches.

Most points wins. A tie means shared victory.

That is the whole game. If someone at your table asks "what's a meeple?", our short history of [the meeple](/articles/what-is-a-meeple/) has the answer.
