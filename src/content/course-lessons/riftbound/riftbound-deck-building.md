Upgrading a preconstructed deck taught you what a few good cards can do. Building from scratch is the next step: every choice is yours, from the Legend down to the last rune. It sounds like a lot, but it follows a clear order, and the deck building rules from the [upgrades lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-deck-upgrades/) keep you inside the lines.

## Step 1: choose a Legend

Your Champion Legend makes two decisions for you. Its **domains** set your deck's domain identity: every card and rune must fit inside them. Its **champion tag** decides which champion units can be your Chosen Champion and which Signature cards you can play.

Read the Legend's ability closely. It is the one card you will have every game, because it starts in play and can't be killed. A Legend that rewards playing lots of units points to one kind of deck; one that rewards spells or holding points to another.

## Step 2: write the plan in one sentence

Before you pick a single card, finish this sentence: "This deck wins battlefields by..." Some answers:

- "...getting there first with cheap units and defending what it takes."
- "...holding one battlefield with tough units and trading efficiently until the late game."
- "...surprising the opponent with Action and Reaction cards in showdowns."

Every card that does not serve the sentence is a candidate to cut.

## Step 3: the champion and the core

Pick your Chosen Champion, the champion unit with your Legend's tag. It starts the game face up in its own zone, so you can count on access to it. You can run up to 3 copies of it in total, including the one in the Champion zone.

Next, pick the cards that make your plan work and play 3 of each. Consistency wins more games than variety. A single copy turns up in your first six cards only about one game in six.

Add up to 3 Signature cards for your champion if they help the plan. That limit is 3 in total, not 3 of each.

## Step 4: build the curve

Lay the cards out in columns by energy cost: 0 to 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 or more. This shape is your **curve**, and it should match your rune count. Going first, you have 2 runes on turn 1, 4 on turn 2, and 6 on turn 3.

Three checks:

- **Can you do something on turns 1 and 2?** Units enter exhausted, so a unit played on turn 1 can move on turn 2, and one played on turn 2 waits until turn 3. A deck with nothing cheap gives up the first battlefields.
- **Is the top end small?** Expensive cards win late games, but a hand full of them loses early ones. Keep the 6 and up column short.
- **Do the power costs fit?** Count the power symbols in each domain. That count will set your rune split.

A good way to check your shape is to lay out a preconstructed deck the same way. Riot built those decks to play smoothly. If your columns look very different, have a reason.

## Step 5: count your units

Units are the only cards that take and hold battlefields. Spells and gear help them do it. Count your units, and ask whether you can still contest both battlefields if your opponent removes two of them. For sealed decks, Riot's own Pre-Rift advice is at least 15 units. Constructed decks vary more, but a deck light on units needs a clear reason.

## Step 6: runes and battlefields

**Runes.** Exactly 12, all from your Legend's domains. Split them in proportion to your power symbols, using the odds in the [runes lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-runes-energy-power/).

**Battlefields.** Three, each with a different name. In a single two-player game, one is chosen at random, so all three should suit your plan. In a best-of-three match, you choose, and you cannot repeat a battlefield after a game someone won. A deck that wants to hold does well with battlefields that reward holding. A fast deck wants ones that reward conquering.

## Step 7: test and trim

Your Main Deck needs at least 40 cards, and exactly 40 at tournaments. Stay at 40. Every card above it makes your best cards a little less likely to show up.

Then play. Ten games tell you more than an hour of theory. Keep the tally habit from the upgrades lesson: note the cards you were sorry to draw and the ones you always wanted. After ten games, cut the worst two and try new ones.
