Welcome to Level 1. You can play a full game and teach one. Now the goal is to play cleanly, because a clean game is the only kind you can learn from. The slips below come straight from the rules, and many of them are common enough that Riot's Tournament Rules give them their own entries: forgetting to score, forgetting to draw, channeling the wrong number of runes, and forgetting to ready your cards.

None of them mean you are bad at the game. They mean you are new to it.

## Rules slips

**Forgetting to score a battlefield you hold.** Holding is the quiet half of scoring. It happens in your Beginning phase, before you channel, and it is easy to skip when you are excited about the cards in your hand. Say it out loud every turn: ready, score, channel, draw.

**Thinking you can score the same battlefield twice in a turn.** You score each battlefield at most once per turn, by holding or by conquering. If you held a battlefield this turn, lose it in a fight, and take it back, the retake scores nothing.

**Leaving a battlefield empty.** You keep control of a battlefield only while you have a unit there. Move your last unit home, or lose it in a fight, and control is gone. No unit, no hold next turn.

**Forgetting that units arrive tired.** Units enter exhausted, so a unit played this turn can't move this turn unless a card says otherwise, such as one with Accelerate. You can still play a unit straight to a battlefield you control, where it helps defend.

**Expecting energy to carry over.** Unspent energy and power empty out at the end of the turn. Plan your spending before you exhaust a single rune.

**Mixing up recycle and exhaust.** Exhausting a rune makes 1 energy, and the rune stays on the table. Recycling it makes 1 power and sends it to the bottom of your Rune Deck. Recycle when a power symbol asks for it, not by habit.

**Playing ordinary spells at the wrong time.** During a showdown, only cards with Action or Reaction can be played. On an opponent's turn outside a showdown, only Reaction cards can answer something they put on the Chain. A plain spell waits for your own Main phase.

**Forgetting the second player's extra rune.** The player going second channels 3 runes on their first turn, not 2. Only on that first turn.

**Missing the final point rule.** If a conquer would give you your eighth point, it counts only if you have scored every battlefield that turn. Otherwise you draw a card. Holding has no such limit.

## Habits that cost games

**Spreading thin.** Two units at two battlefields often lose both fights. One battlefield you hold every turn is worth more than two you trade back and forth.

**Attacking without counting.** Before you move, add up the Might on both sides. If your opponent has Action or Reaction cards and runes left, assume they can add a little more.

**Spending every rune on power early.** Each rune you recycle in the first few turns is one fewer rune on the table for several turns after. The runes lesson in Level 2 does the math.

**Keeping a hand with nothing to play.** Your opening four should have something to do on your first two turns, usually a cheap unit. Use the mulligan: set aside up to 2 cards and draw that many.

**Playing your best unit into an open fight.** A big unit sent alone to a battlefield where your opponent has runes up can be beaten by a single combat trick. Sometimes the right play is to wait a turn and arrive with company.

## A five-game plan

Treat your first five games as practice with one focus each:

1. **The turn.** Say the four start-of-turn steps aloud every turn.
2. **Scoring.** Track the score on paper and announce each point, the way the Tournament Rules ask players to.
3. **Runes.** Before each Main phase, say how much energy and power you have and what you will spend it on.
4. **Fights.** Count both sides' Might before every move to an occupied battlefield.
5. **The finish.** From 6 points on, plan how you will score the last two, with the final point rule in mind.

Play the same deck for all five. Changing decks hides which mistakes are yours and which are the deck's.

## When a rule question stops the game

Look it up together. Riot's [rules hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/) keeps the Core Rules, and the golden rule settles most arguments: when a card and the rules disagree, the card wins. If you are still stuck, pick the reading that seems fair, play on, and check after. Our guide to [settling a board game rules argument](/articles/board-game-rules-argument/) has more ways to keep the peace.

And if a slip costs you a game, laugh about it. Our guide on [how to be a good loser](/articles/how-to-be-a-good-loser/) is worth a read before your first store night.
