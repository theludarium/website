A preconstructed deck is a good deck. It is also built to teach, so it carries a few cards that are there for variety rather than strength. Upgrading is the art of swapping those out, a few at a time, for cards that fit your plan. Done with care, it costs little and teaches you more about the game than any guide.

## The deck building rules, in one place

Before you buy anything, know the limits. From the Core Rules and the Tournament Rules:

- **1 Champion Legend.** Its domains set your deck's domain identity. Every card and every rune must fit inside it.
- **Main Deck: at least 40 cards,** including your Chosen Champion, which must share your Legend's champion tag. At tournaments, the Main Deck must be **exactly 40**.
- **Up to 3 copies** of any card with the same name. That count includes your Chosen Champion.
- **Up to 3 Signature cards in total,** and they must match your Legend's champion tag.
- **Rune Deck: exactly 12 runes,** all from your Legend's domains.
- **Battlefields:** 3 for two-player play, no two with the same name.
- **Sideboard:** at events that allow one, up to 10 cards, and the 3-copy limit covers deck and sideboard together.

Riot also keeps a banned list for constructed play, posted on the [rules hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/). Check it before you buy a single for store play.

## Find what to cut first

Do not start with what to add. Start with what is not working. Play the deck a handful of times and watch for three kinds of card:

- **Cards you are always sorry to draw.** Too expensive for what they do, or only good in rare situations.
- **Cards that do nothing for your plan.** If your Legend rewards lots of units, a slow spell that only answers one threat may not belong.
- **Units that die to everything.** Low Might for their cost means they rarely hold a battlefield.

Your first cuts are usually the most expensive cards with the least effect, and one-of cards that do something different from everything else in the deck.

## What makes a good replacement?

- **More copies of your best cards.** A preconstructed deck often runs one or two copies of its strongest cards. Getting to three of each makes the deck more consistent, and consistency wins more games than flash.
- **Cheap units.** Units enter exhausted, so a unit played early is a unit that can move sooner. Decks that can put two or three cheap units on the table early contest battlefields first.
- **A way to break a defense.** At least a few cards that remove or weaken a blocking unit, so a stubborn defender cannot hold a battlefield forever.

## Buy singles, not packs

Booster packs are fun, and that is a fine reason to open some. But a pack gives you random cards, while your deck needs specific ones. Buying the exact singles you want, from your local store's case or from other players, almost always improves a deck for less money.

Three habits keep it cheap:

- **Upgrade two or three cards at a time.** Then play again before buying more.
- **Ask the store first.** Many stores sell singles and know which common and uncommon cards are strong but cheap.
- **Trade.** Store nights are full of players with spare copies of what you need. Our guide to [trading and selling games](/articles/trading-and-selling-games/) covers fair trades and condition.

## Tune your runes

Your 12 runes are part of the deck too. The quick start's decks use an even split of 6 and 6 between their two domains, which is a good default. Once you have upgraded, count the power symbols in your Main Deck. If most of them belong to one domain, shift a rune or two toward it.

Remember that every rune you recycle for power is a rune you won't exhaust for energy next turn. A deck with heavy power costs needs its cheap cards to carry more of the load.

## Choose your three battlefields

Your battlefields are the quiet part of deck building. In a single two-player game, one of your three is picked at random. In a best-of-three match, you choose one for each game and can't repeat a battlefield after a game someone won. Either way, all three should help your plan, or at least not help your opponent's. A battlefield that rewards attacking suits an aggressive deck. One that rewards holding suits a patient one.

## When to build your own deck

Once you have upgraded a deck a few times, you will have opinions: which champion you enjoy, which domains feel right, which cards you wish you had more of. That is the moment to build a deck from scratch. Until then, upgrading teaches you the same lessons with less risk.
