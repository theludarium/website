You know the rules, the strategy, the odds, and the tournament procedures. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: a registered deck, best-of-three matches, a 60-minute clock, and a long weekend. This lesson gives you a six-week plan to get there, built from everything in the course.

The plan assumes three to five hours a week. Scale it to your life, but keep all four parts.

## Choose your event first

A plan needs a date. The [competitive ladder lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-tournaments/) lists the first 2027 premier events: Regionals in Madrid and Philadelphia in February, and a Continental in Dallas in March. Riot says Regionals qualify players for Continentals, and that each Continental opens with a day one qualifier for players without an invite.

If a premier event is too far to travel, a closer competitive event works just as well as a target. The plan is the same.

## The four parts of every week

**1. Volume with one deck.** Play the deck you will register, and only that deck. Store nights count. So does play with friends. Aim for at least eight to ten games a week.

**2. Matchups.** Pick the three decks you expect most, using your log from the [metagame lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-metagame/). Find a practice partner willing to play one of them, and play several matches in a row against it. Swap sides sometimes: playing the other deck teaches you what it fears.

**3. The clock.** At least once a week, play full best-of-three matches with a 60-minute timer, under the [Tournament Rules](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-tournament-rules/): sleeved decks, written score, sideboarding between games, and the end-of-round procedure if time runs out. The goal is to finish every match with time to spare.

**4. Review.** After every loss, write short answers to three questions:

1. Which battlefield decided the game, and when could I have changed it?
2. Did I lose a fight I could have counted in advance?
3. Did my opening hand or my mulligan decision set me back?

Patterns show up fast. A mistake you see three times is the easiest one to fix.

## The six weeks

**Weeks 1 and 2: settle the deck.** Play volume and keep a tally of cards you were sorry to draw and cards you always wanted. By the end of week 2, lock your 40, your runes, and your three battlefields. Use the [odds lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-odds-mulligan/) to check your early units and your key cards.

**Weeks 3 and 4: matchups and sideboard.** Test against your three expected decks. Build your 10-card sideboard and write the three-line plan from the [best-of-three lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-best-of-three/) for each: battlefield order, cards in and out, and turn order.

**Week 5: event conditions.** Play every practice match under event rules and the clock. Register your list on paper exactly as you will hand it in, and check it against the deck rules: 40 cards, 12 runes, 3 battlefields with different names, up to 10 in the sideboard, no card past 3 copies across deck and sideboard, and nothing on the banned list.

**Week 6: sharpen and rest.** Fewer games, more review. Reread your sideboard notes until they feel automatic. Ease off in the last few days and arrive rested.

## What to measure

Keep four numbers from week to week:

- **Match record and game record,** separately.
- **Record in each of your three key matchups.**
- **Time left on the clock** at the end of practice matches.
- **Errors caught in review:** forgotten scores, miscounted fights, wasted runes.

If your errors are falling and your matches finish on time, the plan is working, whatever your record.

## The weekend itself

A three-day event is long. A few habits help:

- **Pack the night before:** sleeved deck and spares, a printed copy of your list, dice or counters, a pen and paper for score and notes, water, and snacks. Our [convention packing list](/articles/gen-con-packing-list/) has more ideas for a long gaming weekend.
- **Arrive early** for every round. Lateness can cost a game at competitive events.
- **Eat and drink between rounds,** even when the round runs long.
- **Reset after a loss.** Your next opponent does not know about your last match. Neither should your play.

## The table across from you

Tournament practice can make a game feel like work. Keep one thing in view: every match is a game against another person who loves Riftbound too. Learn names. Talk through the interesting games afterward. The players you meet on the way to a Regional are a big part of why it is worth going. And when you get home, bring what you learned back to your local store, where the next new player is opening their first box.

That is the end of the course. You can choose a first box, play and teach the game, fight and score on purpose, manage runes, build and draft a deck, read a showdown, and walk into a competitive event knowing its rules. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
