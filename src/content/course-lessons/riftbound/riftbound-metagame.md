A **metagame** is the mix of decks people are playing at a given time and place. Every competitive card game has one, and it changes with every new set, banned list update, and big event result. Your local metagame is the one that matters most: the decks you will actually sit across from.

## Know what is legal

Before choosing anything, check the format. The Tournament Rules define **Standard** as the current year's sets plus the previous year's. As of the October 9, 2026 update, Standard includes:

- the Origins supplemental set in Proving Grounds,
- Origins, Spiritforged, Unleashed, Vendetta,
- and Radiance, released October 23, 2026.

Riot also keeps a banned list on the [rules hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/), and Riot has said older sets will start rotating out of Standard no earlier than 2028. <!-- VERIFY: Riot's own post on the first Standard rotation and which sets leave. --> Legacy releases January 29, 2027, and new sets change the field quickly.

## Learn your local field

Big-event decklists tell you what strong players are building, but your store has its own habits. Some players love one champion. Some stores lean toward fast decks, others toward slow ones. Find out what yours plays.

- **Keep a log.** After each match, write down your opponent's Legend, the deck's general plan, and the result. Our [competitive lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-tournaments/) suggests the same habit for practice.
- **Ask.** Players at store nights love to talk about their decks. Ask what people are testing for the next event.
- **Watch the top tables.** After your match, spend a minute watching the players at the top of the standings.
- **Follow big events.** Riot's [organized play news](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/) covers premier events, and results from them shape what stores play a few weeks later.

## Sort the field into plans

You do not need to know every list. Sort what you see into broad plans, the same way the [deck building lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-deck-building/) asked you to describe your own:

- **Fast decks** that take battlefields early with cheap units and try to win the race before the opponent sets up.
- **Defensive decks** that hold one battlefield with tough units and outlast the opponent.
- **Showdown decks** that win fights with Action and Reaction cards and hidden cards.

Most decks are a blend, but almost every deck leans one way. Once you know the blend in your room, you can ask how your deck does against each part of it.

## Choosing a deck

Three questions, in this order:

1. **What do I play well?** A deck you have played fifty times beats a stronger deck you picked up last week. Skill with your own deck is worth a lot.
2. **How does it do against the field?** If your local metagame is mostly fast decks and your deck folds to speed, that is a problem no amount of practice fixes.
3. **Can I tune it?** Often the answer is to keep your deck and adjust: a few cheaper units, a little more removal, a better battlefield.

Switching decks is right when your deck has a bad matchup against a large share of the field and no tuning fixes it. Otherwise, tune.

## Tuning for a field

- **Main deck:** if one plan is everywhere, it is worth main-deck cards that do well against it, since sideboard cards can't help in game 1.
- **Sideboard:** cover the decks that are common but not dominant, using the plan from the [best-of-three lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-best-of-three/).
- **Battlefields:** choose three that suit your plan and hurt the most common opposing plan, if you can find them.

## Expect the field to move

When a deck starts winning, other players adjust to beat it, and then a third deck rises to beat those. That cycle is normal. So treat your log as a living record. What beat you last month may be gone next month, and a deck that seemed too slow may be well placed once the fast decks are hunting each other.

## A note on community

Learning the metagame is also how you learn the people. The players you trade notes with at a store night are the ones who will practice your sideboard plan with you before a Regional. Share what you learn freely. A store with a strong local scene is better for everyone in it.
