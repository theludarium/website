Card games feel random, but the randomness has a shape. Your Main Deck is 40 cards, and your Chosen Champion starts the game in its own zone, so you shuffle and draw from the other 39. Every number in this lesson is worked out from that fact with standard card-drawing math. You do not need to do the math at the table. You need a feel for a handful of results.

## How many cards will you have seen?

You draw 4 to start, then 1 in every Draw phase, including your first turn in a two-player game. So, counting the opening hand:

- **By your first turn:** 5 cards seen.
- **By your second turn:** 6.
- **By your third turn:** 7.
- **By your fifth turn:** 9.

That holds whether you go first or second.

## The chance of finding one card

Say you run 3 copies of a key card. The chance you have at least one by each point:

| Cards seen | 1 copy | 3 copies | 6 copies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 (opening hand) | 10% | 28% | 50% |
| 5 (first turn) | 13% | 35% | 59% |
| 7 (third turn) | 18% | 46% | 72% |
| 9 (fifth turn) | 23% | 56% | 82% |

"6 copies" means two different cards, 3 of each, that do the same job. That column is the real lesson: if your plan depends on one effect, run two cards that provide it. A single 3-of shows up in your opening hand barely more than one game in four.

## Your Chosen Champion is different

Your Chosen Champion starts face up in its zone, and you can play it from there. So you have one copy every game, without drawing it. Extra copies in your Main Deck are backups for when the first one dies. With 3 in total, 2 are in the deck, and you find at least one of those in your first 7 cards about a third of the time.

## How many cheap units?

Units enter exhausted, so units played on your first two turns decide who reaches the battlefields first. Here are the odds of having at least one 2 energy unit, depending on how many you run:

| 2 energy units in deck | In opening 4 | By your second turn (6 seen) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 50% | 66% |
| 8 | 62% | 77% |
| 10 | 71% | 85% |
| 12 | 79% | 91% |

If an early unit matters to your plan, 8 is a floor, and 10 to 12 is comfortable. Cards that cost less also count, if they do the same job.

## What a mulligan really does

The mulligan lets you set aside up to 2 cards, draw that many, and then put the set-aside cards on the bottom of your deck.

Two things follow.

**It is a second look, not a new hand.** If your opening 4 has no copy of a 3-of you want, and you send back 2 cards, your chance of finding one in the 2 new cards is about 17%. Over the whole process, the chance you see that card rises from 28% to about 40%. Useful, but not magic.

**The bottom is a long way off.** With 1 draw a turn and around 35 cards above them, the cards you send back will almost never come back in a normal game. Mulligan away the cards you would least like to draw, not just the ones that do not fit your first turn.

A good habit: keep hands that can act on turns 1 and 2, and send back expensive cards first. A hand with two 2 energy units and two 5 energy cards often keeps the units and sends back one or both of the 5s.

## Why 40 cards beats 45

Your Main Deck must be at least 40, and exactly 40 at tournaments. In casual play you could run more, but every extra card dilutes your best ones. A 3-of in a 40-card deck is in your first 7 cards about 46% of the time. Add 5 more cards, and that drops to about 41%. Stay at 40.

## Rune odds

Your runes have odds too. With a 6 and 6 split, you see at least one rune of each domain in your first 4 runes about 94% of the time, and at least 2 of one particular domain about 73% of the time. The [runes lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-runes-energy-power/) has the full table and what changes with an uneven split.

## A shortcut for the table

When you need a quick estimate during deck building: multiply the copies in your deck by the cards you will have seen, then divide by 39. That is the average number of copies you will have drawn. When the result is well below 1, your chance of having at least one is a bit less than that number. Three copies by your third turn: 3 times 7 divided by 39 is about 0.54, and the true chance is 46%.
