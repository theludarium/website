The Pre-Rift lesson showed you how to build from a sealed pool. Draft adds a step before that: you choose your pool one card at a time, passing the rest to your neighbors. Riot calls Legacy, releasing January 29, 2027, the first Riftbound set designed with drafting as a priority, so draft is likely to grow at stores. This lesson covers the rules and the habits that make good drafters.

## How a Riftbound draft runs

From the Tournament Rules:

- **Three packs each,** with groups as close to eight players as possible, seated randomly.
- Open a pack, take out the rune or token, and check that 13 cards remain. You may keep the rune or token.
- **Pick one card,** put it facedown in front of you, and pass the rest **left**. Keep picking from each pack you receive until it runs out.
- **Pack two passes right.** Pack three passes left again.
- You finish with **39 cards**, your pool.

At casual events you can usually look through your picks between choices. At competitive events you may only look at them during breaks the judge calls, and you can't talk about the draft while it is running.

## The draft deck rules

Draft decks follow the limited rules, which differ from constructed:

- **At least 20 cards** in your Main Deck.
- **Domain identity:** any three domains, or any one domain plus your Legend's domains.
- **Any champion unit** in your domains can be your Chosen Champion, even one that does not match your Legend.
- **No copy limits.** Play as many of a card as you drafted.
- **No Legend or champion?** That is legal. You draw an extra card at the start of your first Beginning phase.
- **Runes from anywhere,** matching your domains, and blank battlefields if you need them.

## What to take early

Early picks should be strong and flexible. A rough order for the first picks of pack one:

1. **Removal that kills units,** especially units holding a battlefield.
2. **Efficient units** with good Might for their cost, since units take and hold battlefields.
3. **Cards that win showdowns,** like cheap tricks that add Might.
4. **Narrow or expensive cards** last, unless they are very strong.

A card with one domain is more flexible than a card with two, since it fits more decks. Early on, flexibility is worth a little power.

## Reading signals

In pack one, you receive cards from the player on your right. If strong cards of one domain keep arriving at your fifth or sixth pick, your right-hand neighbor is probably not taking that domain. That is a signal: the domain is open, and pack three will come from the same direction.

Pack two comes from your left, so its signals tell you about the other neighbor. Use it to confirm your choices, not to start over.

Riot's Legacy plans include **signpost cards** at lower rarities, which point to the set's strategies. Seeing several signposts for one domain pairing late is a strong hint that pairing is open. Our [card drafting explainer](/articles/card-drafting-explained/) covers reading signals in more detail, and our article on [signpost cards in cube](/articles/cube-signpost-cards/) explains the design idea behind them.

## Two domains or three?

The rules allow three domains, which is generous. But runes are the limit. With 12 runes split 4, 4, and 4, you have only about an 86% chance of seeing at least one rune of a given domain in your first 4. With 6 and 6 it is 97%. Power costs in a third domain will sometimes be stuck in your hand.

A good default is two main domains with a light third for a few strong cards that cost little power, especially removal. If your Legend's domains match your best cards, the "one domain plus your Legend's" option can give you exactly that.

## Building from 39

Use the same order as the [Pre-Rift lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-pre-rift-guide/): sort by domain, count units, check your Legend, add tricks and removal, then build runes. Two differences:

- **You have fewer cards.** A draft deck at or near 20 cards draws its best cards more often. Do not add weak cards just to reach a number.
- **You know your pool's story.** You chose each card, so you already know the plan. Build the deck that the middle of your draft pointed to, not the one your first pick hoped for.

## Between games

At casual events you can usually swap cards between your deck and the rest of your pool between games. If an opponent's deck was full of big units, bring in more removal. If you lost to speed, lower your curve.
