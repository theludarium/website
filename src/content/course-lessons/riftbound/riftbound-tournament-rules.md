Welcome to Level 4. You can play the game well. Now you prepare to play it under tournament conditions. Riot's Tournament Rules, last updated October 9, 2026, govern every organized play event, and they get stricter as the stakes rise. This lesson covers the parts a player needs; the full document on the [rules hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/) covers the rest.

## Event levels set the strictness

Every event has an Organized Play Level:

- **Casual:** most store events. Players are expected to know most of the rules. Judges focus on education and sportsmanship, with no priority on technically precise play.
- **Competitive:** events with significant rewards, like invitations. Players are expected to know the Core Rules and the Tournament Rules, and penalties are stricter.
- **Professional:** the top events. Players are expected to know both documents completely.

Casual and Competitive are what you will meet first.

## Your deck and your decklist

At Competitive and Professional events you must register your deck before the first round. The list must record, completely and unambiguously:

- your Champion Legend,
- your three battlefields, each with a different name,
- your Main Deck of **exactly 40** cards, including your Chosen Champion,
- your **12** runes,
- and your sideboard of **up to 10** cards, if the event allows one.

Once the list is handed in, it can't change. A list with an illegal element, such as 39 cards, a card outside your Legend's domains, or 4 copies of a card counting the sideboard, is a **Game Loss**. So is a deck that does not match its list. Check twice. Riot's banned list applies too.

## Sleeves, shuffling, and presenting

- **Sleeves are required** at Competitive and Professional events for your Main Deck, Rune Deck, and battlefields. All sleeves in a deck must match, with opaque backs. At those levels, avoid highly reflective backs and holographic patterns.
- **Shuffle until random.** Pile shuffling can be used to count your deck, but not as your only shuffle.
- **Present your decks.** After shuffling, you hand your Main Deck and Rune Deck to your opponent, who may shuffle them again.

## Notes, score, and resources

- **Notes are allowed.** Your note sheet must be empty and visible at the start of each match. You may take notes during the match and use them in later games of the same match, but not notes from earlier matches during games.
- **Prepared notes between games.** Between games you may look at notes you made before the match, through sideboarding and battlefield reveal, until opening hands are drawn.
- **Devices stay away.** You can't use phones or other electronic devices during a match.
- **Track score in writing.** At high levels, score must be written legibly. Announce every change, and have your opponent acknowledge it. Dice are fine at casual events.
- **Show floating resources.** If you hold energy or power across actions, show it with dice or counters.

## The clock

The Tournament Rules recommend 60 minutes for Swiss rounds. When time runs out, the player whose turn it is finishes it, then three more turns are played. If the game is still going, a player with a lead of **two or more points** wins it. Otherwise the game is a draw. If you are between games when time is called, no new game starts.

Pace matters. Slow play is a Warning, and stalling on purpose is cheating. Practice playing a full best of three in well under an hour.

**Be on time.** Arriving up to 2 minutes late is a Warning with a time extension. Between 2 and 10 minutes late is a Game Loss at Competitive events and above. After 10 minutes, it is a Match Loss.

## Take-backs and judge calls

At Competitive events you can still change your mind about your most recent action, but only right away and before either player has taken another action. You can always ready runes you just exhausted, or take back runes you just recycled, if nothing has happened since. Changing your mind after your opponent reveals information is not allowed, and trying to fish for reactions that way is angle shooting.

If something goes wrong, raise your hand and call a judge. It is not an accusation. Judges assume errors are honest, and a judge call over 1 minute earns a time extension. You can appeal a floor judge's ruling to the head judge, whose decision is final.

## Penalties, in brief

There are four: **Warning, Game Loss, Match Loss,** and **Disqualification.** A few common errors carry no penalty at all:

- **Missed trigger:** no penalty in most cases. Your opponent may choose whether it happens now if it was missed this turn.
- **Forgetting to score a point:** no penalty, and the score is fixed if no more than a full round of turns has passed.
- **Forgetting to draw:** no penalty in most cases, and the draw is made up on the same terms.

Repeat errors add up: three game play Warnings in the same category in one event become a Game Loss, and so do six game play Warnings of any kind at Competitive events.

## Tiebreakers

Players with the same match record are ranked by opponents' match win percentage, then game win percentage, then opponents' game win percentage. Every match you win, and every game within it, counts.

None of this needs to be memorized before your first store event. Read it once, bring a correct list and a sleeved deck, and call a judge whenever you are unsure.
