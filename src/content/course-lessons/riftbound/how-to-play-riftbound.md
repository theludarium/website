Riftbound is a trading card game from Riot Games set in the world of League of Legends. Each player teams up with one champion, plays units to the table, and sends them to fight over shared battlefields. Take a battlefield and you score a point. Keep it and you score again on your next turn. The first player to 8 points wins.

This is an unofficial course, written in our own words from the official rules. It starts where most people start: looking at a shelf of boxes and wondering which one is the right first buy.

## What does a deck need?

The official rules ask for four things, and they are exact about each:

- **1 Champion Legend.** It sits in its own zone all game. Its domains decide which cards your deck can use.
- **A Main Deck of at least 40 cards.** Units, spells, and gear. One of them is your **Chosen Champion**, a champion unit that matches your Legend and starts the game face up beside it. You can run up to 3 copies of any card.
- **A Rune Deck of exactly 12 runes.** Runes are your resource. They live in their own deck, not in the 40.
- **Battlefields.** The places you fight over. In a two-player game, each player brings 3 and uses 1.

You do not need to build any of that yourself to start. A preconstructed deck comes with all of it, ready to shuffle.

## Which box should you buy first?

**[Proving Grounds](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/get-started/)** is the box Riot made for learning. It holds four preconstructed decks built around Annie, Master Yi, Lux, and Garen, plus a rulebook, playmats, and tokens, and it plays 2 to 4 people. One box gets a whole kitchen table playing, and the four decks feel different enough that everyone can find a favorite. <!-- AFFILIATE: Riftbound Proving Grounds -->

**[Proving Grounds, 2nd Edition](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/announcements/products-and-sets-into-2027)** is due February 19, 2027, after the Legacy set. Riot lists it at $40, with four new decks built around Heimerdinger, Karma, Vladimir, and Illaoi, four oversized battlefields, four paper playmats, four score trackers, and a rulebook. Riot also says it holds no cards you cannot get from booster packs. <!-- AFFILIATE: Riftbound Proving Grounds, 2nd Edition -->

So which one? If you want to play this month, buy the first edition. The rules are the same game either way. If you are shopping for a gift in early 2027, the 2nd Edition is the newer box. This course teaches the rules as they stand today.

Both boxes work for store play too. The first edition's cards are legal in Standard, the main tournament format, and Riot has said older sets will start rotating out of Standard no earlier than 2028. <!-- VERIFY: Riot's own post on the first Standard rotation; the rules hub's Tournament Rules do not give a date. -->

## What if you only need one deck?

If a friend already plays, you only need a deck of your own. Many sets bring **Champion Decks**: single preconstructed decks built around one champion, ready to play. Legacy, due January 29, 2027, brings four of them, for Tryndamere, Ashe, Lux, and Garen. <!-- AFFILIATE: Riftbound Champion Decks -->

For two people learning together, the Radiance set has **Showdown Decks**, a pair built to play against each other: Seraphine against Evelynn. Each comes with its own runes and three battlefields. <!-- AFFILIATE: Riftbound Radiance Showdown Decks -->

One nice detail from the tournament rules: at casual events, a deck that exactly matches a preconstructed product may keep cards that are banned in Standard. Change a single card and that exception ends, but it means your boxed deck is welcome at a store night as it is.

## How do you choose a champion?

The game has six domains, each with a color: Fury (red), Calm (green), Mind (blue), Body (orange), Chaos (purple), and Order (yellow). A Legend usually has two of them, and your whole deck must fit inside those two. Two quick tests help more than any color chart:

- **Pick a champion you like.** You will play this deck a lot, and liking your champion makes the learning games more fun.
- **Pick a style you enjoy.** Do you want to flood the board with units, or answer what the other player does? Riot's product announcements often describe each deck's plan.

There is no wrong first deck. Your second deck will be a better choice because of what the first one taught you.

## What to skip for now

- **Booster boxes.** Opening packs is fun, but random cards improve a deck less reliably than buying the few cards you need. The [budget upgrades lesson](/academy/riftbound/riftbound-deck-upgrades/) covers that.
- **Bundles and collector products.** They are made for people who already play and collect. Enjoy them later if you love the art.
- **A paid playmat.** Proving Grounds includes playmats, and any table works.

One thing not to skip once you plan to play at a store: **card sleeves**. The tournament rules say a store's head judge can require them at casual events, and they are required at competitive ones. All your sleeves in a deck must match and have opaque backs.
