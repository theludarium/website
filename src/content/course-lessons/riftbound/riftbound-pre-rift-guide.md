A **Pre-Rift** is the first chance to play a new Riftbound set, the week before it releases. You open fresh product at your local store, build a deck from only what you opened, and play it right away. Riot describes these events as fun, social, and casual, with the focus on learning the new cards. Everything you open is yours to keep.

It is also the best event for a new player. Nobody has seen the cards before, so experience counts for less, and everyone builds under the same rules from the same kind of kit.

## What is in the kit?

Kits change from set to set. For Radiance, Riot listed:

- **Five Radiance booster packs.**
- **A champion pack** themed around one of nine Legends, holding a Legend, a Rare champion, a battlefield, and supporting cards.
- **A promo card** that can't go in your sealed deck. A normal copy of the same card that you open can.

The organizer provides extra runes, and tokens can be anything handy. If you don't like your battlefields, you can use a facedown card as a blank battlefield with no text.

## The sealed deck rules

Sealed changes a few deck building rules, and all of them make building easier. From Riot's Pre-Rift guide and the Tournament Rules:

- **Main Deck of at least 25 cards.** You can go higher.
- **Up to three domains.** Signature spells are allowed if both of their domains are among your three.
- **No copy limit.** Use as many copies as you opened.
- **Runes from anywhere,** as long as they match your domains.
- **No Legend or Champion? No problem.** If your deck is missing either one, you draw an extra card at the start of your first turn.
- **Change between games.** At casual events, you can swap cards between your deck and the rest of your pool between games and rounds.

## A recipe for building in under an hour

The Tournament Rules recommend 30 minutes for building, and up to 45 at events like Pre-Rifts where the cards are new to everyone. That is plenty if you follow a simple order.

**1. Sort by domain.** Six piles, plus one for cards with no domain. Don't read the rares yet.

**2. Count the units.** Riot's own advice is to aim for at least 15 units, about five of them costing 2 energy, with a spread of costs beyond that. Units win sealed games because battlefields are taken and held by units. The two domains with the most good units are usually your colors.

**3. Check your Legend.** If the Legend from your champion pack fits those domains, use it, along with a matching champion. If it pulls you toward weaker cards, the rules let you leave it out and take the extra card.

**4. Add your tricks.** Spells and gear that give Might help your units win fights. Anything that removes an enemy unit is precious; play almost all of it.

**5. Consider a third domain.** Only for a few strong cards, and only if your runes can pay their power costs.

**6. Build your runes.** Count the power symbols in your deck and split your runes to match.

**7. Trim to about 25.** Cut the most expensive, least reliable cards first. A deck close to the minimum draws its best cards more often.

## Playing the night

Pre-Rift opponents are learning too. Read every card the other player plays, ask questions, and expect a few surprises. Keep notes on which of your cards overperformed; it is the fastest way to learn a new set.

Sealed games also reward the basics from earlier lessons: get cheap units onto battlefields early, hold what you take, and save removal for the unit that is keeping you off a battlefield.

## Legacy: the first set built for drafting

The next set, **Legacy**, has Pre-Rift events January 22 to 28, 2027, and releases January 29, 2027. Riot calls it the first Riftbound set designed with drafting in mind. It adds "signpost" cards at lower rarities that point toward strategies, and rune kits will include a new Minion unit card so drafters can fill out their decks. Legacy boosters also change shape: one fewer common, and a new slot that holds a Legend or a battlefield.

Draft already has rules. In the Tournament Rules, each player gets **three packs**. Groups of about eight sit around a table, open a pack, take one card, and pass the rest to the left. Pack two passes right, pack three passes left again. Each player ends with a 39-card pool, and a draft Main Deck needs at least 20 cards. The three-domain rule and the no-copy-limit rule from sealed apply too.

If draft is new to you, our explainer on [card drafting](/articles/card-drafting-explained/) covers how pick-and-pass works and how to read what your neighbors are taking.
