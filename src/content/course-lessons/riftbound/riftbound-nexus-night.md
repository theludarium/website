Your first store event is a bigger step than your hundredth kitchen table game. New room, new people, and a quiet worry that everyone else knows something you don't. For Riftbound, the gentlest first step is a **Nexus Night**, and it was designed to be exactly that.

## What is a Nexus Night?

Riot's organized play overview describes Nexus Nights as **weekly** events at local game stores, on whatever day each store picks. They are the store's regular casual night. The format is up to the store, and players can play one-on-one, two-on-two, or free-for-all. Prizes are small Nexus Night packs, which hold three cards and can include promos.

Every Riftbound event has an Organized Play Level. Nexus Nights run at **Casual**, the lowest. The Tournament Rules say that at this level the focus is on education and sportsmanship, with no priority on technically precise play. You are expected to know most of the rules, and help is part of the deal.

## How do you find one?

Use the event search at [playriftbound.com/en-us/events](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/events/). You can filter by location, event type, format, and distance, and see results on a map.

You register with a **Riot ID**, the same account used for Riot's other games. If you don't have one, create it at Riot's account site before registration opens. On the night, the same site lets you check pairings and report results from your phone.

It still helps to call or message the store. Ask what format they run that week and whether they have loaner decks or a spare seat for new players.

## What to bring

- **Your deck, all of it.** Legend, Chosen Champion, Main Deck, Rune Deck, and your battlefields. If you play a preconstructed deck exactly as it came, it is legal at casual events even if it contains a card later banned in Standard.
- **Sleeves.** The head judge can require them even at casual events. All sleeves in a deck must match and have opaque backs.
- **Something to track damage and buffs.** Dice, beads, or coins. Damage resets every turn, but you need to see it during a fight.
- **Tokens.** Printed tokens are nice, but the rules allow anything you can clearly turn sideways and upright.
- **A way to track score.** A die, a counter, or a score tracker.
- **Your phone,** charged, for pairings and results.

Plan to play the same deck all night. The Tournament Rules expect that at casual constructed events unless the organizer says otherwise.

## How the evening runs

Most store nights follow a familiar shape, though the store sets the details:

1. **Check in** with the organizer, and mention it is your first event.
2. **Get paired.** You will see your opponent and table on the site or from the organizer.
3. **Play your match.** Many events play best of three, with each player choosing a new battlefield for each game.
4. **Report the result,** win or loss, and wait for the next round.
5. **Collect prizes** at the end, if the store gives them out by record or by attendance.

## Table courtesies

These are the habits that make a store table pleasant, and most of them come straight from the Tournament Rules.

- **Shuffle well, then offer your deck.** You present your shuffled Main Deck and Rune Deck to your opponent, and they may shuffle it again.
- **Say what you are doing.** "I exhaust two runes and recycle a Fury rune to play this unit at my base." Clear words prevent most arguments.
- **Know what is public.** The number of cards in each hand, every trash, every card on the table, the score, and what is on the Chain are all public. Ask to read an opponent's card any time.
- **Pass clearly in showdowns.** Say "pass" out loud, so both players know when the fight begins.
- **Take-backs.** At casual events, you can change your most recent decision if neither player has done anything since. After that, the action stands.
- **Call a judge, kindly.** If you disagree on a rule, ask the organizer. It is not an accusation; it is how the game stays fair.

## How to be a great opponent

Win or lose, say "good game." If you see a rules slip that hurts your opponent, point it out. If your opponent is new, offer to explain a card. Most players remember their first store night, and you can be the reason someone comes back.
