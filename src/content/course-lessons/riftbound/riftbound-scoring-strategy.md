Every point in Riftbound comes from somewhere. In a two-player game there are only two battlefields, each can score for a player once per turn, and the first player to 8 wins. That small number of moving parts means you can count the race, and players who count make better choices about when to fight.

## Where points come from

- **Holding:** 1 point for each battlefield you control at the start of your turn.
- **Conquering:** 1 point when you take control of a battlefield you have not scored this turn.
- **Card effects:** some cards and battlefields give points directly.
- **Burn out:** when an opponent must draw from an empty Main Deck, you may be the one who gains a point.

Each battlefield scores for you at most once per turn. So in a two-player game, the most you can earn from battlefields in one turn is 2.

## Why holding is the engine

A conquer pays once. A hold pays every turn you keep the battlefield. A battlefield you take on turn 3 and keep for four turns is worth 5 points: 1 for the conquer and 4 for the holds.

That makes defense worth as much as attack. A unit that stays put and keeps your battlefield safe is scoring for you, even if it never moves again. New players often send every unit forward; strong players ask which battlefield they can keep, and build a wall there.

## Counting turns to 8

Each turn, run a quick count for both players: how many points they need, and how many points a turn they are earning right now.

- **Holding one battlefield:** about 1 point a turn, plus whatever you conquer.
- **Holding both:** 2 points a turn. From 2 points, that is three turns to 8.
- **Holding none:** you are scoring only through conquers, which your opponent can see coming.

If your count says your opponent wins a turn before you do, a calm hold will not save you. You need to take a battlefield from them, or at least empty it. If your count says you win first, protect what you hold and make them come to you.

## The final point rule, used on purpose

A conquer gives you your last point only if you have scored every battlefield that turn. Otherwise you draw a card instead. Holding has no such limit. That rule creates a few patterns worth knowing by heart.

**At 7 points, any hold wins.** If you start your turn at 7 holding a battlefield, you score your eighth in your Beginning phase. So when your opponent sits at 7, the only safe defense is to leave them holding nothing by the end of your turn.

**At 6 holding one, a conquer of the other wins.** You hold for your seventh. Then you conquer the other battlefield, and since you have now scored both this turn, that conquer gives you the eighth.

**At 7 holding nothing, you need both.** A single conquer only draws you a card. To win on a conquer you must conquer both battlefields in the same turn, which is hard. More often the plan is to conquer one, keep it through your opponent's turn, and win on the hold.

**Emptying a battlefield counts as defense.** In combat, if both sides are wiped out, the battlefield becomes uncontrolled. Against an opponent at 7, trading your units for theirs at their only battlefield can be enough to stop the win.

## Where to put your units

Once you are counting, the placement questions get easier:

- **When you are ahead in the race,** concentrate on defending the battlefield you hold. Make your opponent attack into Tank units and your ready runes.
- **When you are behind,** spread your threat. Two battlefields under pressure are harder to defend than one.
- **When the scores are close,** contest the battlefield your opponent is holding. Every turn they do not hold it is a point they do not get.

Remember that units entering play arrive exhausted. A unit you play this turn defends this turn if you play it straight to a battlefield you control, but it can attack only next turn. Think one turn ahead.

## Battlefields with text

Many battlefields reward holding or conquering them with an extra effect. Those triggers happen only when the battlefield is scored, so at most once per turn for each player. A battlefield with a strong hold effect is worth defending even harder, and one with a strong conquer effect rewards whoever takes it, so think twice before leaving it empty or lightly defended.
