Trading card games have a reputation for long teaches, and Riftbound has plenty of words that sound technical: channel, recycle, showdown, domain identity. The good news is that a new player needs almost none of them to start. They need the goal, the four start-of-turn steps, and how a fight works.

Here is a script in the order you would say it, with the moments to stop talking. Print the [Riftbound teach sheet](/academy/riftbound/teach-sheet/) and set it between the players.

## Before anyone sits down

Set up both sides first, so the teach starts with the game ready. Use two preconstructed decks; a Proving Grounds box has four. Legend and Chosen Champion face up. Main Deck and Rune Deck shuffled. Each player's battlefield in the middle.

Hand your learner the more forgiving deck. A deck that plays lots of units teaches the board better than one built around tricky spells.

## Minute 1: the goal

Say: "Those two cards in the middle are battlefields. You score a point when you take one, and another point every turn you still hold it. First to 8 wins."

Point at the Legend. "This is your champion. Your deck is built around them."

## Minutes 2 and 3: the start of every turn

Walk the four steps on the teach sheet, out loud, on your own first turn.

1. "Ready everything." Turn your cards upright.
2. "Score a point for each battlefield I hold." Point at the empty battlefields. "None yet."
3. "Channel 2 runes." Flip two runes onto the table.
4. "Draw a card."

Then: "Whoever goes second gets 3 runes on their first turn instead of 2, to make up for going second."

## Minutes 4 and 5: paying for cards

Hold up a card and point at the upper left corner.

- "The number is energy. Turn that many runes sideways."
- "These symbols are power. For each one, put a rune of that color on the bottom of your rune deck."
- "You can do both with the same rune."

Play a unit. "Units come in tired. They can't move this turn." That single sentence prevents most first-game confusion.

Stop here. Ask: "Questions on paying for things?"

## Minutes 6 to 8: moving and fighting

Move one of your units, on a later turn, or just show it with a spare card.

- "To move, turn a unit sideways. It goes from home to a battlefield, or back home."
- "If the battlefield is empty, you take it. One point."
- "If my units are there, we fight. Add up your Might, I add up mine, and we each deal that much damage. Anything with damage equal to its Might dies."
- "If only your units are left, you take it. If any of mine are left, yours go home."

Then the one rule that wins games: "The last point is special. Holding a battlefield at the start of your turn always counts. Taking one only counts if you score both battlefields that turn."

## Minute 9: the end of the turn

"When you're done, say so. All damage heals, and it's my turn."

## Minute 10: start, and teach the rest live

Say: "That's enough to play. You'll see the rest as it happens." Then deal the opening hands and walk the mulligan: "Want to swap up to 2 cards? Put them aside, draw that many, then they go to the bottom."

The rest you teach when it comes up:

- **Showdowns.** The first time a fight starts, explain that both players can play Action and Reaction cards before the damage, taking turns, starting with the attacker.
- **Keywords.** Read each one aloud the first time it shows up. The previous lesson has a short list.
- **Hidden cards.** When someone hides a card at a battlefield, explain that, starting on the next turn, it can be played without paying its normal cost, as a surprise.
- **Burning out.** Almost never happens in a first game. Skip it unless a deck runs dry.

## What to leave out

- **Deck building rules.** Domain identity, copy limits, and signature cards can wait until someone wants a second deck.
- **The full timing rules.** The Core Rules are long and precise; a first game needs none of it.
- **Strategy.** Let people find their own first plan.

## Coaching during the first game

Help with the rules, not the decisions. "That unit can't move yet, it came in this turn" is helpful. "You should attack there" is playing for them.

Watch for a learner who spends every rune each turn on power and runs short the next. One gentle question helps: "How many runes will you have left to turn sideways next turn?"
