Riftbound is not only a duel. The Core Rules list sanctioned modes for three and four players, and the store sets the format for its Nexus Nights, so ask yours about multiplayer nights. <!-- VERIFY: whether Riot's organized play materials name 2v2 or free-for-all as Nexus Night formats. --> Multiplayer games are some of the most social Riftbound you can play, and they ask for skills a duel never tests.

## The modes at a glance

| Mode | Players | Battlefields | Points to win |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duel | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| Free-for-all, 3 players | 3 | 3 | 8 |
| Free-for-all, 4 players | 4 | 3 | 8 |
| 2v2 teams | 4 | 3 | 11 |

Every player still brings 3 battlefields. In a three-player game, each player picks one of theirs at random. In the four-player modes, the player going first removes their battlefields from the game, and each other player picks one at random, which makes 3.

**First turns change too.** In the three- and four-player modes, the player going first skips their first draw, and the player going last channels an extra rune on their first turn.

## The limit that shapes everything

Combat can only happen between exactly two players. So:

- You can't move units to a battlefield that already has units from two other players.
- While two players are fighting at a battlefield, or a fight there is about to start, nobody else can move or play units there.

In practice this means a battlefield where two rivals are locked in a fight is closed to you, and the other battlefields are more open. Watch for the moment two opponents commit to the same battlefield. That is often your turn to take another one with little resistance.

## The final point gets harder

The final point rule still applies: a conquer gives you your last point only if you have scored every battlefield that turn. With three battlefields in play, that means scoring all three in one turn, which is rare. In multiplayer, most games are won on a **hold**. Plan to sit at 7 holding something when your turn comes around, and expect others to work hard to stop it.

## Free-for-all strategy

- **Do not lead too early.** The player in front becomes everyone's target. A steady second place with a battlefield held often wins in the end.
- **Count every opponent's race,** not just the leader's. A player at 6 holding one battlefield can win with a hold next turn.
- **Let fights happen.** When two opponents fight, both lose units. Spend that turn building or taking an open battlefield.
- **Say what you see.** "If nobody stops her, she wins next turn" is fair table talk. Making deals you will not keep is not.

Our article on [kingmaking in board games](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) covers how to keep a free-for-all fair when one player can no longer win but can still decide who does.

## 2v2: teams to 11

Teams sit in an order that alternates: a player from one team, then a player from the other, then the first team's second player, and so on. The key rules, in plain words:

- **Points are shared.** Both teammates have the same score, and the team wins together at 11.
- **Control is not shared.** Your teammate's battlefields are theirs. You can't move your units to a battlefield your teammate's units occupy, and you can't hide cards there.
- **Each battlefield scores once per team per turn.** A battlefield your teammate controlled at the start of your turn can't be scored by your team through a conquer that turn. No passing a battlefield back and forth for extra points.
- **You can help on your teammate's turn.** When your teammate invites you, using their own priority, you can play spells and use abilities on their turn, even ones without Action or Reaction.
- **Hands are private, but you may show them.** You can show your hand to your teammate at any time.
- **Different Legends and battlefields.** Teammates can't use the same Champion Legend or the same battlefields.

The final point rule changes slightly: to win with a conquer, a team must score every battlefield that turn, except any their teammate occupied at the start of that turn.

## 2v2 strategy

Talk first, play second. Before each game, agree on roles: one teammate who holds a battlefield, one who pressures. During play, keep it quick; the Tournament Rules ask teammates to communicate in a reasonably timely way.

Because points are shared and each battlefield scores once per team per turn, two teammates holding two different battlefields earn the most. Two teammates crowding the same battlefield earn less than you might think.
