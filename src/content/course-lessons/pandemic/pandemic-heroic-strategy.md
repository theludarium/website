The rulebook suggests playing with six Epidemics once you have mastered the Standard game. That is Heroic, and it is the top of the base game's difficulty dial. Most groups lose there for a while. This lesson explains what actually changes, using the rules and the deck math from the [odds lesson](/academy/pandemic/pandemic-probability/), and then gives a plan built around it.

Our [Pandemic strategy guide](/articles/pandemic-strategy/) covers the Heroic basics, including roles and Events. This lesson goes one level deeper, into timing.

## What changes, and what does not

Start with what stays the same. With 2 or 4 players, the Player deck at Heroic is 51 cards. At Standard it is 50. In both, the team gets 25 draws, so 26 turns with actions. The useful cards are the same too: 45 City and Event cards go into the deck either way. **Heroic does not shorten your clock.**

What changes is the pressure:

- **More Epidemics, closer together.** Piles of 9 and 8 instead of 10. An Epidemic arrives about every four or five draws.
- **The infection rate climbs sooner in the game.** The track reads 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4. At both Standard and Heroic, the rate reaches 3 after the third Epidemic and 4 after the fifth. With Heroic's smaller piles, each of those steps comes earlier, and you play longer at 4.
- **Double Epidemics become possible.** At Standard with 2 or 4 players, they cannot happen. At Heroic, they can, though rarely.
- **One more 3-cube city.** Every Epidemic fills a new city to 3 and puts it in the stack. Six Epidemics means six of these over the game.

## The good news inside the bad news

More Epidemics means more reshuffles. Each reshuffle puts only the cities flipped since the last one back on top. So at Heroic, the stack is often small, and the same handful of cities keeps coming back.

That cuts both ways. A small stack means a 3-cube city in it comes up fast. But it also means the team only needs to watch a few cities at a time. Heroic rewards teams who know exactly which cities are in the stack, and punishes teams who treat the whole map.

## A plan in three beats

Think of every pile of the Player deck as one beat of the game. Each beat has the same three parts.

**1. Right after the Epidemic: control the stack.**

- Read the discard pile aloud before the shuffle, so everyone knows the stack.
- Play Resilient Population between Infect and Intensify, on the new 3-cube city, if you have it.
- Use the first turns of the beat to bring every 3-cube city in the stack down to 2. The Medic and Quarantine Specialist earn their place here.

**2. The middle of the pile: build cures.**

- Once the Epidemic of this pile is out, the rest of the pile is safe. Count the cards left in it, and you know how many turns of calm you have.
- Spend that calm on shares and cures. This is when a collector should be travelling to a station.

**3. Before the next pile: prepare the discard pile.**

- The cities flipped during this beat are now in the discard pile, and they become the next stack. Any of them at 3 cubes is the next emergency.
- Treat those cities before the boundary, and park the Quarantine Specialist where she guards the most of them.
- If the next draw could take the last card of one pile and the first of the next, keep an Event ready for a double Epidemic.

## Cure what the cards give you

At Heroic, there is less time to choose an order of cures. Cure whichever color comes together first. The [card flow lesson](/academy/pandemic/pandemic-share-knowledge-strategy/) still holds: one collector per color, meetings planned a turn ahead. But be ready to change collectors when the draws favor a different color.

Each cure helps the infection fight too. A cured color clears with one Treat action per city, and the Medic clears it just by passing through. Eradication is worth chasing only when a cured color is down to a few cubes near your pawns.

## Your outbreak budget

The eighth outbreak loses. At Heroic, plan to keep a cushion. If the marker reaches 5 by the middle of the game, switch more actions to the stack, even if a cure slips by a turn. A single chain reaction can take two or three outbreaks at once.

Watch the cube supply as well. Each color has 24 cubes. Outbreaks at rate 4 can drain a color faster than it looks.

## A practice path to a Heroic win

1. **Play Heroic with open hands.** The rulebook allows experienced groups to choose open hands. It lets you learn the pressure without the extra communication load.
2. **Switch to closed hands** once you win, and use the turn talk from the [team planning lesson](/academy/pandemic/pandemic-team-communication/).
3. **Log every game.** Date, roles, Epidemics, result, and which loss came closest. After five Heroic games, the log shows your team's weak spot.
