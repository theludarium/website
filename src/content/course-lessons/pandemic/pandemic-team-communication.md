The Introductory game has everyone play with cards face up. Standard and Heroic do not. The [rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-zman/uploads/2024/09/Pandemic_Rulebook.pdf) says to keep your cards private in those games, so that everyone has information to contribute. Experienced groups may choose open hands, but the default is closed.

That one change is the start of Level 2's last idea. Closed hands do not make Pandemic a game of secrets. They make it a game of conversation, and conversation can be done well or badly.

## What closed hands change

With open hands, one player can see every card and work out every turn. That is how the quarterback problem starts: the strongest planner runs the game and everyone else moves pieces. Our article on the [quarterback problem](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/) covers why it drains the fun out of co-ops.

With closed hands, nobody sees everything. The collector knows what they need. The player across the table knows what they hold. A good plan needs both. Each voice is needed, because each player holds a piece of the picture.

Nothing in the rules stops you from saying what you hold. You can name colors, counts, and even specific cities. The cards stay in your hand; the knowledge goes on the table.

## A three-part turn talk

Pandemic turns can stall in long debate. A short routine keeps them moving.

**1. Report.** Before planning, the active player says what they hold: colors and counts, plus any city cards that matter for a share, and any Event cards. Others add one line each only if something changed since last turn.

**2. Propose.** Anyone can offer a plan. Keep each one short: "Treat Istanbul twice, then drive toward the Scientist." Ask questions more than you give answers. "What worries you most on the board?" helps more than a full turn handed over.

**3. Decide.** The active player chooses. The rulebook is plain: players give each other advice, and the player whose turn it is decides. Once they decide, the table moves on.

## Habits that keep everyone in the game

- **Active player speaks first.** Before anyone else proposes, the player whose turn it is gets a moment to say what they see. Quiet players often have the best read, but only if they speak before the loud plan arrives.
- **Offer, do not order.** "One option is..." gives a teammate a choice. "You should..." takes it away.
- **Respect the call.** If a teammate picks a plan you would not, play your next turn around it. Arguing after the decision costs time and trust.
- **Rotate the big picture.** Each game, give one player the job of watching the Infection discard pile and another the job of counting the Player deck. Different players see different parts of the board.
- **Keep a time sense.** Most turns need a minute or two of talk, not ten. Save long discussion for the turn after an Epidemic and the last few turns of the game.

## When someone does take over

It happens, often with kind intent. Someone knows the game well and wants the team to win. A few gentle fixes:

- **Name the rule once, at setup.** "We plan together, and each player makes the call on their turn." Said before the game, it reminds rather than scolds.
- **Ask the expert to go last.** They can still share their read, after everyone else has had a turn to think.
- **Give them a job.** Card counter or discard-pile reader puts their skill to work without running other turns.

Our list of [co-op games without a quarterback problem](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/) has more fixes, and other games built to avoid it.

## Sharing information well

Some things are worth saying every turn, and some are worth saying only when they change.

| Say every turn | Say when it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Your colors and counts | A new Event card in your hand |
| A city card someone else needs | That you are near the hand limit |
| Where you plan to end your turn | That a color's cards are running low |

Where you end your turn matters more than it sounds. It tells the next player where a share is possible, and where the Medic or Quarantine Specialist can lean on you.

## Why this is Level 2

Level 1 gave you strong single turns. Level 2 links them. A midgame plan, a share planned a turn ahead, an Event held for its moment: each one needs more than one player. The routine in this lesson is what lets four separate turns work as one plan.
