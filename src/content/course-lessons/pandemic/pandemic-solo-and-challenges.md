Most game courses in the Academy end with a tournament lesson. Pandemic is different. Everyone at the table is on the same team, and the opponent is the game itself. So competition looks different here: harder settings, solo play, and the quiet pride of a group that finally beats Heroic. Here is what exists, and what does not.

## Is there a Pandemic tournament scene?

Not a big one, and it is worth being clear about that.

The [World Boardgaming Championships](https://www.boardgamers.org/) (WBC), the long-running tournament convention in Pennsylvania, crowns champions in many games each year. Pandemic is not among the events on its [2026 winners list](https://www.boardgamers.org/wbc_results.html).

<!-- VERIFY: check the WBC 2027 event list in case a Pandemic event is added. -->

Z-Man Games did once run a competitive format called **Pandemic Survival**. Teams of two raced each other at the same time, on identical setups: the same roles, the same infected cities, and the same Player cards in the same order. The first team to find all four cures, or the last team still standing, won. Regional events fed national finals and a world championship. Z-Man still posts the [Survival rules](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-zman/uploads/2024/09/Pandemic_Survival_Public_Rules.pdf) on its Pandemic page, but its site no longer lists a current season.

## Run a Survival-style night at home

The Survival format makes a great hosted night if you have two copies of the game. Set up both boards identically and stack both Player decks in the same order. One host flips a single Infection deck for everyone, so the Survival rules leave out any Event that changes the Infection deck. Two teams of two play at once, and the race is on.

Official Survival events needed 6 to 12 teams. At home, two teams and a host are plenty.

## Solo play, the official way

Z-Man publishes [official solo rules](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-zman/uploads/2024/09/Pandemic_Solo-Rules-Variant.pdf). They change setup and one action:

- Remove the Researcher. Deal yourself 3 roles in a row, left to right. All three share one hand of cards. The Researcher's unused pawn marks whose turn it is.
- Take the Event cards out of the Player cards. Shuffle the City cards, deal 3 to your hand, and deal 3 face up beside the board. That row is the **archive**. Shuffle the 5 Events back into the rest and build the Player deck as normal.
- Take each role's full turn in order, left to right. Your hand limit is still 7.
- **Share Knowledge** changes: put the City card that matches your city into the archive, or take the matching card out. The archive has no limit.

Solo play is the best way to learn the rhythm of the Infection deck without anyone watching. It is also a good way to prepare before you teach. Our guide to the [best solo board games](/articles/best-solo-board-games/) has more on playing alone well.

## Online play

Pandemic is on [Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=pandemic), in your browser, listed for 1 to 4 players. Designer Matt Leacock's [Pandemic page](https://www.leacock.com/pandemic) lists the older phone, Steam, Switch, and Xbox versions as no longer available, so Board Game Arena is the place to play online.

## A challenge ladder

Without a circuit, you set your own goals. A ladder that works for most groups:

1. Win at 4 Epidemics with hands open.
2. Win at 5 Epidemics with hands private.
3. Win at Heroic, 6 Epidemics.
4. Solve Z-Man's ten free puzzles from the [Pandemic page](https://www.zmangames.com/game/pandemic/), using the method in the [puzzles lesson](/academy/pandemic/pandemic-puzzles-and-scenarios/).
5. Play the free scenarios, starting with "Isolation."
6. With On the Brink, add its challenges one at a time. The Bio-Terrorist puts one player against the team, the closest the family comes to head-to-head play.

Keep a short log: the date, players, roles, Epidemics, the result, and which losing condition came closest. After ten games, the pattern shows you what to work on.

## Finding people to play with

Pandemic does not need a tournament. It needs two to four people who want to come back.

- **Your local game store.** Many stores run open game nights, and a co-op is an easy sell to a mixed table. Z-Man Games is part of Asmodee, which has a [store locator](https://storelocator.asmodee.com/) for finding participating stores.
- **Libraries and community centers.** Many host board game meetups and keep games to borrow.
- **Start your own.** Co-ops are great for new groups, because nobody has to beat a friend to have a good night. Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) walks through it.

If you are near Indianapolis, join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

When your group wants more co-ops after Pandemic, our list of [co-op games without a quarterback problem](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/) is a good next stop.

## How to play like a strong team

- **Debrief every game.** Two minutes on what worked and what nearly lost it.
- **Rotate who leads the planning.** The quietest player often sees what the loudest one missed.
- **Count out loud.** The Infection discard pile, the cards to the next Epidemic, the turns left.

That is the end of the course. You can now play, teach, open, use every role, read the Infection deck, put odds on the next Epidemic, count the endgame, beat Heroic, choose your next box, solve the puzzles, and set your own challenges. The game is the reason. The people across the table are the point.
