Welcome to Level 5. You have a deck you know, a practice habit, and a few store events behind you, and you might be wondering how far this goes. The answer: all the way to a World Championship in Japan. You do not need to aim there to enjoy the game. But knowing the ladder helps you pick the next step that fits you.

## The three levels

Bandai's tournament rules sort official events into three levels:

- **Level 1, Casual:** store events such as Store Tournaments, Pirates Party, release events, sealed, and draft. Learning and fun come first.
- **Level 2, Competitive:** Store Championships and other special events. Rules are enforced more strictly.
- **Level 3, Professional:** the Championship series, including Treasure Cups, Regionals, Finals, and the World Championship.

Moving up a level changes the feel more than the game. The rules of play are the same. The pace, the rules enforcement, and the stakes go up.

## The Championship season

Bandai's **[Championship 26-27](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/events/2026/championship/)** is the main competitive series, and it runs in seasons.

**Store Championships** are held at selected local stores, in North America and Europe only. They are the natural next step after regular Store Tournaments.

**Regionals** are large events that crown the top player of each region. Season 2 runs from August to December 2026. In North America and Europe, the top 16 at a Regional earn an invite to that season's Finals, and Regionals with 800 or more players give 32 invites. Each Regional is run by its own organizer with its own registration page.

**Finals** close each season. Season 2 Finals are scheduled for January 2027. In North America and Europe, you need an invite.

**The World Championship Finals** are held in Japan. The top finishers at each region's Finals advance there.

## Treasure Cups

**Treasure Cups** are year-round Standard tournaments with their own prizes. From the 2026 season, they run only in person, often as side events at Regionals. They play best of one, with 35-minute matches, and larger events cut to a top group for the final rounds.

## What changes at bigger events

- **Formats.** Most official events use Standard. There is also an Extra format where nearly every card ever printed is legal, used for special events.
- **Match structure.** Professional events usually play best of one in the Swiss rounds and best of three in the top cut.
- **Deck lists.** Championship events require a deck list, and some, like Finals, ask for it before the event.
- **Rules enforcement.** Judges hold players to the rules more tightly, and small mistakes can bring penalties.

## How to practice like a competitor

The [six-week practice plan](/academy/one-piece-card-game/one-piece-card-game-tournament-practice/) at Level 4 turns these habits into a schedule. In short:

- **Play one deck.** Dozens of games with one Leader teach you more than a few games with many.
- **Practice on a clock.** A 35-minute game goes faster than it sounds. Learn to make routine choices quickly so you have time for the hard ones.
- **Review your losses.** Where did you take damage you could have countered? When did you rest a Character and lose it?
- **Learn the other decks.** You do not need to own them. Read the popular Leaders in Bandai's card list so nothing surprises you at the table.
- **Use the tutorial app** for quick games when nobody is free.

## Finding good local play

The best opponents are often regulars at one store.

- **Bandai TCG+.** Its store list shows which stores near you run official events.
- **Your local game store.** Ask staff which night the One Piece players meet, and whether there is a casual night alongside the tournament.
- **Start your own.** Two friends with starter decks is a play group. Our guide on [how to start a play group](/articles/how-to-start-a-play-group/) shows how to keep one going, and [how to run a tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers brackets if your group grows.

If you are near Indianapolis, browse [Indianapolis board game stores](/articles/indianapolis-board-game-stores/) and join the [Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.
