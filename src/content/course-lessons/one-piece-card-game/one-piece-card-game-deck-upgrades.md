A starter deck is a real deck. You can win games and store events with it. But every starter is built to teach, so it carries a few cards that do less than they could. Swapping those for better ones is the cheapest way to improve, and it teaches you more about the game than anything else you can buy.

This lesson is about how to think through upgrades. We do not name specific cards, because the best upgrades depend on which Leader you play and change with every set.

## Singles, not packs

A booster pack holds a random handful of cards from one set. The cards you want for your deck are a few specific ones, in a few specific colors. Packs are a fun way to collect, and a slow, costly way to finish a deck.

Buying **singles**, individual cards, is how most players upgrade. Local game stores often sell them from a binder or a display case, and many trade too. You can look up any card by name or number in Bandai's official [card list](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist/) to read its text before you buy.

## Know your deck before you shop

Five games with the starter tell you more than any list online. Keep notes as you play:

- **Which cards win games?** Those are worth a second or third copy, up to the 4-card limit.
- **Which cards sit in your hand?** A card you never want to play is the first one to cut.
- **What keeps beating you?** If big Characters run you over, you may need removal. If you run out of cards, you may need draw.

Then make a short list: three or four cards to add, and the same number to cut. Keep the deck at exactly 50.

## Watch your Counters

New players shop for big Characters with exciting text. Experienced players also count Counters.

Every Character with a Counter value is a card you can trash during your opponent's attack to protect your Leader or a key Character. A deck with too few Counter cards loses Life fast and cannot protect what it builds. When you cut a card, check what it did as a Counter before you let it go. When you add one, ask whether it does both jobs.

## Watch your cost curve

Lay your deck out by cost, low to high. Most decks want plenty of cards they can play in the first few turns, when you have only 1 to 4 DON!!, and only a few expensive ones. If your upgrades are all big cards, you will have hands you cannot play early.

## Stay in your colors

Every card must share a color with your Leader. A two-color Leader opens up both colors, which is one reason two-color starters, like the Red/Blue and Blue/Black ones coming in December, have room to grow.

## Check that a card is legal

Most official events use the **Standard** format, which sets which cards are legal. Standard uses the block symbol, a number printed in the lower right corner of each card. For events from April 2026 to March 2027, blocks 2 to 5 are legal and block 1 is not, and each April the oldest block drops out. Bandai also keeps a [Banned/Restricted Card List](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/news/restriction.html) of banned cards and banned pairs. A card can be a great deal because it is no longer legal in Standard, so check its block symbol and that list before you buy an older single.

New cards from a set become legal at casual store events on release day (or a store's pre-release), and at bigger competitive events 7 days after release.

## Buying a second starter

If you love your starter, a second copy of the same deck can be a simple upgrade. A starter runs several copies of most of its cards, so a second copy helps most with the ones it has only 1 or 2 of. Check the decklist in Bandai's card list first. It also leaves you with a full spare deck to lend a friend.

## The few accessories worth buying

**Sleeves.** You need opaque sleeves for any store tournament, and they protect cards you paid for. Bandai sells its own, such as [Official Card Sleeves 17](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/sleeve047.html), 70 sleeves due in December. Other opaque sleeves work as long as all your deck sleeves match and none have reflective backs. Buy a few spare. <!-- AFFILIATE: ONE PIECE CARD GAME Official Card Sleeves 17 -->

**A few packs, for fun.** If you want to open something, small products like the [Double Pack Set Vol. 13](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/dp13.html) give you packs and a little extra at a set price. Treat them as a treat, not a plan. <!-- AFFILIATE: ONE PIECE CARD GAME Double Pack Set Vol. 13 -->

**A deck box.** Anything sturdy that fits a sleeved deck, a Leader, and 10 DON!!.

## A budget habit that lasts

Set a monthly number you are happy to spend, and buy toward the list you made, not toward the newest set. If you keep the starter cards you cut, you can trade them at the store or hand them to the next friend you teach.
