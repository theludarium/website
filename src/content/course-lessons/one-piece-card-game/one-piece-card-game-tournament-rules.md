Welcome to Level 4. You know the game well. Now you are learning the event around it. Bandai's [Tournament Rules Manual](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/rules/) covers every official event. At store events, the manual asks judges to teach as much as enforce. At Level 2 (Competitive) and Level 3 (Professional) events, it calls for the highest level of enforcement. This lesson walks through the parts that change your day.

Everything here comes from the manual posted on Bandai's rules page in January 2026 (the document itself says last updated October 17, 2025). Event pages can set their own details, so read your event's page too.

## The clock

The manual's standard structure for qualifying rounds is **best of one, 30 minutes, plus 5 minutes of extra time**. Top cut matches are **best of three, 60 minutes, plus 10**. The final match (top 2) of a Championship event has no time limit. Online events use 35 minutes. Event pages can set their own clock, and Bandai's pages for Store Tournaments and Treasure Cups set best of one with **35 minutes plus 5**, as the [store tournament lesson](/academy/one-piece-card-game/one-piece-card-game-store-tournament/) mentions. When an event page and the manual differ, the event page tells you that event's clock, and the organizer announces the time per round before round one.

## When time is called

If the game is not over when time runs out, the manual's end of round procedure decides it:

1. If one player has won more games in the match, they win.
2. Otherwise, the active player finishes their turn. That is turn 0. Then the players take three more turns.
3. If nobody has won after those turns, the player with more **Life cards** wins.
4. Still tied: more cards left in their **deck**.
5. Still tied: more **Characters** on the table.
6. Still tied: the player who last removed a card from their Life area wins.

Extra turns have their own 5-minute limit. In Swiss, a game can still end in a draw. In single elimination, draws are not allowed.

Notice what the tiebreaks reward: Life first. In a close game near time, protecting your Life and keeping Characters on the table can decide it.

## Deck lists

Every Championship level event requires a deck list. Local events can require one if they say so in advance. The manual is specific:

- Write each card's **collector number and full name**. No shorthand.
- The deck you play must match the list exactly.
- **No side decks.** The 50 cards on your list are your deck for every game.
- Getting the count wrong is a Game Loss: a list or deck with 49 or 51 cards, or a deck that does not match the list.

Count your list twice. Then count your sleeved deck against it.

## Communication

The manual expects clear, accurate talk:

- **Say what you do.** Announce attacks, targets, and effects as they happen.
- **A move is committed** once you announce it or take your hand off the card. You cannot take it back.
- **If what you say and what you do disagree,** and what you said is legal, what you said counts.
- **Point out mistakes.** If your opponent fails to follow a rule, even by accident, you must tell them right away, and call a judge if needed.
- **Call a judge at the time,** not later. Judges answer rules questions but never give strategy advice.

## Penalties, in plain words

The manual lists penalties from lightest to heaviest: Caution, Warning, Game Loss, Match Loss, Disqualification, and Suspension. A few you should know:

- **Game rules errors** (playing a rule wrong, or forgetting an automatic effect) are usually a **Warning**. The judge rewinds or fixes what they can. A forgotten effect that said "you may" is treated as if you chose not to use it.
- **Looking at or drawing extra cards,** such as looking at 4 cards when an effect says 3, or drawing 3 when you should draw 2, is a Warning, with a fix.
- **Being late:** a Warning within 5 minutes of the round start, a Game Loss after 5, and a Match Loss after 10.
- **Slow play:** a Warning, rising to a Game Loss if it continues. The manual's examples include thinking over one play for more than a minute.
- **Outside help** during a match, such as advice from a friend, is a Game Loss or Match Loss.

## Small rules that catch people

- **No notes** during matches. You may check your deck list between matches, not between games.
- **No phones** or other devices during a match, and nothing unrelated on the table.
- **No eating or drinking** at tournament tables, even between rounds.
- **Shuffle so your opponent can see,** then present your deck to be cut or shuffled.
- **Searches and shuffles** should take no more than about a minute.
- **Bring photo ID.** Events with age limits or invitations require it.
