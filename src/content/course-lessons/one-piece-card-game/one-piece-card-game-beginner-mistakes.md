Welcome to Level 1. You can play a full game and teach one. Now the goal is to play cleanly. Your first three games are where habits form, and some of those habits are quietly wrong. This lesson collects the slips that come up again and again, checked against the official Comprehensive Rules, version 1.2.1, and the strategy traps that cost new players the most.

## Rules slips: timing

Most rules mistakes in this game are about **when** something works.

- **Attacking on turn one.** Neither player can battle on their own first turn. That includes the player going second.
- **Attacking with a Character you just played.** A Character cannot attack on the turn it is played unless its text says so, as with Rush.
- **Countering on your own turn.** Counter values and [Counter] Events are used in the Counter Step, which only happens when you are the one being attacked. On your turn, a card's Counter value does nothing.
- **Giving DON!! for defense.** A given DON!! adds 1000 power **during your turn** only. Tucking DON!! under your Leader before passing does not protect it on your opponent's turn.
- **Using a [Main] Event in a battle.** [Main] Events and [Activate: Main] effects work in your Main Phase, but not in the middle of a battle.

## Rules slips: the table

- **Attacking an active Character.** You can attack the opposing Leader, or a Character that is **rested**. A Character standing upright is not a legal target.
- **Two Blockers in one battle.** The defender can use Blocker only once per battle. The Blocker rests and becomes the new target.
- **Forgetting the Refresh.** At the start of your turn, every DON!! you gave comes back to your cost area, and then all your rested cards stand up. New players often leave DON!! tucked under a Character for a second turn by mistake.
- **The sixth Character.** You can have 5 Characters. To play a sixth, you trash one of your five first. That is a rule, not a K.O., so an [On K.O.] effect on the trashed card does not happen.
- **Triggers are optional.** When a Life card with a Trigger comes up, you choose: reveal it and use the Trigger, or add it to your hand like any other Life card. A used Trigger card goes to the trash unless it says otherwise.
- **The redraw.** It is all or nothing. Shuffle your whole opening hand back and draw 5 new cards, once. And set your Life cards from the top of your deck without looking at them.

## Strategy traps

Rules slips are easy to fix once someone points them out. These cost more, because they are legal.

**Swinging with everything.** Attacking with a Character rests it, and a rested Character is a legal target on your opponent's turn. If the attack is unlikely to land, or the Character matters more than one Life card, leaving it standing can be the stronger play.

**Playing every Character in your hand.** Each Character in hand is also a Counter. A hand of zero cards cannot protect your Leader at 1 Life. Before you play the last card in your hand, ask what it would do as a shield next turn.

**Countering early hits.** At 5 Life, a hit on your Leader costs you one Life card and hands you a card. Throwing two Counters to stop it costs you two cards. Early on, taking the hit is often the better trade. The [blocking and countering lesson](/academy/one-piece-card-game/one-piece-card-game-blocking-and-counters/) covers this in detail.

**Ignoring your Leader.** Many Leaders have an effect that fires every turn or every attack. New players forget them for whole games. Read your Leader out loud at the start of each turn until it sticks.

**Ignoring their Leader.** Your opponent's Leader tells you what their deck wants to do. Read it before the game begins, and ask to see it again whenever you like; it is open information.

## A three-game habit

Say each phase out loud for your first three games, as the [turn lesson](/academy/one-piece-card-game/one-piece-card-game-rules/) suggests. Then add two more phrases: "Is that Character rested?" before you choose a target, and "Counter step?" before you compare power on your opponent's attacks. Those two questions catch nearly every timing slip above.

Keep the official [Comprehensive Rules](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/rules/) bookmarked for the rest. When card text and the rules disagree, the card wins, so read the card first and the rules second.
