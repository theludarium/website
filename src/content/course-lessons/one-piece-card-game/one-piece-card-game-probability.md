Every game of One Piece starts the same way: 50 cards, shuffled, 5 in your hand, and some face down in your Life. That fixed start means the odds of seeing a card are the same every game, and you can know them. They explain why decks play 4 copies of key cards, when the redraw is worth it, and how often a Trigger will save you.

All the numbers here are worked out from a 50-card deck and the setup in the official rules. They are rounded to whole percentages.

## Seeing a card early

Here is the chance of having at least one copy of a card, depending on how many copies (or how many cards that do the same job) are in your deck:

| Copies in deck | Opening hand (5) | With a redraw | 6 cards seen | 7 cards seen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10% | 19% | 12% | 14% |
| 2 | 19% | 35% | 23% | 26% |
| 3 | 28% | 48% | 32% | 37% |
| 4 | 35% | 58% | 41% | 46% |
| 8 | 60% | 84% | 67% | 73% |
| 12 | 76% | 94% | 83% | 87% |
| 16 | 87% | 98% | 92% | 95% |

"With a redraw" assumes you redraw every hand that misses. "Cards seen" counts your opening hand plus draws. Going first, you have seen 6 cards on your second turn. Going second, you have seen 6 on your first turn and 7 on your second.

## What the table says

**One card is a long shot.** Even with 4 copies, you see a specific card in your opening hand only about a third of the time. If your deck needs that card early, you will be without it in most games.

**Jobs beat cards.** Eight cards that do the same job, such as eight cheap Characters that draw or search, show up in about 60% of opening hands and more than 80% with a redraw. That is why strong decks build around roles, not single cards. The deck building lesson picks this up.

**The redraw is strong.** It nearly doubles your chance of finding a 4-copy card. But it is all or nothing: you shuffle back every card, including the good ones. Redraw when the hand cannot do what your deck needs early, not just because one card is missing.

## Your Life cards

After you draw your opening hand, the top cards of your deck become your Life, face down. For a Leader with 5 Life, that is 5 cards you will not draw.

Any single card has a 10% chance (5 in 50) to be in your Life. And the chance that at least one of your 4 copies of a card is stuck there is about 35%, the same as the chance of drawing one in your opening 5. Those cards are not lost. They come to your hand as you take damage.

## How many Triggers to expect

Triggers live in your deck, so some will land in your Life. With a 5-Life Leader:

| Trigger cards in deck | Expected in Life | At least 1 in Life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 0.4 | 35% |
| 8 | 0.8 | 60% |
| 12 | 1.2 | 76% |
| 16 | 1.6 | 87% |

Triggers are a bonus, not a plan. Even with 16 in your deck, you expect fewer than two among your five Life cards. Count on them as a pleasant surprise in the game, and be wary of a Trigger on your opponent's last Life card when you count lethal.

## Counters in hand

The same math answers a defense question. If your deck has 25 cards with a Counter value, you expect 2.5 of them in a 5-card opening hand, and the chance of opening with none is about 2.5%. With 20, you expect 2, and the chance of none is about 7%. Each extra Counter card in your deck makes every hand a little safer.

## Running out of cards

Your deck is a clock too. After a 5-card hand and 5 Life cards, a 50-card deck has 40 left. You lose when your deck reaches 0 cards, so draw effects bring that clock closer. In most games it never matters. In a long, defensive game, count your deck before you draw extra.

## Using odds at the table

You will not quote percentages mid-game, and you should not try. Learn three anchors: one card in a third of hands, eight cards in most hands, and the redraw nearly doubling your chance. Those three carry you through deck building, redraw decisions, and lethal turns.
