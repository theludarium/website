The One Piece Card Game is a two-player trading card game from Bandai. Each player builds a crew around one Leader card, plays Characters to the table, and attacks the other player's Leader. Every hit takes away a Life card. Land a hit when your opponent has no Life left and you win.

This is an unofficial course, written in our own words from the official rules. It starts where most people start: standing in a store, looking at a wall of boxes, wondering which one to buy.

## What does a deck need?

Every player needs three things, and the official rules are exact about them:

- **1 Leader card.** It sits face up for the whole game. Its colors decide which cards your deck can use, and its Life number decides how many hits you can take.
- **A 50-card deck** of Characters, Events, and Stages. Every card must share a color with your Leader, and you can run no more than 4 cards with the same card number.
- **A 10-card DON!! deck.** DON!! cards are your resource. They are a separate stack, not part of the 50.

A starter deck gives you all three. That is the main reason to begin with one: a recent starter is legal right out of the box, so you can learn the game and play at a store with no deck building at all. Very old starters can hold cards that have rotated out of the main tournament format, which lesson 5 explains.

## Starting before December?

You do not have to wait. Any current starter deck follows the same rules: a Leader, 50 cards, and 10 DON!!.

If two of you are learning together, a two-deck learning set is the easiest start. The **[Set Sail Deck Set (SD-01)](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/sd01.html)** has two constructed decks, each with its own 10 DON!!, one OP-17 booster pack, a two-player playsheet, two guide books, and two deck cases, at a suggested price of about $25. <!-- AFFILIATE: Set Sail Deck Set [SD-01] -->

For a family or a group of three, the **[Learn Together Deck Set](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/decks/ld01.php)** has three decks, three guide books, three deck cases, three small extra card packs, and a two-player playsheet. <!-- AFFILIATE: Learn Together Deck Set -->

## How do you choose a color?

The six colors are red, green, blue, purple, black, and yellow, and a Leader can have one or two of them. Each color leans toward a style, but the cards themselves say more than any color chart. Two quick tests help:

- **Pick a character you like.** You will play this deck a lot. Liking your Leader makes the learning games more fun.
- **Pick a style you enjoy.** Do you want to attack every turn, or answer what the other player does? The product page usually says which in a line or two.

There is no wrong first deck. Your second deck will be a better choice because of what the first one taught you.

## What to skip for now

- **Booster boxes.** Packs are fun to open, but random cards do not make a starter better as reliably as buying the few cards you need. Lesson 5 covers that.
- **Premium collections and gift sets.** They are made for collectors. Enjoy them later if you like the art.
- **A playmat.** The tournament rules say playmats are not required, and your starter's playsheet does the same job.

One thing not to skip: **card sleeves**, once you plan to play at a store. The tournament rules require opaque sleeves on your deck.

## Learn it free first

Bandai has an official [Teaching App](https://app.onepiece-cardgame.com/en/) that walks you through a practice game, in your browser or on your phone, and a [How to Play page](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/beginners/how-to-play.php) with a video and a quick beginner's guide. Try a few games there, and you will walk into the store already knowing the turn.
