You know the rules, the math, the event policies, and how to pick a deck. The last step is practice that matches what you will face: one deck, a clock, a full room, and no notes. This lesson gives you six weeks to get there.

Choose your target first. A Store Championship is the natural first competitive event, at Bandai's Level 2. A Regional, at Level 3, is a bigger day. Both appear on Bandai's [events page](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/events/), and store events also show up in [Bandai TCG+](https://lp.bandai-tcg-plus.com/en/).

## The four parts of every week

- **Volume.** Games with your event deck. Ten a week is a good target; more is better.
- **Clock.** At least some games played on a timer, the way the event runs them.
- **Drills.** Ten-minute exercises on one skill.
- **Review.** A few minutes after every loss, and a longer look at the end of the week.

## Week 1: lock the deck

Decide on your deck, using the metagame lesson. Play ten games with it against anything. After each game, note one card that did its job and one that did not. At the end of the week, make no more than three changes.

**Drill:** shuffle and present your deck ten times with a timer running. A tidy, visible shuffle saves time and avoids trouble at a strict event.

## Week 2: the clock

Play every game on your event's clock: 30 minutes is the manual's standard for qualifying rounds, and Bandai's Treasure Cup pages set 35. Check your event's page. When time runs out, play the end of round procedure from the tournament rules lesson: finish the turn, three more turns, then Life, deck, and Characters.

**Drill:** play five turns as fast as you can while still announcing every action out loud. Routine turns should take seconds, so the hard turns get the minutes.

## Week 3: matchups

List the three Leaders you expect to face most. Find a friend who will play them, or borrow the decks. Play at least three games against each, going first in some and second in others.

**Drill:** attack math. Set up a board from one of those matchups and count lethal: hits needed, attacks available, the Counter each attack forces. Then change one card and count again.

## Week 4: the store

Play a Store Tournament or two with your event deck. You get a room of opponents you did not choose, a real clock, and nerves that do not show up at a kitchen table.

**Drill:** before each round, run your pre-game checklist: deck count, sleeves, Leader, 10 DON!!, choice of first or second ready.

## Week 5: the list

Write your deck list now, with each card's collector number and full name, as the manual requires. Count it twice. Then count your sleeved deck against it. Replace any sleeves that look worn.

Keep playing ten games a week, but stop changing cards unless something is clearly broken. Familiarity is worth more than a small upgrade now.

**Drill:** the defense count. At the start of your opponent's turn, say your Life, your Counter cards in hand, and your Blockers before they attack.

## Week 6: taper

Play fewer games, and make them clean ones. Reread the parts of the Tournament Rules Manual that cover your event. Check the event page for start time, format, and what to bring. Sleep well the night before.

**Drill:** none. Arrive rested.

## Reviewing a loss

The most useful habit in this plan. After each loss, answer three questions:

1. **Which turn did the game turn?** Not the last turn; the one where it started going wrong.
2. **What did I know then?** Hand size, Life, active DON!!, the trash.
3. **What would I do now?** One different choice, said in one sentence.

At the end of each week, look for the same answer showing up twice. That is your next thing to practice.

## Keep it a game

Six weeks is a lot of One Piece. If it stops being fun, cut the volume and keep the reviews. The goal is to walk in prepared, not worn out, and to enjoy a full day of games with people who love the same thing you do.
