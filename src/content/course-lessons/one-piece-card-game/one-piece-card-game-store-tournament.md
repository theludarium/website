Walking into your first card store event can feel like walking into someone else's living room. Everyone seems to know each other, and the words fly fast. The good news: the One Piece Card Game's entry-level event is designed for exactly your situation, and the rules behind it expect you to be learning.

## What is a Store Tournament?

**Store Tournaments** are Bandai's regular official events at local stores. They run in seasons through the year, and each season comes with its own prize packs. For example, Store Tournament 2026 Vol. 4 runs from October 1 to December 31, 2026, with stores choosing their own dates inside that window.

Bandai's tournament rules sort events into three levels. Store events are **Level 1, Casual**, described as a place focused on learning and fun. Rules still apply, but staff are expected to explain mistakes so players learn the right play, not just penalize them.

If even that sounds like a lot, look for **Pirates Party** events. Bandai describes them as more casual than Store Tournaments and easy for anyone to join, with a promo card for taking part.

## How do you find one?

Store events run through **[Bandai TCG+](https://lp.bandai-tcg-plus.com/en/)**, Bandai's app for iPhone and Android.

1. Make a Bandai Namco ID. TCG+ requires one to register.
2. Download Bandai TCG+ and set the game title to ONE PIECE CARD GAME.
3. Search the store list for stores near you, and look for their Store Tournament events.
4. Apply for the event in the app.
5. Contact the store as well. Each store manages its own entries, so confirm your spot directly.

TCG+ also shows your pairings and results on the day, so keep it on your phone.

## What to bring

From Bandai's tournament rules, for a constructed event:

- **A legal deck.** 1 Leader, a 50-card deck in your Leader's colors with no more than 4 of any card number, and a 10-card DON!! deck. A recent starter deck qualifies as it comes. Older ones can hold cards that have left the Standard format (lesson 5).
- **Opaque sleeves** on all 50 deck cards, all identical. Your Leader and DON!! cards must be sleeved or unsleeved so they look different from your deck.
- **Spare sleeves**, in case one splits.
- **Dice or counters** if your deck needs them to track power or effects. Dice must be clearly marked and unweighted.
- **Your TCG+ account** on your phone, any entry fee, and photo ID in case it is asked for.
- **A pen**, for any result slips.

In North America, your Leader and deck must be English-language cards. Real cards only: photocopies and other unofficial cards are not allowed.

## How the event runs

Each match is a single game, best of one, with **35 minutes** on the clock and **5 minutes** of extra time. If time runs out mid-game, the tournament rules add a few extra turns to settle it. Stores pick the structure that suits their community: Swiss rounds, where you keep playing and face people with a similar record, or single elimination.

At the start of each game:

1. Shuffle your deck well, then hand it to your opponent to cut or shuffle.
2. Decide who chooses first or second at random: a die, a coin, or rock-paper-scissors.
3. Draw, redraw if you want, set out Life cards, and play.

Report your result in the app or to staff. At the end, everyone who took part gets a tournament pack, and the winner gets a winner pack.

## Table etiquette

- **Say what you do.** "Attacking your Leader with my Leader, two DON!! on it, 7000." Clear words prevent nearly every argument.
- **Let your opponent answer.** After an attack, pause for Blocker and Counter before moving on.
- **Ask staff right away.** If you are unsure of a rule, raise your hand before the game moves on. Bandai's event pages warn that a mistake often cannot be fixed once the battle has gone further.
- **Ask before you touch.** Ask before picking up an opponent's card to read it.
- **No notes, no phone.** Bandai's rules do not allow players to take notes or use a phone during a match.
- **Keep your area tidy.** Lay your cards out as the official playsheet shows, which the tournament rules require. Spread your Life cards so anyone can count them at a glance, keep given DON!! visible under their cards, and keep the trash in one pile.
- **Shake hands, or nod, and thank them.** Win or lose, the person across from you is the reason you came.

## Your first night, set realistic goals

You will probably lose more games than you win. That is normal, and it is also the fastest way to learn the game. Count something else as success: finishing every round, learning two new cards, and leaving with one person you would like to play again.
