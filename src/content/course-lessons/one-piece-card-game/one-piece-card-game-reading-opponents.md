Your opponent's hand is hidden, but very little else is. The One Piece Card Game puts nearly everything on the table, and the tournament rules make a lot of it public by name. A player who reads that information well seems to know what is coming. They do not. They have just counted.

## What is public

Bandai's Tournament Rules Manual lists what counts as public knowledge. Your opponent must answer truthfully if you ask about any of it:

- How many cards are in their hand.
- How many cards are in their deck and their DON!! deck.
- The number and names of the cards in their trash.
- How many Life cards they have left.
- The printed text of any card named or clearly described.
- Which cards have been played this turn.

What is private: the cards in their hand, their deck, and their Life, and what was played last turn unless an effect still shows it. Do not ask about those, and do not answer about your own. Asking to read a card on the table is always fine; ask before you pick it up.

## Their DON!! say a lot

From the DON!! lesson at Level 1: rested DON!! stay rested until their owner's next Refresh, and active DON!! stay active through your turn.

So at the end of your opponent's turn, look at their cost area.

- **No active DON!!** means that on your turn they cannot pay for a [Counter] Event that costs DON!!, or any other defense that needs DON!!. Their defense this turn is Blockers and Counter values from hand.
- **Some active DON!!** that they could have spent means one of three things: they had nothing to spend it on, they are holding a [Counter] Event, or they want you to think so.

You will not always know which. But a player who usually spends everything and suddenly leaves 2 standing is telling you something. Count that cost into your attack math.

## Their trash is a list

The trash is open, and you can look through it. Two uses:

- **Copies used.** A deck can run up to 4 of a card number. If 3 copies of their best Counter Event are in the trash, there is at most 1 left.
- **Their deck's shape.** By midgame the trash shows which Events, Characters, and Counters they play. Even if you do not know the Leader well, the trash teaches you the deck.

## Their hand, by behavior

Hand size is public. What they do with it is a clue.

- **Countering hard at high Life.** Spending two cards to stop an early Leader hit costs more than the hit. A player who does it is either protecting something important or has cards to spare.
- **Not countering a Character they need.** Either the hand is thin on Counters, or it is full of cards they would rather play.
- **Holding cards with DON!! to spare.** If they had the DON!! and did not play a Character, those cards may be Counters, Events, or cards waiting for a better turn.
- **Playing out their whole hand.** That is a player who either expects to win soon or has nothing to protect. Check their Life before you decide which.

Treat each read as a guess with odds, not a fact. Stack two or three clues before you bet a game on one.

## Their Leader sets the frame

Every card in their deck shares a color with their Leader, and many decks lean on what the Leader does. Read their Leader at the start of every game, and ask what it wants. A Leader that rewards having many Characters wants a wide board. One that gains power with DON!! wants to keep its DON!! around. That narrows what the hidden cards are likely to be.

## Fair reading

Reading your opponent is skill, not trickery. Bandai's rules require players to give accurate information and to answer questions about public knowledge truthfully. Lying about public information, or giving false information about private information, can lead to disqualification. Read the board, not their face, and keep your own answers accurate too.
