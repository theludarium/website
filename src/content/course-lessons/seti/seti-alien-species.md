Two alien species wait face down on every SETI board. Nobody knows which two they are until the players find enough traces of life. This lesson explains how discovery works, from the English rulebook (October 2024) and the official FAQ, and how to compete for it. It stays spoiler-light on purpose.

## A note on spoilers

The rulebook advises reading a species' rules only when that species is discovered. Part of the fun of SETI is the moment a board flips and the game changes under you. So this lesson names the species, since the rulebook's component list names them, but says nothing about what any of them do. If you want the full surprise, skip the next paragraph.

The base game has five: the Mascamites, the Exertians, the Anomalies, the Centaurians, and 'Oumuamua. Each game uses two at random, and the other three go back in the box unseen.

## The three kinds of life trace

Under each face-down alien board are three discovery spaces, one for each kind of life trace. The rulebook names their usual sources:

- One kind comes mostly from **scanning**, through sector rewards and some cards.
- One kind comes mostly from **landing** on planets and moons.
- One kind comes mostly from **analyzing data**.

There is also a universal trace, a wildcard that counts as any of the three.

## Marking a trace before discovery

When you earn a trace, place a marker on an empty matching discovery space under either alien board. Each discovery space pays **1 publicity and 5 points** right away, and may bring a reward later when that species is discovered.

If both matching discovery spaces are taken, and neither species is discovered yet, place your marker in an **overflow space** below one species instead, for **3 points**. You may also choose an overflow space even when another space is open. Markers in overflow count as traces for that species on cards, but they earn nothing at discovery.

## Neutral markers in smaller games

With 1 to 3 players, neutral markers wait above 20 and 30 points on the score track. When anyone reaches or passes one of those scores, a nonplayer research group moves a neutral marker into the leftmost empty discovery space, starting under the left species. Only one marker moves per crossing. Neutral markers never go on overflow spaces or on the alien boards themselves.

That means discovery comes faster at smaller player counts, and the score track sets the timing. When a rival nears 20 or 30, expect a discovery space to close.

## The moment of discovery

When all three discovery spaces under a board are filled, by players or neutral markers, the species is discovered at the end of that turn, after any milestones. Then:

1. Flip the board.
2. Read that species' rule sheet aloud.
3. Follow its setup, including the rewards for the players who marked its discovery spaces.

From then on, the species adds its own deck of alien cards, which are a little stronger than regular cards, and new spaces on its board. Marking a trace there works like a discovery space: choose any empty space of the matching color and take its reward. Some species also add a step to final scoring.

## Racing for discovery

**Early traces are worth the most.** A discovery space pays 5 points and 1 publicity, plus whatever the species gives at discovery. An overflow space pays 3. The gap closes once the six discovery spaces fill.

**Spread across both species.** Discovery rewards go to the players on that board's discovery spaces. A marker under each board gives you a stake in both reveals.

**Balance the three kinds.** One gold tile face scores a set for each group of three different traces you marked, overflow included. Steady scanning, landing, and analyzing feed all three.

**Time a discovery.** You often choose which board gets your trace. Completing a board on your own turn means the discovery happens when you are ready, not when a rival is.

## Teaching a discovery

When a board flips, pause the game. Have one player read the rule sheet aloud while everyone looks at the new board. It takes a few minutes and keeps the surprise fair for the whole table.
