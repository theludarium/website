SETI is a game of many small scores: 3 points here, 2 there, 5 for a discovery space. The gold tiles are where those habits turn into big end-game numbers. This lesson covers the scoring rules from the English rulebook (October 2024) and the official FAQ, then how to choose your tiles.

## Points during play

Most points arrive before the end. A partial list:

- 3 points for the first orbiter at a planet.
- Printed points for every landing on a planet.
- 2 points for marking the second position in a sector's data slot.
- Sector win rewards.
- 2 points for the first tech from each stack.
- 2 points for data on the upper space of a computer tech.
- 5 points and 1 publicity for each discovery space; 3 points for an overflow space.
- Card effects and completed missions.

Your starting score also matters a little: the first player starts at 1, the second at 2, and so on.

## Gold milestones

At the end of a turn in which your score reaches or passes **25, 50, or 70**, you mark one of the four gold scoring tiles. The first player to choose a tile takes its highest value, the second takes the next, and so on. There is room for everyone on every tile.

You can never mark the same tile twice, so a player who passes 70 ends with markers on three tiles. If several players cross milestones on the same turn, the active player chooses first, then the others clockwise. Passing 100 points does not trigger the milestones again.

## The four tiles, both faces

Each tile is double sided, and setup puts a random face up. At the end, you score your marker's value **once per set**.

**Tech**
- One face: each set of one probe, one telescope, and one computer tech. In other words, count the type you have least of.
- Other face: each pair of techs. Five techs make two pairs.

**Mission**
- One face: each completed mission, conditional or triggerable.
- Other face: each pair made of completed missions and end-of-game scoring cards, in any mix. The FAQ adds that end-of-game cards count even if they scored 0.

**Income**
- One face: each set of three tucked cards, one of each type: credit, energy, card.
- Other face: the number of tucked cards of your most common type, credit or energy.
- On both faces, only tucked cards count, not what is printed on the starting income card.

**Other**
- One face: each set of three life traces, one of each kind. Overflow markers count.
- Other face: each sector win paired with an orbiter or lander. Count the smaller of the two numbers. Repeat wins at one star count, and landers on moons count.

## How to choose

Do the math as value times sets. A tile where you take the top value but make two sets can lose to a tile where you take a lower value and make five.

**Claim the tile you already lead.** At 25 points you can usually see your engine. A player with three tucked credits should look hard at the income tile's credit or energy face; a player with techs in all three colors should look at the tech tile.

**Take value before rivals do.** Since the first marker on a tile gets its biggest value, reaching 25 first is a real edge. If two players want the same tile, the one who crosses first wins that race.

**Plan the second and third.** Your tiles should reward the same play. Tech pairs and sector wins plus orbiters both reward a probe and tech plan. Mission pairs and three-type income both reward card play.

**Deny when it is close.** Taking a rival's best tile does not stop them from marking it. It only moves them down one value. Denial rarely beats your own math.

## End-of-game scoring

Once the last player passes in round 5:

1. Score end-of-game scoring cards, the ones with gold boxes.
2. Score gold tiles.
3. Score any extra step a discovered species adds.

Most points wins. If players tie, the rulebook says they have earned that tie, and it stands.

## A scoring habit

Each time you claim a tile, write down its sets so far. At the start of round 5, count again and spend your last turns on the cheapest set you can finish.
