Every probe in SETI ends its trip the same way: it leaves the solar system board and becomes an orbiter or a lander on the planetary board. That choice, made a dozen times a game, shapes your whole economy. This lesson lays out the costs from the English rulebook (October 2024) and the official FAQ, then the reasoning behind each choice.

## Launching

Launching costs 2 credits and puts a probe on Earth. By default you can have only one probe in space, meaning anywhere on the solar system board. Orbiters and landers do not count toward that limit, so turning a probe into one frees you to launch again.

One probe tech raises the limit to two, and its immediate bonus is a free launch. Even with it, the Launch action still places one probe at a time. A few cards, such as Falcon Heavy, ignore the limit for their own launches only.

Movement is a free action: each energy moves a probe one space, and some cards can be discarded for a move. Leaving asteroids costs an extra move. Moving onto a space with a publicity icon gains 1 publicity, every time, even twice in one turn.

## Orbiting

**Cost:** 1 credit and 1 energy, as a main action, at any planet except Earth.

**Pays:** the bonuses printed above the planet, which usually include an income increase. The first orbiter at each planet also scores 3 points and covers that space. There is no limit on orbiters.

An orbiter also helps everyone who lands later: any orbiter at a planet, yours or a rival's, cuts the landing cost from 3 energy to 2.

## Landing

**Cost:** 3 energy, or 2 if any orbiter is already at that planet, as a main action. A probe tech reduces that by 1 more.

**Pays:** the bonuses in the middle of the planet, which always include a life trace and some points. The first lander on each planet also gains data and covers that space. Mars has two first-lander spaces. There is no limit on landers on a planet.

**Moons.** You can land on a moon only with the probe tech that allows it, or a card effect. It costs the same as landing on its planet, discounts included, and each moon holds just one lander. Moons give their own printed rewards.

## Orbit or land?

Compare them on what they cost and what they give.

| | Orbit | Land |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | 1 credit, 1 energy | 2 or 3 energy |
| Main reward | Planet bonus, usually income | Points and a life trace |
| First-player bonus | 3 points | Data |
| Helps rivals? | Yes, cheaper landing | No |

A few patterns follow from the table.

**Orbit early.** Income compounds. An income increase in round 1 or 2 pays out three or four more times. Points from a landing are the same in round 5 as in round 1.

**Land where someone already orbits.** Paying 2 energy instead of 3 is a third off. If a rival has orbited a planet, that discount is yours too.

**Be first when it is close.** The 3-point first orbiter bonus and the first-lander data go to whoever arrives first. If two probes are heading for the same planet, the first-arrival reward often decides who should commit.

**Count life traces.** Landing is the main way to earn one of the three kinds of life trace. Discovery spaces under the alien boards pay 1 publicity and 5 points each, so a lander that claims one is worth far more than its printed points. Once those spaces fill, extra traces still score 3 points in an overflow space.

**Watch the gold tiles.** One gold tile side scores a set for each sector win paired with an orbiter or lander. Another scores income card sets. If you have claimed one of those, its count changes your answer.

## Probe tempo

A full probe cycle, launch, travel, and orbit or land, takes at least two main actions and often three. Rotation pushes and cards with move corners are the cheapest travel. A second probe in space, from the probe-limit tech, lets you launch while the first is still flying, which shortens the gap between arrivals.

Do not leave a probe drifting. A probe in space scores nothing until it becomes an orbiter or lander, and it blocks your next launch.
