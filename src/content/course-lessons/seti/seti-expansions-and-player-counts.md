You have learned the rules, the board, the economy, discovery, scoring, and the solo rival. This last lesson looks at the wider game: how SETI shifts with the number of players, what the Space Agencies expansion adds, and how to keep improving once there is no rulebook left to learn.

## How player count changes SETI

The core rules never change, but a few setup numbers do, and they shift the feel.

**Four players.** No neutral milestones, so all six discovery spaces must be filled by players. Discovery tends to come later and depends on the table. Sector races get crowded. Each end-of-round stack holds 5 cards. Expect a long game, at about 40 minutes per player.

**Three players.** One neutral marker sits above 20 and one above 30. Discovery gets a push as scores climb. This is a comfortable middle count for learning.

**Two players.** Two neutral markers above each of 20 and 30, so as many as four discovery spaces can be filled by nonplayer research groups. Discovery comes sooner, and you will often have the sectors to yourselves to fight over. Each end-of-round stack holds 3 cards.

**Rotation at every count.** Every count gets one first-pass rotation per round, plus one per tech researched. More players usually means more techs taken, so the board turns more often at a full table.

## The base game

If you are buying, [SETI: Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CZBCRC3S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) holds everything in this course: the solar system, all five alien species, and the solo mode.

## SETI: Space Agencies

Space Agencies is the first expansion, also designed by Tomáš Holek. The [CGE Space Agencies page](https://www.czechgames.com/games/seti-space-agencies) lists a Q4 2025 release and has the English rulebook. It still plays 1 to 4.

According to the expansion rulebook, it adds:

- **11 asymmetric organizations**, each with its own powers, starting setup, and income. They replace the starting income cards.
- **21 quick start cards** that set up orbiters, traces, signals, and resources before play begins.
- **Three new alien species**, mixed with the original five, still two per game.
- **42 new cards** for the main deck, and signal tokens awarded by some new effects.
- **Solo additions**: new rival action cards and long-term objectives.

Games with organizations are **four rounds long**: you play only rounds 2 to 5, and everyone starts at 0 publicity. The rulebook suggests a first game with organizations 1 to 4, which are less complex. The quick start cards can also be used as an optional module without organizations, to vary the opening.

New cards and species can go into any game, so even groups who skip the organizations get more variety.

## Where to play

SETI has no official organized play or tournament circuit that we could find, so this course stops at mastery rather than a championship. For opponents, try BoardGameGeek's SETI forums for local groups, your local game store's open game nights, and the [Indianapolis lounge list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

We found no official online version of SETI open to the public, so this course does not point to one.

## A six-week practice plan

1. **Week 1: rules check.** Play a game with the [rules mistakes lesson](/academy/seti/seti-rules-mistakes/) open, and fix every slip.
2. **Week 2: economy.** Track resources spent and income each round, as in the [income lesson](/academy/seti/seti-income-and-economy/).
3. **Week 3: the board.** Call every rotation before it happens, and use at least one push on purpose.
4. **Week 4: sectors.** Win three sectors in one game, each by planning the final signal.
5. **Week 5: gold tiles.** Pick a tile at setup and score at least four sets on it.
6. **Week 6: the rival.** Beat the solo rival one level above your comfort, then try Space Agencies organizations.

## Mastery is a habit

Strong SETI play comes down to a few habits every turn: count resources, read the rotation counter, check the sectors one signal from done, and keep the gold tiles in mind. None of that needs a rulebook. It needs repetition, and five rounds of a good game is the best kind.
