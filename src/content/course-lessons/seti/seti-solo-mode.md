SETI's solo game pits you against a rival research institution run by a small deck of action cards. Everything it needs comes in the [base game box](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CZBCRC3S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). It uses the full multiplayer rules for you and simple priority rules for the rival. This lesson follows the solo section of the English rulebook (October 2024) and the solo answers in the official FAQ.

## Setup

Set up a normal two-player game, then:

- **Choose a difficulty.** There are five, shown by gold stars on the back of each rival board. The 1-star and 2-star levels share a board. The rulebook suggests 1 star for your first game, because it has no objectives.
- **Give the rival a color.** Put its progress marker on the starting space of its progress track. Both sides start at 4 publicity, and the first player starts at 1 point as usual.
- **Build the rival deck.** Shuffle the four basic action cards. At 3 stars or higher, add one random advanced action card. Keep the other advanced cards nearby, and set aside each species' special action card.
- **Build the objectives stack** (not at 1 star). The rival board shows how many level I, II, and III objectives to use. Stack them with level III at the bottom and level I on top, then reveal the top three.

Neutral milestones work as in a two-player game: two markers each at 20 and 30.

## How the rival takes turns

On its turn, reveal the top card of the rival deck. The rival does the first action on that card it can, top to bottom. There is always one it can do. When it starts a turn with no cards left, it passes: shuffle its played cards into a new deck for next round, and it takes the top end-of-round card. If it passed first, rotate the solar system.

The rival's actions mirror yours in simple forms. It launches, flies to the first reachable planet on a list, lands or orbits, scans by priority, analyzes when its computer is full, and researches techs. A decision arrow on each card breaks ties left or right.

## The rival's economy

The rival does not track credits, energy, or cards. Instead, every credit, energy, or card it would gain advances its **progress marker** one space, and an income increase advances it four. Crossing certain spaces on the track adds a random advanced action card to the top of its deck, so it gets stronger as it gains.

It spends publicity on techs at 6, like you, preferring the tech shown at its progress position if that stack still has its 2-point tile. It does not use tech powers; it discards techs later to boost certain actions.

The rival also hits gold milestones. It always takes the first, most valuable space on a tile, but never scores gold tiles. If every first space is taken, it claims nothing.

## Objectives, the heart of solo

From 2 stars up, you always have three objectives in front of you. Each has tasks, often the same icons as triggerable missions: visit a comet, reach 9 publicity, hold 5 data in your pool. When all of an objective's tasks are marked, it is complete; at the end of your turn, move it to your completed pile and reveal a new one.

At the end of rounds 1 to 4, before income, **spend completed objectives equal to the round number**: 1, then 2, then 3, then 4. That is 10 across the game. For each one you cannot spend, the rival advances **3 spaces** on its progress track.

At the end of round 5, the rival scores **5 points for every objective** you did not complete, in the row and still in the stack.

## Beating the rival

**Pace objectives, not points.** Falling short at the end of round 1 or 2 speeds the rival up through its progress track, which brings advanced cards sooner. An early shortfall costs you all game.

**Read the next three tasks before every turn.** Many tasks reward a move you were going to make anyway. Fold them into your plan, such as a comet on the way to a planet.

**Take first places early.** The rival grabs the top space on gold tiles and blocks first-orbiter and first-lander bonuses like any player. Racing it to 25 points keeps those values for you.

**Use your two-player rules.** The neutral milestones at 20 and 30 still fill discovery spaces when either of you crosses them, so plan your own traces before those spaces close.

## Climbing the stars

Move up one level when you win comfortably twice in a row. Two steps change the game most: 2 stars adds objectives, and 3 stars gives the rival an advanced card from the start. The 4-star example in the rulebook uses 2 level I, 6 level II, and 7 level III objectives, so the stack also gets steeper at the top.
