The rotating solar system is the first thing anyone notices about SETI, and the first thing new players treat as weather: something that happens to them. It is closer to a clock. It turns at known moments, in a known order, by a known amount. This lesson explains the rules from the English rulebook (October 2024), then how to use them.

## What triggers a rotation?

Only two things:

1. **Researching a tech.** Every time, whether it is your Research action or a card that grants a tech. You rotate before you choose the tech. A card that shows both a rotation icon and a tech rotates once, not twice.
2. **The first pass of each round.** The first player to pass rotates the system, in every round including round 5. The rotation token sitting on the end-of-round stack is the reminder.

Those are the two triggers the rulebook gives. That means at least five rotations a game, plus one for every tech anyone takes.

## Which disc turns?

There are three stacked discs over the solar system board. The rotation counter on the tech board shows which one is next:

- **First rotation:** turn disc 1, the top disc.
- **Second:** turn disc 2. Disc 1 sits on it and turns with it.
- **Third:** turn disc 3. The other two ride along.
- Then back to disc 1.

Every rotation is one sector counterclockwise. Advance the counter each time.

So anything on disc 1 moves with every rotation, anything on disc 2 moves two times in three, and anything on disc 3 moves only once in three. That rhythm is worth learning: look at which disc a target sits on, and you know how often it drifts.

## What happens to probes?

Probes on the discs ride along with them. The discs also have cutouts, which creates two special cases:

- A probe sitting in a gap may **stay put** while the planets in its ring move past it.
- A probe in a cutout may be **pushed** by a turning disc into the next space.

A push is free movement. If the space it lands on shows a publicity icon, the owner gains that publicity at once, even on another player's turn. The FAQ on the back page of the rulebook confirms this for comets.

## Why sectors care

The board is divided into eight sectors, each lined up with one nearby star at the edge. Many effects mark a signal "in the sector with" something: the basic Scan action marks one in Earth's sector, and cards and techs point at sectors with Mercury or with your probes. When a disc carrying those objects turns, the sector they point to changes. A rotation can move your Scan target from a star you are about to win to one you have never touched.

## Using rotation on purpose

**Read the counter before you plan a trip.** If a rotation is likely before your probe arrives, plan against the board as it will be, not as it is. A target three moves away can become two, or five.

**Research to move first, then fly.** Since you rotate before choosing a tech, a Research action can swing a planet toward your probe. A free move from a push, plus a tech, is a strong turn.

**Pass first to control the turn.** If you are ready to pass anyway, being first means you choose when that round's rotation happens. Use it to push your probe onto a comet, to shift Earth's sector toward a star you are building in, or to pull a planet away from a rival's waiting probe.

**Watch other players' publicity.** A rival at 6 or more publicity can research and rotate on their turn. If your plan depends on the board staying still, finish it now or plan for both versions.

**Count to the end of the round.** If two players are close to passing, the next rotation is coming soon. Moves that pay off only after that rotation are often better saved.

## A note on setup

Every game starts from a random layout, either from CGE's free [solar system app](https://seti-solarsystem.czechgames.com) or by shuffling the discs and sector boards yourself. The discs must line up into eight clean sectors with one star in each. Because the starting layout changes, rote openings do not exist; read each board fresh.
