[SETI](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CZBCRC3S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) looks like a lot on the table. There is a solar system with three turning discs, eight stars ringed with data, a tech board with 12 stacks, two mystery boards, and cards covered in icons. A host who explains every icon before the first turn loses the room.

The core loop is small, though: take one main action, pay its cost, and add free actions around it. This script teaches that loop first. Print the [SETI teach sheet](/academy/seti/teach-sheet/) and put it in the middle of the table, and keep the [how to play lesson](/academy/seti/how-to-play-seti/) nearby for the details.

## Before anyone sits down

Do the whole setup in advance, including the random solar system and the data on every star. Hand out player boards, starting income cards, resources, and five cards. Leave the tucking choice for the teach, since nobody can make it yet.

Keep the five alien rule sheets in the box. The rulebook's advice is to read a species' rules only when it is discovered, and that is half the fun of the game.

## Minute 1: the goal

"We each run a space research institute. Over five rounds we explore the solar system, scan nearby stars, and analyze data. Somewhere out there are two alien species, face down on this board. Whoever finds the most science, measured in points, wins."

## Minutes 2 to 4: three resources

Hold up each one.

- **Credits** are money. Launching and scanning cost credits, and so do most cards.
- **Energy** powers everything that moves or computes: probe moves, landing, scanning, analyzing.
- **Publicity** is the track by the solar system. It caps at 10. Six buys a tech; three buys a card.

Then the income card: "At the end of each round you get what's printed here, plus one resource for every card you tuck underneath." Now let each player tuck one card, and point out that the resource in the card's bottom-right corner arrives right away too.

## Minutes 5 to 9: eight actions

Point to the main actions on the player board and walk them in pairs.

1. **Launch and move.** Two credits puts a probe on Earth. Each energy moves it one space. Asteroids cost an extra move to leave.
2. **Orbit or land.** At a planet, orbiting is cheap and usually grows your income. Landing costs more energy but gives points and a life trace. An orbiter already there makes landing cheaper, whoever owns it.
3. **Scan and analyze.** Scanning marks at least two signals at the stars and pulls data into your pool. Fill the six top spaces of your computer, then analyze to mark a life trace.
4. **Cards and techs.** Play a card for its effect, or spend six publicity on a tech that upgrades your probes, telescopes, or computer.

The eighth is passing. Save it for the end of the teach.

## Minutes 10 to 12: one probe's trip

Show a real turn. Launch, spend two energy to reach a planet, and say "next turn I could orbit here for one credit and one energy." Point out any publicity picked up on the way. This one example teaches movement, costs, and the planetary board at once.

Then show rotation: "When anyone researches a tech, or when the first player passes each round, a disc turns. Top, then middle, then bottom." Turn the top disc once and turn it back so they see the planets move.

## Minutes 13 to 15: passing and the end

"When you are done for the round, pass. Discard to four cards. The first to pass turns the solar system. Then take a card from this round's stack; earlier passers get more choice. After round 5, we score these four gold tiles and any end-of-game cards."

Mention the gold tiles in one line: "When you hit 25, 50, and 70 points, you claim one. First on a tile gets the biggest number."

## What to leave out

- Alien species rules. Read them aloud only when a species is discovered.
- The details of sector wins. Explain them the first time a sector fills.
- Each tech's power. Read a tech when someone researches it.
- Mission card wording. Read each one when it is played.

## Coaching the first round

Expect quiet early turns while people read cards. Nudge each player to try one probe action and one scan in round 1, so they see both halves of the game. Remind the table that free actions happen only on your own turn, and that milestones resolve between turns.

The [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) article has general habits that help here too: teach the goal first, keep it short, and let the game do the rest.
