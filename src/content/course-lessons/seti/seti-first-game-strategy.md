New players often spend their first round reading cards and their last round wishing they had more energy. A loose plan fixes both. This one is not the only way to win, and it will not beat an experienced player on its own. It is a sturdy first-game frame that teaches you how the game's money works.

## Count your starting budget

You begin with 4 credits, 3 energy, 4 publicity, and 5 cards. That is not much. Here is one probe's trip, priced from the rulebook:

| Step | Credits | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch a probe | 2 | 0 |
| Move two spaces | 0 | 2 |
| Orbit the planet | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **3** | **3** |

That trip spends almost everything, over two or three turns. A single Scan costs 1 credit and 2 energy. Round 1 is short on purpose. From round 2 you collect income at the end of every round: 3 credits, 2 energy, and 1 card printed on your income card, plus one resource for every card you have tucked.

## Opening hand: what to tuck

At setup you tuck one card. Look at your hand and ask which resource your likely first moves need most. Probe-heavy plans burn energy. Scanning burns both, but energy faster. Cards are flexible because they can become anything through the 2-for-1 trade, but they are slower.

Keep cards whose main effect you can afford in round 1 or 2. A strong card you cannot pay for until round 4 is often better as income now.

## Rounds 1 and 2: build

CGE's own FAQ says that early in the game, a card's effect is rarely worth skipping an income increase. Take that seriously. Good round 1 and 2 targets:

- **An orbiter.** Orbiting is cheap, the first orbiter at a planet scores 3 points, and the bonuses above most planets include an income increase.
- **The computer's early spaces.** Covering the second space gains 1 publicity, and covering the fourth lets you tuck a card for income. Any scan that fills your data pool feeds these.
- **A first tech.** You start at 4 publicity and need 6. Moving onto comets and many planets gains 1 publicity each, and every player who helps complete a sector gains 1. The first tech from any stack also scores its 2-point tile, and a tech researched in round 2 works for four rounds.

## Round 3: turn the corner

By round 3 your income should cover at least one meaningful action per turn without trades. Now start aiming for points:

- **Land** where an orbiter already sits, to pay 2 energy instead of 3, and take the life trace.
- **Fill your computer** and analyze for a life trace of the third kind.
- **Look at the gold tiles.** You claim your first at 25 points, and the first player on a tile takes its biggest value. Pick one that matches what you are already doing.

## Rounds 4 and 5: cash in

Stop building income in round 5. No income comes after the last round, so a round 5 tuck gives only the resource you gain the moment you tuck. The FAQ notes that skipping an income increase in the last round can be the right call.

Spend everything. Leftover credits, energy, and data score nothing. Discard cards for their corner effects before you pass, since you drop to 4 cards when passing anyway.

## When to pass

Passing early has two rewards. The first player to pass rotates the solar system, which you can use to push your own probe or move a planet away from a rival. And each end-of-round stack has one more card than there are players, so earlier passers choose from more cards. The cost is the turns you give up.

Pass when your next action would be weak or unaffordable, not when you are merely low. A turn spent trading three cards for a credit is usually a sign to pass.

## A first-game checklist

- Tuck at every chance in rounds 1 and 2.
- Get one orbiter and one tech by the end of round 2.
- Mark at least one life trace of each of the three kinds.
- Claim a gold tile at 25 that fits your game.
- Spend everything in round 5.
