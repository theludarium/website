SETI is a game about running out. Sooner or later you will find the perfect move and be one energy short. The fix is not a better move. It is an economy built for the moves you plan to make. This lesson covers the rules from the English rulebook (October 2024) and the official FAQ, then how to shape your income.

## What each resource buys

| Resource | Main uses |
| --- | --- |
| Credits | Launch (2), Orbit (1), Scan (1), most card costs |
| Energy | Each move (1), Orbit (1), Land (3, or 2), Scan (2), Analyze (1) |
| Publicity | A tech (6), a card (3), some telescope tech costs |
| Cards | Main effects, free-action corners, signals, income |

Energy shows up on almost every line, which is why it runs out first for so many players. A turn that moves two spaces and lands costs 4 or 5 energy.

## How income works

From round 2 on, at the end of each round you collect the 3 credits, 2 energy, and 1 random card printed on your starting income card, plus one resource for every card tucked beneath it. A card's income type, credit, energy, or card, is printed on the card.

You tuck a card whenever an effect gives you an income increase. The main sources:

- **Setup**, where everyone tucks one card.
- **Orbiting**, since most planets' orbit bonuses include an income increase.
- **The fourth space of your computer**, covered when you place data.
- **Cards and other effects** that show the income icon.

When you tuck, you gain that resource immediately, too. The FAQ adds that you may decline to tuck, which can be right in the last round.

## Shape your income

**Match income to your plan.** A probe-heavy plan needs energy. A scanner needs energy and some credits. A card-heavy plan needs credits for card costs. Look at the card in your hand you most want to play later and count what it will cost.

**Early tucks pay most.** A tuck in round 1 pays four more times. A tuck in round 4 pays once. A round 5 tuck gives only the instant resource.

**Watch the gold tiles.** The income tile scores either sets of all three tucked types or your largest single tucked type of credits or energy, depending on its face. The cards printed on the starting income card never count; only the tucked ones do. If you plan to claim it, that changes which cards you tuck.

## Publicity, the third currency

You start at 4 publicity, and the track stops at 10. Anything that would push you past 10 is wasted. The common sources:

- Moving onto a space with a publicity icon, such as a comet.
- Being part of a completed sector, winner or not.
- Covering the second space of your computer.
- Claiming a discovery space under an alien board.
- Many cards, including some free-action corners.

Spending it is just as simple: 6 for a tech, 3 for a card, and some telescope tech activations. If you are sitting at 9 and about to gain more, spend first.

## Trades and card corners

As a free action you can trade two of one kind, cards, credits, or energy, for one of any of those. It is a poor rate, but it saves a turn. Every card can also be discarded for the free action in its top-left corner, and many give a move, publicity, or data.

Before you pass, remember you will discard down to 4 cards. Any card you would lose there is better spent on its corner.

## Techs as economy

Researching costs 6 publicity, rotates the solar system first, and gives a tech tile with an immediate bonus. The first player to take from each of the 12 stacks also scores its 2-point tile. You can own each tech only once.

Techs are long-term income in disguise. A landing discount saves an energy on every landing for the rest of the game. A second probe slot speeds every trip. A computer tech adds 2-point spaces and rewards to every computer fill. The earlier you research, the more turns the tech works, and the more likely the 2-point tile is still there.

## A quick economy check

At the start of each round, ask three questions. What will my best two actions this round cost? Does my income plus what I hold cover them? If not, which tuck or trade closes the gap? A minute of planning beats a turn spent trading three cards for one energy.
