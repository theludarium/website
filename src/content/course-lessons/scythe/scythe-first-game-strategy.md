Scythe rewards a plan more than a clever turn. The player who knows which six stars they want, and which actions get them there, usually beats the player who reacts to the board. This lesson gives a first-game plan that works with any faction and any player mat, using only the rules and simple counting.

## Pick your stars before your first turn

There are ten star spaces on the Triumph Track, and you need six. Four of them come straight from your bottom-row actions:

| Star | Bottom action | Times needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All six upgrades | Upgrade | 6 |
| All four mechs | Deploy | 4 |
| All four structures | Build | 4 |
| All four recruits | Enlist | 4 |

The others are all eight workers on the board, one objective, up to two combat wins, 18 popularity, and 16 power.

Look at your player mat. Which bottom-row actions are cheapest? Which sit under a top action you will want often? Those are your first stars. Look at your objectives too: one of them may line up with what you already plan to do.

A common first-game plan is mechs, recruits, workers, one objective, and either upgrades or structures, plus one of power or popularity near the end. It avoids depending on combat, which beginners cannot plan around.

## Take two actions a turn

The fastest players in Scythe are the ones who rarely waste half a turn. Each section has a top and a bottom action, and the bottom one pays coins and moves you toward a star. A turn where you can only afford the top action is a turn behind.

The trick is to pay for the bottom action with the top action of an earlier turn. If Enlist costs food, the turn before should produce food. If Deploy costs metal, plan the Produce that makes it. Ask, every turn: "what will my next section need, and am I making it now?"

## Spread your workers

Workers produce one resource of the terrain they stand on. You start with two workers next to your home base. Early Moves should push workers onto the terrain your bottom row needs:

- **Mountains** make metal, for Deploy.
- **Forests** make wood, for Build.
- **Farms** make food, for Enlist.
- **Tundra** makes oil, for Upgrade.
- **Villages** make more workers.

Each Produce action reaches up to two territories, so it helps to stack a few workers on each of two terrains rather than one worker on four. Producing on a village adds workers, which also moves you toward the eight-worker star. Remember that Produce costs more as your mat empties, so time the big worker turns.

## Hit encounters early

Your character is the only unit that triggers encounters, and the encounter options are strong for a single action. The three options on a card often include one that raises popularity, one that pays coins for a bigger reward, and one that costs popularity for a bigger gain. Popularity is your scoring multiplier, so early encounters that raise it, or at least protect it, are worth the trip.

Send your character toward the nearest encounter tokens on your first few Moves while your workers take the other move.

## Plan your Factory visit

The first player to reach the Factory sees the most Factory cards, one more than the number of players, and each later visitor sees one fewer. Each player can take only one card. A Factory card works as a fifth section, and its bottom action is a stronger Move: one unit up to two times. Holding the Factory at the end also counts as three territories.

Do not rush it on turn two, but do not leave it until the end. A character that finishes a few encounters near the middle of the map is in a good place to arrive in the first third of the game.

## Watch the clock

The game ends the moment anyone places a sixth star. In a first game, check the Triumph Track every round. When the leader has four stars, start spreading your units across more territories and carrying resources to where you control them, because land and resources pay at the end.

## Keep popularity safe

Popularity at 7 or more moves you from the lowest scoring tier to the middle one. That raises every star, territory, and resource pair you score. A first-game rule of thumb: never let popularity fall below 7 in the last third of the game without a very good reason.
