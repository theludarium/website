Scythe looks like a war game, with giant mechs and a mysterious Factory in the middle of the map. But mostly you build an economy: produce resources, spend them on upgrades and buildings, and turn that engine into stars and coins. Fights happen, but they are rare and expensive.

This lesson walks the rules in our own words, from Stonemaier's rulebook and FAQ. The [official rules page](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/scythe/rules/) has the rulebook, quick reference guides, and a link to a free Dized tutorial.

## How do you win?

The player with the most coins at the end wins. The game ends the instant any player places their sixth star on the Triumph Track. Then everyone adds coins for stars, territories, and resources, at a rate set by their popularity.

## How do you set up?

![An abstract Scythe board at setup: the Factory in the center, five home bases around the edge, each with a character and two workers next to it, and a side panel of decks and mats](/academy/scythe/setup.svg)

1. **The board.** Put an encounter token on each of the 11 encounter territories. Shuffle the combat, encounter, and objective decks. Deal Factory cards face down, one more than the players, and return the rest unseen. Draw one structure bonus tile at random.
2. **Mats.** Deal each player one faction mat and one player mat at random. Sit by your faction's home base. Two pairs are banned: Rusviet with Industrial, and Crimea with Patriotic. If you draw one, swap the player mat (brand-new players in their first game are exempt).
3. **Faction mat.** Set your power token where the mat shows, draw the combat cards it shows, put your four mechs on their spaces, and keep your six stars handy.
4. **Player mat.** Set popularity and take coins as the mat shows. Draw two secret objective cards. Place your six technology cubes, six workers, four structures, and four recruits on their spaces.
5. **The map.** Put your character on your home base and one worker on each of the two land territories next to it.
6. **First player.** The lowest number on a player mat goes first. Play runs clockwise.

## What happens on a turn?

Your player mat has four sections. Each section pairs one **top-row action** with one **bottom-row action**. On your turn, move your action token to a section other than the one you chose last turn. Then take the top action, the bottom action, or both, top first. For each action, pay the cost shown in the red boxes, then gain the benefit in the green boxes.

That is the whole turn. The next player may even start while you finish a bottom-row action.

## The top-row actions

- **Move:** move up to two different units (your character, mechs, or workers) one territory each. Or gain $1 instead.
- **Bolster:** pay $1, then gain 2 power or draw 1 combat card.
- **Trade:** pay $1, then gain any 2 resources on a territory with your worker, or gain 1 popularity.
- **Produce:** pick up to two territories you control. Each worker there produces one of that terrain's resources. A worker on a village produces a new worker. The cost grows as workers leave your mat.

## The bottom-row actions

- **Upgrade** (pay oil): move a cube from the top row to a bottom-row cost. One top action gets stronger and one bottom action gets cheaper.
- **Deploy** (pay metal): put a mech on a territory you control with a worker. Your character and all your mechs gain that mech's ability.
- **Build** (pay wood): put a structure on a territory you control with a worker. It adds a bonus to the top action above it.
- **Enlist** (pay food): take a recruit: a one-time bonus now, and an ongoing bonus whenever you or a neighbor takes that section's bottom action.

Every bottom-row action also pays the coins printed beside it.

## Combat and encounters

Combat happens after a Move, when your character or mech shares a territory with an opponent's character or mech. Both players secretly dial up to 7 power, never more than they have, and may add one combat card per character or mech there. Highest total wins; ties go to the attacker. The loser's units there retreat home. The [combat lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-combat-power/) has the math.

When your character moves onto an encounter token, its movement stops. After any combat, draw an encounter card and pick one of its three options. The first time your character ends a Move on the Factory, look through the Factory cards left there and keep one. It works as a fifth section of your mat.

## The six stars

You place a star when you first reach a goal on the Triumph Track: all six upgrades, all four mechs, all four structures, all four recruits, all eight workers on the board, a completed objective, a combat win (up to two), 18 popularity, or 16 power. Stars never come off.

## How does scoring work?

Find your popularity tier. At 0 to 6, each star pays $3, each territory $2, and each two resources $1. At 7 to 12, it is $4, $3, and $2. At 13 to 18, it is $5, $4, and $3. The Factory counts as three territories. Add the structure bonus tile's coins and the coins in hand.
