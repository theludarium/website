In Scythe, your faction is only half of your setup. The other half is the player mat, dealt at random, and it shapes your game just as much. Two players with the same faction and different mats will play very different games. This lesson shows how to read a mat and why Stonemaier bans two pairings.

## What a player mat sets

Every base player mat has the same eight actions, but in a different arrangement. Each mat sets:

- **The pairs.** Which top-row action sits above which bottom-row action. A section you can take every other turn with both actions is gold.
- **Bottom-row costs.** How much oil, metal, wood, or food each bottom-row action costs, and how far upgrades can reduce it.
- **Bottom-row coins.** How many coins each bottom action pays.
- **Upgrade spaces.** Which top-row boxes hold cubes you can lift off, and which bottom-row cost boxes can take one. Red boxes with a solid black border cannot be upgraded.
- **Turn order and start.** The mat's number sets turn order, lowest first. The mat also sets your starting popularity and starting coins. Everyone draws two objective cards.

The rulebook explains the pattern: higher-numbered mats start with slightly better popularity and coins, because those players are more likely to get one fewer turn than the first player.

## The four layers of a section

Look at one section of your mat from top to bottom. It has four layers that grow as you play:

1. **The top action,** with green boxes covered by cubes. Upgrading lifts a cube off and reveals a bigger benefit.
2. **A structure space** below it. When you build that structure, its bonus is revealed, and you gain that bonus whenever you take this section's top action.
3. **The bottom action,** with red cost boxes that upgrades can remove.
4. **A recruit space.** When you enlist that recruit, you gain its ongoing bonus whenever you, or the players to your left and right, take this section's bottom action.

So every bottom-row action you take reshapes a section. The question is which section to reshape first.

## Reading upgrades

Upgrade takes a cube from any green box on the top row and puts it on any open red box with a bracketed border on the bottom row. It does not have to be the same column. That means each upgrade does two things at once: one top action gets stronger, and one bottom action gets cheaper.

Good early targets usually share a theme:

- **Make your most-used top action stronger.** An extra unit moved, extra power from Bolster, or an extra territory produced, on a section you take often.
- **Make your planned star cheaper.** If you plan to deploy all four mechs, cutting the metal cost of Deploy saves resources four times.

Six upgrades is a star, so on a mat with cheap Upgrade the upgrades are an early star and an engine at once.

## Structures and recruits

Structures each tie to a top action. The rulebook's structures are the Mill (an extra Produce territory that produces as if it were a worker), the Armory (1 power whenever you Trade), the Monument (1 popularity whenever you Bolster), and the Mine (a tunnel only you can use). Building in the section you take most pays you most often.

Each time you enlist, you also claim one of the four one-time bonuses on your faction mat: 2 power, 2 coins, 2 popularity, or 2 combat cards. A recruit's ongoing bonus fires on bottom-row actions in the section they came from, including your neighbors' turns. Enlist early from a section your neighbors will use too, and you collect on their turns as well as yours.

## The banned combinations

The official FAQ bans two pairs: Rusviet with the Industrial mat, and Crimea with the Patriotic mat, because experienced players can finish the game too quickly with them. If you draw one, return the player mat and take another. Brand-new players in their first game are exempt. Stonemaier's tournament guidelines exclude the same two pairs.

The logic is easy to see once you can read a mat. Rusviet's Relentless lets it repeat one section. Industrial is the first mat in turn order. A strong repeatable section on the first seat means fewer turns to six stars. Crimea's Coercion covers a missing resource with a combat card once per turn, which makes any mat with a gap in its resources run faster. The FAQ gives the reason for both bans in one line: speed in experienced hands.

## A quick mat checklist

Before turn one, answer four questions out loud:

1. Which section can I take with both actions soonest?
2. Which bottom action is cheapest, and which star does it feed?
3. Which top action do I want to upgrade first?
4. Where will my first structure go, and which top action does it boost?

Faction plus mat is your real setup. The [factions lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-factions-guide/) covers the other half.
