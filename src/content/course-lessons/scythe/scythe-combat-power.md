Welcome to Level 3. Combat in Scythe is rare, and that is why each fight matters. A win can mean a star, a territory, resources, and an opponent's units sent home. A loss can mean all of that in reverse. The good news is that Scythe combat has almost no luck once you understand what is public.

## How a combat works

Combat happens at the end of a Move action, when your character or mechs share a territory with an opponent's character or mechs. If there are several, the attacker picks the order.

1. Both players secretly choose a number on their power dial: from 0 to 7, and never more than their current power.
2. Each player may tuck one combat card behind the dial for each of their characters and mechs in the fight.
3. Reveal together. Total power wins. **Ties go to the attacker.**
4. Both players pay the power they dialed. Combat cards go to the discard pile.

The winner takes control of the territory and the resources there, places a star if they have fewer than two combat stars (Saxony has no limit), and, if they attacked, loses 1 popularity per enemy worker forced to retreat. The loser sends every unit in that territory to their home base, leaving resources behind.

## The combat card deck

There are 42 combat cards:

| Value | Cards | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 16 | 38% |
| 3 | 12 | 29% |
| 4 | 8 | 19% |
| 5 | 6 | 14% |

The average card is worth about 3.1. Only one card in seven is a 5. Bolster offers one card or 2 power, so on average the card is worth about one power more. But a card only counts if you have a character or mech slot in the fight to hold it, while power on the track also deters fights that never happen.

## What is public, and the maximum

Almost everything in a Scythe fight is open:

- **Power** sits on the Power Track for everyone to see.
- **The number of combat cards in hand** is public information, though not their values.
- **Card slots** equal the characters and mechs in the fight, plus one for Rusviet's People's Army when a Rusviet worker is there.

So you can always work out an opponent's maximum: the lower of their power and 7, plus 5 for each card they could play, limited by their hand and their slots.

**Example.** You attack with one mech, 9 power, and one combat card in hand. The defender has one mech, 4 power, and one combat card. Their maximum is 4 + 5 = 9. Dial 7 and add your card, worth at least 2, for a total of 9 or more, and you win even against their best possible reveal, because ties go to the attacker.

When your guaranteed total reaches their maximum, the fight is a sure thing. When it falls short, you are guessing what they will spend, and they are guessing too.

## Abilities that shift the math

These act before the dials are revealed, so count them first.

- **Nordic, Artillery:** pay 1 power to make the opponent lose 2 power, once per combat.
- **Saxony, Disarm:** in a territory with a tunnel or your Mine, the opponent loses 2 power, once per combat.
- **Crimea, Scout:** take one random combat card from the opponent, once per combat. Their maximum drops and yours may rise.
- **Rusviet, People's Army:** one more card slot when a Rusviet worker is in the fight.

The rulebook resolves the attacker's abilities first, then the defender's. Reducing an opponent's power can push their maximum below your guaranteed total.

## What a fight really costs

A win is not free. You pay the power you dialed, you may pay popularity for workers, and you have used a Move action on a turn you could have spent growing. The loser also gets something back: if they revealed at least 1 power, they draw a combat card as they retreat. And their units are only sent home, not destroyed. They can move back out on a later turn.

So a fight is worth it when at least two of these are true:

- It earns one of your two combat stars.
- It takes a territory with resources you can use or score.
- It takes the Factory, which counts as three territories at the end.
- It stops a rival from reaching a star, or ruins their next turn.

## Deterrence is the real battle

Most combat in Scythe is the fight that does not happen. A player with high power and a few combat cards next to their border is expensive to attack, because every opponent can work out that maximum too. Keeping power at a level that matches your neighbors' guaranteed totals is often enough to keep them away.

That is also why the 16 power star is popular late: it doubles as protection.
