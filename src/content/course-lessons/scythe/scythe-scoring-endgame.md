Scythe has no fixed number of rounds. The game ends the instant any player places their sixth star, and everyone scores at that moment. That makes the end of the game a weapon. This lesson does the scoring math, then shows how to use it.

## The scoring table

At the end, each player adds four things to the coins they already hold:

| Popularity | Each star | Each territory | Each 2 resources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 6 | $3 | $2 | $1 |
| 7 to 12 | $4 | $3 | $2 |
| 13 to 18 | $5 | $4 | $3 |

Plus the coins from the structure bonus tile in play, which scores your structures on one of six patterns, such as tunnels or lakes next to them.

The Factory counts as three territories for whoever controls it.

## What a tier is worth

Take a player with 6 stars, 10 territories, and 16 resources (8 pairs).

| Tier | Stars | Territories | Resources | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 6 | $18 | $20 | $8 | $46 |
| 7 to 12 | $24 | $30 | $16 | $70 |
| 13 to 18 | $30 | $40 | $24 | $94 |

Each tier step is worth $24 here: one coin more for every star, territory, and pair. That is why one popularity point at 6 or 12 can be worth more than a whole turn of coins, and why a point at 9 is worth nothing at the end. Know where the edges are: **7 and 13.**

The [rules lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-rules-mistakes/) covered the cost of forcing workers out. Near the end, a fight that drops you from 7 to 6 can cost far more than the fight gains.

## Territories are the hidden score

Players count stars all game and forget territories, yet at the middle tier each territory pays $3, almost as much as a star. You control a territory if you have a worker, mech, or character there, or a structure with no enemy unit on it. Each unit only holds one territory, so in the last turns:

- **Spread out.** Move workers off stacked territories onto empty neighbors. Two workers on one hex score one territory; on two hexes, they score two.
- **Use your structures.** A structure holds its territory alone, which frees a worker to move elsewhere.
- **Use Speed and mechs.** A mech carrying workers can drop them across the map in one Move.
- **Hold the Factory.** Three territories for one unit is the best square on the board.

## Resources score in pairs

Resources only count where you control the territory, and they score per pair. An odd resource is worth nothing. Carry resources onto territories you hold, and if you can spend an odd one on a final action, do. Resources sitting on territory a rival can walk into are a gift to them.

## Who ends the game

The player who places the sixth star decides when everyone scores. If you can place your sixth and you are ahead, the game is yours. If you are behind, placing it ends the game with you losing, so spend the turn spreading out, gaining popularity, or taking land instead. And you must place a star when you reach a goal, so watch which goals your next action would trigger before you take it.

That leads to three habits.

**Count stars every round.** When any player reaches four, the game may have only a few turns left. When any player reaches five, assume each of their turns could be the last.

**Score before you place.** Before you take an action that places your sixth star, add up your total and the closest rival's, using the table. It takes a minute, and it is the most important minute of the game.

**Get your land out early.** Because the game can end on someone else's turn, the spread-out position you want at the end needs to be in place one or two turns before the rival's sixth star, not after.

## Stars late in the game

Two stars are easiest to take late: 16 power and 18 popularity. Bolster with a Monument adds both power and popularity, and the right recruits help too. Popularity also raises your tier, so a late push toward 18 can pay twice: a star, and a higher rate on everything else.
