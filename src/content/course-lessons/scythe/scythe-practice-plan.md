Playing a lot of Scythe makes you better slowly. Practicing it makes you better quickly. The difference is focus: each game has one skill you are working on, and you check afterward whether you did it. This plan uses six weeks, one skill each, building on every level of this course. It assumes two or three games a week, some of them solo against the Automa or online.

## Before you start: the score log

Keep a simple log for every game: date, faction and mat, player count, your final coins, your number of turns, how many stars you placed, your popularity tier at the end, and who placed the sixth star. Add one line on what you would change.

Two numbers matter most. **Coins per turn** (final coins divided by your turns) tells you how efficient your engine is. **Stars at the end** tells you whether you are keeping up with the race. Both should rise over six weeks.

## Week 1: two actions a turn

Play against the Automa at the Autometta level, as Stonemaier recommends until you have beaten it once, then step up. Count how many of your turns took both a top and a bottom action. Aim to raise that count every game.

Review the [first-game strategy lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-first-game-strategy/) before you start. The goal is a smooth engine where each turn's top action pays for the next turn's bottom action.

## Week 2: every faction

Play one game with each base faction you know least, ideally all five over the week, with random player mats (no banned pairs). Before each game, write the faction's bent rule and your first mech in one line, using the [factions guide](/academy/scythe/scythe-factions-guide/).

After each game, note one thing the faction did well and one thing you did not use. By the end of the week, you should be able to sit down with any faction and have a plan before turn one.

## Week 3: combat math

Play multiplayer games, in person or online, and make one rule: before every fight, yours or an opponent's, say each side's maximum possible total out loud. Use the [combat lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-combat-power/).

Track every fight in the log: who attacked, whether the result was certain, and what the winner gained. You are learning to spot the sure wins and to avoid the coin flips.

## Week 4: the endgame

This week, count. When any player reaches four stars, work out everyone's score at their current popularity tier. Do it again at five. Use the table in the [scoring lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-scoring-endgame/).

Two drills:

- **Spread out early.** In the last three turns, move your workers onto as many territories as you can, and log how many territories you held at the end.
- **Score before you place.** Never place your sixth star without adding up your total and the closest rival's first.

## Week 5: the auction

Play every game this week with a faction and mat auction, using the [auction lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-faction-auction/). Use your coins per turn from the log to price each pairing. After the game, compare your bid with how the pairing played. Were you right about the turns it would save?

If you play solo this week, practice the pricing instead: deal three random pairings, write a price for each, then play the one you priced highest and check it.

## Week 6: tournament conditions

Play under the rules of the event you are aiming at. If it follows Stonemaier's guidelines, that means four or five players, auctioned pairings, no in-game trading, and a time budget. Try a timer for top-row actions, as suggested in comments on the guidelines page. Aim to finish four-player games in under two hours.

Also rehearse the etiquette: announce bottom-row actions, keep your tracks readable, and start your turn while the previous player finishes a bottom-row action.

## On the day

- **Read the event's rules** for mat pairing, timing, and tiebreakers before the first round.
- **Play your plan, not your mood.** A loss in round one under a 7-point placement system still scores points.
- **Count stars every round,** and score before you place the sixth.
- **Log the games.** The notes from an event teach you more than any other games you play.
