Scythe has a lot of pieces, and new players often expect a long war game. The good news for a host is that one turn is small: pick a section, take one or two actions. A good teach makes that turn clear in the first five minutes and lets the rest arrive when it is needed.

This lesson gives you a host script, the order to say it in, and a plan for the first few turns. The [How to Play Scythe](/academy/scythe/how-to-play-scythe/) lesson has the rules themselves.

## Before anyone arrives

- **Set up the board yourself.** Encounter tokens, decks, Factory cards, and the structure bonus tile take a while. A set table is a better first impression.
- **Deal the mats in advance,** but redraw any banned pair (Rusviet with Industrial, Crimea with Patriotic) unless everyone is brand new. Stonemaier allows the banned pairs for first-time players.
- **Set up each player's mats completely.** Cubes, workers, structures, recruits, mechs, power, popularity, and coins all go where the mats show.
- **Print the [Scythe teach sheet](/academy/scythe/teach-sheet/)** and put it where everyone can reach it.

## The host script

Say it in this order. It takes about ten to fifteen minutes.

**1. The goal (one minute).** "We want the most coins at the end. The game ends the moment anyone places their sixth star. Stars, land, and resources all turn into coins, and your popularity decides the rate."

**2. Your turn (two minutes).** Point at a player mat. "Your mat has four sections. Each turn, move your token to a new section, not the one you used last turn. Take the top action, the bottom action, or both, top first. Pay what is in red, gain what is in green."

**3. The top row (three minutes).** Walk Move, Bolster, Trade, and Produce, one sentence each. Show that Move can gain $1 instead, and that Produce gets more expensive as workers leave the mat.

**4. The bottom row (three minutes).** Upgrade, Deploy, Build, Enlist. Each costs one resource type: oil, metal, wood, and food. Each pays coins. Then the key idea: "Uncovering a space on your mat makes something better. Upgrading moves a cube from top to bottom, so the top action gets stronger and the bottom gets cheaper."

**5. The stars (two minutes).** Point at the Triumph Track. "Each of these is a goal. Hit one, place a star. Most of them come from doing the bottom-row actions until a row is empty."

**6. Your faction (two minutes).** Each player reads their faction ability aloud and looks at their four mech abilities. Remind them that a mech's ability applies to the character and every mech once deployed.

**7. Stop.** Do not teach combat or encounters in detail yet. Say: "Combat only happens when characters or mechs meet. I will explain it the first time anyone is about to fight. Encounters, the same."

## The first three turns

Most first games stall because players take only a top action. Coach toward two actions a turn.

- **Turn one:** ask each player which section lets them both act and pay. Often it is a Produce or Trade that feeds the bottom action below it.
- **Turn two:** point out that their workers are their engine. Moving workers onto different terrain means future Produce actions make the resources their bottom row needs.
- **Turn three:** show the character's path to the nearest encounter token. Encounters are an early boost to popularity, power, coins, or resources.

## Teach combat when it happens

The first time someone moves toward an enemy character or mech, pause. Explain the dial (up to 7 power, never more than you have), the one-card-per-unit rule, and ties going to the attacker. Point out what the loser loses: their units there go home, and the resources stay behind for the winner. And what the attacker pays for a win: one popularity per enemy worker forced out.

## The sample scoring round

The rulebook suggests pausing after the first star is placed, and everyone works out their score as if the game ended. It costs five minutes and pays for the rest of the game, because players see that popularity is a multiplier and that territories count. Nothing is actually scored.

## Common host mistakes

- **Teaching every faction's mech abilities.** Each player only needs their own.
- **Explaining the Factory card in full.** Say that the first visit gives a card with extra actions, and explain it when someone gets there.
- **Letting the quiet player take only top actions.** Ask, every few turns, "can you take the bottom one too?"
