Welcome to Level 5. Scythe is a competitive game with a loyal following, but it is not run like a card game with a ranked ladder. Stonemaier says so plainly on its tournament guidelines page: there is no official Scythe tournament organization. Competitive Scythe is run by fans, stores, clubs, and conventions. This lesson shows what those events look like and where to find them.

## How a Scythe tournament usually runs

Stonemaier's [suggested tournament guidelines](https://stonemaiergames.com/?p=10589) are the closest thing to a standard, and organizers often borrow from them. The main points:

- **Teaching or experienced.** An event for new players needs at least three rounds: a teaching game, an elimination round, and a final. Experienced events can skip the teaching round.
- **Table size.** Four or five players per table. If a table drops to two, split them among other tables.
- **Setup.** Random faction and mat pairings, the two banned pairs excluded, auctioned for coins off the final score. The [auction lesson](/academy/scythe/scythe-faction-auction/) covers how.
- **No in-game trading.**
- **Advancing.** A 7-point placement system: first place at a table earns 7 points, second 6, and so on, with ties broken by total coins from earlier games. The top players advance to a five-player final table.
- **Rulings.** The tournament coordinator has the final say on rules disputes.

The page also describes a past multi-day Gen Con event with a seeding round, several elimination rounds, and a final table. It sets no time limits, though comments on it suggest timers for slow turns, and one says experienced players should finish in 90 to 120 minutes.

## Store and club events

Most Scythe tournaments are local: a game store, a café, or a club runs a one-day event with two or three rounds. The guidelines suggest that a host store collect a small entry from each player to buy a prize from the store. To find one:

- **Ask your local game store** whether it runs or would host a Scythe day. The guidelines page gives them a ready-made format.
- **Search BoardGameGeek.** The Scythe forums on BoardGameGeek carry event announcements and regional groups.
- **Check convention schedules.** Regional board game conventions often list Scythe events.

## Online play

Online play is the fastest way to get many competitive games in.

- **Scythe: Digital Edition** is the official digital version, developed by The Knights of Unity with Stonemaier Games. It is available on Steam, iOS, and Android, with AI opponents and online play. Stonemaier notes that changes to online play are up to the developer and the digital publisher.
- **Community events.** The Scythe Discord community has hosted Digital Edition tournaments open to anyone who signs up. A comment on Stonemaier's tournament page announced one.
- **Virtual tabletops.** Stonemaier's Scythe digital page lists a Tabletopia version (also on Steam), where you move the pieces yourself with no rules automation.

A digital game enforces the rules for you, which makes it a good place to learn the timing of a strong opening. A tabletop event will expect you to know the rules yourself, so play both.

## Etiquette that wins friends

The guidelines ask players to be respectful and flexible, for example letting someone claim missed bottom-row coins before their next turn. Three habits help:

- **Announce bottom-row actions** so neighbors can claim recruit bonuses without asking.
- **Keep your power and popularity tokens where everyone can read them.** Combat math depends on it.
- **Start your turn while the previous player finishes a bottom-row action,** as the rules allow. It keeps the table moving.
