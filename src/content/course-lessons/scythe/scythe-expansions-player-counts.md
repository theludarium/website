Welcome to Level 4. Scythe's base game covers one to five players and already has a solo mode. Stonemaier then added three main expansions and several smaller add-ons. This lesson covers how the game changes with player count and what each box adds, so you can pick the one that fits your group.

## Player counts in the base game

The base game plays one to five. At every count the map stays the same size, so the count changes how crowded it feels.

- **Two players.** The two home bases may be far apart, and there is room for both to build without contact. Combat is rarer, and the race to six stars is the whole game.
- **Three and four.** The Factory becomes contested, and players start to meet at their borders by midgame.
- **Five.** Every home base is filled, borders are tight, and neighbors' recruit bonuses fire often. Five also means more waiting, so the overlapping turns rule (start your turn while the previous player finishes a bottom-row action) matters most here.

Our article on [player counts](/articles/board-game-player-counts/) covers how to tell when a game is best at a given count.

## Solo: the Automa

The base game includes the Automa, a solo opponent run by a deck of cards and a star tracker card. Each Automa card tells it how to move and what it gains, and the star tracker sets how quickly it places stars, which is how you choose the difficulty. There are four star tracker cards: Autometta for beginners, Automa for normal play, Automaszyna for veterans, and Ultimaszyna, with its own extra rules, for the very best. The Automa rules strongly recommend Autometta until you have beaten it once.

The Automa does not use a player mat or produce resources in the usual way, so a solo game is faster than a multiplayer one and a good way to practice your own engine. Stonemaier's rules page links a community variant for playing with more than one Automa. Our guide to the [best solo board games](/articles/best-solo-board-games/) puts Scythe's solo mode in context.

## Invaders from Afar

[Scythe: Invaders from Afar](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01L0VX6CG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) adds two factions, Albion and Togawa, with their own home bases on the board and pieces for up to seven players. Albion places flags that count for scoring, and Togawa places traps that punish opponents who step on them. It also includes two new player mats, numbered 2a and 3a so they slot into turn order, and Automa support for the new factions.

**Buy it if** you play with six or seven, or if your group has played the five base factions enough to want new ones.

## The Wind Gambit

[Scythe: The Wind Gambit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B072HZSLL6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) adds two modules that can be used together or apart. **Airships** give each faction a large flying unit that ignores rivers and lakes, with two abilities drawn at random each game. **Resolutions** are tiles that replace the standard game end with a different trigger, one tile per game from a set of eight.

**Buy it if** your games feel the same from game to game, or if your group would like a different end condition now and then.

## The Rise of Fenris

[Scythe: The Rise of Fenris](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07DFYKQ15?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the third and final main expansion. It has an eight-episode campaign that is resettable and replayable, plus eleven modules that can be used instead of the campaign or after it, including a fully cooperative mode. It works with the other expansions.

**Buy it if** you have a steady group that can meet for eight games, or if you want a co-op way to play.

## The Modular Board

The [Scythe Modular Board](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07RX6XR6Z?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) replaces the fixed map with a double-sided board and tiles, plus home base tiles, so the map and the factions' starting places change every game. Setup gains a drafting step, and the rules include a variant for a tighter map at low player counts.

**Buy it if** your group has learned the base map well and wants openings that cannot be memorized.

## Smaller add-ons

- **[Scythe Encounters](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07J2KPCXB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** adds new encounter cards to the deck.
- **The [Scythe Board Extension](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01JGOMR6K?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** enlarges the board for more room around the pieces.

## Which order?

A sensible order for most groups:

1. **Base game,** until every player knows the five factions.
2. **Invaders from Afar** for groups of six or seven, or **The Wind Gambit** for variety.
3. **The Rise of Fenris** once your group is ready for a campaign.
4. **The Modular Board** for groups who play often and want a new map every game.
