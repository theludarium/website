Welcome to Level 5. Terra Mystica has no official organized play program from its publishers, but it has a long-running online competitive scene. This lesson covers where it lives and how the formats work.

## The Snellman site

[terra.snellman.net](https://terra.snellman.net/) is a free site for playing Terra Mystica online, built by Juho Snellman. The Fire & Ice rulebook thanks him for providing an online playtesting platform. Games are asynchronous: you take your turn when you have time, and a game can run for days. That suits a game this long, and it is where the long-running league below is played.

Two features make it a training ground:

- **Game records.** Every move of a game is logged, so you can study games between strong players turn by turn.
- **Options.** Games can use the expansions and the rule variants the community has settled on.

## The Terra Mystica league (tmtour.org)

The site's home page links a long-running tournament at [tmtour.org](http://tmtour.org), played since 2014 in two-month seasons with divisions, promotion, and relegation. Its posted rules include:

- **Leagues of seven players** (some bottom leagues have eight), playing seven four-player games. Each player meets every other player exactly twice and plays each seat once.
- **Points by finishing place:** 6 for first, 3 for second, 1 for third, 0 for fourth, with shared places splitting the points.
- **A chess clock:** 5 days to start plus 1 day per round, with an 8-hour grace period each turn. Players who run out of time are removed from the game.
- **Rules:** the standard rules on the Snellman site, plus the Fire & Ice variable turn order (passing order sets the next round's order), and parts of the mini expansion: the four extra Town tiles, the Bonus card that scores for Shipping, and the Temple Scoring tile.

Every player, new or returning, signs up for each season on the tournament site. Sign-ups open two weeks before a season starts, and new players begin in one of the bottom leagues.

## Board Game Arena

[Board Game Arena](https://boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=terramystica) hosts Terra Mystica. Its game page lists it as free to play in the browser, with options for the Fire & Ice factions and a faction auction. Rankings make it a good place for practice games against players at your level.

There is also a licensed digital edition of Terra Mystica from DIGIDICED, on the App Store and on Steam.

## The World Boardgaming Championships

The World Boardgaming Championships (WBC) has run a Terra Mystica event, coded T_M. According to the WBC's own event reports, it debuted in 2017, its 2018 semifinal and final picked factions by bidding, and it returned in 2023 after three years away, with 28 players and a final of Dwarves, Darklings, Nomads, and Mermaids. The 2023 report said the GM hoped to run an Age of Innovation event the following year, but the WBC event reports for 2024 to 2026 list no Terra Mystica or Age of Innovation event. Check the current WBC event list before planning a trip around it.

## Finding local play

For face-to-face games:

- **Game stores.** Capstone Games has a store finder on its site. Heavy games like this often appear on a store's strategy night.
- **BoardGameGeek.** The Terra Mystica forums are the place for rules questions and strategy threads, and the regional forums list local game groups.
- **Indianapolis.** If you are near Indianapolis, join [the Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## Choosing a format to start

- **New to competitive play:** a few turn-based games on Board Game Arena against players near your rating.
- **Ready to commit:** one Snellman league season, starting in a lower division.
- **In-person goals:** a WBC event or a convention tournament once you are comfortable with faction bidding.

Whichever you choose, read the event's rules first: online leagues often add variable turn order or mini expansion pieces, and in-person events may pick factions by bidding. Online games teach fast because you can play many of them. In-person games teach table speed and reading people. Both help.
