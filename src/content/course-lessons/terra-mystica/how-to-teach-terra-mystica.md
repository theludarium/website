Terra Mystica looks like a wall of icons, and new players often brace for a long teach. The good news: almost everything a player needs is printed on their own Faction board. A good teach points at that board, explains the pattern once, and lets the board carry the details.

Plan on about twenty minutes. Set up the rulebook's introductory game first, so nobody has to choose a faction or a starting spot before they know what those choices mean. Print the [Terra Mystica teach sheet](/academy/terra-mystica/teach-sheet/) for the middle of the table.

## The script, in order

### 1. The goal (one minute)

"Most Victory points after six rounds wins. We all start on 20. We score during the game for building what each round rewards, and at the end for cults, our biggest connected area, and leftover money."

### 2. Your land (two minutes)

Point at the terrain ring on each Faction board. "You can only build on your Home terrain, the big one on your ring. To grow, you dig other terrain into yours. Each step around the ring is one Spade. Next door is one Spade, across the ring is three."

Then show where Spades come from: "Right now, 3 Workers buy 1 Spade."

### 3. Your buildings (four minutes)

This is the heart of the teach. Point at the building tracks.

"Building takes the leftmost piece off your board, and every empty space shows new income. Dwellings give Workers. Upgrade a Dwelling to a Trading house for Coins and Power. Upgrade a Trading house to your Stronghold for your faction's big ability, or to a Temple for Priests and a Favor tile. One Temple can become your Sanctuary."

Add the one cost rule that shapes the map: "A Trading house costs half the Coins if an opponent's building is right next to it."

### 4. The eight actions (four minutes)

Read them from the action overview tiles or the [teach sheet](/academy/terra-mystica/teach-sheet/): transform and build, Shipping, cheaper Spades, upgrade, send a Priest to a cult, Power actions, Special actions, and pass. Say clearly: "One action per turn, around the table, until everyone passes. You can take the same action again on later turns."

### 5. Power (three minutes)

Use one player's board as the demo. Move tokens as you talk.

"Gaining Power moves tokens from Bowl I to II. Only when Bowl I is empty do they move from II to III. You can only spend from Bowl III, and spent tokens go back to Bowl I." Then show the conversions printed in Bowl III, and the six shared Power actions on the board: "Each of these goes once per round. First come, first served."

Then the neighbor rule: "When someone builds next to you, you may take Power equal to the Power value of your buildings next to it. It costs you points: one fewer than the Power you take. All or nothing."

### 6. Cults, Towns, and scoring (three minutes)

"Four cult tracks. Climb with Priests, Favor tiles, and some cards. Climbing gives Power, a bonus at the end of most rounds, and big points at the end."

"Four of your buildings in a connected cluster, worth 7 or more Power value together, make a Town. Towns pay points and a reward." Point at the Scoring tiles: "Each round pays for something different. Read ahead."

### 7. Faction powers (two minutes)

Go around the table. Each player reads their faction's starting ability and Stronghold ability aloud from the board. The rulebook asks that everyone know every faction's abilities before play starts, and this is the quickest way.

## Running the first round

Round 1 is where new players freeze, because every action looks equally good. Give them a nudge each: "This round's Scoring tile pays for X. What on your board does X?" Most first turns are a Dwelling, a Trading house next to a neighbor, or a Power action.

Watch for two things. First, income: remind everyone they take round 1 income on top of their starting resources. Second, passing: when someone passes, walk them through returning their Bonus card, taking an unused one, and checking whether the card they returned scores on the way out.

## What to leave out

Skip the end-game tie rules, the full Favor tile list, and every faction you are not playing. Explain Favor tiles when someone builds their first Temple, and Town tiles when someone is one building away from a Town. Teaching at the moment of need keeps the opening twenty minutes short.

The Ludarium's general guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) covers the habits behind this script: goal first, touch the pieces, and save the edge cases.
