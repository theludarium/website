This is the last lesson. By now you know the rules, the Power cycle, the costs of terraforming, every base faction, the cult tracks, and the endgame math. What separates a solid player from a strong one is practice with a purpose. This plan assumes about three games a week, mostly asynchronous online games that you can fit around life.

## The habits behind the plan

**Play one faction until it is boring.** Every faction bends a different rule. If you switch every game, you relearn the bend each time. Two weeks with one faction teaches you its rhythm: when its Stronghold should land, what its Power cycle looks like, and which Scoring tiles it loves.

**Review every game.** Asynchronous games on the Snellman site keep a full log, so you can step through your game afterward. Look for the round where your score fell behind the leaders' pace, then look for the cause one or two rounds earlier. Most mistakes in Terra Mystica are made before they show up on the score track.

**Study strong players.** Find a recent game from a top division of the league, pick one player, and follow only their turns. Ask why each action came in that order. It is slow at first and becomes a fast way to learn openings.

## Week 1: one faction, base rules

Pick a faction from the middle of Feuerland's balance table, such as the Witches, Nomads, or Halflings. Play three games with it. Focus on one thing: spend all of Bowl III before every pass. Note how often you manage it.

## Week 2: the same faction, a new focus

Same faction, three more games. Now focus on the plan: before placing your first Dwelling, write down the round for your Stronghold, your first Temple, and your first Town. Afterward, compare the plan with what happened.

## Week 3: a contrasting faction

Switch to a faction that plays very differently. If you played the Witches, try the Darklings (Priests for Spades) or the Engineers (Bridges and cheap buildings). Focus on leech decisions: say every offer's Power and point cost, and record whether you took it and why. Use the [power bowls lesson](/academy/terra-mystica/terra-mystica-power-bowls/) as your reference.

## Week 4: openings drill

Set up a few games at home, play every seat yourself, and stop after the first two rounds. Use random Scoring tiles and Bonus cards. For each setup, try two openings for the same faction, such as Stronghold first versus Temple first, and compare your income and position at the start of round 3.

## Week 5: endgame counting

Play three full games, and from round 5 on, count final scoring before every action: area places, each cult track, and leftover resources. Use the checklist from the [endgame lesson](/academy/terra-mystica/terra-mystica-endgame-scoring/). The goal is to know, before you pass in round 6, roughly where you will finish.

## Week 6: faction bidding

Competitive games often pick factions by bidding, like the Fire & Ice auction, where players bid points from a starting 40. Practice judging factions against a setup: for each game this week, look at the Scoring tiles, Bonus cards, and map, rank the factions you would want, and estimate how many points each is worth. Feuerland's starting-points table, with its 15 to 30 point spread, is a useful anchor for how far apart factions can be. Then play your top pick.

## Then: your first league season

After six weeks, sign up for a season of the Terra Mystica league at tmtour.org, linked from the Snellman site. Keep the review habit: one written note per game. The league plays seven games per season, so you get seven reviews against players at a similar level.

If you prefer real-time play, Board Game Arena's ranked games work the same way. Set a number of games per week and review each one.

## Keep it social

Terra Mystica is a long game, and practice can make it feel like work. Mix in games with friends at home, where you teach rather than compete, using the [how to teach lesson](/academy/terra-mystica/how-to-teach-terra-mystica/). Teaching makes you explain your own reasoning, which is its own kind of practice. And if you are near Indianapolis, [join the Indianapolis list](/lounges/indianapolis/) for first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.
