Welcome to Level 2. Terra Mystica's Power system confuses new players because the tokens never leave: they just move around three Bowls. Once you see it as a cycle with a fixed size, the decisions get much clearer.

All rules here come from the current English rulebook from Capstone Games.

## How the cycle moves

You have 12 tokens (fewer if you sacrifice some). Gaining Power moves one token per point:

1. From Bowl I to Bowl II, while Bowl I has any tokens.
2. Then from Bowl II to Bowl III.
3. When all tokens are in Bowl III, extra Power is lost.

Spending moves tokens from Bowl III back to Bowl I.

Here is the key number. If all 12 tokens sit in Bowl I, you need 12 Power just to move them into Bowl II, and 12 more to move them into Bowl III. So a full cycle takes 24 Power gained to make 12 Power spendable. Power you gain while Bowl I still has tokens only sets up future spending. Power you gain once Bowl I is empty is spendable at once.

That is why Bowl I matters. When Bowl I is empty and Bowl II is full, every point of Power you gain is a point you can spend this turn.

## What Power buys

The six shared Power actions each go once per round, to whoever takes them first:

| Power action | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Build a Bridge | 3 Power |
| 1 Priest | 3 Power |
| 2 Workers | 4 Power |
| 7 Coins | 4 Power |
| Transform with 1 Spade | 4 Power |
| Transform with 2 Spades | 6 Power |

At any time on your turn you can also convert: 5 Power for a Priest, 3 Power for a Worker, 1 Power for a Coin. Compare the rates. Through conversions, 4 Power is 4 Coins. Through the Power action, it is 7. The actions are almost always the better deal, which is why they go fast and why going first in a round matters.

## Sacrificing tokens

When you are short in Bowl III, you can move tokens from Bowl II to Bowl III, removing one more token from Bowl II for each one you move. Two tokens from Bowl II become one spendable token and one token gone for good.

Every sacrificed token makes each future cycle smaller. In round 1 that cost repeats for five more rounds. In round 6 it costs nothing, because there is no future cycle. A useful rule of thumb: sacrifice freely in the last round or two, and early only when the action it pays for is worth a lot, such as the 2-Spade action that lets you build in a key spot.

## Leech: Power from your neighbors

When an opponent builds or upgrades next to your buildings, you may gain Power equal to the Power value of your adjacent buildings, paying one Victory point less than the Power gained.

| Power offered | Points it costs |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 4 |

A 1-Power leech is free, so always take it unless your bowls are full. For bigger offers, ask what the Power becomes. If it moves tokens from Bowl II to Bowl III, you can spend it now: 4 Power is a 7-Coin action, and 7 Coins is far more than 3 points early in the game. If it only moves tokens from Bowl I to Bowl II, it is setup, not spending, and the trade is weaker.

Also remember timing. Early points matter less than early resources, because resources compound into buildings that score for the rest of the game. Late in round 6, Power you cannot spend is worth very little and the points are final. Many experienced players take most leeches in the early rounds and turn more of them down at the end.

## Where Power comes from

Beyond leeches, Power comes from Trading house and Stronghold income, two Bonus cards and two Favor tiles that add Power income, the Town tile worth 8 Power, the fire cult bonus on one Scoring tile, and the cult tracks themselves: 1, 2, 2, and 3 Power when you reach spaces 3, 5, 7, and 10.

That last source is easy to miss. A Priest sent to a cult order for three steps can carry you past a Power space, and that Power arrives at once.

## Plan the cycle across a round

A smooth round often looks like this. Income moves your tokens forward. Early leeches and cult steps fill Bowl II and start Bowl III. Your first actions take the Power action you most need before someone else does. Your last action before passing spends whatever is left.

The worst outcome is a full Bowl III at the end of a round while Bowl I is empty: that is a whole cycle of spending power that you paid for and never used.
