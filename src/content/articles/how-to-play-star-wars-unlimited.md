To learn how to play Star Wars: Unlimited, you need four ideas. Each player has a leader and a base. You play units into two arenas, ground and space. You pay for cards with resources that you choose from your own hand. And you win by destroying your opponent's base. Everything else in the game hangs on those four ideas, and players take turns one action at a time, so the game feels fast even when the board fills up.

**The quick answer:** set out your base and leader, draw six cards, and put two of them facedown as resources. Then players alternate single actions (play a card, attack, use an ability, take the initiative, or pass) until both are done. In the regroup phase, everyone draws two cards, may add one more resource, and readies everything. Repeat until a base hits zero.

This guide covers the full rules for a first game, the formats, and what to buy first. Rules are explained in our own words from the official Comprehensive Rules (version 8.0, July 2026). When a card disagrees with the rules, the card wins.

## What is Star Wars: Unlimited?

Star Wars: Unlimited (often shortened to SWU) is a trading card game from Fantasy Flight Games, with Danny Schaefer as lead designer. The first set, Spark of Rebellion, launched in March 2024, and new sets have arrived roughly every four months since. The most recent is Homeworlds, the ninth set, released in October 2026.

The main format is a two-player duel. A separate multiplayer format, Twin Suns, seats three or four players with two leaders each. Games are quick: retail listings for the starter products give about 20 minutes for a two-player game, and tournament matches are usually best of three.

Three things set SWU apart from older card games:

- **Alternating actions.** You never sit and watch a long turn. You do one thing, then your opponent does one thing.
- **Any card can be a resource.** There are no land or energy cards. You choose which cards in your hand to give up as resources.
- **Two arenas.** Ground units fight ground units, space units fight space units, and both can hit the enemy base.

## What do you need to play Star Wars: Unlimited?

Each player needs one deck. For the standard two-player format, called Premier, a deck is:

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Leader | Exactly 1 |
| Base | Exactly 1 |
| Main deck | At least 50 cards (units, events, upgrades) |
| Copies | No more than 3 of any card |
| Sideboard | Up to 10 cards, for tournaments |

You also need damage counters (dice work), an initiative counter, and any token cards your deck makes. Leaders and bases sit on the table, not in your deck.

## What are the card types in Star Wars: Unlimited?

| Card type | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Leader** | Starts in play with a passive or action ability. Later it can deploy as a powerful unit. |
| **Base** | Your life total. Most bases have 30 HP. Some trade a little HP for an ability. |
| **Unit** | Characters and vehicles. Each one is a ground unit or a space unit. |
| **Event** | A one-time effect. Play it, resolve it, discard it. |
| **Upgrade** | Attaches to a unit and changes its power, HP, or abilities. |
| **Token** | Units or upgrades created by other cards, such as Shield tokens. |

Every card also has aspect icons: Vigilance, Command, Aggression, Cunning, Heroism and Villainy. Your leader gives you two aspects and your base gives you one. More on why that matters below.

## How do you set up a game?

1. **Place your base** at the top center of your play area.
2. **Place your leader** below your base, Leader side up.
3. **Decide the initiative.** Pick a player at random. That player chooses who starts the game holding the initiative counter.
4. **Shuffle and draw six cards.**
5. **Mulligan once if you want.** Shuffle your hand back and draw six new cards. You must keep the second hand.
6. **Resource two cards.** Each player picks exactly two cards from hand and places them facedown as resources, ready to use.

Choosing those first two resources is your first real decision. Keep cheap cards you can play early, and resource the expensive ones or the ones that do not fit your plan.

## How does a round of Star Wars: Unlimited work?

Each round has two phases: the action phase and the regroup phase.

### The action phase

The player holding the initiative goes first. Then players alternate, taking exactly one action each time. On your turn you choose one:

1. **Play a card.** Exhaust (turn sideways) resources equal to its cost, plus any aspect penalty.
2. **Attack with a unit.** Exhaust a ready unit you control and attack.
3. **Use an action ability.** Some cards, including many leaders, say "Action" followed by an effect.
4. **Take the initiative.** Grab the initiative counter. You will go first next round, but you are done taking actions for the rest of this phase.
5. **Pass.** Do nothing this turn.

The action phase ends when both players pass in a row, or when one player takes the initiative right after the other has passed.

### The regroup phase

1. **Draw two cards.** Each player.
2. **Resource one card.** Starting with the player who has the initiative, each player may put one card from hand into play as a resource. It is optional.
3. **Ready everything.** Turn all exhausted units, resources and leaders upright.

Then a new round begins.

## How does initiative work in Star Wars: Unlimited?

Initiative is the clever part of the game. Going first in a round is a real advantage: you get to play your big unit before your opponent can answer, or attack before they set up a defense.

But taking the initiative costs you every action you had left this round. So each round becomes a small game of chicken. Do you grab the counter now, while your opponent still has resources to spend? Or do you keep playing cards and risk them grabbing it first?

A good beginner rule: take the initiative when you have spent most of your resources and have nothing important left to do. Once you take it, your opponent can keep acting alone until they pass, so do not take it while they still have a big play waiting.

## How do resources and aspects work?

Every card has a cost in the top left corner. To play it, you exhaust that many resources. Resources are just cards from your hand placed facedown, so the bigger your resource area, the less you have in hand. Every resource you add is a card you will never cast.

Aspects add a second cost. If a card shows aspect icons that your leader and base do not provide, you pay 2 extra resources for each missing icon. That is called the aspect penalty. You can include any card in any deck, but cards outside your colors cost more, so most decks stick to the aspects their leader and base give them.

## How do attacks and damage work?

To attack, exhaust one of your ready units and choose a target:

- **An enemy unit in the same arena.** Ground units attack ground units. Space units attack space units.
- **The enemy base.** Any unit, ground or space, can attack the base.

Units come into play exhausted, so a new unit usually cannot attack until the next round. When a unit attacks another unit, both deal damage equal to their power to each other at the same time. When a unit attacks a base, the base takes the damage and does not hit back.

Damage stays on a unit from round to round. When a unit's damage equals or exceeds its HP, it is defeated and goes to the discard pile.

One keyword changes targeting on day one: **Sentinel.** If an enemy unit in an arena has Sentinel, your units in that arena must attack the Sentinel unit instead of other units or the base. Sentinels are walls, and most early games come down to how you get past them.

## How do ground and space arenas work?

The two arenas are separate fights side by side. Each player's ground units sit on one side of their base, and space units on the other.

The split matters in two ways. First, a big ground unit can never block a space attacker, so a player with a strong space fleet can hit your base while your ground army stands around. Second, some cards care about which arena they are in, such as a unit that gets stronger when you control more units in its arena.

When you build or read a deck, count how many units it has in each arena. A deck with no space units has no way to stop a fleet from attacking its base.

## How do leaders deploy?

Your leader starts the game on its Leader side, which usually has an ability you can use from the start. Most leaders also have an **Epic Action** on that side: once you control a certain number of resources (the number is printed on the card), you can deploy the leader.

When it deploys, the leader flips to its Leader Unit side and enters the ground arena ready, which means it can attack right away. If your deployed leader is defeated, it flips back to its Leader side. Because the Epic Action can only be used once per game, it does not come back as a unit. Some newer leaders deploy differently, such as pilots that attach to a vehicle as an upgrade, and their cards say so.

Timing the deploy is one of the biggest skill tests in the game. Deploy too early and your opponent picks off your leader. Deploy too late and you wasted its power.

## How do you win Star Wars: Unlimited?

Reduce your opponent's base to 0 HP. The game ends the moment that happens. If both bases reach 0 at the same time, the game is a draw. You can also concede at any time.

## What do common keywords mean?

You will see these on your first night. Each is printed in bold on the card.

| Keyword | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sentinel** | Enemy units in this arena must attack this unit first. |
| **Ambush** | When played, this unit may attack an enemy unit right away, even though it entered exhausted. |
| **Overwhelm** | When attacking, extra damage beyond what defeats the defender hits the enemy base. |
| **Raid X** | Gets +X power while attacking. |
| **Restore X** | When it attacks, heal X damage from your base. |
| **Saboteur** | Can ignore Sentinel, and removes Shield tokens from the unit it attacks. |
| **Shielded** | Enters play with a Shield token, which prevents the next damage it would take. |
| **Grit** | Gets +1 power for each damage on it. |

## What are the Star Wars: Unlimited formats?

| Format | Players | Deck | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Premier** | 2 | 1 leader, 1 base, 50+ cards, max 3 copies | The main competitive format, using recent sets |
| **Eternal** | 2 | Same as Premier | Every released card is legal |
| **Twin Suns** | 3 or 4 | 2 leaders, 1 base, 80+ cards, 1 copy of each | Casual multiplayer free-for-all |
| **Draft and Sealed** | 2 | Built from packs you open | Prereleases and draft nights |

**Twin Suns** deserves a note, because it plays differently. Each deck has two leaders and only one copy of any card. Instead of a single initiative counter, there are three counters to grab: the initiative, a blast counter (deal 1 damage to each opponent's base) and a plan counter (draw a card, then put one from hand on the bottom of your deck). Taking any counter ends your turns for the round. You also cannot simply pass while a counter is still available. It is a loud, social format, built for a kitchen table.

## Tips for your first games of Star Wars: Unlimited

1. **Resource your expensive cards early.** On turn one, a 7-cost card is mostly a resource in disguise.
2. **Count your opponent's resources.** Ready resources tell you what they can still play this round.
3. **Do not take the initiative too early.** Every action you skip is an action your opponent gets for free.
4. **Hit the base when the trade is bad.** If attacking a unit would just trade your unit away, attacking the base is often better.
5. **Watch both arenas.** Before you attack in space, ask what their ground units will do to your base next round.

## How do you teach Star Wars: Unlimited to a friend?

Teach it in the order of this guide: goal (destroy the base), the table layout (base, leader, two arenas, resources), one action at a time, then the regroup phase. Skip keywords until they appear. Most people understand the game by the second round.

## FAQ

### Is Star Wars: Unlimited easy to learn?

Yes, for a trading card game. The turn structure is simple, the card text is short, and the alternating actions keep everyone engaged. Expect one or two practice games before it clicks.

### How many cards are in a Star Wars: Unlimited deck?

A Premier deck has at least 50 cards plus one leader and one base, with no more than three copies of any card. Twin Suns decks have at least 80 cards, two leaders, and only one copy of each card.

### What is the best Star Wars: Unlimited starter deck?

For two new players, the Icons 2027 Edition Starter Box, which releases November 20, 2026. For a single player heading to a store, a Spotlight Deck. For a group of three or four, the 2026 Twin Suns decks.

### Can you play Star Wars: Unlimited with more than two players?

Yes. Twin Suns is the official multiplayer format for three or four players. Each player uses a two-leader, 80-card deck and fights everyone else at the table.

### How long does a game of SWU take?

Retail listings for the two-player starter products give about 20 minutes per game. Expect your first games to run longer while you read cards, and Twin Suns games to run longer because more bases are in play.
