A large group game night is not a regular game night with more chairs. At eight people, you can wing it. At forty, the thing that decides whether the night works is not the games; it is whether people can find a table, learn a game without you, and move to the next one when it ends. Forty people without a plan is thirty people standing near the snacks.

This is the plan. It covers the room, the game mix, the people you need, and a schedule that fills three hours without a lull. It is written for a church hall, an office, a community room, or a very large living room.

## How many tables and games do you need for 40 people?

Plan for every person to be seated and playing at the same time, with slack. Forty people at an average of five per table is eight tables. Set up ten, so two are always open when a game ends and a group is looking for the next one.

For games, the rule is one game per table plus half again as many in reserve. Ten tables means about fifteen games out, with a few extras in a box. Do not bring forty games. Choice paralysis is real, and a wall of boxes makes people stand and browse instead of sit and play.

Nothing on the table should take longer than 45 minutes. Nothing should take more than five minutes to teach. Catan and Wingspan stay home tonight, not because they are bad but because a 90-minute game locks a table and splits the room into people who are in and people who are waiting.

## How should the room be laid out?

**Tables by type, with signs.** Party games on one side, strategy games in the middle, dexterity near the door where the noise does not matter. A folded card on each table with the game name and player count lets people pick without asking.

**A walkway.** Leave a clear path through the room. Forty people moving between tables need to not climb over chairs.

**Food away from the games.** A food table at one end, games at the other. Nobody holds a plate over a board.

**One table empty on purpose.** Call it the lobby table. People who just arrived or just finished a game sit there until a game opens. A host can see at a glance who needs placing.

**Noise.** Forty people in a hard-floored room is loud. If you have a choice of rooms, take the one with carpet. If you do not, put the party games as far from the strategy tables as the room allows.

## Who do you need to run it?

You cannot host forty people alone. Three roles, filled by people who agreed to them in advance.

**The floor host.** Stands, walks, and places people. Watches for the person standing alone and walks them to a table. Does not sit down to play. This is the most important job in the room and the one the organizer should take.

**Teachers.** One per two or three tables, four or five total. Each teacher knows two or three of the games cold and can explain any of them in under five minutes. They float, teach, and leave. They do not play unless a table is short. Our guide to [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) is the training manual.

**The clock.** One person watching the schedule, calling transitions, and running the big-table game in hour two. Can double as a teacher when nothing is scheduled.

Recruit these people a week out. Ask directly, name the role, and tell them which games to learn. People say yes to a specific job and no to "can you help?"

## What does the schedule look like?

Three hours. Arrival is staggered whether you like it or not, so the first thirty minutes have to work for a room that is half full.

**0:00 to 0:30, arrival.** Party and dexterity tables open. These absorb people in any number and can add a player mid-game. Teachers stand at the strategy tables and start a game as soon as three people sit down. Floor host at the door.

**0:30 to 1:30, open play.** Everything is running. Floor host places people as games end. Teachers float. The clock person gives a five-minute warning before the big game.

**1:30 to 2:00, the big game.** One game that takes 15 to 20 people, announced to the whole room. Werewolf with a good narrator, Two Rooms and a Boom, or a giant round of Wavelength with two teams of ten. People who want to keep their table game going can, but most will come, and the shared event is the thing people remember from the night.

**2:00 to 2:45, open play again.** Second wind. This is when people try the game they were watching earlier. Teachers push the strategy tables because the party crowd has thinned.

**2:45 to 3:00, close.** Announce last games fifteen minutes before the end. Start boxing dexterity games. A hard stop is a kindness to the people who have to drive.

## How do you make a stranger feel placed, not processed?

Forty people means half the room does not know the other half. The techniques that work at a dinner party scale up with some effort.

**Name tags.** Yes. First name, large letters. Nobody likes them and everybody uses them.

**Mixed seating on purpose.** When the floor host places someone, place them at a table with people they came without. Friend groups that sit together all night went to a different event.

**The ask.** The floor host's line is not "do you want to play?" It is "this table needs one more for Azul, come on." A specific invitation gets a yes. An open question gets "maybe later."

**A teacher who leaves.** Once a table knows the game, the teacher walks away. A table of strangers with a teacher hovering stays strangers. A table left alone with a game has to talk.

## What goes wrong at a large game night?

**The browsing wall.** Forty games stacked against a wall and people standing in front of it reading boxes. Fix: games go on tables, not shelves, and the floor host places people.

**The locked table.** Four friends start a long game at 7:15 and are still playing at 9:45. Fix: nothing over 45 minutes on the tables.

**The empty second hour.** Early arrivals finish two games, see nothing new, and leave at 8:30. Fix: the big game at 1:30 and a visible "coming up" sign so people know there is a reason to stay.

**The teacher who plays.** A teacher sits down at the first table and never gets up again. Fix: tell teachers in advance that they do not play until 2:00.

**Noise that kills the strategy tables.** Fix: room layout, and a dexterity zone near the door.

If this is a corporate event, the structure is the same and the stakes of the placement work are higher, since the people who stand alone at a company game night are the ones you most wanted at a table. ## FAQ

### How many games do you need for a game night of 40 people?

About fifteen set out on ten tables, with a handful in reserve. More than that produces browsing instead of playing.

### What games work for a group of 40?

Party games for six to ten (Codenames, Wavelength, Just One), light strategy for three to five (Azul, Ticket to Ride, Kingdomino), a couple of dexterity games, and one game that can take 15 to 20 players at once, like Werewolf or Two Rooms and a Boom.

### How many helpers do you need for a 40-person game night?

Five or six: one floor host who places people, four or five teachers who each know a few games, and one person watching the clock.

### How long should a large group game night run?

Three hours, with a shared big-table game in the middle and a firm end time announced in advance.
