A tribal cube is a cube where the archetypes are creature types instead of strategies. Elves are the ramp deck. Goblins are the aggro deck. Zombies grind, Merfolk tempo, Spirits fly. A drafter who picks up a Goblin lord on pick two knows exactly what the next 43 picks are for, which makes tribal cubes the easiest kind of cube to draft on sight and one of the hardest to build correctly.

The difficulty is math. Tribal payoffs are only good when the tribe is in the deck, and a 40-card deck with 15 creatures needs roughly ten of them to share a type before a lord is better than a bear. That means every tribe needs a lot of members, and a cube only has so many slots. This article is about that math: how many tribes, how many cards each, where changelings go, and what the Lorwyn-era cubes learned by getting it slightly wrong.

## How many tribes should a tribal cube support?

Fewer than you want. The arithmetic for a 360-card, eight-player cube:

- Each drafter sees 45 cards and plays about 15 creatures.
- A tribal deck wants 9 to 12 on-type creatures and 3 to 5 payoffs (lords, type-matters spells).
- To let one drafter reliably assemble that from 45 picks shared with seven others, a tribe needs about 25 to 30 members and 6 to 10 payoffs in the cube.

That is 30 to 40 cards per tribe. In a 360, after lands and removal, that density leaves room for at most eight tribes, and comfortably for fewer. Eight is also the number Lorwyn used (Kithkin, Merfolk, Faeries, Elves, Goblins, Treefolk, Giants, Elementals), which is not a coincidence; the designers were solving the same problem for a 15-card booster.

The public Lorwyn-Morningtide cube discussed at [Riptide Lab](https://riptidelab.com/forum/threads/soshis-lorwyn-morningtide-cube.398) started with five major tribes and four minor groups at a little over 300 cards, and after the first playtests its designer reported that the cube felt powerful but rarely let anyone run a single tribe; one reviewer had already suggested cutting to four or five tribes. That is the signature failure of a tribal cube with too many tribes: everyone ends up with a two-tribe pile and no lord that works.

Our recommendation for a first tribal cube: **six tribes at 360, eight at 540.** Each tribe gets a primary color and a secondary color, arranged so that the ten pairs are covered and each color hosts two or three tribes.

## Which tribes have enough cards?

A tribe needs 25 to 30 playable creatures within one or two colors at a flat power level, plus 6 to 10 payoffs. Not every famous tribe qualifies. The ones we consider deep enough, from our own list-building:

| Tribe | Colors | Depth | Payoff examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elves | G, Gb | Very deep | Elvish Archdruid, Imperious Perfect, Elvish Warmaster |
| Goblins | R, Rb | Very deep | Goblin Chieftain, Goblin Warchief, Krenko, Mob Boss |
| Zombies | B, Ub | Very deep | Lord of the Accursed, Diregraf Captain, Cemetery Reaper |
| Humans | W, Wx | Very deep (too broad) | Thalia's Lieutenant, Champion of the Parish, Hanweir Militia Captain |
| Merfolk | U, Ug | Deep | Lord of Atlantis, Master of the Pearl Trident, Merrow Reejerey |
| Spirits | WU | Deep since Innistrad | Supreme Phantom, Rattlechains, Spectral Sailor |
| Vampires | BR, Bw | Deep | Legion Lieutenant, Stromkirk Captain, Vampire Nocturnus |
| Wizards | UR | Moderate | Naban, Dean of Iteration; Wizard's Lightning; Vodalian Hexcatcher |
| Soldiers / Knights | W, Wr | Deep | Field Marshal, Knight Exemplar, Benalish Marshal |
| Slivers | 5c | Deep but warping | Every Sliver is a lord; see below |

Dinosaurs, Dragons, Angels, and Demons have depth but sit at four to six mana, which makes a whole deck of them a curve problem. Faeries and Rogues are deep but make a tempo deck rather than a tribal one. Slivers deserve a line of their own: a Sliver deck is nine lords and nothing else, and it either dominates or gets every lord Bolted. Most tribal cubes leave Slivers out or run them as a splashable colorless-style changeling package.

## How many members and payoffs per tribe?

The numbers above as a house guideline for a 360 with six tribes:

- **Members:** 26 to 30 creatures that carry the type, spread 1-drop through 5-drop with the curve the tribe's strategy wants. Goblins want eight one- and two-drops; Zombies want more three- and four-drops.
- **Payoffs:** 6 to 8, and at most three of them should be lords. The rest should be type-matters cards that scale rather than anthem: Elvish Visionary-style cantrips, Goblin Matron tutors, Gravecrawler-style recursion, Merfolk Trickster tempo. A cube with nothing but lords plays as "who drew more lords."
- **Type-matters spells:** 2 to 4 noncreature cards per tribe. Lead the Stampede, Goblin Grenade, Door of Destinies-type cards. These are what make a tribe a deck and not a pile.
- **Gold signposts:** 1 to 2 per tribe in its two colors, and they should name the tribe.

Multiply out: 36 to 44 cards per tribe, six tribes, 216 to 264 cards. The remaining 100 to 144 slots in a 360 go to removal, fixing, Equipment, and the changelings.

## Where do changelings go?

Changelings (every creature type at once) are the glue of a tribal cube and the easiest place to overbuild. A changeling is a member of every tribe, which sounds like it fixes the density problem; in practice a cube with 30 changelings is a cube where every deck is the same deck with a different lord.

A house guideline: **8 to 12 changelings in a 360,** each costed and sized so that it is a fine playable without a tribe (Universal Automaton, <!-- AFFILIATE --> Changeling Outcast, Mothdust Changeling, Taurean Mauler, Chameleon Colossus, Mirror Entity, Shapesharer). Put the cheap ones in and leave the expensive ones out, because a cheap changeling helps the tribe hit its density and an expensive one is just a good creature. The Lorwyn cube above ran duplicates of its changelings and wondered how many to include; that question never fully resolves and that is fine.

Also watch the "Kindred" or tribal-type noncreature cards (Kindred Charge, Crib Swap, Nameless Inversion). They count toward the type for cards that care, and they are good removal on their own. Three or four earn a slot.

## How do you keep removal and non-tribal cards from drowning the theme?

A tribal cube still needs about 40 removal spells in a 360, and the removal should answer lords specifically. The Lorwyn cube's thread flagged slightly low removal and an Equipment (Deathrender) that nothing in the cube could answer as problems. Two design responses:

1. **Prefer removal that scales against lords.** Lords are usually 2/2s or 3/3s for three. Lightning Bolt, Fatal Push, Journey to Nowhere, and Reflector Mage all answer them cleanly; Murder-at-three is less exciting. Keep a few sweepers that punish going wide (Pyroclasm, Golgari Charm, Electrickery) at low density so the tribal decks must think about overextending.
2. **Keep off-theme good cards rare.** A tribal cube with 60 slots of generic staples becomes a staples cube where a few lords happen to live. If a card does not carry a tribe's type or kill something, it needs a specific reason to be there.

Equipment deserves a note. It is colorless, goes in any deck, and makes a lord-less draw good anyway, which cuts against the theme. Two or three pieces at most.

## Should the tribes map to the ten color pairs?

Mostly yes, with a twist. Each tribe gets a home color and a secondary color, and the ten pairs get covered by overlap. An example map for six tribes:

- Soldiers: W, secondary R (Boros)
- Spirits: U, secondary W (Azorius)
- Zombies: B, secondary U (Dimir)
- Vampires: B, secondary R (Rakdos)
- Goblins: R, secondary G (Gruul)
- Elves: G, secondary B (Golgari)

That leaves Orzhov, Izzet, Selesnya, and Simic without a dedicated tribe, so a drafter in those colors builds a two-tribe deck (Spirits-Soldiers in WU-WR space works, for example). Alternatively, the four uncovered pairs become the cube's "good stuff" lanes with no tribe, as a pressure valve for drafters who get cut. Decide which, and say so in the primer. The themed-cube rules for [keeping a constraint fun](/articles/themed-cube-ideas/) apply here harder than anywhere else: the constraint must create decks, and a tribe with 18 members does not.

## FAQ

### What is a tribal cube?

A tribal cube is a cube whose archetypes are creature types rather than strategies; each color pair or color supports one tribe with enough members and payoffs to draft a cohesive tribal deck.

### How many tribes should a tribal cube have?

Six at 360 cards and eight at 540, with 25 to 30 members and 6 to 10 payoffs each. More tribes than that and drafters cannot assemble any single one.

### How many changelings should a tribal cube run?

Eight to twelve in a 360, favoring cheap ones. Too many changelings make every tribe interchangeable.

### Which tribes work best in a cube?

Elves, Goblins, Zombies, Merfolk, Spirits, Vampires, and Soldiers or Knights all have the depth. Slivers and big-creature tribes like Dragons are usually left out or run as small packages.
