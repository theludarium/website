The famous cubes are not famous because they are the strongest. They are famous because each one tested a single design idea in public, with a card list anyone could read, and the result settled an argument. This article profiles five: the Ravnica Cube, Nega-Cube, Chromatic Cube, Tinkerer's Cube and the all-changeling cube. Four ran on Magic Online or Arena; the fifth is a paper cube that became an example in the best essay on theme written in years.

For each, we give the designer where the public record names one, the concept, and the lesson. For the general case of how to build around a theme, start with [themed cube ideas](/articles/themed-cube-ideas/). This is about what the experiments showed.

## 1. The Ravnica Cube: a flat power band works if everyone knows it

**Designer:** Usman Jamil, per Ryan Overturf's January 2022 Star City Games preview, ["Everything I Know About Ravnica Cube's Debut on Magic Online"](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/everything-i-know-about-ravnica-cubes-debut-on-magic-online/).

**Concept:** Only cards from the Ravnica sets, built for people who loved drafting those blocks. More than 30% of the list is gold. There are no fetchlands. Overturf called it "among the flatter powerbands that we've seen in the digital Cube offerings" and predicted midrange and control would dominate while aggro struggled, because heavy multicolor, no fetchlands and efficient removal and sweepers favor the player who can wait.

**What it proves:** A flat power band is a legitimate choice, and it is a choice about pace. When no card is a bomb, games are decided by sequencing and mana, three-color decks become normal, and the aggressive drafter needs to know in advance that the environment is not built for them. Niv-Mizzet Reborn and Chromatic Lantern are the signposts; Hero of Precinct One and Battle Screech are the aggro drafter's consolation. The lesson for a paper cube with a flat band: say so in the primer, or your fast-deck players will think the cube is broken when it is only slow.

## 2. Nega-Cube: the leftovers are a whole format

**Designer:** Natalie Weizenbaum, per Overturf's March 2021 preview, ["Everything I Know About Nega-Cube's Magic Online Debut"](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/everything-i-know-about-nega-cubes-magic-online-debut/).

**Concept:** A cube made only of cards that were absent from the major Magic Online cubes. A meta-cube. If a card was in the Vintage, Legacy or Modern Cube, it was out. What remained was Attrition, Blood Artist, Goblin Bombardment, Mind Control, Tidings, Esika's Chariot, Korvold, Glare of Subdual and Precursor Golem.

**What it proves:** The card pool is far deeper than the staples lists suggest, and synergy fills the gap that raw power leaves. Overturf noted the designer's write-up organized the environment by "archetype clusters" rather than the ten color pairs, and that "knowing where the archetypes overlap is every bit as important" as the signposts themselves. Rakdos Sacrifice, white tokens, blue blink and green ramp all worked without a single card most cubers would call a staple. If you have been telling yourself your budget cube needs Ragavan to be good, Nega-Cube is the counter-example.

## 3. Chromatic Cube: give people permission to go big

**Source:** Chris Kvartek's July 2025 Arena announcement, ["Chromatic Cube Draft on MTG Arena"](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/mtg-arena/chromatic-cube-draft-mtg-arena).

**Concept:** A 540-card singleton cube of expensive, many-colored spells, with ramp, removal and early fixing arranged so that the late game arrives reliably. The 2025 update changed 45 of them. The stated goal was "allowing players to consistently play multiple colors and a higher mana value than what is typical," with two guard rails: players "can't afford to do nothing during the first four turns," and they "should not be overly penalized for ramping and playing expensive cards."

**What it proves:** You can push the curve up without breaking the format, but only if you push the fixing and the early plays up with it. The Chromatic Cube's early turns are full of mana rocks, Farseeks and cheap removal precisely so the seven-drops get cast. The common failure of a "big spells" paper cube is that the owner adds the dragons and forgets the Rampant Growths. The Arena list is a working answer to the question of how much support a top-heavy cube needs.

## 4. Tinkerer's Cube: archetypes are optional

**Source:** David McDarby's April 2023 Arena announcement, ["Tinkerer's Cube Draft on MTG Arena"](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/mtg-arena/tinkerers-cube-draft-mtg-arena).

**Concept:** A "synergy cube" of roughly 555 cards (485 nonland and 70 lands by our count of the published list) where the stated goal is finding unusual interactions rather than raw power. The announcement says it has no traditional color-pair archetypes; many cards are described as "a unique archetype amongst themselves." The framing line: "What can you make?"

**What it proves:** The ten-guild structure most cubes use is a convention, not a law. A cube can be built as a web of small engines that overlap in unpredictable ways, and experienced drafters will enjoy the discovery. The trade is onboarding. A first-time drafter in the Tinkerer's Cube has no lane to follow and no signpost to read. If your table is mostly veterans who have drafted your list fifty times, a Tinkerer's-style rebuild of one section is a way to make them look at the cards again. If your table has new players every month, it is the wrong model, and a one-page draft guide for first-timers is a better investment.

## 5. The all-changeling cube: a theme creates its own counter

**Designer:** Tim Myers, described as a Midwest cubehead who has won a CubeCon, in Parker LaMascus's September 2026 Lucky Paper essay, ["The Self-Defeating Irony of Cube Conceits"](https://luckypaper.co/articles/the-self-defeating-irony-of-cube-conceits/).

**Concept:** A cube in which every creature has changeling, so every creature is every creature type, and the tribal payoffs all fire all the time.

**What it proves:** LaMascus's point, with this cube as the lead example, is that over-supplying a theme makes the theme's enablers cheap and the answers to it precious. In a cube where every creature is a lord's best friend, the scarcest and most valuable picks are the removal spells that break the engine. "The more we warp a cube around a single conceit, the more we incentivize our players to beat that theme." He makes the same observation about a friend's two-mana-or-less cube (mana sinks become the best cards) and his own cube, which was a "mono midrange" experience in 2020 until he leaned into blue control, green ramp and combo to add variety and give midrange a reason to exist.

This is the most useful lesson on the list, because it predicts how every themed cube will drift. Drafters are optimizers. Whatever you make abundant, they will learn to beat, and whatever beats it becomes the real theme.

## What the five have in common

| Cube | Single idea tested | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Ravnica Cube | Flat power, heavy gold | Works; favors slow decks; must be announced |
| Nega-Cube | Only non-staples | Works; synergy replaces power |
| Chromatic Cube | High curve, many colors | Works if early plays and fixing scale with it |
| Tinkerer's Cube | No fixed archetypes | Works for veterans; hard for new drafters |
| All-changeling cube | One mechanic everywhere | Drafters optimize against it; answers become the theme |

Every one of these cubes is honest about its premise in the first paragraph of its write-up. That is the common thread. The theme is stated, the trade-offs are stated, and the drafter can decide whether to sit down. ## FAQ

### What is the most famous themed cube?

There is no single answer, but the Magic Online and Arena spotlight cubes (Ravnica Cube, Nega-Cube, Chromatic Cube, Tinkerer's Cube) are among the most widely drafted, because they ran as official events with published card lists.

### Who designed the Ravnica Cube on Magic Online?

Usman Jamil, according to Star City Games' January 2022 coverage of its debut.

### What is a meta-cube?

A cube built in relation to other cubes rather than to a theme. Nega-Cube, made only of cards absent from the major MTGO cubes, is the best-known example.

### Why do themed cubes drift away from their theme?

Because drafters value scarcity. When a theme's pieces are everywhere, the cards that beat the theme become the rare, high-value picks, and the format reorganizes around them.
