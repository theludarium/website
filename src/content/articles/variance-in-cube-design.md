Cube variance gets talked about as if it were weather: something that happens to the games, to be endured or complained about. It is not. Variance in a cube is a setting, and the cube owner is the one who sets it. Every card that goes in either turns the dial up or turns it down, and a cube with a clear design has the dial where the owner wants it.

This piece is about where the dial is, what moves it, and why the right setting is different for different rooms. It is not an argument for low variance or high. It is an argument for choosing.

## Where does variance enter a cube?

Luck gets into a draft at four points, and they are worth separating because they respond to different tools.

**The packs.** Which cards you see is random. In a 360 cube, a drafter sees 45 of 360 cards, an eighth of the list. Whether the Rakdos sacrifice deck is open depends on who opened Mayhem Devil and whether the person to your right is also in black. This is the variance of the draft, and it is mostly set by cube size and lane overlap.

**The draw.** Which cards you draw in the game, and in which order. This is the variance everyone means when they say "I got mana screwed." It is set by land counts, curve, and the number of cards in the cube that smooth draws: cantrips, scry, looting, card selection.

**The matchup.** Which deck you face. A cube with ten lanes has forty-five possible pairings, and some are lopsided. White weenie against a cube's one sweeper-heavy control deck is a bad matchup; the same deck against Simic ramp is a good one. This variance is set by how many lanes exist and how evenly they are matched.

**The cards.** Some cards are themselves random. Coin flips, Chaos Warp, cascade, Possibility Storm. A smaller category, but the one people remember.

Most conversations about cube variance are about the fourth kind, and most of the actual variance in a cube is in the first three. A cube with no coin-flip cards can still be a lottery if it has 540 cards, no fixing, and eight unmatched lanes.

## What turns the dial up?

A short list of things that increase variance, in rough order of how much they matter:

- **Bigger cube, same draft.** A 720 cube drafted by eight people shows each drafter 45 of 720 cards. Lanes are harder to find and harder to complete. The draft itself becomes luckier.
- **Less fixing.** Fewer dual lands means more two-color decks that stumble, which means more games decided by whether the second color showed up. This is the biggest single lever most owners have, and it is covered in [cube mana fixing](/articles/cube-mana-fixing/).
- **Higher power with fewer answers.** When the top cards are far better than the median and there are few ways to deal with them, games are decided by who drew the top cards. Vintage Cube with power and no Force of Will would be very high variance; with Force, it is less so.
- **Uneven lanes.** If aggro beats control and control beats ramp and ramp beats aggro, every matchup is a coin flip the drafters did not choose.
- **Fewer smoothing effects.** A cube without Preordain, Faithless Looting, Ponder, and the scry lands has more games decided by mana.
- **Cascade, discover, and friends.** Bloodbraid Elf is fine. A cube with six cascade cards and a Possibility Storm is a different kind of evening.
- **Swingy finishers.** Cards like Craterhoof Behemoth, Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, and Expropriate turn a close game into a decided one in a single draw step.

## What turns it down?

The inverse, mostly, plus a few specific tools.

- **Smaller cube or bigger packs.** A 360 drafted by eight shows each player an eighth of the list. A 360 with 18-card packs shows more.
- **More fixing.** Fetch lands, shock lands, and a full set of duals mean more decks that function.
- **Flat power.** When the median card and the best card are close, the best deck wins more often than the best draw.
- **Cheap interaction.** Lightning Bolt and Fatal Push are variance reducers because they let the player who drew worse still affect the board.
- **Card selection.** Brainstorm, Preordain, and friends let the player choose from more of their deck.
- **Mulligan rules.** The London mulligan is already a variance reducer. Some cubes add a free first mulligan for seven-card hands with zero or six-plus lands. That is a house rule, not a standard, and it is a clean variance dial that costs nothing.

## Why would anyone want more variance?

Because variance is also what makes a game worth playing for the weaker player, and most rooms have weaker players.

Think about who shows up to a draft night. If the same two people win every week, the other six are not having fun, and they stop coming. A cube with higher variance gives the sixth-best drafter at the table a real chance of winning, which keeps them at the table. A perfectly skill-testing cube is also a cube where the outcome is known before the first pack is opened.

Variance also makes stories. The game where someone cascaded into Emrakul, or topdecked the one out, or drew nine lands in a row, is the game people tell at the next draft. A cube with the dial at zero produces correct games and no stories.

The famous example is the MTGO Vintage Cube, which has the dial fairly high on purpose: power, [fast mana as a core feature](https://mtgo.com/news/may-2024-vintage-cube-update), and games that can end very early. That is its design, and it works because the people who draft it know what they signed up for. A cube for a Thursday night of mixed skill levels should probably sit somewhere lower, but not at the bottom.

## How do you pick a setting?

Ask what the cube is for, which we treated in [what is your cube for](/articles/what-is-your-cube-for/). Then:

| Room | Dial setting | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Eight experienced players who want a test | Low-medium | Flat power, full fixing, cheap interaction, few swingy cards. Let skill decide. |
| A weekly group of mixed skill | Medium | Good fixing, a few swingy finishers, one or two cascade cards. Enough luck that anyone can win a given week. |
| Occasional friends and family | Medium-high | Fewer answers, more big dumb threats, a free mulligan rule. Games should feel like events. |
| A chaos or art cube | High, by choice | Chaos Warp, coin flips, Possibility Storm. The variance is the point. |

The honest problem is that many owners want the first row for themselves and the second row for their group. The group wins that argument, or the group stops showing up.

## How do you tell the dial is wrong?

Too high: the same complaints repeat. "I never cast a spell." "He just drew the Hoof." "My deck was fine, I just flooded." If more than a third of your games are being described as non-games, turn it down.

Too low: the better players win every time, and the games are long and grinding. New people play once and do not return. Nobody has a story from last week. Turn it up.

Cube Commonwealth has moved the dial a few times. The clearest change was adding fixing: when we went from around 20 dual lands to the full 40 in a 540, the number of games decided by mana dropped and attendance rose, because people who had been losing to their own lands started winning some. <!-- EDITOR: confirm --> We also keep two or three swingy cards per color, cards like Sneak Attack and Through the Breach, because the room likes the stories. That is not a cube theory. It is a room.

## FAQ

### Is variance bad in cube?

No. Variance is a setting the designer chooses. Too little makes a cube predictable and discouraging for weaker players; too much makes games feel like they were not played.

### What cube cards add the most variance?

Mostly not the obvious ones. Poor fixing, a too-large cube, and a wide gap between the best and median cards add more variance than any coin-flip card.

### How do I reduce variance in my cube?

Add fixing, flatten power, add cheap interaction and card selection, and consider a free mulligan for unkeepable sevens.

### Should a casual cube have more variance than a competitive one?

Usually, yes. A casual room benefits from games any player can win and from memorable swings. A competitive room wants the better drafter to win more often.

## Set it on purpose

Variance is going to be in your cube either way. The only question is whether you decide where. Count your fixing, look at your power gap, and move the dial toward the room you actually have. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
