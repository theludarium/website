The Pauper Cube is a commons-only Magic cube that has been in continuous public development since 2008, which makes it one of the longest-running cube projects anywhere. Its home is [thepaupercube.com](https://www.thepaupercube.com/), its list lives on [CubeCobra](https://cubecobra.com/cube/list/thepaupercube), and it is updated twice a year by a small team rather than a single owner.

If the MTGO Vintage Cube is the format's ceiling, the Pauper Cube is its proof that the ceiling is not where the fun lives. This profile covers its origin, how it is maintained, its ten archetypes, and what a club like ours borrows from it.

## Where did the Pauper Cube come from?

The site's own history is brief: the cube was "originally created by Adam Styborski in 2008." It started as a multicolor cube of commons and, in the site's words, has become "a collection of synergistic cards that encourage repeatable and varied play across each of the different color pairings."

That shift, from a pile of the best commons to a designed environment with ten lanes, is the Pauper Cube's whole story. Early pauper cubes were power-ranked lists of Lightning Bolt, Counterspell, Savannah Lions and Rancor. The site still names those four as featured cards. What changed is that the list around them became deliberate.

Styborski holds the copyright on the site, but the credits list others: Eric Klug for initial development, with Alex Ullman, Usman Jamil and Craig Sybert credited for additional development, along with the fans. The current blog posts are written under the handle omniczech. That spread of names is unusual for a cube and is the first thing worth noticing about it.

## How is the Pauper Cube maintained?

Two things set it apart from a personal cube.

**A fixed public cadence.** The site states the cube is updated "twice annually, roughly in April and October," with a possible third update when a year needs it. The blog bears that out: a Spring 2025 hotfix in May 2025, "Quick Hits" posts after Tarkir: Dragonstorm, Final Fantasy and Edge of Eternities, a Fall 2025 update at the end of October, and a Spring 2026 update posted May 16, 2026.

**Community voting.** The navigation links to a separate polling site where players vote on cards. <!-- EDITOR: confirm how the poll feeds the update and describe it in one sentence --> Feedback also runs through a public Discord and an email address. Decisions are still made by the team, but the input is structured rather than ad hoc.

The construction rule is written down too. Any card with common legality qualifies, but it must have a physical printing as a standalone card, so no digital-only or conjured cards. The site's author runs 40 to 50 copies of each basic land because the cube includes common dual lands.

That is a complete governance model in four sentences: who decides, when, by what rule, with what input. Most cubes have none of it written down. Keeping a changelog is the first step toward getting there.

## What are the Pauper Cube's archetypes?

The site's [archetype guide](https://thepaupercube.com/solsets-archetype-guide/) assigns one lane to each color pair.

| Pair | Archetype | Example cards named in the guide |
|---|---|---|
| Azorius | Blink | Momentary Blink, Mnemonic Wall |
| Dimir | Control | Dinrova Horror, Pestilence |
| Rakdos | Aristocrats | Carrion Feeder, Plagued Rusalka |
| Gruul | Midrange monsters | Generous Ent, Oliphaunt |
| Selesnya | +1/+1 counters | Topan Freeblade, Nyxborn Hydra |
| Orzhov | Life drain | Tithe Drinker, Pillory of the Sleepless |
| Izzet | Spells matter | Guttersnipe, Murmuring Mystic |
| Golgari | Graveyard recursion | Gurmag Angler, Pulse of Murasa |
| Boros | Go-wide aggro | Battle Screech, Rally the Peasants |
| Simic | Tempo-ramp | Coiling Oracle, Krosan Tusker |

The guide also states a few numbers as design facts: about a third of white's creatures cost two or less, over a third of blue's cards draw a card, and a Golgari deck with lots of self-mill may want a list a bit bigger than 40 cards. Those are house guidelines for this cube, not standards, but they show the designers counting.

The guide's most quotable idea is that commons exclude "the extremities of magic": no unconditional sweepers, no planeswalkers, no giant catch-up creatures. So most decks are two-color midrange, the gap between aggro and control is small, and games are won by "the compounding nature of small strategic decisions." If you are deciding between this style and a powered list, [pauper cube vs vintage cube](/articles/pauper-cube-vs-vintage-cube/) lays out the trade.

## Why does a commons cube have this much staying power?

Three reasons, all of them about people rather than cards.

**It is cheap to own and cheap to copy.** The site invites anyone to clone the CubeCobra list and change it. Because nearly every card is a common, a newcomer can assemble a real, tested environment for far less than any powered or Legacy-level list. That low floor is why so many groups start with it, including ours in its earliest form. <!-- EDITOR: confirm whether Commonwealth's first cube was pauper -->

**It is boring in the right way.** Twice a year, on schedule, with a post explaining the changes. No surprise overhauls. Players can learn the environment and trust that it will still be mostly the same next season.

**It has outlived any single person's attention.** A cube with one owner dies when that owner moves, has a child or gets bored. A cube with a team, a site, a Discord and a published rule set does not. The names in the credits have changed over eighteen years. The cube has not stopped.

That last point is the one The Ludarium cares most about. A cube community that depends on one person's box is fragile. [The community cube](/articles/community-owned-cube/) is our own attempt to spread the ownership around, and the Pauper Cube is the model we point to.

## What to borrow, and what not to

Borrow:

- **One lane per pair, written down.** The guide format, a paragraph and a handful of example cards per pair, is the most usable primer structure we know.
- **The calendar.** Two updates a year with a named month each. Your drafters will plan around it.
- **The construction rule.** "Commons with a real paper printing" is one sentence. Write yours.
- **The basic-land note.** Telling drafters to bring 40 to 50 of each basic is a small courtesy that prevents a real problem.

Do not borrow the list wholesale if your group is not a pauper group. The archetypes are tuned to what commons can do. Battle Screech is a Boros payoff because there is no Hero of Bladehold at common. In a higher-power cube those lanes would need different signposts, and our article on signpost cards explains why.

## FAQ

### What is the Pauper Cube?

A commons-only Magic cube created by Adam Styborski in 2008 and maintained since by a team, with its list on CubeCobra and updates published at thepaupercube.com roughly every April and October.

### How many cards are in the Pauper Cube?

The site does not state a fixed number; the CubeCobra list is the source of truth and it is large enough that the site warns that acquiring every card can be difficult. <!-- EDITOR: check CubeCobra count and insert -->

### Can any common go in the Pauper Cube?

Any card with common legality qualifies, as long as it has a physical standalone printing. Digital-only and conjured cards are excluded.

### Where do I give feedback on the Pauper Cube?

Through the project's Discord, its polling site, or the contact email listed on thepaupercube.com.
