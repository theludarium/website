The best games to play while the turkey cooks need no table, no setup, and no rulebook. Spoken word games like Contact and Twenty Questions, pass-around games like [Telestrations](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F699VTB2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Just One](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07GJ7NDZQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and a pencil-and-paper round of Categories all work from a couch, a kitchen stool, or the floor. Everyone can play, and anyone can step away to baste.

The dining table is busy on Thanksgiving. It is getting set, or it is holding pies, or it is about to. That leaves the hours before dinner with a full house and nowhere to spread out a board. These games fill that gap.

**The quick answer:** for a no-equipment start, play Contact or Twenty Questions. For a box you can play on laps, bring Just One, Telestrations, or [Wavelength](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07T446163?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a big group that wants something rowdy, play Fishbowl with scrap paper and a mixing bowl.

## What makes a good game for the hours before dinner?

The hours before a holiday meal are crowded and choppy. People arrive in waves. The cook is in and out. Kids want something to do, and grandparents want to sit down.

A game that works here has a few traits:

- **No table.** You play from a couch, a kitchen island, or a lap.
- **No setup.** If it takes ten minutes to lay out, the oven timer will beat you.
- **Easy to join and leave.** Someone always needs to check on the gravy.
- **Short rounds.** Two to ten minutes, so the game survives interruptions.

Save the board games for after dinner. Our guide to [Thanksgiving board games](/articles/thanksgiving-board-games/) covers the table games for that part of the day.

## Which games need nothing at all?

These need only voices, and sometimes a scrap of paper. They are perfect when the kitchen is full and everyone is standing.

### Twenty Questions

One person thinks of a thing. Everyone else gets twenty yes-or-no questions to figure out what it is. The classic first question is "Is it an animal, vegetable, or mineral?" though that one is not yes or no, so most groups allow it as a freebie. Ages five and up can play, which makes it the best mixed-age pick on this list.

### Contact

A word game for groups of three or more who like puzzles. One leader thinks of a secret word and says only its first letter. Everyone else tries to think of other words that start with that letter and gives clues for them, such as "a fruit that keeps the doctor away." If another player thinks they know the word, they call "Contact!" and both count down from five, then say the word together. If they match before the leader guesses it, the leader has to reveal the next letter. The group wins by finding the secret word. It sounds complicated and clicks in one round.

### Fishbowl

A party game for big groups, with nothing but paper and a bowl. Everyone writes three to five names or nouns on slips and drops them in the bowl. Split into two teams. Over three rounds, players pull slips and get their team to guess them within a time limit: first with any words they like, then with just one word, then with charades only. The same slips come back each round, so the jokes build. It is the homemade version of Monikers.

### Ghost

A spelling game for older kids and adults. Players take turns adding one letter to a growing word fragment. The goal is to avoid being the one who completes a real word of four letters or more. If you think someone added a letter with no real word in mind, you can challenge them. Each time you lose a round, you earn a letter of G-H-O-S-T. Spell the whole word and you are out.

### Gratitude web with a ball of yarn

This one is less a game than a ritual, and it fits the day. Sit or stand in a circle. The first person holds the loose end of a ball of yarn, says one thing they are thankful for, and tosses the ball to someone across the circle while keeping hold of their strand. By the end, everyone holds a piece of a web. It takes five minutes and works with toddlers and grandparents in the same circle.

## Which boxed games can you play on a couch?

Some boxed games travel well because everything happens in your hand or on your lap. Bring one of these to the living room.

| Game | Players | Time | What you need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Just One | 3 to 7 | 20 min | Easels on laps | Mixed ages, cooperative |
| Telestrations | 4 to 8 (12 with Party Pack) | 30 min | Sketchbooks on laps | Laughs with any group |
| Wavelength | 2 to 12 | 30 min | One dial passed around | Adults and teens who like to argue |
| [Herd Mentality](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093HBBMPT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) | 4 to 20 | 20 to 30 min | Pads and pencils | A big crowd |
| [The Mind](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07C4F3KLF?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) | 2 to 4 | 15 min | A hand of cards and one cushion | A quiet corner |

### Just One

Just One is a cooperative word game. One player is the guesser. Everyone else secretly writes a one-word clue for the mystery word on a small easel. Before the guesser looks, the clue-givers compare, and any identical clues are thrown out. It rewards picking a clue that is good but not too obvious. Everything fits on your lap.

### Telestrations

Telestrations is telephone with pictures. Each player gets a small dry-erase sketchbook and a secret word. Draw your word, pass the book, guess the next drawing, pass again, and keep going. At the end, flip through each book and see how "pumpkin pie" turned into "a sad moon." No table needed, and no drawing skill needed either.

### Wavelength

Wavelength is a team game about reading minds. One player sees a hidden target on a dial between two ideas, such as "cold to hot" or "useless to useful." They give one clue. Their team talks it out and turns the dial to where they think the target is. Points go to how close they land. The device passes hand to hand, so it plays fine from a sofa.

### Herd Mentality

Herd Mentality asks a question, such as "What is the best pie?" Everyone writes an answer. If you match the most common answer, you earn a cow. The goal is to think like the group, not to be clever. It seats up to twenty, so it handles a full house.

### The Mind

The Mind is a quiet cooperative card game. Players hold numbered cards and try to play them onto a single pile in rising order without talking. A sofa cushion works as the pile. It is the right pick for the two or three people who would rather sit in a calm corner.

## Which pencil-and-paper games work for a crowd?

Grab a stack of index cards and a few pens. These games need nothing else.

**Categories.** Pick a letter and five categories, such as "a food," "a city," and "something in a kitchen." Everyone writes one answer per category that starts with the letter, against a two-minute timer. Answers nobody else wrote score a point. It is the homemade version of Scattergories, and kids can play with easier categories.

**Exquisite corpse.** Fold a sheet into three. The first person draws a head and folds it out of sight, leaving a few lines showing. The next draws a body. The last draws legs. Unfold for the reveal. It is silly, and it gives restless kids something to do at the kitchen counter.

**Hangman.** You know it already. It still works, and it is a good fallback for a child who wants a game with one adult while everyone else is busy.

## How do you mix games with cooking?

A Thanksgiving kitchen runs on timers. Games can run on them too.

- **Start the game where the cook is.** Contact and Twenty Questions work across a kitchen island, so the cook can play while stirring.
- **Pick games with a natural pause.** Rounds of Just One or Fishbowl end every few minutes, so people can drop out to set the table and come back.
- **Give kids a job in the game.** Timer keeper, slip shuffler, or score writer. Kids with a job stay in the room.
- **Stop when dinner is ready.** Do not try to finish. Promise a rematch after pie.

## FAQ

### What is the best game to play while cooking Thanksgiving dinner?

Twenty Questions or Contact. Both need no equipment, both work standing up, and the cook can join from the stove.

### What games can you play without a table?

Spoken games like Twenty Questions, Contact, and Fishbowl need nothing. Boxed games like Just One, Telestrations, Wavelength, and Herd Mentality all play on laps.

### What is a good no-setup game for kids and adults together?

Twenty Questions works from about age five. Telestrations is also a strong mixed-age pick, because a bad drawing is part of the fun.

### How long should pre-dinner games last?

Keep each one short, about five to twenty minutes. That leaves room for interruptions and for the meal itself.
