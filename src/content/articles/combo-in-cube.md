Combo in cube is a policy decision, not a card decision. Every cube owner lands in one of three places: combo is a supported archetype with enough redundancy to draft on purpose, combo is tolerated as something that occasionally assembles itself, or combo is designed out. All three are defensible. What is not defensible is pretending to be in one place while the card list says another.

This article names the combos that actually show up in cube, counts how many pieces each one needs, and lays out what each of the three policies requires of your list.

## What counts as combo in a cube?

A combo deck wins by assembling specific cards rather than by attacking with creatures or grinding card advantage. In cube the useful distinction is between **two-card kills**, where two specific cards together end the game, and **engines**, where a card generates so much value that the game ends a few turns later.

Two-card kills are the policy question. Engines are a power-level question covered in [card advantage engines](/articles/card-advantage-in-cube/).

The second distinction is between combos that are **hard-locked** to specific cards and combos that are **slot-based**, where any of several cards fills each role. Slot-based combos are the only ones draftable on purpose in a singleton format. That is the whole design insight: in a cube with one copy of each card, a combo needs redundancy on both halves or it will not be drafted, only stumbled into.

## Which combos actually appear in cube?

**Sneak and Show.** Show and Tell (three mana, each player puts a card from hand onto the battlefield) or Sneak Attack (four mana, pay one red to put a creature into play with haste, sacrificed at end of turn) plus a creature worth cheating: Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Griselbrand, Atraxa, Archon of Cruelty. Two slots, each with three to five cards. Highly draftable, and the deck still has to survive to turn three or four.

**Reanimator.** A reanimation spell (Reanimate, Animate Dead, Exhume, Necromancy, Persist) plus a way to get the creature into the graveyard (Entomb, Buried Alive, Faithless Looting, Thrill of Possibility, or just discarding to hand size) plus the same creature pool as above. Three slots, each deep. The MTGO Vintage Cube's May 2026 update put Exhume back in while calling it "currently the worst reanimation spell in the Cube," specifically for redundancy ([source](https://www.mtgo.com/news/sos-vintage-cube-update)). That is what supporting combo looks like: adding a weaker piece because the lane needs a sixth copy.

**Splinter Twin.** Splinter Twin or Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker plus Pestermite, Deceiver Exarch, Zealous Conscripts, Restoration Angel or Felidar Guardian. Both halves are enchant-or-creature that untaps or copies. Infinite hasty tokens. Two slots, three or four cards each, and every piece is a playable card on its own, which is the gold standard for cube combo design.

**Thassa's Oracle and friends.** Thassa's Oracle (two mana, enters and wins if your library is smaller than your devotion to blue) plus Demonic Consultation or Tainted Pact (exile your library to find a named card) wins on the spot for three or four mana total. The Oracle is in the current MTGO list; Consultation is not. Oracle plus Underworld Breach plus Brain Freeze is the other version.

**Tinker.** Tinker (three mana, sacrifice an artifact, fetch any artifact onto the battlefield) plus a single target such as Blightsteel Colossus or Bolas's Citadel. One card plus one target. The closest cube gets to a one-card combo, and the reason so many unpowered lists cut either Tinker or the targets.

**Storm and Breach.** Underworld Breach (two mana, cards in your graveyard gain escape for the turn), Yawgmoth's Will, rituals, Lion's Eye Diamond, Brain Freeze, Tendrils of Agony. The most piece-hungry combo and the only one that needs fast mana to function. If you cut tier-two fast mana (see [fast mana in cube](/articles/fast-mana-in-cube/)), storm leaves with it.

**Oath of Druids.** One enchantment plus any big creature plus a deck that plays no other creatures. Draftable in powered cubes, unsupportable in anything else.

**Fastbond and Zuran Orb, Auriok Salvagers and Lion's Eye Diamond, Witherbloom Apprentice and Chain of Smog.** The current MTGO designers have been adding two-card infinites that few paper cubes copy. The August 2025 update kept Crucible of Worlds "to keep redundancy for the Fastbond + Zuran Orb combo" ([source](https://www.mtgo.com/news/eoe-vintage-cube-update)), and the October 2026 update added Auriok Salvagers because the client can now automate the loop. Paper cubes should notice the reasoning rather than copy the cards: supported combo means every half has backups.

## How many pieces does each combo need?

| Combo | Slot A (and depth) | Slot B (and depth) | Needs fast mana? | Pieces playable alone? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sneak and Show | Show and Tell, Sneak Attack, Through the Breach (3) | Emrakul, Griselbrand, Atraxa, Archon (4+) | No | Half |
| Reanimator | Reanimate, Animate Dead, Exhume, Necromancy (4+) | Same creature pool, plus Entomb and looters (6+) | No | Half |
| Splinter Twin | Twin, Kiki-Jiki (2) | Exarch, Pestermite, Conscripts, Resto, Felidar (5) | No | Yes |
| Oracle | Thassa's Oracle (1) | Consultation, Tainted Pact, Breach plus Brain Freeze (2 to 3) | No | No |
| Tinker | Tinker (1) | Blightsteel, Citadel, Myr Battlesphere (2 to 3) | No | Target only |
| Storm | Breach, Will, Tendrils (3) | Rituals, LED, Lotus (6+) | Yes | No |

Depth counts are typical for a 540-card Vintage-band cube and are a house guideline. The last column is the one to read closely. Combos whose pieces are good cards on their own (Twin, most of Reanimator) are safe to support, because a drafter who does not assemble them still has a deck. Combos whose pieces are blank outside the combo (Oracle, Storm) punish the drafter who commits early and misses.

## Policy one: supported

You want combo to be a lane drafters can pick on purpose. Requirements:

- Each half of each supported combo has at least three cards, ideally four.
- Enablers (looters, tutors, cantrips) are dense enough that the deck functions on turns one through three.
- The rest of the table has answers: graveyard hate (Scavenging Ooze, Containment Priest), counterspells, discard, and cheap removal for Twin creatures.
- You name the supported combos in your primer so a new drafter knows they exist.

Supported combo is what the MTGO Vintage Cube does. It is a lot of slots, usually 30 to 40 in a 540, and it is the most rewarding for experienced drafters.

## Policy two: tolerated

You do not build lanes, but you do not remove incidental combos either. Twin and Exarch can both be in the cube because both are fine cards. Reanimate can be in the cube because Griselbrand is a fine Sneak Attack target. You accept that once a season someone assembles something.

This is the most common policy by default and the least common by intention. It works if you accept the one rule: every piece must be a playable card on its own. Under this policy, Thassa's Oracle, Demonic Consultation and Tinker go, because they are blanks outside their combo, and Splinter Twin stays.

## Policy three: designed out

No two-card kills at all. Each candidate combo loses one half entirely. Sneak Attack stays, Show and Tell goes. Kiki-Jiki stays, Exarch and Pestermite go. Reanimate stays, Griselbrand goes. The games become board-centric and the draft gets simpler, which is the right answer for a teaching cube or a Pauper list.

The cost is a flatter draft. Combo is the archetype that rewards the drafter who reads the cube rather than the pack. Removing it removes a skill axis, and the slots it frees usually go to a third midrange lane or more removal.

## FAQ

### Is Splinter Twin fair in cube?

More than most combos. Both halves are playable on their own, the combo costs seven mana over two turns, and any instant-speed removal breaks it. It is the model for a supported cube combo.

### Is Thassa's Oracle too strong for cube?

It is less strong than it is unpleasant. The combo costs three or four mana and the pieces are blanks otherwise. Cubes that keep it do so because it is a known lane, not because it is fair.

### How many combo slots should a cube have?

Supported combo usually takes 30 to 40 slots in a 540-card cube, counting enablers. Tolerated combo takes zero extra slots. These are house guidelines.

### Do combo decks hurt aggro?

Yes, if they are faster than aggro. Twin on turn four and Reanimator on turn two or three both race a two-drop deck. Supporting combo means also supporting cheap interaction so aggro has a way to fight.

## Pick a policy and write it down

Whichever of the three you choose, put it in your primer in one sentence so drafters know what they are drafting into.
