The best Catan: Seafarers scenario for most groups is **Heading for New Shores**, the first map in the box: a familiar island in the middle, small islands across the water, and bonus points for anyone who sails out and settles them. After that, play **Through the Desert** for the best mix of land and sea, **The Four Islands** for the sharpest opening decision, and **Cloth for Catan** when your group wants a scenario that scores in a completely new way. Every one of them needs [Catan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plus the [Seafarers expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and every one has a matching playbook lesson in our free [Seafarers course](/academy/catan-seafarers/).

**The quick answer:** start with **Heading for New Shores**. Play **Through the Desert** next. Save **The Pirate Islands** and **The Wonders of Catan** for a group that already knows ships cold, and use **New World** once you have played the fixed maps and want endless replay.

Still deciding whether to buy? Our guide to [the best Catan expansions](/articles/best-catan-expansions/) covers the whole line, and [Seafarers or Cities & Knights](/articles/seafarers-vs-cities-and-knights/) settles the most common buying question.

## How we ranked the scenarios

The 2025 sixth edition rulebook splits the campaign in two. Scenarios 1 to 4 use only the core Seafarers rules: ships, gold fields, the pirate, and bonus points for reaching new land. Scenarios 5 to 8 each add rules of their own, and the New World variant builds a random map. Each has separate 3 player and 4 player setups, and the rulebook suggests playing the fixed, balanced map before any random setup.

This ranking is a judgment based on what each map's rules reward. We weighed teach cost, how many real choices the map creates, whether a player who starts badly can still steer the game, and replay value.

## The top three: start here

### 1. Heading for New Shores

**Victory condition:** 14 VP. **The twist:** your starting settlements go on the main island, and the first settlement you build on each small island earns you 2 extra VP. Other players settling there first does not take the bonus away from you.

This is the scenario that teaches Seafarers. The main island plays like the Catan everyone knows, so the table can lean on habits while the sea does its work. The new question is simple and constant: spend your wood and wool on a ship toward the small islands, or on one more road at home? Gold fields pay a resource of your choice, which makes any trip toward one tempting.

**Why it ranks first:** the lowest teach cost and still a real decision on every turn. Because each player can claim the island bonus on every small island, nobody gets locked out of the sea, which keeps first games friendly. It is also one of the two scenarios the Cities & Knights rulebook names as working well when you combine the expansions. The walkthrough is in our [Heading for New Shores](/academy/catan-seafarers/heading-for-new-shores/) lesson, and the rules are in [how to play Seafarers](/academy/catan-seafarers/how-to-play-seafarers/).

### 2. Through the Desert

**Victory condition:** 14 VP. **The twist:** a band of three desert hexes cuts the largest island in two. Everyone starts on the bigger side. The thin strip of land beyond the desert and each of the small islands count as unexplored regions, and your first settlement in each one is worth 2 extra VP.

You can march roads the long way around the desert or sail to the outer islands, and this map makes both answers reasonable. Our lesson on [ships or roads](/academy/catan-seafarers/ships-or-roads/) is built around exactly this decision.

**Why it ranks second:** it has the best balance of land and sea in the box,. It is the other scenario the Cities & Knights rulebook recommends for combined play. See the [Through the Desert playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/through-the-desert/) for an opening plan.

### 3. The Four Islands

**Victory condition:** 13 VP. **The twist:** there is no main island. You may place your two starting settlements on one island or split them across two, and those become your home islands. Every other island is unexplored for you, and your first settlement on each one is worth 2 extra VP.

The opening is the whole game here. Two settlements on one island give you a tight economy; splitting them gives you more reach. Then the race is on for the islands that are unexplored for you.

**Why it ranks third:** the most interesting opening decision of the basic four, with no extra rules. It is also our pick for three players: each island bonus is shared by fewer rivals, so a player who chooses a quiet home island still has room to grow. It is a weaker choice for combining with Cities & Knights, which the rulebook warns against for maps with many small islands. The [Four Islands playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-four-islands/) covers both openings. In the 5 to 6 player extension, this scenario becomes The Six Islands.

## The deep ones: new rules, new scoring

### 4. Cloth for Catan

**Victory condition:** 14 VP, or the game ends when five or more villages have run out of cloth at the end of a turn, and the player with the most VP wins (most cloth breaks a tie). **The twist:** the small islands hold villages, each with its own number disc and a supply of five cloth tokens. You may not settle the small islands. Instead, connect a route from one of your buildings to a village. You take one cloth right away, and from then on you take one cloth from that village every time its number is rolled. Every pair of cloth is worth 1 VP.

Everyone places three starting settlements, there is no Longest Route, and routes that reach a village are locked in place. You cannot move the pirate until you have reached a village yourself, and then it may steal cloth.

**Why it ranks fourth:** it is the most strategic scenario in the box. The cloth economy runs beside the normal one, so you can win by building, by trading with villages, or by draining the villages to end the game on your terms. A leader wants the villages empty; a player behind wants them full. It ranks below the top three only because of its teach. The [Cloth for Catan playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/cloth-for-catan/) works through the timing.

### 5. The Wonders of Catan

**Victory condition:** finish building your wonder, or have 10 or more VP while your wonder is a higher level than every other player's. **The twist:** five wonder tiles sit beside the board, each with a requirement. Some need a building at a marker on the map, some need cities, one needs a port city and a five-piece trade route. Meet a requirement and you may claim that wonder for yourself alone; each player claims only one. You pay a starting cost, then build four levels that each cost the same five resources.

There is no pirate, and the first settlement on each small island is worth just 1 extra VP.

**Why it ranks fifth:** it gives Catan something it rarely has, a visible finish line that everyone can watch you approach. A player behind on points can still win by building, which keeps more of the table in the game. Its weakness is that wonder levels eat resources fast, so dice luck matters at the end. The [Wonders of Catan playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-wonders-of-catan/) compares the five wonders.

## Situational picks: great for the right table

### 6. The Fog Islands

**Victory condition:** 12 VP. **The twist:** part of the board starts empty. When you place a road or ship next to an intersection that borders an empty space, you flip the top hex from a face-down stack and place it there. Land gets a random number disc, and you take one resource of the type that hex produces. Sea is just sea.

**Why it ranks sixth:** it is exciting, but it leans on luck. A player who flips a strong hex near home gets a lift that the others did not earn by planning, and nobody can count pips on land they have not seen. It also has no island bonus points, which makes it a shorter race to 12. The [Fog Islands playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-fog-islands/) explains how to time your discoveries, and [exploring for the bonus points](/academy/catan-seafarers/exploring-for-bonus-points/) covers the general skill.

### 7. The Forgotten Tribe

**Victory condition:** 13 VP. **The twist:** you start on a large island, and settlements may only be built on hexes that have a number disc. Out at sea, eight VP tokens, four face-down development cards, and several ports sit on edges around small islands. Build or move a ship onto one of those edges and you claim it. VP tokens are worth 1 VP each. Development cards count as if you just bought them. A port you collect goes next to one of your coastal settlements.

The robber may only move to hexes with number discs. Because ships can be moved, one ship can sweep several prizes over a few turns.

**Why it ranks seventh:** it is a fun treasure hunt, but the prizes are first come, first served, so the game can tilt toward whoever gets boats out earliest. That is a real skill, and the [Forgotten Tribe playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-forgotten-tribe/) teaches it, but it gives a slow starter fewer ways back than the maps above.

### 8. The Pirate Islands

**Victory condition:** capture the pirate fortress in your color and have 10 or more VP. **The twist:** this is a battle scenario. Out west, each player's pirate fortress sits on three pirate lair tokens. A pirate fleet circles the middle of the board after every roll, moving by the lower die, and attacks any player with a building next to where it stops, at a strength equal to that die.

You fight back with warships. You build one unbranched line of ships along the shortest path to your beachhead and fortress, and Knight cards turn ships in that line into warships. Once the line arrives, you roll a die at the end of your turn: beat it with your warship count to remove a lair token, or lose ships. Clear all three and the fortress becomes your settlement. There is no robber, Longest Route, or Largest Army.

**Why it ranks eighth:** it is the most dramatic map in the box, and groups who want conflict should try it. But it is also the most scripted: everyone builds the same kind of line toward the same kind of goal, and the fortress fight is a die roll. The [Pirate Islands playbook](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-islands/) covers how many warships to bring before you attack, and [the pirate and the robber](/academy/catan-seafarers/the-pirate-and-the-robber/) explains the pirate in the other scenarios.

## The variant

### 9. New World

**Victory condition:** 12 VP. **The twist:** there is no fixed map. You shuffle the listed hexes and place them at random, then agree as a table whether to shift any to change the size or number of islands. Red number discs are moved apart if they land next to each other, and players take turns placing random ports. Like The Four Islands, you choose home islands with your starting settlements, and your first settlement on each other island is worth 1 extra VP.

**Why it ranks ninth:** New World has the most replay value of anything in the box, but it ranks last as a starting point for exactly that reason. Random maps can be lopsided, and reading one well takes practice with the fixed maps first. Once your group has played the scenarios above, it becomes the one you return to most. ## FAQ

### How many scenarios are in Catan: Seafarers?

The 2025 sixth edition has eight scenarios plus the New World variant for random maps. This guide follows the sixth edition; if you own an older printing, check its rulebook, since scenario lists can differ between editions.

### What is the hardest Seafarers scenario?

Cloth for Catan asks the most of your planning, because it adds a second economy and a way to end the game early. The Pirate Islands has the most new rules, but its battles come down to dice.

### Which Seafarers scenario is best with Cities & Knights?

The Cities & Knights rulebook recommends Heading for New Shores and Through the Desert. It warns against scenarios that reveal hidden parts of the map or spread players across many small islands, such as The Fog Islands and The Four Islands, because they make the barbarians much stronger. Our lesson on [barbarians and defending Catan](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarians-and-defending-catan/) explains why.
