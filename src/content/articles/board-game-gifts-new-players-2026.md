## For families

### Ticket to Ride

Players collect colored train cards and spend matching sets to claim routes across a map of North America, trying to connect the cities on their secret tickets. Longer routes score more. Tickets you fail to finish count against you, which gives every game a little late tension.

**Who it suits:** a family with kids eight and up, or grandparents who like a game with a board they can see. The box says ages 8 and up, 2 to 5 players, 30 to 60 minutes.

**Why it works as a gift:** it looks good on a table, the turn is one of three simple choices, and nobody has to be a "gamer" to enjoy watching their line of trains stretch across the country. Days of Wonder refreshed the base game in 2025, so newer copies come with the current rules.

**Price:** about $40 to $45 at major retailers. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $39.99 at Walmart and Miniature Market and $43.99 at Target (October 2026) --> See the [Ticket to Ride page at Days of Wonder](https://www.daysofwonder.com/game/ticket-to-ride/).

**The free teacher:** [Coast to Coast](/academy/ticket-to-ride/), our full Ticket to Ride course. Lesson one covers the rules, lesson two is a ten-minute teach, and the emails go on to when to draw, when to claim, and when a ticket is worth the risk.

### Carcassonne

Each turn you draw a land tile, add it to the map so the edges match, and may place one of your meeples on a road, city, monastery, or field to claim it. The countryside grows under everyone's hands, and by the end the table has built a little medieval France together.

**Who it suits:** families with kids seven and up, and anyone who likes puzzles more than conflict. 2 to 5 players, 30 to 45 minutes.

**Why it works as a gift:** the current Z-Man edition includes two small expansions in the box, The River and The Abbot, so it grows with the player. It is also one of the best two-player games in this price range, which makes it a safe pick for a household of any size.

**Price:** about $32 at most major retailers. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $31.99 at Asmodee North America, Target, and Walmart (October 2026) --> See the [Carcassonne page at Z-Man Games](https://www.zmangames.com/game/carcassonne/).

**The free teacher:** [Tile by Tile](/academy/carcassonne/), our full Carcassonne course. It teaches the game without farmers first, the way the rulebook suggests, and adds them once the table is ready.

## For couples

### Azul

Players take all the tiles of one color from a small display, place them in rows on their own board, and each full row sends one tile to a wall, where tiles that touch score more. Tiles you cannot use fall to the floor and cost points.

**Who it suits:** couples, and any pair who wants a game that is calm on the surface and sharp underneath. 2 to 4 players, 30 to 45 minutes, ages 8 and up. At two players, taking the tile your partner needs becomes half the game.

**Why it works as a gift:** the tiles are heavy, bright, and pleasant to hold, so it looks like a present the moment the lid comes off. It also teaches in about ten minutes and plays in under an hour, which fits a weeknight after dinner.

**Price:** about $32. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $31.99 lowest (October 2026) --> See the [Azul page at Next Move Games](https://www.nextmove-games.com/en/azul/azul-game/).

**The free teacher:** [Wall to Wall](/academy/azul/), our full Azul course, from your first pick to your final bonus.

## For the friend who loves birds

### Wingspan

Each player attracts birds to three habitats on their own board: forest, grassland, and wetland. Every bird you play makes one of your actions stronger, so a slow start turns into a chain of food, eggs, and cards by the last round. The base game has 170 different real birds, each on its own card.

**Who it suits:** the birder, the gardener, the nature lover, or anyone who would rather build something lovely than knock someone else down. 1 to 5 players, 40 to 70 minutes, and our course suggests ages 10 and up. It plays solo too, against a small deck that runs an automated opponent, which makes it a good gift for someone who lives alone.

## For party hosts

### Codenames

Two teams face a grid of 25 words. A spymaster on each team gives a one-word clue and a number, and their teammates guess which words on the grid they mean. Touch the assassin and your team loses on the spot.

**Who it suits:** the friend who hosts, the family that fills a living room at the holidays, and the coworker who runs the office party. 4 to 8 or more players, about 15 minutes, ages 10 and up.

**Why it works as a gift:** it teaches in a few minutes, holds a crowd, and leaves the table arguing happily about a clue. It is also a reliable pick for someone who says they do not like games.

**Price:** about $20 to $25. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $19.99 to $24.99 at major retailers including Target and Miniature Market (October 2026) --> See the [Codenames page at Czech Games Edition](https://czechgames.com/games/codenames).

### King of Tokyo

Each player is a giant monster rolling six dice up to three times, keeping what they like. Claws hit other monsters, hearts heal, lightning earns energy to buy power cards, and three or more matching numbers score points. One monster holds Tokyo (two, with five or six players) and gets hit by everyone else. First to 20 points, or the last monster standing, wins.

**Who it suits:** a mixed table of kids and adults, or the friend who likes noise and dice. 2 to 6 players, about 30 minutes, ages 8 and up. <!-- VERIFY: player count, time, and age against IELLO's King of Tokyo page (iellogames.com did not load during fact check; these match our course YAML) -->

**Why it works as a gift:** big chunky dice, cardboard monsters, and a game where getting knocked out of Tokyo is as funny as holding it. The dice need no reading, and the power cards are short, so a younger kid can play alongside the grown ups.

**Price:** about $32 to $45, depending on retailer. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $31.99 to $45.99 for the current edition (October 2026), with Target at $45.99 --> See the [King of Tokyo page at IELLO](https://iellogames.com/jeux/king-of-tokyo/).

### Flip 7

A push-your-luck card game. On your turn you flip a card or stop. Each number card adds to your score for the round, but flip a number you already have and you bust and score nothing. Collect seven different numbers and you end the round on the spot with a bonus.

**Who it suits:** big groups, families, and anyone who likes a game you can explain while shuffling. The box says 3 or more players, about 20 minutes, ages 8 and up.

**Why it works as a gift:** it is the stocking stuffer of the year. Small box, low price, and a game that plays a whole table at once. Give it with one of the bigger games above and the giftee has a warm-up and a main event.

**Price:** about $8. <!-- VERIFY: Board Game Oracle showed $7.95 to $7.99 at Walmart, Miniature Market, and Game Nerdz (October 2026) --> See the [Flip 7 page at The Op](https://theop.games/products/flip-7).

## How to give a board game so it gets played

1. **Include the teacher.** Print the course's teach sheet (every course page links it) and put it on top of the rulebook. A one-page sheet is far less scary than a booklet.
2. **Offer to teach the first game.** If you will see them over the holidays, say so on the tag. A gift you play together the same day is a gift that gets played again. Our guide on [how to teach a board game](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) has the method.
3. **Pull the shrink wrap.** Open it and punch the cardboard before you wrap it, if you know the person well enough. It removes the last small barrier between the box and the table.

Want to watch a game played before you buy? Ask at your local game store; many run open game nights. In Indianapolis, the [Ludarium lounge waitlist](/lounges/indianapolis/) gets the first word on local learn-to-play nights as The Ludarium grows.

## FAQ

### What is the best board game gift for someone who has never played modern board games?

Ticket to Ride for a family, Azul for a couple, and Codenames for a group. All three teach in minutes, and each has a free Ludarium Academy course the new player can use to learn it.

### What is a good board game gift under $10?

Flip 7. It costs about $8, plays three or more, and takes about 20 minutes. It comes with a free course too.

### Is Wingspan too hard for a new player?

Not for someone who enjoys a puzzle. It is the longest and deepest game on this list, but the base game includes a swift-start guide, and our free course teaches the rules in plain words before it gets into strategy.

### Which of these games works for two players?

Azul and Carcassonne are both excellent at two. Ticket to Ride and Wingspan work at two as well. Codenames needs at least four, and Flip 7 is built for three or more.
