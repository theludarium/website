## What kinds of places can you play board games in Indianapolis?

Indianapolis has the same four categories most mid-size cities have, and knowing which one you want saves a wasted drive.

**Board game cafes.** Coffee by day, beer and wine by night, a library of a few hundred games, and staff who teach. These are the best fit for first-timers, dates, and families.

**Board game bars and breweries with a shelf.** Several Indianapolis breweries and bars keep a cabinet of games near the back. The selection is usually light and party-focused, but the price of entry is a pint.

**Friendly local game stores with play space.** The city's hobby shops often have tables in the back and a calendar of open play, Magic: The Gathering events, and D&D nights. Less of a cafe feel, more of a clubhouse.

**Public game nights at libraries, churches, and community rooms.** Free, scheduled, and often the most welcoming of all. The Indianapolis Public Library runs [family game nights at Central Library](https://attend.indypl.org/event/16309333) and a Saturday game club at the West Indianapolis branch, and Hamilton East Public Library in Fishers hosts a [free adults-only game afternoon](https://fishersin.gov/event/shuffle-roll/) on the first Thursday of each month.

## How do you pick the right board game cafe in Indianapolis for your group?

Ask four questions before you choose.

1. **How many of you are there?** Groups of two to four fit anywhere. Six or more should call ahead; the big tables go first on weekends.
2. **How loud do you want it?** Bars get loud after nine. Cafes stay quieter. A couple on a first date wants a different room than a bachelor party.
3. **Do you want to be taught?** If nobody in your group knows games, pick a place with staff who teach. That service is the main reason to pay a cover.
4. **Are kids coming?** Daytime at a cafe is fine. Evenings at a bar are not. Check the venue's age policy.

Once you have answered those, the choice usually makes itself.

## Which Indianapolis neighborhoods are best for a game night out?

Walkable neighborhoods with evening foot traffic are where board game venues do best, in Indianapolis and everywhere else. In this city, that points to a handful of areas.

- **Fountain Square and Fletcher Place** have the independent, slightly artsy feel where small venues thrive. Elf 'N Moon is the anchor here, open until 11 most nights.
- **Mass Ave and downtown** are the easiest for groups coming from different directions. Good Games on South Meridian is the downtown table.
- **Broad Ripple** is the city's long-standing nightlife strip, and a few of its bars keep games on hand, though none is a dedicated game venue yet.
- **Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and the north suburbs** have the family crowd and the daytime options: Moonshot Games, Gray Gauntlet Games, Hero's Emporium, Next Level Games, and the Fishers library's monthly afternoon.

If a neighborhood you love has no place to play, that is not a dead end. A standing weekly table at a brewery is a game night, and it takes one person willing to be there every week to start one.

## What should a first-timer expect at an Indianapolis board game cafe?

The same thing a first-timer should expect anywhere, and we wrote a full guide to [what a board game cafe is](/articles/what-is-a-board-game-cafe/) for exactly that reader. The short version:

- Someone will seat you and explain pricing.
- You tell the staff your group size and taste. They pull three games and teach one.
- You play two or three games over two to three hours.
- Nobody expects you to know anything. The teaching is the product.

Good starter games to ask for: Ticket to Ride, Azul, Codenames, Sushi Go, The Crew, and Wingspan if you have a couple of hours and want something with birds on it.

## Where can you play D&D or Magic in Indianapolis?

Game stores, mostly. Friendly local game stores run the city's recurring Dungeons & Dragons tables, Magic: The Gathering drafts, and Commander nights. Downtown, [Good Games](https://meetup.com/goodgames) runs a free Casual Commander night on Sundays, a Lorcana league on Wednesdays, and Pokémon League on Saturdays. In Noblesville, Moonshot Games runs Friday Night Magic, and Hero's Emporium in Carmel hosts Magic nights, Commander sessions, and free RPG days. Elf 'N Moon in Fountain Square runs beginner RPG learn-to-play sessions; check its calendar for the night.

Indianapolis has most of the ingredients. A large metro with a dense core and growing suburbs. A deep bench of families with young children in the north. Universities nearby. ## FAQ

### Is there a board game cafe in Indianapolis?

Yes, and the number of places to play is growing. The verified list above is maintained by our editor; check it for the current options nearest you.

### Do Indianapolis board game cafes take reservations?

Most do for groups of six or more, and weekends fill up. Call ahead for a Friday or Saturday evening.

### Are board game cafes in Indianapolis kid-friendly?

Cafes during the day usually are, with games for younger players on the shelf. Bars in the evening generally are not. Check the age policy before you go.

### Can I play board games for free in Indianapolis?

Yes. Library game nights are free, and many breweries keep a game shelf you can use with a drink purchase.
