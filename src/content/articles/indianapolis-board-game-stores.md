If you are searching for board game stores in Indianapolis, the useful answer is not a ranked list. It is a map. The metro is spread out, the stores are spread out with it, and the right shop for you is usually the one you can reach on a weeknight. This guide sorts the confirmed stores by area, says what each is known for, and tells you which ones let you sit down and play.

Stores open, move, and close faster than any article can follow. Every venue below was checked against its own site or a current listing in October 2026. If something has changed, tell us and we will fix it.

## What makes a game store worth driving to?

Before the list, a quick frame. A good friendly local game store, the hobby's term for an independent shop, does three things a website cannot.

- **It lets you see the box.** Weight, component quality, and table footprint are hard to judge from a product photo.
- **It has a calendar.** Open play, Magic: The Gathering nights, D&D tables, and learn-to-play sessions are how a store becomes a community rather than a shelf.
- **It has a person who has played the game.** Ask what a store employee would buy for a family with a nine-year-old and you get a better answer than any algorithm.

If a store near you does all three, it is worth the drive even when the price is a few dollars higher than online. The staff and the tables are what you are paying for.

## Which game stores are downtown?

**[Good Games Indianapolis](https://www.goodgamesna.com/venue/good-games-indianapolis/)**, 111 S Meridian St. The downtown anchor, with one of the biggest play spaces in the city and events most days of the week. The [public calendar](https://meetup.com/goodgames) lists free Casual Commander on Sunday afternoons, a Lorcana league on Wednesdays, and Pokémon League on Saturdays. If you work downtown or are visiting for a convention, start here.

**[Paradigm Games](https://paradigmgames.co/)**, 2479 N Meridian St. A Wizards Play Network store north of downtown, open Tuesday through Friday 2 to 10 p.m. and weekends noon to 5, with Commander on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday, Standard on Wednesday, Friday Night Magic draft, a Sunday Pokémon League, and a free board game library.

## What are the options on the south and east sides?

**[The Game Preserve](https://www.gamepreserve.com/)**, Greenwood (1551 E Stop 12 Rd), with a second location near the Fashion Mall on the north side. Founded in 1980, with a broad selection from family games to heavy strategy; check its Facebook page for events.

**[Hitherto Coffee & Gaming Parlour](http://hithertocoffee.com/)**, Greenfield (1558 N State St). Technically a cafe, but it sells games as well as lending them, so it earns a spot here. Coffee, pastries, and a large library. The best daytime option on the east side, and a good one for families.

**[Elf 'N Moon](https://elfnmoon.com/)**, Fountain Square (1354 Shelby St). A game store with free first-come tables and a free library, open into the evening seven days a week, with beer for 21+. The easiest place near downtown to sit down and play.

**Saltire Toys & Games**, Lawrence (11135 Pendleton Pike). The east side's store, a Wizards Play Network shop that sells games and hosts play; check its listings for the current schedule.

## Which stores serve Carmel, Fishers, and Noblesville?

The north suburbs have the densest cluster of shops in the metro, which tracks with where the families are.

**[Gray Gauntlet Games](https://graygauntletgames.com/)**, Fishers (7998 Centerpoint Dr). Play space plus a busy calendar of Magic (Commander, Pauper, drafts) and Pokémon.

**[Moonshot Games](https://moonshotgamestore.com/)**, downtown Noblesville (50 N 9th St). Demo copies you can try, a dedicated game room, and events including Friday Night Magic. A good store for someone who wants to play before buying.

**[Hero's Emporium](https://heroslgs.com)** and **[Next Level Games](https://www.nextlevelgames.store/events?location=carmel)**, both in Carmel. Tabletop, card, and RPG stores with Magic nights and game events. Hero's Emporium (now at 12697 N Pennsylvania St) hosts weekly Commander and ongoing RPG campaigns; Next Level runs Pauper, Standard, and free Friday Commander.

**[Game Time](http://www.gametimeindy.com/)**, Castleton (8244 Center Run Dr). A card shop with in-store play and tournament hosting, open until 10 p.m. on Fridays, which is the busy night; the store does not post a weekly schedule online, so call ahead.

Westfield does not yet have a dedicated game store we could confirm. Residents tend to drive to Carmel or Noblesville, both under 15 minutes.

## How should you choose a store if you are new to the hobby?

Pick by what you want to do, not by what the store sells.

1. **You want to try games before buying.** Moonshot Games keeps demo copies. Elf 'N Moon and Paradigm have free libraries. Hitherto has a full lending library.
2. **You want Magic.** Almost every store on this list runs it. The question is which night fits your week; see our guide to [where to play Magic in Indianapolis](/articles/where-to-play-magic-indianapolis/).
3. **You want D&D.** Good Games and Hero's Emporium host RPG play. North-side players should read [where to play D&D in Carmel and Fishers](/articles/dnd-groups-carmel-fishers/).
4. **You want a quiet afternoon with a family.** Hitherto in Greenfield during the day, or the Game Preserve at Fashion Mall Commons on a weekend.

Then walk in and say, "I'm new, what should I try?" Every good store has heard that question a thousand times and has an answer ready.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest board game store in Indianapolis?

Good Games downtown has one of the largest play spaces. The Game Preserve has the longest history and two locations.

### Are there board game stores in Carmel, Indiana?

Yes. Hero's Emporium and Next Level Games are both in Carmel, with Gray Gauntlet Games in Fishers and Moonshot Games in Noblesville nearby.

### Can I play board games at a store without buying anything?

Usually yes for open play. Organized events often carry a small fee. Buying something small is appreciated if you stay a while.

### Is there a board game store in Westfield, Indiana?

Not one we could confirm as of October 2026. Carmel and Noblesville stores are a short drive.
