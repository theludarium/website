A third place is somewhere you go regularly that is neither your home nor your workplace, where you are known by name and nobody needs a reason to be there. The term comes from sociologist Ray Oldenburg, who argued that these places hold communities together. Most adults today do not have one. This article explains what a third place is, why they have been disappearing, and what it takes to find or build one.

## What is a third place, exactly?

Oldenburg laid out the idea in his 1989 book *The Great Good Place*. His examples were the pub, the cafe, the barbershop, the diner, the general store, and the main street. He listed the traits a true third place shares:

- It is on neutral ground. Nobody is the host and nobody is the guest.
- It is a leveler. Your job title does not matter inside the door.
- Conversation is the main activity.
- It is accessible and accommodating. You can show up without planning.
- It has regulars, and the regulars set the tone.
- It keeps a low profile. It is plain, not fancy.
- The mood is playful.
- It feels like a home away from home.

Read that list twice and notice what is missing: an appointment. The first place (home) and the second place (work) run on obligation. The third place runs on habit and welcome. You go because you can, and people are glad you came.

## Why did adults lose their third places?

The decline has been going on for decades, and several forces pushed in the same direction.

**Suburban design.** Neighborhoods built around the car rarely have a walkable corner where people run into one another. If you have to drive to it, it has to be a destination, and a destination needs a reason.

**The collapse of membership.** Political scientist Robert Putnam documented in [*Bowling Alone*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Alone) how membership in unions, parent-teacher associations, fraternal lodges, and other civic groups fell across the second half of the twentieth century, and how more people bowled while fewer bowled in leagues. Those institutions were third places with a schedule attached.

**Work that follows you home.** When the laptop is always open, the line between the first and second place blurs, and there is less room left for a third.

**The screen as a substitute.** Online communities offer some of what a third place offers: regulars, in-jokes, a sense of being known. They do not offer a face, a handshake, or the small physical rituals of being in a room together.

**Rising costs.** Rent pressure closes the plain, cheap places first. Oldenburg's "low profile" trait is exactly what the market punishes.

The result shows up in the data on connection. The [U.S. Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on loneliness](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf) reported that roughly half of American adults said they experienced loneliness, and put the mortality impact of social disconnection on par with smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. The [Harvard Study of Adult Development](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/04/over-nearly-80-years-harvard-study-has-been-showing-how-to-live-a-healthy-and-happy-life/), which has followed participants since 1938, has found that close relationships, more than money or fame, are what keep people happy and healthy into old age. Third places are where many of those relationships used to be maintained.

## Why isn't a bar or a coffee shop enough?

They can be, and for some people they are. But two things have changed.

Most coffee shops today are optimized for laptops, not conversation. The single-seat counter, the headphones, the quiet rule. You can be among people without being with them. Oldenburg would call that a second place in disguise.

Bars still work as third places for some, but they carry costs that grow with age: late hours, alcohol, noise, and a social script that rewards extroverts. Plenty of adults want the regulars and the welcome without the hangover.

What is missing in both cases is an activity that pulls strangers into the same conversation. That is the gap a game fills.

## What makes a game table a good third place?

Put a copy of Codenames on a table and two things happen. People sit down. And within a minute, they are talking to one another about something other than the weather.

A game gives a third place structure without an agenda. Go down Oldenburg's list and see how a good game table fits each trait:

| Oldenburg's trait | How a game table delivers it |
|---|---|
| Neutral ground | The cafe or lounge is nobody's living room |
| Leveler | A surgeon and a line cook have the same odds in Azul |
| Conversation is the main activity | Table talk, bluffing, and teaching are the game |
| Accessible | You can join a public table alone and be welcomed |
| Regulars | Weekly game nights build a known cast fast |
| Low profile | A table, chairs, and a shelf is all it takes |
| Playful mood | Built in |
| Home away from home | A standing seat at the Tuesday table is yours |

This is why [board game cafes](/articles/what-is-a-board-game-cafe/) have spread to most mid-size cities over the past decade. They are one of the few new third places to emerge at scale, and they did it without reinventing anything. They just gave adults a reason to sit down together.

## How do you find a third place as an adult?

Start with where you already go, then look for the regulars.

1. **Pick a time, not a place.** Third places are built on repetition. Choose one evening a week you can protect, then find something that happens then.
2. **Look for hosted activities.** A public game night, a run club, a chess meetup, a trivia night. The host does the social work so you do not have to.
3. **Show up three times before judging.** The first visit you are a stranger. The second, a face. The third, a regular. Most people quit after one.
4. **Learn names.** The fastest way to become a regular is to treat others like regulars.
5. **Bring one person once.** A friend lowers the barrier for the first visit. After that, go alone so you have to talk to new people.

Finding a [public game night near you](/articles/board-game-night-near-me/) is the fastest version of this. The activity is built in, strangers are expected, and the host wants you there.

## Can you build a third place if your town doesn't have one?

Yes, and it takes less than people think. The minimum viable third place is a standing time, a public location, and one person who is reliably there.

A weekly table at a brewery, a library meeting room, or a church hall, announced once and repeated without fail, becomes a third place in about two months. The hard part is not the setup. It is being there on the week nobody else comes, and the week after that, until the regulars form.

## Why does this matter for health, not just happiness?

Because the research on connection keeps pointing the same direction. A [meta-analysis of 148 studies](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316) covering more than 300,000 people found that stronger social relationships were associated with about a 50 percent greater likelihood of survival over the study periods, an effect the authors compared to quitting smoking. The Surgeon General's advisory links poor social relationships to higher risk of heart disease, stroke, and dementia. A third place is not a cure for anything. It is a habit that keeps the ties maintained, the way a weekly run keeps a heart maintained.

The LEV Institute, which shares a founder with The Ludarium, frames this as "togethering": regular, in-person time with people you like, with an activity as the excuse. The activity can be anything. Games happen to be very good at it.

## FAQ

### What are examples of third places?

Cafes, pubs, barbershops, libraries, parks, community centers, churches, bookstores with seating, and game cafes. The common thread is that you can show up without an invitation and expect to see people you know.

### Is a gym a third place?

It can be, if you go on a schedule and talk to the same people. Most gym visits are solitary, which makes them closer to a second place for the body than a third place for the person.

### Can an online community be a third place?

It can offer some of the traits, especially regulars and a playful mood. It cannot offer physical presence, and the strongest findings in the research above come from in-person relationships and day-to-day social integration rather than screen time.

### How often do you need to go somewhere for it to count as a third place?

Weekly is the practical threshold. Less than that and you stay a visitor; the regulars do not learn your name and you do not learn theirs.
