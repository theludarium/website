The best co op card games put everyone on the same side and then hide information from each other, so the group has to read each other instead of one person running the show. Our top picks are The Crew: Mission Deep Sea for three to five players, Hanabi and The Mind for quick play at almost any table, Codenames: Duet for two, and Marvel Champions for groups who want a deep, ongoing game. All thirteen games below are played with cards, and every one of them is a real cooperative game: you win or lose together.

**The quick answer:** for a game night with friends who don't play much, start with The Mind or The Crew. For two players, Codenames: Duet or The Fox in the Forest Duet. For adults who want something to dig into for months, Marvel Champions or Arkham Horror: The Card Game.

## What makes a cooperative card game good?

Cards solve the biggest problem in cooperative games. When every piece of information sits face up on a shared board, the most experienced player can work out everyone's best move and start telling them what to do. That is the [quarterback problem](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/), and it can turn a co-op into one person playing while everyone else watches.

A hand of cards is private by nature. If nobody can see your hand, nobody can play your turn for you. The best cooperative card games build on that in one of three ways:

1. **Limited communication.** You can talk, but only in narrow ways. The Crew, Hanabi and The Mind live here.
2. **Personal decks.** Each player has their own deck and their own decisions. Marvel Champions, Sentinels, Arkham and Lord of the Rings work this way.
3. **A shared puzzle with a clock.** Everyone works on one problem under pressure. Regicide, The Game and Paleo fit here.

Pick the style that fits your group, then pick the game.

## The 13 best co op card games

### 1. The Crew: Mission Deep Sea

[The Crew](https://link.amazon/B099S9RKf) is a trick-taking game like Hearts or Spades, turned cooperative. Each mission tells the team which player has to win which cards, and you have almost no way to talk about your hand. Mission Deep Sea, by Thomas Sing and published by KOSMOS, is the second box in the series; the first, The Crew: Quest for Planet Nine, won the Kennerspiel des Jahres in 2020. Either box is excellent.

It plays best with three to five players, and a two-player mode adds a dummy crew member. Missions take five to fifteen minutes, so it is easy to play "just one more." We think it is the best introduction to trick-taking there is, and we explain why in our [full guide to The Crew](/articles/the-crew-card-game/). To get good fast, the [Academy course on The Crew](/academy/the-crew/) walks from first mission to card counting.

### 2. Hanabi

In [Hanabi](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MXTGT21?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) , you hold your cards facing away from you. Everyone can see your hand except you. Together, the group tries to play cards in order, one to five, in each of five colors, like setting off a fireworks show in sequence. On your turn you can give a clue, play a card, or discard. Clues are rationed, and three bad plays end the game early.

Antoine Bauza designed it, and it won the Spiel des Jahres in 2013. It fits in a pocket, plays two to five in about 25 minutes, and rewards groups who enjoy quiet, careful thinking. Every clue carries more meaning than its words, which is what makes it so satisfying when the group starts to click.

### 3. The Mind

[The Mind](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07C4F3KLF?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is almost nothing on paper. Each player gets a few cards numbered 1 to 100. Without talking or signaling, the group has to play every card to the center in rising order. If someone plays a 40 while someone else still holds a 31, you lose a life. Each level adds more cards.

Wolfgang Warsch designed it, and it plays two to four in about 20 minutes. It teaches in one minute, which makes it a great opener for friends who don't play games yet. The tension of a silent table, everyone waiting to see who moves first, is unlike anything else on this list.

### 4. The Game

[The Game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07NDKPSB4?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) by Steffen Benndorf hands everyone cards numbered 2 to 99 and sets out four piles: two that must climb from 1 and two that must fall from 100. On your turn you play at least two cards, and you cannot say exactly what you hold. You can say "don't play on this pile" or "I can help here," and that is all. The one escape hatch: a card exactly ten away in the wrong direction lets you reverse a pile.

It plays one to five in about 20 minutes and works for families with kids old enough to count to 100.

### 5. Regicide

[Regicide](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CXS1171H?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a cooperative fantasy game played with a standard deck of 52 cards. The twelve face cards become enemies: four Jacks, then four Queens, then four Kings. You attack with number cards, and each suit has a power: hearts heal the deck, diamonds draw cards, clubs double damage, and spades shield you from the enemy's counterattack. After you attack, the enemy hits back, and you have to discard enough cards to absorb it.

Designed by Paul Abrahams, Luke Badger and Andy Richdale and published by Badgers from Mars, it plays one to four in 10 to 30 minutes. The rules fit on a card, and you can try it with any deck you already own before buying the illustrated edition.

### 6. Codenames: Duet

[Codenames: Duet](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FJMNPGXM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the cooperative two-player version of Codenames, by Scot Eaton and Vlaada Chvátil. Both players sit across from each other and look at a shared key card from opposite sides. Each of you sees which words your partner needs to find, and you take turns giving one-word clues to help each other find all fifteen agents before you run out of turns. Hit an assassin and you both lose.

It is our favorite co op card game for 2 when one or both of you love words. For tactics, the Academy lesson on [Codenames: Duet strategy](/academy/codenames/codenames-duet-strategy/) is a good next step.

### 7. The Fox in the Forest Duet

[The Fox in the Forest Duet](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TSY8L2K?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) takes the trick-taking idea from The Crew and builds it for exactly two. You and your partner play tricks that move a shared token along a forest path, trying to collect every gem before your rounds run out. You both want the same thing, but you cannot see each other's hand, so every lead is a small act of trust.

Designed by Joshua Buergel and published by Renegade Game Studios, it plays in about 30 minutes. If you liked The Crew but only have two players, start here.

### 8. Paleo

[Paleo](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08KPRLNFY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a card-driven survival game. You lead a small Stone Age tribe, and each player works through their own deck of encounter cards: hunting, gathering, exploring, facing danger. You make your choices with only a hint of what is coming, then pool the group's people and tools to handle each card. The tribe wins by completing a mission, such as finishing a great cave painting, before too many members fall.

Peter Rustemeyer designed it, Hans im Glück published it, and it won the Kennerspiel des Jahres in 2021. Z-Man Games publishes it in English. It plays one to four in 45 to 60 minutes, and it comes with a series of scenarios that teach the game as you go.

### 9. Aeon's End

[Aeon's End](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B072BG9Z9V?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a cooperative deck-builder. Each player is a mage defending the last city from a monstrous Nemesis. You buy spells, gems and relics to improve your deck over the game. Two twists make it special: you never shuffle your discard pile, so you can plan your next hand precisely, and a shuffled turn-order deck means you never know who acts next.

Designed by Kevin Riley, Nick Little and Jenny Iglesias and first published in 2016 by Action Phase Games, it plays one to four in about an hour. If you enjoy Dominion but want everyone on one team, this is the one. Our guide to the [best deck-building games](/articles/best-deck-building-games/) covers more in the genre.

### 10. Sentinels of the Multiverse

[Sentinels of the Multiverse](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0992PXW6L?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a comic book battle in a box. Each player picks a hero with a unique deck. The villain gets its own deck, and so does the environment, which can help or hurt everyone. Turns cycle between villain, heroes and environment until the villain falls or the heroes do.

Get the Definitive Edition from Greater Than Games, designed by Christopher Badell and Adam Rebottaro. It plays one to five in 30 to 60 minutes. The game tracks a lot of small modifiers, so it suits players who enjoy bookkeeping and big combos.

### 11. Marvel Champions: The Card Game

[Marvel Champions](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07W1BF6D5?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is our pick for the best cooperative card game for adults who want an ongoing hobby. Each player is a hero, such as Spider-Man or Captain Marvel, with a card that flips between their hero side and their everyday alter-ego side. You fight a villain while stopping its schemes, and every hero deck pairs a fixed set of signature cards with one of several styles of play you choose.

Michael Boggs, Nate French and Caleb Grace designed it for Fantasy Flight Games in 2019. It plays one to four in 45 to 90 minutes. Like the next two games, it is a Living Card Game: expansions come with fixed contents, so there are no random packs to chase. The core box is a complete game.

### 12. Arkham Horror: The Card Game

[Arkham Horror: The Card Game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0999D3P8S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a horror story you play through as a campaign. Each player is an investigator with a custom deck. You explore locations, gather clues, and face monsters across a series of linked scenarios, and choices and injuries carry from one scenario to the next. Skill tests use a bag of tokens, so even your best plan carries a little dread.

Designed by Nate French and MJ Newman for Fantasy Flight Games, the revised core set supports one to four players in one box, which the original did not. Games run 60 to 120 minutes. If your group loves a story more than a puzzle, start here.

### 13. The Lord of the Rings: The Card Game

[The Lord of the Rings: The Card Game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09HWFK7DG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is one of the oldest games on this list and still one of the hardest. Each player controls up to three heroes from Middle-earth and builds a deck around them. You quest through scenarios while an encounter deck throws enemies and treacheries at you, and your threat rises every round. Let it climb too high and you are out.

Nate French designed it for Fantasy Flight Games, which first released it in 2011. The revised core set, released in 2022, supports one to four players and adds a campaign mode. Games run 30 to 90 minutes. It rewards careful deckbuilding more than anything else here, which makes it a favorite for solo players too.

## What are the best co op card games for 2?

Almost every game here works with two players, but these shine at that count:

- **Codenames: Duet.** Built for two, warm and chatty.
- **The Fox in the Forest Duet.** Built for two, quiet and clever.
- **The Mind.** Two players makes it a staring contest with numbers, in the best way.
- **Regicide.** Plays smoothly at two with one deck of cards.
- **Marvel Champions.** Two heroes is a sweet spot: deep, but fast enough for a weeknight.

The Crew works at two with its dummy-player rule, but it is better at three or more; our lesson on [The Crew by player count](/academy/the-crew/the-crew-player-counts/) explains the difference. ## What are the best cooperative card games for adults?

Any game on this list works for adults, but if you want something with depth that rewards repeat play, look at the Living Card Games and the heavier entries:

| If your group wants... | Play |
|---|---|
| A story that unfolds over many nights | Arkham Horror: The Card Game |
| Superheroes and fast combos | Marvel Champions |
| The hardest puzzle on the shelf | The Lord of the Rings: The Card Game |
| Deck-building with no luck of the shuffle | Aeon's End |
| A tense, short game with one rule | The Mind |
| A brain-burner that teaches teamwork | The Crew |

## How do you choose a cooperative card game?

Ask three questions.

**How many players, most of the time?** Two-player-only games are wonderful but sit on the shelf when friends visit. If your group size changes week to week, The Crew, The Game and Hanabi flex the best.

**How long do you have?** Under 30 minutes: The Mind, The Game, Regicide, Hanabi, Codenames: Duet. An evening: Paleo, Aeon's End, Sentinels. A campaign over many nights: Arkham or Lord of the Rings.

**Does anyone take over?** If your table has a natural quarterback, choose a limited-communication game. The Crew, Hanabi and The Mind make it impossible to direct other players, which keeps everyone in the game.

## FAQ

### What is the best cooperative card game?

For most groups, The Crew: Mission Deep Sea. It teaches in minutes, plays three to five, and gets harder as you improve. For a deeper hobby game, Marvel Champions.

### What is a good co op card game for 2 players?

Codenames: Duet and The Fox in the Forest Duet are built for two. The Mind, Regicide and Marvel Champions also play very well with two.

### Are there cooperative card games you can play with a normal deck of cards?

Yes. Regicide was designed for a standard 52-card deck, and the published edition just adds illustrations and rule cards.

### What is a Living Card Game?

It is Fantasy Flight Games' name for card games sold with fixed contents instead of random booster packs. You buy a core set and add expansions you choose. Marvel Champions, Arkham Horror: The Card Game and The Lord of the Rings: The Card Game all work this way.

### Can cooperative card games be played solo?

Several can: The Game, Regicide, Paleo, Aeon's End, Sentinels, Marvel Champions, Arkham Horror and The Lord of the Rings all have solo rules.
