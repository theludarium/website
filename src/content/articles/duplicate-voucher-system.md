The duplicate voucher cube rule gives each drafter one token that can be exchanged, after the draft, for a second copy of a card they already drafted. The cube stays singleton. The decks do not. One voucher per player, one copy per voucher, and a short list of cards it cannot be used on.

This is a house rule, not a format with an official page. It exists because the singleton rule, which makes a cube easy to build and balance, also makes some decks worse than they should be: a two-drop aggro deck wants two Savannah Lions, and a tribal deck wants its lord twice. This article explains the procedure we use, the variations other groups run, and the exclusions that keep it from breaking the cube.

## Why would a singleton cube allow duplicates at all?

Cubes are singleton by convention. The MTG Wiki's cube history notes that the earliest cube-like formats, Wagic and Big Box Drafts in 1999, were singleton too, and the convention has held because it is the simplest way to guarantee variety and keep a card list honest. The counterargument is familiar: some archetypes are built on redundancy, and singleton starves them.

Two standard answers already exist. The first is to add near-duplicates: Savannah Lions and Isamaru, Hound of Konda are different cards that do the same job. The second is to break singleton in a few slots on purpose, which some owners do for lands and the occasional aggro one-drop.

The voucher is a third answer. Instead of the cube owner deciding where duplicates live, each drafter decides once, after they have seen their pool. The cube's list stays clean, and the decision becomes a drafting skill.

## How does the duplicate voucher work?

**What you need**

- One voucher token per drafter. A poker chip, a blank card, or a slip of paper that says "VOUCHER" works. Keep them in the cube box.
- A proxy stock: blank-backed cards or sleeves with paper inserts, and a pen. The second copy is almost always a proxy. Our position on proxies at a casual table is in [MTG proxies: ethics and etiquette](/articles/mtg-proxies-ethics/).
- The exclusion list, printed, visible on the table.

**Procedure**

1. Before the draft, give each player one voucher. Announce the exclusion list.
2. Draft normally. Vouchers are not drafted and are not cards in the pool.
3. After the draft, before deck-building ends, a player may place their voucher face up on a card they drafted. That card must not be on the exclusion list.
4. The player writes a proxy of the card, puts it in a sleeve matching the cube's, and adds it to their pool. The voucher is spent. The proxy is a legal copy of the card for the rest of the night.
5. A voucher not used by the end of deck-building is returned to the box. It does not carry to the next week.
6. The proxy is destroyed or filed at the end of the night. The duplicate never enters the cube.

**Timing rule.** The voucher is cashed after the draft, not during. Doing it during would let a player cut a card from a pack knowing they already own two. After-draft cashing keeps the draft itself singleton and keeps the information private until building.

## What should the exclusion list say?

The rule breaks when the second copy is a card the cube already struggles to contain. Our list, which is a house guideline, not a standard:

| Excluded category | Reason | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Fast mana | Two Sol Rings is a different game | Sol Ring, Mana Crypt, Moxen |
| Power-tier card advantage | Doubling the best cards narrows the draft | Ancestral Recall, Time Walk |
| Combo pieces | A second copy removes the "find it" step | Sneak Attack, Channel, Tinker |
| Planeswalkers | Redundant walkers are the strongest redundancy | all |
| One-card win conditions | Two Griselbrands in a reanimator deck | Griselbrand, Emrakul |
| Lands | Fixing is sized on purpose | all nonbasic lands |

What is deliberately **allowed**: creatures with mana value three or less, removal spells, counterspells, card-draw spells below power tier, equipment, and tribal lords. The intended use is a second Lightning Bolt, a second Kird Ape, a second Goblin Guide, a second Lord of Atlantis. That is where redundancy helps the decks that need help and hurts no one.

For an unpowered or Legacy-level cube, delete the rows that do not apply and add any card the group considers the cube's ceiling. The question to ask about each card: would two of this in one deck make the game about that card? If yes, exclude it.

## What does the voucher do to drafting?

The voucher is cashed after the draft, but every experienced drafter plans for it during.

**Aggro gets its curve.** The player in white-red aggro drafts one-drops knowing they can double the best one. That makes the archetype draftable in a cube where it would otherwise fall short, which is the oldest problem in cube design; the long version is in [aggro in cube](/articles/aggro-in-cube/).

**Removal gets safer to pass.** If a drafter can double their best removal spell, the fifth-best removal spell in the pack is less valuable, and midrange drafters stop taking every Doom Blade on sight.

**Choosing the card is a real decision.** The temptation is to double the best card in the pool. Usually the right call is to double the card the deck most lacks: the second two-drop, the second answer to a flyer, the third counterspell. Players who understand their deck's weak point use the voucher well; players who copy their bomb often find the bomb was already enough.

**Building takes five minutes longer.** Give it. Vouchers get cashed in the last two minutes of deck-building when players finally see the gap.

## What variations do other groups run?

- **Two vouchers, no stacking.** Two tokens, each for a different card. More redundancy, more aggro, slightly less variety in the games.
- **Voucher as a draft pick.** Instead of a free token, a player may spend a pick on a face-down "voucher" card in a pack. Twelve voucher cards shuffled into a 360 cube means not everyone gets one. It turns the voucher into a drafting cost and is the most interesting version for a veteran pod. <!-- EDITOR: confirm which version Cube Commonwealth uses -->
- **Archetype vouchers.** The voucher may only be used on a card with mana value two or less. It targets aggro directly and nothing else.
- **Sideboard vouchers.** The copy may start in the sideboard and come in after game one. It gives a tuning tool to decks that are fine in game one and get hated out in game two.
- **Voucher for a basic land upgrade.** Some groups allow the voucher to fetch a duplicate of any nonbasic land in the pool instead of a spell. We exclude lands; others swear by it.

## How do you run it fairly?

- **Print the list.** Arguments about whether a card is "really a combo piece" happen when the list lives in someone's head.
- **The owner rules on the spot.** A card not on the list is allowed. If the owner wants to exclude it, they change the list for next week.
- **Match sleeves.** The proxy must be indistinguishable from the back. A marked second copy is a mapped library.
- **Track usage for a month.** Note which cards get doubled. If the same card is doubled four weeks running, that is information about the cube, not the rule. Add a second real card that does the same job and move on.
- **Make it optional for new players.** A first-time drafter does not need one more decision. Hand them the voucher, explain it in one sentence, and tell them it is fine to never use it.

## FAQ

### What is a duplicate voucher in cube?

A house rule that gives each drafter one token, cashed after the draft for a proxy second copy of a card they drafted. The cube stays singleton; one deck per night gets a duplicate.

### When is the voucher cashed?

After the draft, during deck-building. Cashing it during the draft would change picks and reveal information, so most groups forbid it.

### Which cards can't be duplicated?

Each cube sets its own list. The common exclusions are fast mana, power-level card advantage, combo pieces, planeswalkers, one-card win conditions, and nonbasic lands.

### Does the duplicate stay in the cube?

No. The proxy is removed at the end of the night. The cube's list does not change.

## One token, one decision

The voucher is a small rule. It fixes one specific problem without touching the list, and it hands each drafter a choice that reveals how well they understand their deck. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
