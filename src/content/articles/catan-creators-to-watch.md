The best Catan creators aren't just teaching you to win. They're building the tables where people meet to play. Catan has sold more than 45 million copies in over 40 languages, and Netflix now holds the rights to Catan films and series ([Variety](https://au.variety.com/2025/film/news/catan-movies-tv-series-netflix-29170)). Yet the people shaping how the game is played day to day are a handful of YouTubers and streamers with audiences in the thousands, not millions.

That smallness is the point. This scene grew up online during the pandemic. Colonist.io, the free browser version most creators play on, went from about 53,000 active users in January 2020 to roughly 702,000 a year later ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/colonist-settlers-of-catan-online-free-game)). What came out of it looks less like an influencer economy and more like a network of clubhouses: Discord servers, weekly tournaments, classes, and a pipeline where viewers become rivals and rivals become champions.

So this guide ranks creators by more than subscriber counts. For each one, we look at three things: how they play, how they talk about the game, and what they do to bring people together. <span class="brand-line">The game is the reason. The people are the point.</span>

Four creators anchor the English-language Catan scene, and each one brings people together in a different way. ## DyLighted: the professor

DyLighted is the scene's biggest teacher, with about 72,400 subscribers and more than 15 million channel views ([PLAYBOARD](https://playboard.co/en/channel/UCUsKNYO4po7rXrKJ2K8v42g)). His channel pitches itself plainly: learn Catan from a regional champion ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@DyLightedCatan)).

**How he plays.** DyLighted is a production-first player. Asked what he checks first when placing, he named production and resource rarity, with extra weight on ore, wheat and sheep ([Colonist Blog](https://blog.colonist.io/interview-series-1-dylighted/)). His uploads mix live "Road to Rank 1" ladder climbs, cash-tournament streams, and focused lessons like *The Secret to Beating Catan's Strongest Strategy*.

**How he talks.** Calm and analytical. His channel tagline invites you to follow his thoughts and learn something, which is exactly the experience: you watch someone reason through a board out loud. He frames Catan as a game with no ceiling, saying even the top players keep getting better.

**How he brings people together.** DyLighted runs a Discord, streams on Twitch, and keeps a Patreon ([Twitch](https://m.twitch.tv/dylighted_catan?lang=en)). His real community footprint is bigger than his own channels, though:

- **He's the on-ramp.** Elliott Yap, Australia's 2023 national champion, started by watching pros like DyLighted before diving into Discord leagues ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/board-game-champ-paves-road-to-victory-piece-by-piece)).
- **He's the benchmark.** Other creators title videos around beating him, such as a Catan Championship premium-tournament semifinal review called *I Beat Dylighted at Catan*. Being the person everyone wants to beat is its own kind of community glue.

**Watch him if** you want to understand *why* a move is right, not just see it made.

## DandyDrew: the coach

Drew Elliott won the 2024 CATAN US National Championship ([CATAN](https://www.catan.com/catan-fans/news/new-us-catan-champion-2024)) and calls himself the #1-ranked Colonist player, "and bald," in his channel bio ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@dandydrew)). With about 30,000 subscribers, he's half DyLighted's size but arguably the scene's most connected figure.

**How he plays.** Drew came up through the Ore-Wheat-Sheep strategy and credits a chess background for his speed under pressure. He calls Catan roughly 80% skill and 20% luck, comparing it to poker ([The Beaver](https://thebeaverlse.co.uk/a-dive-into-the-catan-competitive-scene-with-dandydrew/)). He's also known for a "Side-by-Side" setup that other creators have studied with him on camera.

**How he talks.** Drew promises live play-by-play commentary meant to be both educational and entertaining. What sets him apart is that he treats the *table* as part of the board. He runs his own sales business and says he won plenty of early games on negotiation alone. He also draws a sharp line between European tournaments, where players find the best placements and let the dice decide, and American ones, which run on table talk and deal-making.

**How he brings people together.** Drew's community work happens mostly on other people's channels:

- **Coaching on camera.** He appears as a guest coach for rising creators, from "I Hired a Catan Coach" videos to a backseat series with Catan Jack in October 2026.
- **Playing with peers.** He plays group games with fellow creators such as FlyingChinaman and Rayman, and flew to Los Angeles to play Catan Jack in person.
- **Mentoring champions.** Elliott Yap met Drew through Discord and learned from him on the way to Australia's 2023 title ([Player2](https://www.player2.net.au/2025/08/interview-catan-national-champion-elliott-yap/)).

**Watch him if** you want to win the conversation, not just the board.

## ThePhantom: the grinder

ThePhantom's whole channel description is four words: "Forever improving Catan content" ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/@thephantomcatan)). With about 11,000 subscribers, he's built his audience on consistency rather than credentials.

**How he plays.** ThePhantom specializes in ranked 1v1 on Colonist, a faster, more cutthroat format than the four-player game most people know. His long-running "Road to Rank #1" series follows him season by season, from Season 12 through Season 15, including a climb into the world's top 30 and a head-to-head against the #1-ranked player.

**How he talks.** High energy and all caps: titles like *THE LAST HOUR!!!* and *ENTERING TOP 30 IN THE WORLD!!!* tell you what you're getting. It's sports-broadcast Catan, built on stakes and suspense.

**How he brings people together.** ThePhantom's strength is ritual. He posts daily, and fans treat it that way; one commenter described his uploads as part of their life they look forward to each day. Every video links his Discord and a tip jar. Because he plays ranked matchmaking on camera, his viewers sometimes end up as his opponents. One wrote that after watching every video, he was thrilled to finally appear in one.

**Watch him if** you want a daily habit and a front-row seat to the competitive ladder.

## Catan Jack: the entertainer

Jack Usher has played Catan competitively for years but is new to YouTube ([YouTube](https://m.youtube.com/watch?pp=0gcJCU0KAYcqIYzv&v=rwoWuHcC2kY)). His channel bio sets the tone: gaming for silly little guys and gals ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/@catanjack)). At roughly 10,000 subscribers, he's the fastest-rising personality on this list.

**How he plays.** Jack plays high-level, but he leans into bold, unusual setups and then lets the table react. A typical upload commits hard to a sheep-port road game that "looks ridiculous" on paper and forces everyone to respond. His focus is less on optimal math and more on reading the room: who's tilting, who to trade with, when to stay quiet.

**How he talks.** Jack is the comedian of the group. His videos treat psychology as seriously as production, and he leans into the drama of a game going sideways. That makes him the most watchable creator for someone who plays Catan with friends, not on a ladder.

**How he brings people together.** Jack does the most *in person* of anyone here:

- **Face-to-face games.** When DandyDrew came to Los Angeles, the two sat down to play at a real table.
- **Tabletop guest spots.** In October 2026 he appeared on in-person studio playthroughs of Catan, a format closer to game night than to online ranked play.
- **Stakes tournaments.** He has run $250 buy-in tournaments through his Discord.
- **Learning in public.** His backseat series with DandyDrew turns coaching into content, so viewers learn alongside him.

**Watch him if** your Catan nights are as much about the people at the table as the points on the board.

## Rising names and the clubhouses they share

Behind the big four is a long tail of smaller creators, and a set of Discord servers where all of them meet. Colonist features these channels on its community page ([Colonist](https://colonist.io/community)):

- **Treeckosaurus** (~7K) and **TheWandererCatan** (~3K): ranked gameplay and analysis.
- **Catan Blunders**, **BuddyCatan**, **itsOKKCatan** and **Hakuna Matata Catan** (1K to 2K each): smaller channels worth following early.
- **FlyingChinaman** and **Trissie89**: Twitch streamers who play live with viewers.

Coaching is a growing niche too. One Colonist-sponsored creator, whose *17 Years of Catan Knowledge in 25 Minutes* is a popular primer, advertises 1-on-1 coaching and says his students include a 2024 US finalist.

The real gathering places, though, are the servers:

| Community | Members | What happens there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Catan Universe](https://discord.com/servers/catan-universe-462177898695229442) (official) | ~59K | Find opponents, join tournaments, find creators streaming live |
| [Colonist](https://colonist.io/community) | 43K+ | Voice chat, tips, chatting with creators |
| [Catan Championship](https://discord.com/servers/catan-championship-704840188106375208) | ~9K | Two base leagues, a Cities & Knights league, weekly puzzles, classes taught by top players |
| [Catan Community](https://discord.com/servers/catan-community-894372075622526986) | ~6.4K | Weekly open tournaments, monthly leagues with cash prizes, 1v1 to 8-player modes |

These servers explain how the scene produces champions: Elliott Yap became Australian champion after joining Discord servers like Catan Community and Catan Championship ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/board-game-champ-paves-road-to-victory-piece-by-piece)).

## When Catan goes mainstream

Occasionally the scene spills into the wider streaming world, and these moments show Catan working as a social game for audiences who don't follow the ladder.

- **Twitch Plays Catan (November 2025).** Sponsored by Charles Schwab, this event put eight streamers and their chat communities against each other, with viewers voting on placements and trades in real time. The lineup ranged from actor-rapper Khleo Thomas and strategy streamer Lunity to former *Big Brother* winner Daniel Gheesling and Indiana Pacers forward Aaron Nesmith ([Schwab](https://www.aboutschwab.com/newsroom/story/schwab-twitch-plays-catan-tournament)).
- **Board AF (Smosh).** In a 2022 episode, Rachel from Smosh brought her favorite game, Catan, to the show's table ([TheTVDB](https://www.thetvdb.com/series/board-af/episodes/11231830)).
- **Watch It Played.** Rodney Smith's long-running rules channel has a full *Catan: How To Play* video, still a common first stop for new players ([TheTVDB](https://thetvdb.com/series/watch-it-played/episodes/9023358)).

The common thread: Catan plays best as a shared event, whether that's a chat voting on trades or four friends at a table.

## What the best creators get right

Look past the subscriber counts and the same four habits show up in every creator who has built something lasting:

1. **They give people a place to go.** Every creator on this list points viewers to a Discord. The video is the invitation; the server is where people stay.
2. **They turn viewers into players.** ThePhantom's fans land in his ranked games. DyLighted's viewers become his rivals. The line between audience and opponent is thin, and that's the appeal.
3. **They show up for each other.** DandyDrew coaching on other channels and flying to play Catan Jack in person is the scene's clearest sign of health. Creators who collaborate grow the whole table.
4. **They make the social game visible.** Drew's table talk and Jack's tilt-reading prove Catan isn't just math. It's trading, persuading and reading people, the skills that make game night fun.

The lesson for the rest of us is simple. Watch these creators to sharpen your placements, then take what you learn back to a real table with real people. <span class="brand-line">The game is the reason. The people are the point.</span>
