The cube primer FAQ is where the questions go that are asked at the table every week by someone new. It is the most practical section of the primer and the easiest to write, because you do not invent the questions; you collect them. If a question has been asked twice in a month, it goes in the FAQ, with a two-sentence answer and a link if the answer is longer than that.

This article covers how to collect the questions, how to answer them so they stop being asked, the fifteen or so that nearly every cube's FAQ ends up holding, and the ones that belong elsewhere. It is part of a series on writing a cube primer; the FAQ closes the primer, right after the philosophy and before the links.

## Where do FAQ questions come from?

From the table. Keep a note on your phone during draft nights for a month and write down every question a drafter asks the host or a regular. Not the rules questions about specific cards, which are the judge's job, but the ones about your cube, your night, and your rules.

After a month the list will have twenty or thirty entries, and most will be the same six questions asked by different people. Those six are your FAQ. The rest are either covered in the primer already, in which case the primer section needs to be easier to find, or they are one-offs that do not need a permanent answer.

Add to it the same way after each update. A new rule or a new archetype produces new questions for about three weeks; whichever survive that long get an entry.

## How should an FAQ answer be written?

Two sentences. The first answers the question directly, with a number or a yes or no where possible. The second gives the reason or the exception. If a third sentence is needed, it is a link to the section or article that holds the long version.

"Can I add cards to my deck between rounds? Yes, from anything you drafted, and you can change basics freely. You cannot add cards from the box." That answers the question, gives the boundary, and is done. Compare the version that starts with the history of why the rule exists; the reader stops before the answer arrives.

Write the question the way it is asked at the table, not the way a rules document would phrase it. "What if we have nine people?" is a real question. "What is the policy regarding non-standard pod sizes?" is not something anyone has ever said.

## Which questions does nearly every cube FAQ need?

Collected across our own table and the public primers we read, these recur almost everywhere. Your answers will differ; the questions will not.

**About the cube**

1. How big is it and how strong is it? One line, with the power tier named.
2. Is there power, or fast mana, or combo? Yes or no, and the reason.
3. Why isn't [famous card] in it? Usually the same answer for every card; give the general reason and link to the philosophy section.
4. Are there proxies? Which cards, how marked, and the policy on them. The broader argument is in [MTG proxies: ethics, etiquette, and playing on a budget](/articles/mtg-proxies-ethics/); the FAQ only needs your table's answer.

**About the draft**

5. What happens with seven or nine players? The fallback format and how the packs change.
6. Is there a pick timer? Yes or no, how long, who calls it.
7. Can I look at my picks during the pack? Your rule, in one line.
8. Can I talk during the draft? What is allowed and what is not.

**About deckbuilding and play**

9. How many cards in a deck, and where do lands come from? Forty, seventeen lands, provided, or your variation.
10. What counts as my sideboard? Your rule.
11. How many rounds, and best-of-what? Your format and the time limit.
12. What happens if a game is still going when time is called?

**About the night**

13. Do I need to bring anything? Usually nothing but a way to track life. Say what is provided.
14. Do I have to stay for all three rounds? Yes, and why: a drop leaves someone without an opponent.
15. Is it fine to come and just watch the first time? Your answer, and whether a spectator can jump in if a seat opens.

Fifteen is a lot for an FAQ, and most primers settle at eight to twelve. Start with the ones that have actually been asked; add the others as they come.

## Which questions do not belong in the FAQ?

Three kinds, each with a better home.

**Card rules questions.** "Does Blood Moon turn my Urza's Saga into a Mountain?" is a rules question, and the answer is in the Comprehensive Rules, not your primer. If one interaction comes up every single week because your cube has two cards that confuse people together, one line is fine. More than two or three such lines and you are maintaining a rules document that will go wrong.

**Design justifications.** "Why do you run this card over that one" is a changelog question. Link to the changelog entry, where it is dated and reasoned, rather than answering it fresh in the FAQ. The format for those entries is in [keeping a cube changelog](/articles/cube-changelog/).

**Logistics that change.** Where to park, what time the venue closes, whether there is food. Those belong in the weekly post. An FAQ that says the venue closes at ten will be wrong the week the venue changes, and nobody will remember to fix it.

## How do you keep the FAQ honest?

Reread it every three months and delete the questions nobody asks any more. An FAQ grows if left alone, and a thirty-question FAQ is a document nobody reads, which defeats the purpose. The questions that were urgent during a rules change six months ago may now be settled; if so, the answer lives in the house rules and the FAQ entry can go.

The other check: ask a recent first-timer which questions they had that the primer did not answer. They will tell you two, and both go in.

## An FAQ layout that works

| Group | Questions | Where the long answer lives |
|---|---|---|
| About the cube | Size, power, proxies, why not [card] | Philosophy section, changelog |
| About the draft | Odd player counts, timer, talking, reviewing picks | House rules |
| About building and play | Deck size, lands, sideboard, rounds, time | House rules |
| About the night | What to bring, staying, spectating | The weekly post for details |

Grouping the questions with small headers lets a reader with one question find it in seconds. An ungrouped list of fifteen questions makes them read all fifteen.

## What about an FAQ for a themed cube?

Themed and restricted cubes need a longer FAQ, because the restriction produces questions a standard cube never gets. A Pauper cube fields "does this count as a common if it was only common in one printing" weekly. A set cube fields "why isn't the best card in the set here." A tribal cube fields "what if I want to play two tribes." Those answers belong in the FAQ, because they are asked constantly and the answers are short. The themed-cube primer article in this series covers the restriction section itself; the FAQ is where the edge cases go.

## FAQ

### How many questions should a cube primer FAQ have?

Eight to twelve, as a house guideline. Start with the questions actually asked at your table and add others as they come up.

### Should card rules questions go in the cube FAQ?

Mostly no. The Comprehensive Rules cover them. One line for an interaction that confuses people every week is fine; more than that becomes a rules document.

### How long should each FAQ answer be?

Two sentences: the direct answer, then the reason or exception. If more is needed, link to the section that holds it.

### How often should the FAQ be updated?

After each cube update, add any new question that survives three weeks of being asked. Every three months, remove the questions nobody asks any more.

## The questions are the welcome

An FAQ written from real questions tells a new drafter that other new drafters have been here, were confused in the same ways, and were answered. That is a kind of welcome, and it is one more reason someone comes back. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
