Most board game gifts are a coin flip. You buy a friend a game you think they will like, and it either becomes a favorite or joins their Shelf of Shame. Board game expansion gifts are different. When you give an expansion to the person who already owns the base game, you are not guessing at taste. You are adding to something the group already plays, and the whole group gets the benefit.

This is the fifth article in The Shared Shelf series from The Ludarium, and it is about turning birthdays and holidays into a library-building tool. We will cover why expansions should always go to the base-game owner, how to keep a simple gift list per person, why a group wishlist beats a group chat scramble, and the etiquette that keeps gifting from getting weird.

The underlying idea is [The One Copy Rule](/articles/one-copy-rule/): a play group needs one copy of each game, not one per person. Gifting is one of the cleanest ways to put that rule to work.

## Why should expansions go to the base-game owner?

An expansion is useless without its base game. A Wingspan expansion gifted to someone who does not own Wingspan is a box of cards with nowhere to go. Gifted to the person who owns the group's copy of Wingspan, it is a bigger Wingspan on the next game night.

That is the whole logic. On a Shared Shelf, the group's copy of a game lives with one owner. Any expansion for that game belongs with the same owner, so the box, the expansion, and the person who knows how to set it up all stay together. Splitting an expansion from its base game across two houses means the group needs both people present to play the full game. Keeping them together means one person brings one bag.

This is also why [Claim a Lane](/articles/claim-a-lane/) matters for gifting. When each person owns a lane, it is obvious where every expansion goes. The co-op owner has Pandemic, so Pandemic expansions go to the co-op owner. Nobody has to think about it.

## Why is a $25 expansion a group asset?

Here is an illustration. Say the group's copy of Carcassonne has been played thirty times. The owner paid about $40, so by cost per play it has already earned its keep. Now a friend gives the owner a $25 expansion for their birthday.

Nothing about that gift stays private. The next ten plays of Carcassonne are the expanded version, and all four people at the table get it. The $25 was spent by one person, received by another, and enjoyed by four. Divided across the table, it is one of the cheapest ways to add new play to a game the group already loves.

Compare that to a $25 standalone game gifted on a hunch. It might be great. It might also be a duplicate of something on the pooled shelf, or a game that fits no lane, or a game the recipient plays once out of politeness. An expansion to a group staple has none of those risks, because the group has already voted with thirty plays.

There is a second reason expansions are good gifts: they are the purchase people least often make for themselves. Owners tend to buy new base games before they buy expansions for old ones. Gifting the expansion fills a gap the owner would have left open. [Board Game Expansions: When They're Worth It and When They're Not](/articles/expansions-worth-it/) covers which expansions deserve the slot.

## How do you build a gift list per person?

The simplest tool here is a short list for each member of the group: the base games they own, and the expansions or accessories the group would actually use. Three to five items per person is plenty.

A per-person list might look like this (illustration):

- **Dana (strategy lane):** owns Wingspan, Terraforming Mars, 7 Wonders. Wants: a Wingspan expansion, a Terraforming Mars expansion, card sleeves for 7 Wonders.
- **Sam (party lane):** owns Codenames, Just One, Wavelength. Wants: a second Codenames word pack, a replacement timer.
- **Priya (co-op lane):** owns Pandemic, The Crew, Spirit Island. Wants: a Spirit Island expansion, a Pandemic expansion.
- **Jordan (2-player and filler):** owns Patchwork, Jaipur, Hive, Sushi Go!, Love Letter. Wants: a Hive expansion piece set, a nicer bag for travel.

Notice what is on these lists and what is not. No new base games unless the group has an empty lane. No duplicates of anything on the pooled shelf. Mostly expansions, upgrades, and the small practical things (sleeves, bags, inserts) that make a shared game last longer.

Keep the lists in the same place as your pooled shelf. If you ran the audit in [How to Audit Your Group's Game Shelf](/articles/audit-your-group-shelf/), add a "wants" column and you are done.

## Why keep a group wishlist?

The per-person list tells you what to buy for one friend. A group wishlist tells you what the whole library is missing. It is the list of games that would fill an empty lane or an uncovered player count, with the lane owner's name next to each.

The group wishlist does three things:

1. **It stops duplicate gifts.** If two people both want to buy the strategy owner a Wingspan expansion for the holidays, the wishlist is where they find out. Mark an item "claimed" and move on.
2. **It makes group gifts easy.** A $100 campaign game like Gloomhaven is a lot for one person. Four people at $25 each is a birthday present. The wishlist is where the group organizes that.
3. **It keeps the One Copy Rule honest.** Anyone tempted to buy a game for themselves checks the wishlist first and sees whether the group already has it or has it covered.

Keep it short. A wishlist of forty items is a Shelf of Shame waiting to happen. Five to ten items, each with a lane and an owner, is the sweet spot.

## What is the etiquette of gifting on a Shared Shelf?

Gifting into a shared library has a few rules that a normal gift does not. They are simple, but they are worth saying.

- **The gift is the recipient's.** An expansion you give to the co-op owner belongs to the co-op owner. It lives with the base game. If they leave the group, it leaves with them, same as in [The Shared Shelf Social Contract](/articles/shared-shelf-social-contract/). Give it freely or do not give it.
- **Ask before gifting outside a lane.** If you want to give the party owner a heavy strategy game, check first. It may be a wonderful gift, but it may also sit unplayed because the group already reaches for the strategy owner's shelf for that kind of night.
- **Do not gift duplicates.** Check the pooled shelf. Always. A gifted second copy of Catan is the most avoidable gift in the hobby.
- **Group gifts need one organizer.** Someone collects the money, buys the game, and hands it over. Four separate Venmo threads is how a group gift turns into three gifts and an apology.
- **A gifted expansion is not an obligation to play it.** The group decides what hits the table. If the expansion turns out to be a dud, say so kindly and move on. The base game is still great.
- **Accessories are always welcome.** Sleeves, inserts, bags, a good timer. Nobody buys these for themselves, everyone is glad to have them, and they make a shared game survive more hands.

## Why gifting is the Shared Shelf at its best

Think about what a group does when it gifts this way. Each person buys something they will never own, for a game they do not keep, that they will play at someone else's table. Nobody is building a private collection. Everyone is building the same library, one expansion at a time.

That is the One Copy Rule turned into generosity. Over a few years of birthdays and holidays, a group's staple games pick up every expansion worth having, the small games get sleeves and bags, and the big campaign game gets bought by four people together. The library gets better, and nobody spent more than they would have spent on gifts anyway.

The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What is a good board game gift for a friend who already has a lot of games?

An expansion for a game they already own and play often. It cannot be a duplicate, it cannot miss their taste, and it improves a game their group has already voted for with real plays. If you are not sure which expansion, accessories such as card sleeves, a box insert, or a travel bag are safe and always appreciated.

### Should you give a board game expansion to someone who does not own the base game?

No. An expansion needs its base game, and splitting the two across different owners means the group needs both people present to play. Give expansions to whoever owns the base game in your group, so the box, the expansion, and the setup knowledge stay together in one bag.

### How do friends organize a group gift for an expensive board game?

Pick one organizer. They put the game on the group wishlist, mark it claimed, collect each person's share, buy the game, and hand it to the lane owner who will store it. Four people at $25 each covers a $100 campaign game. One organizer and one purchase avoids duplicate buys and awkward reimbursements.

### Why are board game expansions better gifts than new games?

A new game is a guess about taste and might duplicate something the group already owns. An expansion adds to a game the group already plays, so every player at the table benefits from the gift. A $25 expansion given to the base-game owner becomes a shared asset that improves the next dozen game nights for everyone.
