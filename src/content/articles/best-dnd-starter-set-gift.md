A D&D starter set gift is one of the few presents that can start a weekly tradition. It is also one that often ends up unopened, because the recipient did not know what to do next. The difference is usually not the box. It is whether anyone at the table was willing to be the Dungeon Master.

At the time of writing there are two current Dungeons & Dragons starter sets from Wizards of the Coast, at very different prices and built for very different tables. This guide explains which to give, who it fits, and the two small additions that turn the box into a game night.

## Which D&D starter sets exist right now?

**Dungeons & Dragons Starter Set: Heroes of the Borderlands** <!-- AFFILIATE --> is the newest, released in September 2025 and built for the 2024 rules. It lists at $49.99, per [EN World's coverage of the price announcement](https://www.enworld.org/posts/9706779/); [Noble Knight listed it at $44.95](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2148328562/Starter-Set---Heroes-of-the-Borderlands) at the time of writing. It is a big box: three adventure booklets, class boards for building characters from cards and tiles, nine double-sided poster maps, over 200 cards, over 270 tokens, and eleven dice.

**Dungeons & Dragons Starter Set: Dragons of Stormwreck Isle** <!-- AFFILIATE --> is the 2022 set built for the 2014 rules. It is a thin box with a rulebook, a single campaign adventure, five pregenerated character sheets, and one set of dice. [D&D Beyond's marketplace lists it from $14.99](https://marketplace.dndbeyond.com/category/starter-set) at the time of writing, though stock varies and some retailers have run out.

Both are complete: a group can open either box and play without buying anything else. The question is which kind of group.

## Heroes of the Borderlands vs Dragons of Stormwreck Isle

| | Heroes of the Borderlands | Dragons of Stormwreck Isle |
|---|---|---|
| Approximate price | $45 to $50 | $15 to $20 |
| Rules | 2024 edition | 2014 edition |
| Character creation | Class boards and cards, no character sheet | Five pregenerated sheets |
| Adventure format | Short scenarios, about one to two hours each | One connected campaign |
| Maps and tokens | Poster maps, 270+ tokens | None (theater of the mind) |
| Best for | Board gamers, families, groups with no experienced player | A reader who wants the real thing cheaply |

Reviewers broadly agree on the trade. [RPGBOT's review](https://rpgbot.net/heroes-of-the-borderlands-is-the-new-dnd-starter-set-worth-the-cost/) calls Heroes of the Borderlands worth it for groups of mostly new players, especially board gamers, and lists the higher price and the lack of a connecting story as the main downsides. Stormwreck Isle is the traditional approach: read the rulebook, hand out character sheets, run a campaign cover to cover.

## Which one should you give?

Give **Heroes of the Borderlands** if any of these are true:

- The recipient has played modern board games but never a role-playing game.
- The gift is for a family or a friend group, not one person.
- Nobody in the group has been a Dungeon Master. The scenarios are short and the DM guidance sits right in the booklets, which lowers the bar for whoever volunteers.
- You want the box itself to feel like a present. It does.

Give **Dragons of Stormwreck Isle** if:

- The recipient is a reader who has watched actual-play shows and wants to learn properly.
- Budget is under $20.
- Someone in their group already plays and can run it.
- You know they will want to move on to the full rulebooks soon. The character sheets in this set are the ones they will keep using.

If you have no information at all, give Heroes of the Borderlands. The extra $30 buys a much higher chance that the box gets opened and played the same week. We have written a longer piece on [how to play D&D for beginners](/articles/how-to-play-dnd-for-beginners/) that pairs well with either box.

## What should you add to the box?

The starter set is complete, but two cheap additions change the odds that it becomes a habit.

**A second set of dice.** Each box includes one set. At a table of four or five, dice get passed and the game slows. A set of polyhedral dice runs about $4 to $15 <!-- AFFILIATE --> ([a basic Chessex seven-die set is $3.49 at Miniature Market](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/chessex-dice-set-opaque-pastel-blueblack-7-chx25466.html) at the time of writing), and giving every player their own is a small ceremony that new players remember.

**A promise to run the first session, or a link to someone who will.** This is the real gift. Most starter sets go unplayed because nobody wants to be the Dungeon Master. If you can run session one, write that on the card. If you cannot, find them a drop-in game at a local store or a [D&D group near them](/articles/how-to-find-a-dnd-group/). In Indianapolis, several stores run learn-to-play tables, and our own lounge will host beginner one-shots when it opens.

Nice but optional:

- **A dry-erase battle mat** (about $20 to $30; [Chessex's reversible Battlemat is $22.99 at Miniature Market](https://miniaturemarket.com/reversible-battlemat-1-inch-square-blackgrey-235-x-26-chx96480.html) at the time of writing) if they got Stormwreck Isle, which has no maps.
- **A folding DM screen** (about $16 to $20; [the official 2024 screen is $15.99 at Miniature Market](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/dd-5e-rpg-dungeon-masters-screen-2024-wocd3897.html) against a $19.99 list price at the time of writing). Mostly theater, but new DMs like having a wall to hide behind.
- **Pencils.** Dozens. They vanish.

## What about the Essentials Kit and third-party boxes?

The **D&D Essentials Kit** (2019) was a middle option with a DM screen, a longer adventure, and rules for playing with just one player and one DM. It is built for the 2014 rules and has been harder to find since the 2024 edition arrived; if you see it at or under its $24.99 list price ([Miniature Market had it at $19.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/wocc70080.html) at the time of writing), it remains a good gift for a pair.

Other publishers make excellent beginner boxes too. The **Pathfinder Beginner Box** <!-- AFFILIATE --> is the most common alternative and is a more rules-heavy game; our [D&D vs Pathfinder for beginners](/articles/dnd-vs-pathfinder-beginners/) comparison explains the difference. For a complete newcomer who asked for "D&D," give them D&D. The name on the box matters to a beginner.

## Is a starter set a good gift for a kid?

Yes, from about age ten with an adult willing to run it, and from twelve or thirteen for a group of friends on their own. Heroes of the Borderlands is the better kid gift: the tokens, maps, and cards give young players something to hold, and the short scenarios fit an after-school window. The reading level of Stormwreck Isle's rulebook is a stretch for younger kids without help.

## Why a starter set is worth giving at all

A D&D starter set is a box of permission. It says: you and four friends are allowed to sit at a table every other Thursday, turn your phones face down, and make up a story together for three hours. Most adults have not had that permission since college. Many kids have never had it.

The dice and maps are props. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What is the best D&D starter set for beginners?

Heroes of the Borderlands (2025), for most new groups. It costs about $45 to $50 at the time of writing, uses the current rules, and is designed so that nobody needs prior experience to run it.

### Is Dragons of Stormwreck Isle still worth buying?

Yes, if budget is the constraint or the recipient wants a traditional campaign with character sheets. It was listed from $14.99 at D&D Beyond at the time of writing, though stock is thinning.

### Does a D&D starter set include everything you need?

Both current sets include rules, an adventure, characters, and dice. Add a second set of dice and pencils. Heroes of the Borderlands also includes maps and tokens; Stormwreck Isle does not.

### Do you need a Dungeon Master to use a starter set?

Yes. One player runs the game. Heroes of the Borderlands makes this easier with short scenarios and guidance in the booklets, but someone still has to volunteer.
