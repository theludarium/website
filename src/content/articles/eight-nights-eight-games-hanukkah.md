Looking for Hanukkah game gifts that fill all eight nights? Here is a plan: one game per night, starting small and quick and building to a bigger family game on the last night. Each one is a real, in-print game that works across ages, and the whole set costs less than one big electronic gift.

Many families give a small gift on some or all of the nights. Games make a good fit because each one is also a reason to stay at the table after the candles are lit. This is a family game guide, not a guide to the holiday itself; every home has its own customs, and the plan bends to fit them.

**The quick answer:** Start with dreidel and Spot It! on the first night, add Outfoxed!, Sushi Go!, Kingdomino, Codenames, Azul, and Cascadia across the middle nights, and finish with Ticket to Ride. Swap in any game that suits your family better.

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, and ends at nightfall on Saturday, December 12, [according to Hebcal](https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/chanukah-2026). The first and eighth candles both fall on Friday nights (December 4 and December 11). Families who keep Shabbat will know their own practice, so neither night's game needs writing or a score pad.

## How should you plan Hanukkah game gifts across eight nights?

A few principles shaped the order.

- **Start small, end big.** Early nights get quick games that teach in two minutes. The last nights get the longer games, after everyone has warmed up.
- **Weeknights get short games.** Monday through Thursday are school and work nights, so those games run 15 to 45 minutes.
- **Mix the ages.** Two picks are aimed at younger kids, two at teens and adults, and the rest play well across the whole table.
- **One gift, many players.** Every game here plays at least four, so it is a gift for the household, not just one person.

If you only want to buy one or two games, the best single picks are Kingdomino for a family with kids and Codenames for a family of teens and adults.

## Night 1: Dreidel and Spot It!

Start with the game that comes with the holiday. Dreidel needs a top and a pile of chocolate coins or other tokens, and it teaches in a minute. Our guide on [how to play dreidel](/articles/how-to-play-dreidel/) covers the letters, a turn step by step, and house rules that keep the game from dragging.

Pair it with [Spot It!](https://store.asmodee.com/collections/spot-it/products/spot-it-classic-pocket-eco-ml) <!-- AFFILIATE --> for the first gift. Every pair of round cards shares exactly one symbol, and you race to spot it and call it out. Two to eight players, about 15 minutes, ages 6 and up. The pocket edition lists for about $6 at the Asmodee store, and larger editions run up to about $15. It is cheap, fast, and the youngest reader at the table can win.

## Night 2: Outfoxed!

[Outfoxed!](https://www.target.com/p/outfoxed-a-cooperative-whodunit-game/-/A-51116319) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Gamewright is a cooperative whodunit for little kids. A fox stole a pot pie, and everyone works together to find out which suspect did it before the fox escapes. Players roll dice to reveal clues and suspects, then use a decoder to rule suspects out.

Two to four players, about 20 minutes, ages 5 and up, per [Gamewright](https://gamewright.com/product/Outfoxed). About $25 to $29 at major retailers at the time of writing. Because everyone wins or loses together, it is a gentle choice for a family with a preschooler and an older sibling. For more like it, see our list of [cooperative games for kids](/articles/cooperative-games-for-kids/).

## Night 3: Sushi Go!

[Sushi Go!](https://www.ceaco.com/products/sushi-go) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is a card drafting game in a small tin. Everyone takes one card from their hand and passes the rest, building sets of sushi dishes for points over three rounds. It teaches in two minutes and has real choices in it.

Two to five players, about 15 minutes, ages 8 and up. About $17 at Gamewright's store. It is the best first strategy game for kids we know of, and adults keep playing it after the kids go to bed.

## Night 4: Kingdomino

[Kingdomino](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/f6wzmrIbqg/kingdomino) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Blue Orange uses domino-style tiles to build a small kingdom of fields, lakes, forests, and mines. The clever part is the draft: the better the tile you take this round, the later you pick next round.

Two to four players, about 15 minutes, ages 8 and up. About $20 at major retailers at the time of writing. It won the Spiel des Jahres, Germany's game of the year award, in 2017, and it is one of the easiest real strategy games to teach a mixed-age table.

## Night 5: Codenames

[Codenames](https://www.target.com/p/czech-games-edition-codenames-game/-/A-94111185) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Czech Games Edition is the party game for the night the teens and adults take over. Two teams face a grid of 25 words. One player on each team gives one-word clues to steer their teammates to the right words and away from the assassin.

Two to eight or more players, about 15 minutes, ages 14 and up. About $25 at Target at the time of writing. It scales to a full house, and it plays well with grandparents, cousins, and anyone who likes words.

## Night 6: Azul

[Azul](https://www.walmart.com/c/kp/game-azul) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Next Move Games is a beautiful tile-drafting game. Players take colored tiles from shared piles to decorate a palace wall, and every tile you cannot place costs you points. The chunky tiles are half the appeal.

Two to four players, 30 to 45 minutes, ages 8 and up. About $32 to $40, with a $39.99 list price. It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2018. It suits a family where the kids are ready for a game with a bit more thinking in it.

## Night 7: Cascadia

[Cascadia](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/12975B7EQh/cascadia) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Flatout Games and AEG has you build a landscape of habitat tiles and fill it with bears, elk, salmon, hawks, and foxes, each scoring in its own pattern. It is calm, pretty, and rewarding for players who like puzzles.

One to four players, 30 to 45 minutes, ages 10 and up. About $32 to $40 at the time of writing. It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2022. It also has a solo mode, so the person who unwraps it can play it alone on a quiet night.

## Night 8: Ticket to Ride

Finish with the biggest box. [Ticket to Ride](https://www.daysofwonder.com/game/ticket-to-ride/) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from Days of Wonder has players collect colored train cards and claim railway routes across a map of North America, trying to connect the cities on their secret tickets.

Two to five players, 30 to 60 minutes, ages 8 and up. List price is about $55, and [Walmart had it for less](https://www.walmart.com/ip/Ticket-to-Ride-Strategy-Board-Game/11033837) at the time of writing. It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2004 and is still one of the best first "big" board games for a family. Ending on it gives the last night a game long enough to linger over.

## What if you want to spend less?

The full plan runs roughly $200 to $240. To trim it:

- **Swap the big three.** Replace Azul, Cascadia, and Ticket to Ride with small card games under $20, like Love Letter, Qwixx, or Hanabi. Our list of [board games under $20](/articles/board-games-under-20-dollars/) has more.
- **Give one game, play it for several nights.** A single good family game played three or four nights in a row is a gift too. Kids often like a game more on the third play than the first.
- **Use what you have.** A deck of cards, a dreidel, and a jar of chocolate coins can fill a night.

If your family likes to vote on what to play, [Game Night Madness](/game-night-madness/) is a free bracket app where everyone picks from their phones. It works well on a night when nobody can decide.

## What makes a good family game for Hanukkah?

The same things that make any game a good gift. It should teach fast, finish before bedtime, and seat the people who live in the house. For a table that spans three generations, our guide to [board games for all ages](/articles/multigenerational-board-games/) goes deeper.

Mostly, it should get people to stay. The candles burn for a while, and a game is a good way to spend that time together. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What is a good Hanukkah gift for a family?

A game everyone can play together. Kingdomino suits families with kids 8 and up, Outfoxed! suits younger kids, and Codenames suits teens and adults.

### Do I need a gift for every night of Hanukkah?

No. Customs vary from family to family. A plan like this one is just one option, and one good game played over several nights works too.

### What games can young kids play during Hanukkah?

Dreidel works for kids as young as about 4 with help reading the letters. Outfoxed! is rated 5 and up and Spot It! 6 and up, and all three run about 15 to 20 minutes.
