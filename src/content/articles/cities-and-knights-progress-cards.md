[Catan: Cities & Knights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) has 54 progress cards, split into three decks of 18: trade (cloth), politics (coin), and science (paper). The 6th edition rulebook lists 25 different cards across those decks, two of them Victory Point cards that you play the moment you draw them. This guide covers every one: how many copies are in the deck, what it does in plain words, when to play it, and how strong it is. You need the [Catan base game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) to play the expansion.

**The quick answer:** the strongest cards in each deck are **Alchemy** and **Medicine** (science), **Merchant** and **Resource Monopoly** (trade), and **Treason**, **Espionage**, and **Wedding** (politics). The weakest are the ones that depend on a board state you may never see, like **Diplomacy** and **Intrigue**. Learn the decks, because the deck you climb decides which of these cards you will actually hold.

## How progress cards work

Progress cards replace base Catan's development cards. You never buy them; the dice deal them. When the event die shows a city improvement icon (trade, politics, or science), every player checks the red production die against their level on that track. If the red die is inside your range, you draw the top card of that deck, in turn order starting with the roller.

Your range grows as you build city improvements. At level 1 you draw on a red 1 or 2. At level 2 you draw on a red 1, 2, or 3. Each level after that adds one more number, so at level 5 any red die draws a card. You need at least one city to build any improvement, so an early city is your ticket into the decks. Our lesson on [commodities and city improvements](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/commodities-and-city-improvements/) covers the costs.

The rules for holding and playing cards are short:

- **Hand limit of 4.** Over the limit on someone else's turn, discard at once. On your own turn, you have until the end of your Action phase.
- **Play on your Action phase,** as many as you like, including a card drawn this turn.
- **Two exceptions.** Alchemy is played before you roll. Victory Point cards (Printing and Constitution) go face up in front of you the moment you draw them, even on another player's turn. They skip the hand limit and cannot be stolen.
- **No trading progress cards,** and a card that does nothing when played still counts as played.

One more way to draw: if the defenders beat the barbarians and two or more players tie for the most knight strength, no one gets the VP token. Each tied player draws a progress card from the deck of their choice instead. Our lesson on [the barbarian ship and defending Catan](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/barbarians-and-defending-catan/) has the full attack rules.

## Trade progress cards (cloth)

The trade deck turns other players' cards into yours and makes your trades cheaper. A third of it is the Merchant. Trade improvements cost cloth, which cities on pastures produce. Our lesson on [choosing your improvement track](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/choosing-your-improvement-track/) shows how to tell if trade is your track.

### Merchant (6 copies)

Take the merchant piece and put it on any land hex next to one of your buildings. While it is yours, you can trade that hex's resource to the bank at 2:1. Commodities do not count. Holding the merchant is also worth 1 VP, and it moves to whoever plays the next Merchant card.

**When to play it:** on a hex whose resource you hold in bulk, on a turn you plan to trade. Late in the game, play it for the point, or to take the merchant from a rival near 13.

**How strong:** very strong. A movable 2:1 port plus a point, with six copies in the deck. Our lesson on [the merchant and trade](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/the-merchant-and-trade/) goes deeper.

### Resource Monopoly (4 copies)

Name a resource. Each other player gives you 2 of it, or 1 if that is all they have.

**When to play it:** after a round where one resource has been flowing. At four players that is up to 6 cards.

**How strong:** strong. It is smaller than the base game Monopoly (which takes every card of a type), but there are four of them.

### Trade Monopoly (2 copies)

Name a commodity. Each other player gives you 1 of it if they have one.

**When to play it:** when you are a commodity or two short of your next improvement and rivals are saving that commodity for theirs.

**How strong:** solid. Commodities are scarce, so even 2 or 3 of them can buy a level.

### Merchant Fleet (2 copies)

Name one resource or commodity. For the rest of the turn, you can trade it to the bank at 2:1 as many times as you like.

**When to play it:** when you hold a stack of one card, especially a commodity your own track does not use.

**How strong:** good when you plan for it, weak when you do not.

### Commercial Harbor (2 copies)

You may offer each other player one resource card. Each one who has a commodity must give you a commodity of their choice in return. If they have none, you take your resource back. You can make these offers any time during the turn you play it, one offer per player.

**When to play it:** when you have spare resources and the table is sitting on commodities.

**How strong:** middling. Commodities are worth more than resources, but they choose which one you get, so expect their least useful.

### Guild Dues (2 copies)

Look at the hand of a player with more VPs than you, and take any 2 resource or commodity cards you choose.

**When to play it:** when the leader holds a big hand before their turn. You see their plan and take the two cards that matter.

**How strong:** strong, but dead when you lead.

## Politics progress cards (coin)

The politics deck is the attack deck: it moves the robber, removes knights and roads, and takes cards from leaders. Politics costs coin, which cities on mountains produce, so it pairs with a knights plan. Our lesson on [knights](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/knights-build-activate-promote/) covers recruiting and promoting.

### Constitution (1 copy)

Worth 1 VP, placed face up at once, even on another player's turn.

**How strong:** a free point, one of two VP cards in the game.

### Treason (2 copies)

Choose another player. They must remove one of their knights from the board (they pick which one). You may then place one of your knights of the same strength or lower, following the normal placement rules, with the same active or inactive status. They must remove a knight even if you cannot place one. If they remove a mighty knight, you may place a mighty knight even if you have not reached politics level 3.

**When to play it:** right before a barbarian attack, or to clear a knight blocking your road.

**How strong:** very strong. It removes a piece and often adds one of yours.

### Espionage (3 copies)

Look at another player's hand of progress cards and take one if you want it. VP cards cannot be taken.

**When to play it:** on whoever has drawn the most cards lately.

**How strong:** strong, and it shows you their next turn even if you take nothing.

### Wedding (2 copies)

Each player with more VPs than you gives you 2 resource or commodity cards of their choice. Anyone with fewer than 2 gives what they can.

**When to play it:** when two or three players are ahead. They choose the cards, but it can be 6 cards at four players.

**How strong:** strong when you trail, useless when you lead.

### Sabotage (2 copies)

Each player with as many VPs as you or more must discard half of their resource and commodity cards, rounded down.

**When to play it:** when the leaders hold big hands. It also hits anyone tied with you.

**How strong:** a strong catch-up card, though it adds nothing to your own hand.

### Taxation (2 copies)

Move the robber to a new hex. Take 1 random resource or commodity card from each player with a building on that hex (1 card per player, no matter how many buildings they have there). Progress cards cannot be taken. You can only play Taxation after the first barbarian attack, because that is when the robber comes onto the board.

**When to play it:** on a hex where two or more rivals have buildings.

**How strong:** good: a robber move and several steals, no 7 needed.

### Encouragement (2 copies)

Activate all of your knights for free.

**When to play it:** before the barbarians land, with three or more inactive knights.

**How strong:** worth 1 grain per sleeping knight, so great with a big army and weak with one knight.

### Diplomacy (2 copies)

Remove an open road from the board. If it belongs to someone else, it goes back to their supply. If it is yours, you may build one road somewhere else for free. A road is open if one of its ends touches none of that player's other roads or buildings, and it is not part of a continuous route linking two of their buildings or knights.

**When to play it:** to break a rival's Longest Road at its loose end, or to move your own dead-end road.

**How strong:** situational.

### Intrigue (2 copies)

Displace another player's knight without using one of your own. That knight must be on an intersection connected to at least one of your routes. The displaced knight moves as it would in a normal displacement.

**When to play it:** when an enemy knight blocks your road or a building site. See our lesson on [knight tactics](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/knight-tactics/).

**How strong:** situational, often dead in hand.

## Science progress cards (paper)

The science deck builds your own engine: resources, discounts, and control of the dice. Science costs paper, which cities on forests produce. Its cards help you no matter what rivals do, which makes it a safe first track. See our lesson on [the level 3 abilities](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/level-three-improvement-abilities/) for what each track gives you at level 3.

### Printing (1 copy)

Worth 1 VP, placed face up the moment you draw it.

**How strong:** a free point.

### Alchemy (2 copies)

Play it at the start of your Roll Dice phase, before you roll. Set the two production dice to any result you want. Then roll the event die and resolve it as normal.

**When to play it:** when one number pays you a lot, or you need one exact resource to finish a build.

**How strong:** the best science card. To see what a single roll is worth to you, count your pips with our [number probability](/academy/catan/number-probability/) lesson.

### Medicine (2 copies)

Upgrade a settlement to a city for 1 grain and 2 ore instead of the usual cost. One Medicine per upgrade.

**When to play it:** as soon as you can afford it.

**How strong:** strong. It saves 1 grain and 1 ore on the build the whole expansion runs on.

### Crane (2 copies)

Build one city improvement for 1 commodity less than normal. One Crane per improvement. A level 1 improvement costs 1 commodity, so Crane can make it free.

**When to play it:** on a level 4 or 5 improvement, where a metropolis race can turn on one turn. See our lesson on [metropolis races](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/metropolis-races/).

**How strong:** good. One commodity is about two rounds of output from a city on a 6 or 8 hex.

### Irrigation (2 copies)

Take 2 grain for each field hex next to at least one of your buildings. Each field counts once, however many of your buildings touch it.

**When to play it:** when you touch two or more fields.

**How strong:** strong on a grain board, weak with one field.

### Mining (2 copies)

Take 2 ore for each mountain hex next to at least one of your buildings. Like Irrigation, each hex counts once.

**How strong:** strong on an ore board. Mining plus Medicine is a fast city.

### Smithing (2 copies)

Promote up to 2 of your knights for free. A knight can still only be promoted once per turn, and you still need politics level 3 to make a mighty knight.

**When to play it:** before a barbarian attack, or before you displace a rival's knight.

**How strong:** good. Each promotion normally costs 1 wool and 1 ore.

### Road Building (2 copies)

Build 2 roads for free.

**How strong:** good, as in the base game: grab a site or take Longest Road.

### Invention (2 copies)

Swap any two number discs on the board, except a 2, 6, 8, or 12. You do not need a building next to either disc. The robber stays where it is.

**When to play it:** move a 5 or 9 onto your hex and a 3 or 11 onto a rival's.

**How strong:** quietly strong. The swap is permanent.

### Engineering (1 copy)

Build one city wall for free.

**How strong:** modest. Each wall lets you hold 2 more cards on a 7, up to 3 walls.

## FAQ

### How many progress cards are in Cities & Knights?

54: 18 trade, 18 politics, and 18 science. Each deck is sorted and shuffled on its own during setup.

### Can you play a progress card the turn you draw it?

Yes. You can play a progress card on the same turn you draw it, during your Action phase. Alchemy is the exception: it must be played before you roll, so a freshly drawn Alchemy waits for your next turn.

### What is the progress card hand limit?

Four cards, not counting Victory Point cards. If you go over on another player's turn, you discard at once. On your own turn you have until the end of your Action phase.

### Can you trade or steal progress cards?

You cannot trade them. Only Espionage takes a progress card from another player, and it cannot take a VP card. Taxation and the robber take resources and commodities, never progress cards.

### Which progress card deck should I go for?

Go for the deck your cities already pay for: forests give paper for science, pastures give cloth for trade, and mountains give coin for politics. Our lesson on [progress cards by track](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/progress-cards-by-track/) covers how often you will draw at each level.
