The [Sequence](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09FSGVW4X?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) game rules are simple: play a card from your hand, put a chip on the matching picture on the board, and try to get five of your chips in a row. A row of five is called a sequence. With two players or two teams you need two sequences to win; with three players or three teams you need one. Jacks are the twist. Two-eyed jacks are wild, and one-eyed jacks let you pull an opponent's chip off the board.

**The quick answer:** deal cards by player count, then take turns playing one card and placing one chip on a space that shows that card. Draw a new card at the end of your turn. Two-eyed jacks place a chip anywhere open; one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip. The four corners count for everyone. First to the required number of sequences wins.

Sequence was designed by Douglas Reuter and first sold by Jax Ltd in 1982. Goliath Games owns it today. It plays 2 to 12 people, which is why it shows up at so many family tables and big group nights.

## What is on the Sequence board?

The board is a 10 by 10 grid of playing card pictures. The game uses two standard decks of cards, and every card except the jacks appears on the board twice. That is 96 spaces, plus the four corners, for 100 in all.

- **The corners** are marked as free spaces. They belong to every player at once, so a row through a corner needs only four of your own chips.
- **The jacks** never appear on the board. All eight live only in the deck, and they do special things (more below).
- **The chips** come in three colors, one per player or team.

Because each card has two matching spaces, you usually get a choice of where to play. That choice is most of the strategy.

## How many cards do you deal in Sequence?

Shuffle both decks together. Each player gets this many cards:

| Players | Cards each |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 7 |
| 3 | 6 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 6 | 5 |
| 8 | 4 |
| 9 | 4 |
| 10 | 3 |
| 12 | 3 |

Notice the gaps: 5, 7 and 11 are not on the list. Players have to split evenly into teams, so Sequence works with 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 or 12 people. Play starts with the player to the dealer's left and goes clockwise.

## How to play Sequence: what happens on a turn?

Every turn has the same three steps.

### 1. Play a card

Choose a card from your hand and place it face up on your own discard pile, so everyone can see what you played.

### 2. Place a chip

Put one of your chips on either of the two spaces that show that card. If both are already taken, you cannot use the card that way (see dead cards below).

### 3. Draw a card

Take the top card of the deck to bring your hand back to full. Here is the catch: if you forget to draw before the next player draws, you lose that draw and play the rest of the game one card short. It is a small rule that keeps everyone paying attention.

Once a chip is on the board, it stays there unless a one-eyed jack removes it.

## What are the one-eyed and two-eyed jacks in Sequence?

The eight jacks in the deck come in two kinds, and you tell them apart by the face on the card. In a standard deck, the jacks of spades and hearts show one eye, and the jacks of clubs and diamonds show two.

- **Two-eyed jacks are wild.** Play one to put your chip on any open space on the board. They are the best card in your hand, so many players save them to finish a row or block a big threat.
- **One-eyed jacks remove.** Play one to take an opponent's chip off any space. You do not place a chip of your own with it. You cannot remove a chip that is already part of a finished sequence.

That last rule matters. Once a sequence is complete, it is locked. A one-eyed jack is best used before an opponent finishes their row, on the chip that would hurt them most.

## What is a dead card in Sequence?

A dead card is one whose two matching spaces are both covered. You cannot play it normally. On your turn, before you play, announce that you have a dead card, place it on your discard pile, and draw a replacement. Then take your normal turn. You can turn in only one dead card per turn.

## How many sequences do you need to win?

It depends on how many players or teams are in the game:

| Players or teams | Sequences to win |
| --- | --- |
| 2 players or 2 teams | 2 |
| 3 players or 3 teams | 1 |

A sequence is five chips of your color in a straight line: across, down, or diagonal. A corner space counts as one of the five.

When you need two sequences, they can share one chip. So a single straight line of nine of your chips counts as two sequences. Two sequences cannot share more than one space.

## How do Sequence teams work?

With more than three players, everyone splits evenly into two or three teams. Four players make two teams of two; six players can be two teams of three or three teams of two; nine players make three teams of three, and so on.

Team rules:

- **Teammates do not sit together.** Alternate seats around the table so turns rotate between teams.
- **Each team shares one chip color** and builds sequences together.
- **No table talk.** You cannot tell partners what cards you hold, coach them, or hint at where to play. In the official rules, if a player makes a remark that tips off a partner, everyone on that team must discard a card. Most family tables enforce this loosely, but it is worth agreeing up front.

The no-talking rule is what makes team play fun. You are reading your partner's plays and trying to build toward the same line without a word.

## Sequence rules for 2 players

Two-player Sequence uses the same rules with a few numbers changed:

- Each player gets **7 cards**.
- You need **2 sequences** to win.
- There are no teams, so there is no table talk rule to worry about.

Two-player games are more tactical than team games because you control every chip of your color. Blocking matters more, and a one-eyed jack held until the right moment can swing the game. ## Sequence tips for new players

1. **Use the corners.** A row that touches a corner needs only four of your chips. Build toward them early.
2. **Play the space that does two jobs.** With two matching spaces per card, pick the one that extends your row and blocks an opponent at once.
3. **Save your two-eyed jacks.** A wild is most valuable when it completes a sequence or plugs a hole an opponent cannot fill any other way.
4. **Spend one-eyed jacks on four in a row.** Pull the chip that breaks the longest open threat, not just any chip.
5. **Watch the discards.** Both copies of each card are on the board. If one copy is already played and the space is covered, you can count what is left.
6. **Think about the shared chip.** In a two-sequence game, plan your first row so it can extend into a second one through a shared end chip.
7. **Turn in dead cards early.** A dead card is a wasted slot in your hand. Swap it the first turn you can.

## FAQ

### How many players can play Sequence?

Sequence plays 2 to 12 people. With more than three, players split evenly into two or three teams, so the playable counts are 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 12.

### Can a one-eyed jack remove a chip from a completed sequence?

No. Once five chips form a sequence, those chips are safe for the rest of the game. A one-eyed jack can remove any other opponent chip.

### Do the corners count in Sequence?

Yes. The four corner spaces count as a chip for every player and team at the same time, so a row through a corner needs only four of your own chips.

### Can two sequences share a chip?

Yes, one chip. In a game that needs two sequences, your second row can reuse one chip from your first, so nine in a straight line counts as two.

### What happens if you forget to draw a card in Sequence?

If the next player draws before you do, you lose that card. You keep playing with one fewer card in your hand for the rest of the game.
