The best two-player card games fit in a coat pocket and still give you a real duel. For a quick, friendly trading game, start with [Jaipur](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SRMXRZB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a tense battle of nerves over a few cards, play [Schotten Totten](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01DCGBMPU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or [Air, Land & Sea](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08NVB7BS6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For couples who grew up on classic card games, try [The Fox in the Forest](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XP7ZDH1?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or plain old [Cribbage](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000I5NW66?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And for a real fight, [Star Realms](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00HRGMPIU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Radlands](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884136?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Res Arcana](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PFRTSB2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Arboretum](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07CRQ8YHX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) reward a few plays of practice.

**The quick answer:** if you buy one, buy **Jaipur**. It teaches in five minutes, plays in about half an hour, and is still fun after a hundred games. If you want something sharper and shorter, **Air, Land & Sea** fits a whole battle into about 20 minutes.

This list is card games only: no boards to unfold, no miniatures, nothing bigger than a hand of cards and a few tokens. ## What makes a great two-player card game?

A lot of card games say "2 to 6 players" on the box and quietly play worst at two. The ones on this list were built for two, or play at their best with two. They share a few things:

1. **Every card is a decision.** With only two hands in play, there is nowhere to hide. Good duels make each card feel like it matters, and make you wonder what the other person is holding.
2. **Short rounds, quick rematches.** Most of these play in 20 to 30 minutes. That makes "best two out of three" easy, which is half the fun.
3. **A small footprint.** These are games you can play on a coffee table, a café table, or a tray table on a train.
4. **Tension without meanness.** A two-player game is a conversation between two people. The best ones keep it competitive without leaving anyone sore. Our guide to [board games for couples](/articles/board-games-for-couples/) has more on keeping game night friendly.

## Quick duels for any night

These four are the easiest to learn and the quickest to play. Any of them is a good first two-player card game.

### Jaipur

Designed by Sébastien Pauchon and first published in 2009, Jaipur plays 2 in about 30 minutes. You are two merchants in the market of Jaipur. On your turn you either take cards or sell them, never both. Sell a set of goods and you take tokens that shrink in value as the market fills up, so selling early pays more, while selling big earns bonus tokens.

**Why it fits:** [Jaipur](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SRMXRZB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the best all-around pick for two because every turn is a small, clear choice with a little tension in it. Camels add a clever wrinkle: taking them all can refill the market with exactly the cards your opponent wanted. Rounds are short, and you play best two of three. It also shows up on our list of [board game gifts for couples](/articles/board-game-gifts-for-couples/).

### Lost Cities

Designed by Reiner Knizia and first published in 1999, Lost Cities plays 2 in about 30 minutes. You fund expeditions to five lost cities by playing cards in rising order, color by color. Every expedition you start costs you points up front, so you only want to start the ones you can finish well.

**Why it fits:** [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the calmest game here. You barely touch each other, but the shared discard piles mean every card you throw away might help the other person. It is a natural pick for couples who like a quiet puzzle more than a fight. A round takes about ten minutes, so it fits after dinner.

### Schotten Totten

Designed by Reiner Knizia and first published in 1999, Schotten Totten plays 2. Nine boundary stones sit between you. At each stone, both players build a three-card hand, a bit like poker, and the stronger hand claims the stone. Claim five stones, or three in a row, and you win.

**Why it fits:** [Schotten Totten](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01DCGBMPU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) turns one card game into nine small ones played at once. The hard part is commitment: you can only play a card to your own side, and once it is down, it stays. Anyone who knows poker hands will learn it in minutes. It was rethemed in 2000 as Battle Line, with soldiers and more cards, if you like the idea and want a bigger version.

### Air, Land & Sea

Designed by Jon Perry and published by Arcane Wonders in 2019, Air, Land & Sea plays 2 in about 20 minutes. There are three theaters, air, land, and sea, and only 18 cards in the whole deck. Each battle, you get six cards and try to control two of the three theaters. You can play a card face up for its ability or face down as a weak wild card.

**Why it fits:** [Air, Land & Sea](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08NVB7BS6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is about knowing when to quit. You can withdraw from a battle early to give up fewer points, and good players do it often. The first to 12 points wins the war. It is the most "every card matters" game on this list, and the one most likely to make you say "one more."

## Card games for couples who grew up on classic card games

If your history together includes spades, rummy, or euchre at the kitchen table, these two will feel familiar fast.

### The Fox in the Forest

Designed by Joshua Buergel and published in 2017 by Foxtrot Games and Renegade Game Studios, The Fox in the Forest plays 2 in about 30 minutes. It is a trick-taking game, like hearts or spades, built for two. The odd-numbered cards have fairy-tale powers: a 3 lets you change the trump card, and a 5 lets you draw a card and then discard one.

**Why it fits:** the twist is in the scoring. Win too many tricks and you score nothing, so sometimes the smart play is to lose on purpose. [The Fox in the Forest](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XP7ZDH1?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a gentle way to bring a classic card game habit into modern games. If you like it, The Fox in the Forest Duet is the cooperative version, covered in our [two-player co-op guide](/articles/two-player-cooperative-board-games/).

### Arboretum

Designed by Dan Cassar and first published in 2015, Arboretum plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes, and it is at its best with two. Each turn you draw two cards, play one tree into your garden, and discard one. You score paths of trees that rise in value from one tree type to another.

**Why it fits:** [Arboretum](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07CRQ8YHX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) looks peaceful and is not. You can only score a tree type if you hold the highest total of that type in your hand at the end. So every card you keep in hand blocks your partner, and every card you play leaves you exposed. It is the pick for couples who like a quiet game with a sting in it.

## Head-to-head battles

These three are more direct. You attack, defend, and build an engine, all in under an hour. They suit players who already like strategy games.

### Star Realms

Designed by Rob Dougherty and Darwin Kastle and published in 2014, Star Realms plays 2 in about 20 minutes. Each player starts with a small deck and 50 points of authority. You buy ships and bases from a shared trade row, build a stronger deck, and use it to attack the other player until one of you hits zero.

**Why it fits:** [Star Realms](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00HRGMPIU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the quickest way to learn deck-building, the idea behind games like Dominion. It plays fast and fits in a tiny box. Our explainer on [deck-building games](/articles/deck-building-games-explained/) covers how the system works and where to go next.

### Radlands

Designed by Daniel Piechnick and published by Roxley, Radlands plays 2 in 20 to 40 minutes. Each player protects three camps in a post-apocalyptic wasteland, spending water to play people and events and to use their abilities. Destroy all three of the other player's camps and you win.

**Why it fits:** [Radlands](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884136?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the most aggressive game on this list, and the most tactical. Every card can be played for its full effect or thrown away for a quick bonus, so even a bad hand has options. It suits pairs who like a real fight. If one of you plays Magic, it will feel like home. For Magic players, our guide to [two-player cube formats](/articles/two-player-cube-formats/) is the next step.

### Res Arcana

Designed by Thomas Lehmann and published by Sand Castle Games in 2019, Res Arcana plays 2 to 4 in 20 to 60 minutes. You are rival mages. You start with a small hand of artifacts and spend essences like elan, life, calm, death, and gold to bring them into play, claim places of power, and race to 10 points.

**Why it fits:** each player gets only eight artifact cards for the whole game, so it feels like a tight puzzle you solve against each other. Two players is the most focused way to play. [Res Arcana](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PFRTSB2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a good step up for pairs who loved building an engine in Star Realms and want more control. Our guide to [tableau board games](/articles/tableau-board-games/) covers it and its cousins.

## The classic

### Cribbage

Cribbage dates to early 17th-century England and is usually credited to the poet Sir John Suckling. It plays 2 (or 3 to 4) in about 30 minutes. You build a small hand, add two cards to a shared "crib," then score fifteens, pairs, runs, and flushes as you play cards one at a time. Scores go on a pegboard, first to 121.

**Why it fits:** [Cribbage](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000I5NW66?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) has kept couples and families busy for centuries because it rewards practice for decades. You need a board and a standard deck. A two-pack of [Bicycle playing cards](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00001QHVP?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) covers the deck and leaves you a spare. It also makes a lovely bridge game for grandparents; see our guide to [games to play with grandparents](/articles/games-to-play-with-grandparents/).

## FAQ

### What is the best two-player card game?

Jaipur is the easiest one to recommend. It is quick to teach, plays in about 30 minutes, and stays interesting for years. Air, Land & Sea is the best pick for players who want a short, sharp duel.

### What are good card games for couples?

Jaipur, Lost Cities, and The Fox in the Forest are the friendliest. They are competitive, but not mean. Couples who like a cozier evening might also enjoy our list of [board games for couples](/articles/board-games-for-couples/), which covers games that work at two and at four.

### What two-player card games use a regular deck?

Cribbage and gin rummy are the classics. Both need only a standard deck, and cribbage adds a pegboard for scoring. Our guide to [card games for adults](/articles/adult-card-games/) has more ideas for a regular deck.

### Is 7 Wonders Duel a card game?

It is mostly cards, but it uses a board for military and science, so it sits closer to a board game. It is one of the best two-player games of any kind. Learn it free with [how to play 7 Wonders Duel](/academy/7-wonders-duel/how-to-play-7-wonders-duel/).

### Are there cooperative two-player card games?

Yes. The Fox in the Forest Duet and The Crew both work for two on the same team, and [The Crew](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) has a two-player mode in the rulebook. Our [two-player cooperative games](/articles/two-player-cooperative-board-games/) list has a dozen more.

### What is the quickest two-player card game here?

Air, Land & Sea and Star Realms both run about 20 minutes. Lost Cities rounds are even shorter, though a full game is three rounds.
