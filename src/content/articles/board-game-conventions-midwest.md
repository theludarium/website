The best board game conventions in the Midwest are mostly not Gen Con. Gen Con is the biggest, and it is ours, but it is also the loudest, the most expensive, and the hardest to get a hotel for. Within a day's drive of Indianapolis there are a dozen shows that cost less, book easier, and give you more hours at a table per dollar.

This guide covers eleven of them. Every convention below was confirmed on its own website, with the dates and venue as posted in October 2026. Dates shift year to year, so treat the month as reliable and check the exact days before you book.

## Why go to a smaller convention at all?

Gen Con is a city. A regional convention is a neighborhood.

At a 1,000- to 5,000-person show, you can walk into the open gaming hall and find a table in five minutes. The game library is large enough to try anything and small enough to find it. Events rarely sell out months ahead. Hotel rooms are a phone call, not a lottery. And the same two hundred regulars come back every year, so by your second visit you have people to play with.

The trade-offs are real. Fewer publishers, fewer premieres, fewer celebrity designers. If you want the whole industry in one room, that is [Gen Con](/articles/gen-con-first-timer-guide/). If you want to play games for three days with people who will remember you, keep reading.

Details on each follow.

### Con of the North (Bloomington, Minnesota)

A February weekend at the DoubleTree Minneapolis South, with hours that run from Friday noon to the small hours each night. The next show is listed as [February 12–14, 2027](https://www.conofthenorth.org/). A good excuse to see the Twin Cities in winter, if you need one.

### CincyCon (Hamilton, Ohio)

A community-run convention at the [Butler County Fairgrounds in Hamilton](https://www.cincycon.org/location), north of Cincinnati and roughly two hours from Indianapolis. The next dates are [February 26–28, 2027](https://www.cincycon.org/). Closer to Indianapolis than most entries on this list.

### Midwest GameFest (Kansas City)

Held each spring in Kansas City and set for [March 4–7, 2027](https://www.midwestgamefest.org/) at what the organizers describe as a new, larger location. A long drive from Indiana, but a solid pick if you have family in the area.

### Gary Con (Lake Geneva, Wisconsin)

Named for Gary Gygax and held in the town where Dungeons & Dragons was born. Gary Con XIX runs [March 18–21, 2027](https://www.garycon.com/) at the Grand Geneva Resort, with additional event rooms at Harbor Shores. If you play RPGs, this is a pilgrimage; if you play board games only, it is still a very good weekend.

### Buckeye Game Fest (central Ohio)

A board-game-first convention running [March 25–28, 2027](https://www.buckeyegamefest.com/) at [The Ohioan Hotel & Event Center](https://www.buckeyegamefest.com/hotel/), formerly the Nationwide Conference Center, in Lewis Center, north of Columbus. Known for a large lending library and a relaxed open-gaming floor.

### Geekway to the West (St. Louis area)

The board gamer's convention in the region. The main show is held at the Saint Charles Convention Center in St. Charles, Missouri, just outside St. Louis; the 2026 dates were [May 14–17](https://stcharlesconventioncenter.com/?p=2047), and the organizers also run a smaller winter event, Geekway Mini, on [January 22–24, 2027](https://www.geekway.com/). Geekway is known for its game library and its play-and-win program, where attendees who play donated copies are entered to take them home. Badges [sold out in 2025](https://tabletop.events/conventions/geekway-2025), and the show drew about 3,000 people the year before, so buy early.

### Origins Game Fair (Columbus, Ohio)

The second-largest tabletop convention in the Midwest and, for many people, a better experience than Gen Con. Origins runs [June 9–13, 2027](https://www.originsgamefair.com/) at the Greater Columbus Convention Center, less than three hours from Indianapolis. Big exhibit hall, big open gaming, and hotel rooms that can actually be booked.

### GrandCon (Grand Rapids, Michigan)

A downtown convention at DeVos Place, scheduled for [September 3–5, 2027](https://www.grand-con.com/). Grand Rapids is a walkable city with good food, and the show has grown steadily while staying easy to navigate.

### Gamehole Con (Madison, Wisconsin)

An October show at the Alliant Energy Center, next set for [October 15–18, 2026](https://www.gameholecon.com/). It began as an RPG convention and still leans that way, but the board game hall has grown into a destination of its own. Madison in October is worth the drive by itself.

### Chicago Toy & Game Fair (Rosemont, Illinois)

A different kind of show: part public toy fair, part designer and publisher gathering, held in Rosemont near O'Hare. The 2026 fair runs [November 7–8](https://www.choosechicago.com/event/2026-chicago-toy-game-fair-tickets/2026-11-07/) at the Donald E. Stephens Convention Center. Good for families and for designers interested in the mass-market side of the hobby.

### U-Con (Ypsilanti, Michigan)

A compact November convention at the Ann Arbor Marriott Ypsilanti at Eagle Crest, running [November 13–15, 2026](https://www.ucon-gaming.org/). Small, friendly, and close enough to Ann Arbor for a dinner out.

## How do you pick one?

A few rules of thumb.

- **For a first non-Gen Con convention:** Origins. It has the infrastructure of a big show without the scramble.
- **For the most hours of board gaming per dollar:** Geekway, Buckeye Game Fest, or Gamehole Con.
- **For RPG players:** Gary Con and Gamehole Con.
- **For a short, cheap weekend near Indianapolis:** CincyCon or Origins.
- **For designers testing a prototype:** the smaller the show, the more playtests you get. Some of these conventions run designer areas or protospiel-style events in a given year; none advertised one on its main page when we checked, so ask the organizers directly.

Indianapolis also hosts smaller local conventions through the year.

## What should you expect that is different from Gen Con?

Three things.

**You will play more.** At Gen Con, a ticketed schedule and a half-mile walk between buildings eat the day. At a regional show, the library is fifty feet from the tables and the tables are fifty feet from your room.

**You will talk to strangers.** Open gaming at a small convention runs on the "looking for players" flag or sign-up board. Put the flag up, and someone sits down. If that sounds like a lot, our [pacing plan for introverts](/articles/conventions-for-introverts/) was written with exactly these rooms in mind.

**You will come back.** Almost everyone we know who tried one regional convention now goes to two. Budget for it. For how the money compares, see [Gen Con on a budget](/articles/gen-con-on-a-budget/).

The game is the reason. The people are the point. The small conventions understand that better than anyone, because the people are the only thing they have to offer.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest board game convention in the Midwest after Gen Con?

Origins Game Fair in Columbus, Ohio, held in June at the Greater Columbus Convention Center.

### Which Midwest convention is closest to Indianapolis?

CincyCon in Hamilton, Ohio, is about a [two-hour drive](https://1map.com/routes/us-oh-hamilton-11911_to_us-in-indianapolis), and Origins in Columbus is closer to three. Gen Con itself is in downtown Indianapolis.

### Are smaller conventions cheaper than Gen Con?

Usually. Badges tend to cost less, hotels are easier to book without a lottery, and parking and food are cheaper in smaller cities. Check each site for current prices.

### Do I need to buy event tickets at regional conventions?

It varies. Some use scheduled events with sign-ups; many run mostly on open gaming with a library, where you just find a table.
