An artifact cube is a cube where artifacts are the main event instead of the colorless filler. Most cards are artifacts or care about artifacts; the colored cards exist to power them, protect them, or answer them. It is the clearest example of a cube built around a card type rather than a color structure, and it drafts unlike anything else because the deepest column in the cube is the one every drafter can play.

Magic Online ran an Artifact Cube for one week in December 2021, and Ryan Overturf's [Star City Games preview](https://articles.starcitygames.com/select/everything-i-know-about-artifact-cubes-debut-on-magic-online/) of it is the best public account of how such a cube drafts. This article uses that cube and a well-documented paper list as the reference points and covers the four decisions an artifact cube owner has to make that no other cube asks.

## What makes an artifact cube different?

Three structural facts, and all of the design follows from them:

1. **The colorless column is the biggest column.** In a normal 540, colorless cards are 30 to 40 slots. In an artifact cube they are 150 or more. Every drafter can take them, so they get cut from every direction and nothing wheels.
2. **Color matters less, and gold matters least.** Because so much of a deck is colorless, most decks are one color plus artifacts, or two colors with a light commitment. The MTGO cube's author notes that multiple colored pips are a real cost and that only the very best gold cards are worth taking.
3. **One color mostly sits out.** Green's relationship with artifacts is destroying them. The MTGO Artifact Cube ran no green cards at all, apart from a few green activation costs and Birthing Pod. Paper lists usually keep a small green section (Kinnan, Nissa, Ascended Animist, and the artifact hate as a deliberate counter-lane), but it is the smallest color by a wide margin.

The consequence for drafting: pick order inverts. In a normal cube you take the best card and figure out colors later. In an artifact cube you take the colorless engines early because they will not come back, and you settle your color late because the color is a splash.

## How should the sections split?

For a 540, built from the MTGO list's shape and the paper "How to Tinker" list on [CubeCobra](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/bzj) (a 360 unpowered Legacy-plus build with ten named archetypes), a house guideline:

| Section | 540 | 360 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colorless artifacts and artifact creatures | 170 | 110 | The core; curve it like a color |
| White | 60 | 40 | Artifact aggro, Equipment, Stoneforge, Dispatch |
| Blue | 70 | 45 | Card advantage, Tinker effects, Thopter Foundry, Urza |
| Black | 60 | 40 | Recursion, Marionette Master, sacrifice, Trash for Treasure |
| Red | 60 | 40 | Galvanic Blast, Goblin Welder, Daretti, Krark-Clan Ironworks |
| Green | 20 | 10 | Kinnan, Nissa, Reclamation Sage, Cankerbloom as a counter-lane |
| Gold | 30 | 20 | Only the strongest; Thopter-Sword pairs, Breya, Tezzerets |
| Lands | 70 | 55 | Artifact lands, Bridges, Inkmoth, Urza's Saga, the usual duals |

The 170-card colorless section should have its own mana curve, its own creature-to-noncreature ratio, and its own removal (Dispatch is white, but Ratchet Bomb, Meteor Golem, and Steel Hellkite are not). Treat it the way you would treat a color in a normal cube and the rest of the box falls into place.

## Should an artifact cube be singleton?

Mostly yes, with one exception that the MTGO cube made famous: the Modern Horizons 2 Bridges <!-- AFFILIATE --> (Silverbluff Bridge, Drossforge Bridge, and the rest, the indestructible artifact dual lands) appeared four times each. The reason is structural. Affinity, metalcraft, improvise, and the artifact-count payoffs all want lands that count, and a singleton set of ten Bridges would mean most decks saw none. Four copies of each of the six nongreen Bridges is 24 lands, which puts a Bridge in most packs of a 540.

The paper "How to Tinker" list goes the other way: singleton, low land fixing, and an explicit note that drafters should take signets and talismans as their fixing and pick the indestructible lands highly because they will not wheel. Both approaches work. The choice is whether you want artifact lands to be an archetype's reward (singleton) or the cube's floor (duplicated Bridges). For a first build we lean toward duplicating the Bridges, because it makes the whole cube feel like an artifact cube from pack one.

The original Mirrodin artifact lands (Seat of the Synod, Vault of Whispers, Great Furnace, Ancient Den, Tree of Tales, Darksteel Citadel) belong in either version, singleton or not.

## What are the archetypes?

Color pairs still exist as a map, but the lanes are defined by what the artifacts do. The ten from the "How to Tinker" list, which are close to what every artifact cube ends up with:

- **WU Affinity.** Frogmite, Myr Enforcer, Thoughtcast, Thought Monitor, Sojourner's Companion, Master of Etherium. Cheap artifact creatures that discount the spells.
- **WB Stax and attrition.** Smokestack, Tangle Wire, Ichor Wellspring, Mishra's Bauble, Bitterblossom-style token makers, and recursion. The grind deck.
- **WR Equipment.** Stoneforge Mystic, Umezawa's Jitte, Puresteel Paladin, Sram, Senior Edificer, Colossus Hammer, Batterskull.
- **WG Tokens and artifact tokens.** Thopter makers, Treasure, Food, Clues, Academy Manufactor, Anointed Procession. The defensive midrange lane.
- **UB Storm and combo.** Mana rocks, Paradoxical Outcome, Thopter Foundry plus Sword of the Meek, Tezzeret, Agent of Bolas, Krark-Clan Ironworks lines.
- **UR Sneak and Show.** Show and Tell, Sneak Attack, Quicksilver Amulet, Through the Breach into Blightsteel Colossus, Sundering Titan, Wurmcoil Engine.
- **UG Ramp.** Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy, Basalt Monolith, mana rocks into Myr Battlesphere, Nissa, Ascended Animist, Time Spiral.
- **BR Reanimator and Welder.** Goblin Welder, Trash for Treasure, Daretti, Scrap Savant, Entomb targets, Scrapheap Scrounger.
- **BG Dredge and recursion.** Hogaak, Lurrus of the Dream-Den, Recurring Nightmare, Survival of the Fittest, Buried Alive.
- **RG Mass land destruction.** Indestructible lands and mana rocks on one side, Armageddon effects on the other; a control-combo lane that only this cube can support.

Two caveats from the MTGO experience. Dimir was the strongest pair there because blue's card advantage and black's recursion both scale with artifacts, so watch UB's density. And Marionette Master with Krark-Clan Ironworks or Dockside Extortionist was the fastest kill; decide whether you want a turn-four combo in your cube and police accordingly, as covered in our [combo in cube piece](/articles/combo-in-cube/).

## How do you handle artifact hate?

A cube where everything is an artifact makes Shatter a sweeper. That is both a problem and a design tool.

Run artifact removal, but make it one-for-one and mostly cheap: Abrade, Shenanigans, Nature's Claim in green, Dispatch in white, Kolaghan's Command. Keep the mass artifact removal (Shatterstorm, Creeping Corrosion, Vandalblast overloaded, Austere Command) to two or three copies in the whole cube, because each one is a Wrath of God that also hits lands. The paper list's green section exists almost entirely for this purpose: Kataki, War's Wage; Reclamation Sage; Sawblade Slinger; Cankerbloom. Green is the counter-lane, and a drafter who takes it is making a bet on the table.

Kataki deserves a specific note: in a cube with artifact lands, it taxes mana bases, and some tables will want it out.

## How powerful should it be?

The MTGO cube was a Vintage-adjacent list with Urza's Saga, Jitte, Dockside, and Krark-Clan Ironworks; the paper "How to Tinker" list is unpowered Legacy. Either is fine. The thing to decide is the fast mana. Artifact cubes are built on mana rocks, and the line between "Signets and Talismans" and "Mana Crypt, Mana Vault, Grim Monolith, Sol Ring" is the line between a cube that plays on turn four and one that plays on turn two. Our [fast mana article](/articles/fast-mana-in-cube/) draws the line for a normal cube; in an artifact cube, draw it one step more conservatively, because every deck can use every rock.

## FAQ

### What is an artifact cube?

An artifact cube is a cube where most cards are artifacts or reward playing them, with the colored sections built to support artifact strategies and a very small green section.

### Why is there so little green in an artifact cube?

Green's mechanical relationship with artifacts is destroying them, so green has few artifact payoffs. Most artifact cubes keep a small green section as a deliberate artifact-hate counter-lane or drop it entirely, as the MTGO Artifact Cube did.

### Should an artifact cube duplicate the Bridges?

Many do. The MTGO cube ran four of each nongreen Modern Horizons 2 Bridge so that artifact lands appeared in nearly every pack; singleton builds instead treat artifact lands as a scarce reward.

### How big should an artifact cube be?

360 or 540 both work. The colorless section should be the largest column, around 110 cards at 360 and 170 at 540.

## One card type, a whole cube

An artifact cube is the proof that a cube can be organized around something other than color and still produce ten decks. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
