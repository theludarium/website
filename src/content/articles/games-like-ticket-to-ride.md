The best games like [Ticket to Ride](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0975277324?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) depend on what your table enjoyed most. If it was the trains and the routes, play [Ticket to Ride: Europe](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GKBFFRBW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Thurn and Taxis](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000IXS4SY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Railroad Ink](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07GPZYS61?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). If it was collecting the right cards and cashing them in, play [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). If it was the shared map and the quick teach, play [Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or [Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And if your table is ready for a heavier train game, [Power Grid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07HM7WZT9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Brass: Birmingham](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884047?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) are the next stops.

**The quick answer:** if you want more Ticket to Ride, get **Ticket to Ride: Europe**. It is the same game with a few smart new rules. If you want something different that feels just as friendly, get **Splendor** (the same "collect, then cash in" loop in 30 minutes) or **Carcassonne** (a shared map that plays great at two).

Below are 10 picks, sorted by what you liked about Ticket to Ride, with a comparison table, a "which one first" guide, and a plain note on how each one differs.

## What makes a game feel like Ticket to Ride?

Ticket to Ride, designed by Alan R. Moon and published by Days of Wonder in 2004, won the 2004 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 5 in 30 to 60 minutes. When people say they want "something like Ticket to Ride," they usually mean one or more of four things:

1. **Building routes.** You connect cities on a map, and the board slowly fills with everyone's trains. Finishing a long route feels great.
2. **Collecting sets.** Most turns you just draw two cards. The tension is in waiting for the right colors, then spending them all at once.
3. **A light touch of conflict.** No one attacks you. But someone can take the route you needed, and you will feel it.
4. **A teach that takes five minutes.** Draw cards, claim a route, or take tickets. That is the whole game. It is why so many people call it their first modern board game, and why it sits near the top of our [gateway board games](/articles/gateway-board-games/) list.

No game on this list hits all four the way Ticket to Ride does. Pick the one that doubles down on the part your table liked most.

## If you loved the routes and the trains

These three keep the joy of linking places together, each with a twist.

### Ticket to Ride: Europe

Designed by Alan R. Moon and published by Days of Wonder in 2005, [Ticket to Ride: Europe](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GKBFFRBW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 5 in 30 to 60 minutes. It is a standalone game, so you do not need the original box.

**Why it fits:** it is the same game you already know, plus three small additions. Train stations let you borrow one route from an opponent, so getting blocked hurts less. Tunnels add a little push your luck. Ferries need locomotive cards. Many players think it is the better game for a table that has played the USA map a dozen times. Our lesson on [Ticket to Ride maps and expansions](/academy/ticket-to-ride/ticket-to-ride-expansions/) covers the other maps if you want to stay in the family.

### Thurn and Taxis

Designed by Karen and Andreas Seyfarth and published by Hans im Glück in 2006 (Rio Grande Games in English), [Thurn and Taxis](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000IXS4SY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2006 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4 in about 60 minutes. You collect city cards and lay them in a line to build postal routes across old Bavaria, then place post offices along the way.

**How it differs:** your route lives in your hand, not on a shared rail line, so blocking is rare. The tension comes from deciding when to end a route and score it before the next card breaks your chain. It is the closest cousin to Ticket to Ride's card drafting, with a little more planning. It can be harder to find in stores than the rest of this list.

### Railroad Ink

Designed by Hjalmar Hach and Lorenzo Silva and published by Horrible Guild in 2018, [Railroad Ink](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07GPZYS61?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 6 in 20 to 30 minutes. Each round, dice show road and rail pieces, and everyone draws the same pieces onto their own grid, trying to connect the exits around the edge.

**How it differs:** there is no shared board and no blocking. Everyone works from the same dice, so it is a quiet puzzle about who links their network best. It plays solo, travels well, and handles six people with no extra setup. Our guide to [roll and write games](/articles/roll-and-write-games/) explains why the format works so well.

## If you loved collecting the right cards

Ticket to Ride's real engine is set collection: wait for the colors, then spend them in one big move. These three are built on that feeling.

### Splendor

Designed by Marc André and published by Space Cowboys in 2014, [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes. You take gem chips and spend them on development cards. Each card you own works as a permanent discount, so your buying power grows every turn.

**How it differs:** there is no map. But the turn is the same simple choice: take resources now, or spend them for points. It is quick, it plays well with two, and it has a light race at the end that feels a lot like the last few turns of Ticket to Ride. Learn it with [how to play Splendor](/academy/splendor/how-to-play-splendor/), then try our [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/).

### Azul

Designed by Michael Kiesling and published by Plan B Games in 2017, [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2018 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. You draft colored tiles from shared displays and fill rows on your board to build a mosaic.

**How it differs:** like picking train cards from the face-up row, every pick in Azul is about the color you need and the color you leave for the next player. It adds a sharp twist: tiles you cannot place cost you points. It is a little meaner than Ticket to Ride and very good at two. Learn it with [how to play Azul](/academy/azul/how-to-play-azul/) and our [Azul strategy guide](/articles/azul-strategy/).

### Lost Cities

Designed by Reiner Knizia and published by Kosmos in 1999, [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a card game for 2 players that takes about 30 minutes. You play numbered cards in rising order to fund expeditions in five colors. Start an expedition and you owe points until you build it up.

**How it differs:** it is built only for two, and there is no board at all. But the core feeling is the same as holding onto train cards for a long route: commit to a color, then hope the cards come. It is the pick for couples who love Ticket to Ride but want something smaller. Our list of [two player board games for date night](/articles/two-player-board-games-date-night/) has more like it.

## If you loved the shared map and the easy teach

These two keep Ticket to Ride's best quality: you can teach them in a few minutes to almost anyone.

### Carcassonne

Designed by Klaus-Jürgen Wrede and published by Hans im Glück in 2000, [Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2001 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 5 in 35 to 45 minutes. Each turn you draw a tile, add it to a growing map of roads, cities, and fields, and may place a follower to claim a feature.

**How it differs:** you build the map itself instead of filling one in. Roads matter, sharing matters, and you can sneak into someone else's city the way a rival can grab your key route. It plays especially well at two. Learn it with [how to play Carcassonne](/academy/carcassonne/how-to-play-carcassonne/), then read our [Carcassonne strategy guide](/articles/carcassonne-strategy/).

### Cascadia

Designed by Randy Flynn and published by Flatout Games and AEG in 2021, [Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2022 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. Each turn you take a hex habitat tile and a wildlife token and add them to your own landscape.

**How it differs:** each player builds their own map, so there is almost no blocking. The drafting works like Ticket to Ride's face-up cards: four choices, and the one you want may be gone by your turn. It is the calm pick for families and for anyone who found losing a route too stressful. Learn it with [how to play Cascadia](/academy/cascadia/how-to-play-cascadia/). For more games in this style, see our guide to [tile placement board games](/articles/tile-placement-board-games/).

## If your table is ready for a bigger train game

These two take route building seriously. They are longer and deeper, and both reward a table that has played Ticket to Ride many times.

### Power Grid

Designed by Friedemann Friese and first published in 2004, [Power Grid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07HM7WZT9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 6 in about 2 hours. You bid on power plants, buy fuel from a shared market, and connect cities on the map to your network.

**How it differs:** the map feels familiar. You still race to claim connections before someone else does, and getting boxed in hurts. But auctions and a fuel market add real money math. It is a big step up in weight. Learn it with [how to play Power Grid](/academy/power-grid/how-to-play-power-grid/).

### Brass: Birmingham

Designed by Gavan Brown, Matt Tolman, and Martin Wallace and published by Roxley in 2018, [Brass: Birmingham](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884047?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. You build canals, then railways, and industries across the English Midlands.

**How it differs:** this is the heaviest game here and one of the most respected strategy games of the last decade. Your rail network is shared, so a link you build can help a rival too. Save it for a table that already loves planning. Learn it with [how to play Brass: Birmingham](/academy/brass-birmingham/how-to-play-brass-birmingham/).

## FAQ

### What is the closest game to Ticket to Ride?

Another Ticket to Ride. Europe is the usual next box, and the other maps change the game more than you might expect. Outside the family, Thurn and Taxis is the closest, since you also build routes out of card sets.

### What is a good game like Ticket to Ride for two players?

Lost Cities is built for two. Carcassonne, Splendor, and Azul all play very well at two as well. Ticket to Ride works at two too, and our lesson on [two player Ticket to Ride strategy](/academy/ticket-to-ride/two-player-ticket-to-ride-strategy/) explains how the game changes.

### Is Ticket to Ride or Catan better for beginners?

Ticket to Ride is easier to teach and plays up to five without an add-on. Catan has more table talk. We compared them in [Catan vs Ticket to Ride](/articles/catan-vs-ticket-to-ride/), and if Catan is your table's favorite, see our list of [games like Catan](/articles/games-like-catan/).

### Are there train games heavier than Ticket to Ride?

Yes. Power Grid and Brass: Birmingham are both big steps up, with economies, auctions, and long-term plans. They take two hours or so and suit groups that enjoy strategy games.

### Should I get a Ticket to Ride expansion or a new game?

If your table still loves Ticket to Ride, a new map is the easiest win: no new teach, fresh decisions. If the game has started to feel samey, a new box changes more. And if you want to get better at the original first, our [Ticket to Ride strategy guide](/articles/ticket-to-ride-strategy/) covers tickets, long routes, and when to block.
