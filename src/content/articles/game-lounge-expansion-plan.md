People ask whether The Ludarium is a game lounge franchise in the making. The short answer is that we are not planning a franchise, but we are planning more than one room. The long plan is a single membership that works in every city we are in, so a member who moves from Indianapolis to Columbus or travels to Chicago for work walks into a Ludarium there and is already home. This article lays out that plan, the order we intend to do it in, and the many ways it could fail. We are a long way from any of it. We think it is worth saying out loud anyway.

## Why think about expansion before the first lounge opens?

Because the decisions we make for one room either leave the door open to a second or quietly nail it shut.

A membership system that only works in one building, a brand that only means something in one neighborhood, a library and programming approach that lives in one host's head: all of those are fine for a single lounge and a problem for the second. We are not building for the second lounge. We are trying not to build against it.

The other reason is the mission. "Togethering for a lifetime" does not stop at the county line. The problem we are trying to solve, adults who miss the table and cannot find one, exists in every mid-sized American city. One good room in Indianapolis is a start. It is not the point.

## Why one membership instead of a franchise?

A franchise sells the brand and the playbook to local operators, who run their own businesses under the name. It scales fast and it is how most restaurant and fitness chains grew. We are not doing it, for three reasons.

**The host is the product.** A lounge works because of the person who teaches the games and knows the names. Franchise models optimize for consistency of process. We need consistency of care, and that is much harder to license.

**Membership has to be portable.** The thing that makes a multi-city network valuable to a member is that their card works everywhere. Under a franchise, every operator has their own books, and a member from one location is a cost to another. The incentives fight the promise.

**We have watched what happens to rooms when the operator's margin matters more than the room.** The coworking story we keep returning to, in [what coworking got right that game stores got wrong](/articles/coworking-vs-game-stores/), is partly a story about scaling faster than the care could follow.

So the plan is one organization, one membership, and lounges we open and run ourselves, slowly. If that proves impossible to fund, we will say so and rethink.

## What does "one membership, every city" actually mean for a member?

Three things, in the version we are designing toward.

1. **Your membership works at any Ludarium.** You join in Indianapolis. You visit family in another Ludarium city, you walk in, you are a member.
2. **Your history travels.** The games you love, the campaign you are in, the people you play with. A host in the new city can see that you have been teaching Wingspan to newcomers for two years and put you to work.
3. **Your group survives a move.** This is the one we care about most. Game groups die when one person moves. If both cities have a Ludarium, the person who moved has a table waiting, and the group back home has a place to keep meeting. We wrote about the problem in [keeping a game group alive when everyone moved away](/articles/playing-board-games-with-long-distance-friends/). A network of rooms is part of the answer.

## What is the sequence?

Slowly, and in an order designed to find out whether each step works before paying for the next.

| Stage | What has to be true | What we learn |
|---|---|---|
| Content and newsletter | People read and subscribe | Whether the idea resonates beyond our friends |
| Corporate events in Indianapolis | Companies pay and rebook | Whether we can host, and whether events fund the room |
| Early paid test | A few hundred people pay before there is a door | Whether the membership math is real |
| First lounge, Indianapolis | It covers its fixed costs on membership within a year | Whether the room works |
| Second room in Indianapolis, or a second city | The first room is full and churn is low | Whether the model copies |
| Further cities | Each prior step held | Whether a network is worth more than the sum of rooms |

We do not have dates for any of these. The honest version is that every row depends on the one above it, and we will stop at whichever row fails. The membership math in [how a game lounge can pay for itself](/articles/game-lounge-membership-model/) is the gate for the first room. The first room's churn is the gate for the second.

## Which cities, and why?

We are not naming any beyond Indianapolis, partly because we have no business doing so yet and partly because the answer will come from the newsletter list. Where do subscribers live? Where do members move? Where do people write to ask when we are coming?

What we can say is the kind of city. Mid-sized, a lot of adults who played in college and stopped, enough density for a few hundred members within a short drive, and a cost of commercial space that a membership can carry. The Midwest has many of them. Indianapolis is the first because it is home and because a city that hosts Gen Con every summer can clearly support a table the rest of the year.

## What could go wrong?

Most things. Here is our current list.

**The first room does not work.** Then there is no plan. This is the likeliest failure and the reason we are being careful about rent and timing.

**The model does not copy.** The first room might work because of one host, one neighborhood, or one group of founding members, in ways we cannot reproduce. We will not know until we try a second, and we will try a second room in the same city before a second city, so we are changing one variable at a time.

**Capital.** Opening rooms ourselves rather than franchising means we carry the cost. That limits the pace to what membership revenue and patient investors allow. We think slow is correct here, but slow and underfunded is just slow.

**Drift.** Every expansion risks the thing coworking suffered: the rooms stay open and the care leaves. We do not have a clever answer. We have the word togethering, a commitment to hiring hosts first, and the intention to write about it publicly so readers can tell us when we have drifted.

## Why say any of this now?

Because building in public means saying the long plan before we have earned it, and letting people hold us to it. If we are still a single lounge in ten years because that is all that worked, we will have said so plainly along the way. If we are in six cities, you will have watched every one open.

The ambition is not the rooms. It is a lifetime of tables for anyone who wants one, wherever life moves them. The game is the reason. The people are the point. The map is just where the people are.

## FAQ

### Is The Ludarium a franchise?

No. The plan is one organization opening and running its own lounges, with a single membership that works at all of them. Franchising would make membership portability and consistent hosting much harder.

### Will my membership work at other Ludarium locations?

That is the design goal. One membership, every city, with your history and your groups traveling with you.

### When will The Ludarium open in cities other than Indianapolis?

Not until the Indianapolis lounge has proven the model, including covering its costs on membership and holding churn low. We have no dates.

### What kind of city is a good fit for a game lounge?

Mid-sized, with a lot of adults who played games and lost their group, enough density for a few hundred members nearby, and commercial rent a membership can carry.
