If you need to relaunch a game group, you are probably reading this with some embarrassment. Do not. Cube Commonwealth met weekly in Indianapolis for about three years, seated 12 to 16 players a night, and grew a Discord of roughly 300 mostly local members. Then it went quiet. Not a dramatic ending; the night depended on one person, that person had a season where life won, and a few skipped weeks became a pause nobody had announced. The server kept its members. The table stopped.

We are relaunching now as a branch of The Ludarium. This is the playbook we are following, written so another organizer can follow it too. Some of it is what we have done; some is what we are about to do. Where we have not yet seen the result, we say so.

## Why do game communities go quiet?

Almost never because people stopped liking the game. The causes are structural.

- **Single point of failure.** One host, one cube, one calendar. When that calendar fills, the night stops. This was ours.
- **Venue loss.** The brewery got busy, the store changed policy, the host moved. The night floats, then sinks.
- **Unannounced drift.** Three weeks become five. Nobody says "we're pausing," so nobody knows whether to wait or leave.
- **The core thins at once.** Two of the six regulars move away in the same summer, and the floor of eight becomes a floor of five.
- **Growth outran the system.** Sixteen players, one host, no second pod process. The host burns out on the good nights.

Name your cause before you relaunch. If you relaunch with the same structure, you will pause again, usually faster.

## Step 1: Say what happened

Before anything else, post in the server. One pinned message, plain, honest, short. Ours said, roughly: the night stopped because it depended on one person's calendar; here is what is changing; here is the date we are back. <!-- EDITOR: add detail -->

Three reasons this comes first. Members who drifted need permission to come back. Members who stayed need to know the silence was not about them. And the replies tell you who is still local and still interested. That is your real headcount, and it is almost always better than you feared.

What not to do: apologize at length, promise more than you can deliver, or ignore the gap. Acknowledge it in one sentence and move on.

## Step 2: Fix the structural cause before the first night

This is the step most relaunches skip, and it is why most relaunches fail by month three.

For us the fix is hosts, plural. Two or three people who can each run a night end to end: deal packs, run pairings, make a ruling, post results. Each runs a practice night before the relaunch. If only one person can run the night, you have not relaunched; you have restarted the same clock.

Other common fixes, matched to causes:

| Cause | Fix before relaunch |
|---|---|
| Single host | Name and train two more; give each a key, a cube, or both |
| Venue loss | Lock a standing reservation; keep a second venue warm |
| Unannounced drift | Write a rule: the night runs even at four players, and a pause gets announced the day it starts |
| Thinned core | Relaunch with a smaller target and a plan to seat first-timers well |
| Growth outran the system | Write the two-pod process down before you need it |

## Step 3: Shrink the promise

Three years ago the night was 12 to 16 players. The relaunch does not start there, and pretending it will sets you up for a disappointing first month.

Our relaunch target is one pod. Eight players, one night, same time every week, for eight consecutive weeks. That is the entire goal of phase one. No league, no new-player night, no themed weeks. The second pod, the season structure, and the rest come back when the first pod is boring and reliable.

Shrinking the promise also makes the first nights feel full. Eight people at one table is a good night. Eight in a room set for sixteen is a sad one.

## Step 4: Rebuild the core by hand

Broadcasts bring curiosity. Personal messages bring people. Before the first night, the hosts split the list of former regulars and message each one directly. Not a copy-paste. "We're back on [night] starting [date]. Would love to see you. No pressure if life's different now."

The goal is six yeses from people who used to be the core. Six regulars plus a trustworthy weekly headcount reliably produces eight, which we learned the first time around and wrote up in [how to get eight players to show up](/articles/how-to-get-eight-players-for-a-draft/). If you cannot find six, you are not relaunching a community yet; you are starting a new one with a familiar name, and that is fine too, but plan for it.

## Step 5: Reset the server, lightly

Do not rebuild the Discord from scratch. The history is an asset. Do prune it.

- Archive every channel that has not been used in a month.
- Replace the pinned start-here post with a current one.
- Put the relaunch announcement where the weekly post used to go, then replace it with the actual weekly post two days before night one.
- Clear stale roles. Everyone who was a regular is a regular again on their first night back, not before.
- Re-create the opt-in notification role and ask people to re-opt in. The people who do are your early headcount.

The server should look alive and small, not big and dusty. Our full layout is in [Discord for a game group](/articles/discord-setup-for-a-game-group/).

## Step 6: Run the first night as the hundredth night

The temptation is to make night one an event. Resist it. Night one should be an ordinary draft, run exactly as night fifty will be, so people learn the new normal on their first visit.

What that looks like in practice:

- The weekly post goes up on the fixed day, in the fixed format.
- Packs are dealt before anyone arrives.
- Start on the minute, even if three people are parking.
- Three rounds, hard end time, standings read at the end.
- Results posted the next morning, with one photo and one sentence about a deck.

If only five people come, play anyway. Winston or grid draft, two-headed giant, anything. The first time a relaunched night cancels, the relaunch is over. Everyone who skipped learns they were right to.

## Step 7: Hold the night for eight weeks before changing anything

Eight consecutive weeks on the same night at the same place. No exceptions for holidays, no "let's skip since half the group is traveling." A thin week is still a week. The point of phase one is to re-teach the habit, and habits are taught by repetition, not by good nights.

Track one number: players at the table. Not members, not reactions. If the trend over eight weeks is flat or up, you have a night. If it is down, go back to step four and message people again.

## Step 8: Add things back one at a time

After eight weeks, bring back the layers, each one at a time, each given a month before the next.

1. **The second pod,** once the first pod has been at eight or more for four straight weeks.
2. **New-player night,** once there are enough regulars who like teaching.
3. **The league,** in season two, not season one.
4. **The change log and the community cube,** which can restart earlier because they cost nothing and give the regulars something to argue about between nights.

Adding everything back at once recreates the load that broke the first version.

## What is different about relaunching under The Ludarium?

For us, the relaunch is also a change of ownership in spirit. Cube Commonwealth was one person's night. It is becoming a branch of a venture whose purpose is places for adults to play together, regularly, in person. The night is now part of something larger, with more than one person responsible for it, and eventually a lounge in Indianapolis where the cube has a permanent shelf.

We do not know yet whether that structure holds better. We think it will, because the failure we hit was a single calendar, and a branch has several. We will write about it either way. ## The checklist

1. Post what happened. One pin, plain.
2. Fix the structural cause. Usually: more hosts.
3. Shrink the target to one table for eight weeks.
4. Message former regulars one at a time. Aim for six.
5. Prune the server; do not rebuild it.
6. Run night one like night one hundred.
7. Hold eight weeks without changing anything or cancelling once.
8. Add layers back one at a time, a month apart.

## FAQ

### How do you restart a game group that stopped meeting?

Announce what happened, fix the reason it stopped (usually a single host), set a small target such as one table for eight weeks, and personally message former regulars rather than broadcasting.

### Should you start a new Discord when relaunching a community?

No. Keep the server and its history. Archive dead channels, refresh the pinned posts, and ask people to re-opt in to notifications.

### How long does it take to relaunch a game night?

Plan on eight consecutive weeks to re-establish the habit, then a month per added layer such as a second table or a league.

### What if only a few people come to the relaunch night?

Play anyway, with a smaller format. Cancelling the first relaunched night tells everyone who skipped that they were right to.
