[Qwirkle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1933054395?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) rules take about five minutes to teach. Players take turns placing wooden tiles to build lines on the table, where every tile in a line shares either a color or a shape, with no repeats. You score a point for each tile in every line you add to, and you score big when you complete a line of six, called a Qwirkle. When the bag is empty and someone plays their last tile, the highest score wins.

**The quick answer:** draw six tiles. On your turn, either place one or more tiles in a single line, or trade some tiles back into the bag. Every line must share one color or one shape, never both, with no duplicate tiles. Score one point per tile in each line you build onto. A complete line of six scores 12. The first player to empty their hand after the bag runs out gets 6 bonus points and ends the game.

Qwirkle was designed by Susan McKinley Ross, published by MindWare in 2006, and won the Spiel des Jahres (Germany's top game award) in 2011. It plays 2 to 4 people, ages 6 and up, in about 30 to 45 minutes.

## What comes in the Qwirkle box?

- **108 wooden tiles** and a cloth bag to draw them from.

That is it. No board, no cards, no score track beyond a pencil and paper.

The tiles come in **6 colors** (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple) and **6 shapes** (circle, square, diamond, clover, four-point star, and eight-point star). Every color and shape combination exists, and there are **3 copies of each**: 6 times 6 times 3 is 108. Knowing that there are only three of each tile matters later, when you start thinking about strategy.

## How do you set up Qwirkle?

1. **Put all 108 tiles in the bag** and mix them.
2. **Each player draws 6 tiles** and stands them up so the others cannot see them.
3. **Grab a score pad.** Someone keeps score for everyone.
4. **Find the first player.** Each player looks for the largest group of tiles in their hand that share one color or one shape, ignoring duplicates. Whoever has the biggest group goes first and plays it. If players tie, the oldest tied player starts. Play then goes clockwise.

## How to play Qwirkle: what can you do on your turn?

On your turn you choose **one** of two actions.

### Option 1: Place tiles

Put one or more tiles from your hand on the table, following the line rules below. Score your points. Then draw from the bag until you have 6 tiles again.

### Option 2: Trade tiles

Instead of placing, you can swap some or all of your tiles. Set aside the tiles you want to get rid of, draw the same number from the bag, then put the set aside tiles back in and mix. A trade uses your whole turn and scores nothing. You cannot trade more tiles than are left in the bag.

If you cannot place any tile at all, you must trade.

## Qwirkle rules for building lines

This is the heart of the game. Read it twice and the rest follows.

### Every line shares one attribute

A **line** is a straight row or column of tiles touching end to end. Every tile in a line must share **either the same color or the same shape**:

- A color line: one color, all different shapes. A red circle, red star, and red square.
- A shape line: one shape, all different colors. A blue clover, yellow clover, and green clover.

### No duplicates

A line can **never** contain the same tile twice. Two red circles cannot sit in the same line. Because there are only six colors and six shapes, this means **no line can ever be longer than six tiles**.

### Your tiles go in one line

All the tiles you place in a single turn must go in **one line**, and they must all share the attribute that line uses. You can add to both ends of an existing line in the same turn, as long as every tile ends up in that one line.

### Connect to what is already there

After the first play, every tile you place must connect to tiles already on the table. Any tile that touches others, in any direction, has to fit every line it joins. A tile that sits where a row and a column cross has to work in both.

## How does Qwirkle scoring work?

Qwirkle scoring is easy once you see the core idea: **you score whole lines, not just your own tiles.**

### One point per tile in each line you add to

When you place tiles, count every tile in the line you created or extended, including tiles that were already there. Add a fourth tile to a three-tile red line and you score 4, not 1.

### A tile can score twice

If a tile you place joins two lines at once (one row and one column), it counts in both. Say you place a red circle at the end of a row of four red tiles, and the same tile also extends a column of two circles. You score 4 for the row plus 3 for the column, 7 in all.

The first play of the game scores the same way: one point per tile in the line.

## What is a Qwirkle?

A **Qwirkle** is a complete line of six tiles: all six shapes in one color, or all six colors in one shape. When you complete one, you score 6 points for the tiles plus a **6-point bonus**, for **12 points** in all. Players usually shout "Qwirkle!" when they do it, which is half the fun.

Because no line can hold more than six tiles, a finished Qwirkle is also a closed line. Nobody can add to it again.

## How does a game of Qwirkle end?

When the bag runs out, players keep taking turns without drawing. The first player to place **all of their remaining tiles** ends the game immediately and scores a **6-point bonus**. The bag being empty does not end the game by itself.

Add up the scores. The highest total wins.

## Common Qwirkle mistakes

These are the rules new tables most often get wrong.

- **Counting only your new tiles.** You score the whole line, old tiles included.
- **Forgetting the second line.** If your tile touches a perpendicular line, score that one too.
- **Placing tiles in two lines in one turn.** All your new tiles must go in a single line.
- **Allowing a duplicate.** One red star per line, ever.
- **Mixing attributes.** A red circle next to a blue square shares nothing. Each line is color or shape, not a little of both.
- **Ending the game when the bag empties.** Keep playing until someone uses their last tile.

## Qwirkle strategy: how to score more

Qwirkle looks like a light family game, and it is, but good players pull ahead fast. These tips are a good start.

### Score in two directions

The biggest scores come from tiles that build two lines at once. Look for spots where a tile extends a row and a column together. Placing a short line alongside an existing one, so that each tile also forms a little line of two, can rack up surprising points.

### Do not set up Qwirkles for other players

A line of five is a gift to whoever has the sixth tile. Before you make a five-tile line, ask whether the next player could finish it. If you hold the sixth tile yourself, setting it up can be worth the risk.

### Count the tiles

There are only three copies of each tile. If you need a purple star to finish a line and two purple stars are already on the table, only one is left somewhere. Keeping rough track of what has been played tells you which lines can still be completed.

### Build dead ends on purpose

Sometimes the best move is to place a tile that blocks a line, so no one else can extend it. Late in the game, closing off big scoring spots can matter as much as scoring yourself.

### Trade with a purpose

A hand full of duplicates or tiles with nothing in common is hard to score with. Trading costs a turn, so do it when your hand is truly stuck, and keep the tiles that work together.

### Race to go out at the end

Once the bag is empty, the 6-point bonus for playing your last tile can swing a close game. Plan your final turns so you can empty your hand first.

## FAQ

### How many tiles do you start with in Qwirkle?

Six. You draw back up to six after every turn in which you place tiles, until the bag runs out.

### What is a Qwirkle worth?

Twelve points: 6 for the six tiles in the line, plus a 6-point bonus for completing it.

### Can you have two of the same tile in a line in Qwirkle?

No. A line can never contain duplicate tiles, which is why the longest possible line is six.

### Do you have to place tiles touching each other in Qwirkle?

Your tiles must all go into one line and connect to tiles already on the table. You may add to both ends of the same line in one turn.

### How do you win Qwirkle?

Score the most points. The game ends when the bag is empty and one player places their last tile, earning a 6-point bonus.
