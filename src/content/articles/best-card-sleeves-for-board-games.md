The best card sleeves for board games depend on two things: the size of the cards and how hard your group plays them. For standard-size cards (the size of a Magic or poker card, used by Splendor, Root, Terraforming Mars, and many more), [Dragon Shield Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01G25NEW2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the safe pick, with [Ultimate Guard Katana](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J5H1E9K?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [KMC Hyper Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07FYNZNJ2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Ultra Pro Eclipse Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08WX4Y25G?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) close behind. For the odd sizes board games love, like Ticket to Ride's small train cards or 7 Wonders' tall ones, [Mayday Games](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003Y7VK82?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Sleeve Kings](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PVV1WD6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) make sleeves in nearly every size, and [Gamegenic](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FDXX9WL5?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Dragon Shield](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F68ZR4CX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) both sell board game lines too.

**The quick answer:** look up your game's card size first (the chart below covers the popular ones). Then buy a matte sleeve from any major brand in that size, plus about ten percent extra. Sleeve the games you shuffle most and the cards you hold in your hand. Skip the rest.

Below are the sleeve brands worth buying, a size chart for popular games, and a plain guide to which games actually need sleeves.

## What to look for in card sleeves

Sleeves are clear plastic pockets that slide over each card. They keep cards clean, stop edge wear from shuffling, and save a game from one spilled drink. Five things matter when you buy them.

1. **The right size.** This matters most. A sleeve that is too big slides around and catches when you shuffle. One that is too small bends the card. Board games use a dozen common sizes, and some boxes use two or three.
2. **Matte or glossy backs.** Matte backs shuffle more smoothly, stick together less, and glare less under a lamp. Glossy sleeves cost less and work fine for games you shuffle rarely.
3. **Thickness.** Sleeves are measured in microns. Thin sleeves fit more cards in the box. Thick ones last longer and feel sturdier in the hand. Sleeve Kings, for example, sells a standard line at 60 microns and a premium line at 100 microns.
4. **Clear fronts.** Every brand here has a clear front. Some board game lines are clear on both sides, so the card backs still show. That keeps the art and any color-coded backs visible.
5. **Consistency.** In any game with a hidden hand, every sleeve should look the same from the back. Buy enough of one product at once so no card stands out.

A few games use several sizes in one box. Codenames uses three: small word cards, a few larger cards, and square key cards. Dune: Imperium has mini Euro, standard, and a few oversized cards. The Crew mixes mini Euro and standard USA. When a game needs more than one size, Mayday Games and Sleeve Kings both sell bundles sorted by game, which saves the math.

### How many sleeves do you need?

Count the cards in the rulebook's component list, then add about ten percent. A sleeve tears now and then, and an expansion will need matching sleeves later. For reference, the chart lists 144 cards in Ticket to Ride, 126 in Catan, 222 in Wingspan, 90 in Splendor, and about 500 in Dominion's second edition base game.

## The best sleeves for standard-size cards

Standard card game size, 63.5 by 88 millimeters, is the size of a Magic card. It is also the most common size in modern board games. These four brands are the ones trading card game players have trusted for years, and they work just as well on board game cards.

### Dragon Shield Matte

Dragon Shield sells matte, dual matte, classic, and inner sleeves in standard and Japanese sizes, and it has a board game line as well. [Dragon Shield Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01G25NEW2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) sleeves are thick, matte backed, and sold in a box that doubles as a deck box.

**Why it fits:** they are durable and easy to find, and they come in dozens of colors. If you want one sleeve to buy for every standard-size game on your shelf, this is the usual answer. Pick a single color per game so a sleeve from one box never ends up in another. ### Ultimate Guard Katana

Ultimate Guard's [Katana](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J5H1E9K?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) line is a matte standard-size sleeve sold in packs of 100.

**Why it fits:** Katanas are known for shuffling very smoothly straight out of the pack. They suit deck-building games, where you shuffle your own deck many times in one play. Our guide to [the best deck-building games](/articles/best-deck-building-games/) lists the games that shuffle the most.

### KMC Hyper Matte

KMC is a long-running sleeve maker, and [Hyper Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07FYNZNJ2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is its matte standard-size line. KMC also makes inner sleeves for double sleeving.

**Why it fits:** Hyper Mattes are thinner than most premium sleeves. Thinner sleeves mean a sleeved game is more likely to still fit in its box or insert. That is a real problem with card-heavy games like Ark Nova or Terraforming Mars.

### Ultra Pro Eclipse Matte

Ultra Pro is one of the biggest names in card protection, and [Eclipse Matte](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08WX4Y25G?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is its premium standard-size sleeve. The backs are solid color.

**Why it fits:** Ultra Pro sleeves are sold in many shops, so you can often match a set later. The solid backs hide the card back entirely, which is great for card games but a problem in board games where you need to see the back color. Use Eclipse for games like Star Realms or Magic, and a clear-backed sleeve for games that sort cards by back.

## Which board games need sleeves?

Not every game needs them. Sleeves cost money, take time, and make cards thicker, so some boxes stop closing. Sleeve the games where cards get the most wear.

**Sleeve these:**

- **Deck-building games.** In Dominion, Clank!, and Star Realms you shuffle your own deck over and over. Cards wear at the edges fast. Our [Dominion course](/academy/dominion/) and the explainer on [deck-building games](/articles/deck-building-games-explained/) cover how much shuffling these games involve.
- **Card drafting games.** In 7 Wonders and Sushi Go Party! hands pass around the table every round. Our guide to [card drafting](/articles/card-drafting-explained/) explains why.
- **Games with hidden hands.** A bent or stained card can be spotted from across the table. That spoils The Crew, Love Letter, and any game where you hold secrets.
- **Living card games.** Arkham Horror: The Card Game and Marvel Champions are rebuilt and reshuffled every play. See the [Arkham Horror LCG course](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/) and the [Marvel Champions course](/academy/marvel-champions/).
- **Games with snacks.** If there is food at the table, sleeves are cheap insurance. Our guide to [game night snacks](/articles/game-night-snacks/) has the rest of the plan.
- **Games you want to keep for years,** or sell later. Clean cards matter to a buyer, as our guide on [buying used board games](/articles/how-to-buy-used-board-games/) points out.

**You can usually skip these:**

- **Games with few cards that rarely get shuffled,** like Cascadia's 21 wildlife cards or Power Grid's power plants.
- **Games whose box will not close sleeved.** Check the insert first. Some inserts are cut to fit unsleeved cards with no extra room.
- **Light party games that get passed around once a year.** Spend the money on another game.

## FAQ

### What size card sleeves do most board games use?

Standard card game size, 63.5 by 88 millimeters, is the most common. It fits Splendor, Root, Terraforming Mars, Ark Nova, and Pandemic. Mini Euro (about 44 by 68) and Chimera (about 57.5 by 89) are the next most common. Ticket to Ride uses mini Euro and Wingspan uses Chimera.

### Are Magic sleeves the same as board game sleeves?

Magic sleeves fit any board game with standard-size cards. Many board games use other sizes, though, so a Magic sleeve will be too big or too small for them. Check the size chart before you buy.

### Should card sleeves be matte or glossy?

Matte for any game you shuffle often. Matte backs shuffle more smoothly, stick together less in humid rooms, and glare less under overhead light. Glossy sleeves are fine for games you shuffle rarely.

### Do sleeved cards fit in the box?

Often, but not always. Sleeves add thickness, so a game with hundreds of cards takes up noticeably more room. Thinner sleeves help. If the insert is tight, a plain card box or a third-party insert may be the fix.

### Should I double sleeve board game cards?

Rarely. Double sleeving means a tight inner sleeve inside an outer sleeve. It protects valuable trading cards well, but board game cards are easy to replace, and doubled sleeves rarely fit in the box.
