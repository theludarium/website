Every Vintage Cube controversy is the same argument in a different costume: is the MTGO Vintage Cube a museum of Magic's most powerful cards, or a play environment that happens to contain them? The August 2023 overhaul answered that question in writing, and most of the fights since have been about what follows from the answer.

This is a dated tour of the arguments that recur, with sources. Where a position is ours, we say so. Where it belongs to a named writer, we link them. We have not invented any quotes and we have not guessed at what the curator thinks beyond what the posts say.

## Was the 2023 overhaul too much?

On August 15, 2023, Ryan Spain, then Creative Director of Magic Online, posted a changelist of 108 cards and called it the biggest in the cube's history. The post's line that became the whole debate: the Vintage Cube "is not a card museum, it is a premium play experience." Source: [The Vintage Cube Returns, mtgo.com](https://www.mtgo.com/news/mtgo-vintagecubeaugust23).

Out went all ten Signets for all ten Talismans, Consecrated Sphinx, Mulldrifter, Karn Liberated, Oblivion Stone, Sword of Feast and Famine, Palinchron, Thassa's Oracle (to return "when it is better supported") and most of the sacrifice deck. In came The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters, Mystic Forge, Portal to Phyrexia, Triplicate Titan with Flash, and a Lands package.

Ryan Overturf's review the next day at Star City Games ([August 16, 2023](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/vintage-cube-returns-departures-newcomers-and-a-few-surprises/)) praised the effort and the red additions and called Foundry Inspector the one "absolute miss," while questioning the Young Pyromancer and Niv-Mizzet Reborn cuts and arguing blue was notably weaker. That mixed verdict, respectful of the process and skeptical of specific calls, has been the standard shape of Vintage Cube criticism since.

Our view: the overhaul was right and overdue. A cube that had accreted slots for a decade needed someone willing to cut on performance. The two polish passes that followed in October 2023 (19 swaps) and after showed the team knew the pace could not stay at 108.

## Should Time Vault come back?

Time Vault left the Vintage Cube years ago and has not returned. It is not on the [October 7, 2026 list](https://www.mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist). Manifold Key is, pointing at the Monoliths instead.

Overturf made the case for its return in a [May 31, 2023 column](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/lets-shake-up-the-magic-online-vintage-cube/): newer answers such as Force of Negation, Force of Vigor, March of Otherworldly Light, Cathar Commando and Abrade exist now, and the cube could add it, gather feedback, and remove it fast if players hated it. His broader point was that if the cube was going to make bold changes, it should aim them at the top of the power band rather than at the weakest cards.

The curator has not taken the suggestion in three years of posts, which is its own answer. Our read: the Vintage Cube's whole 2026 direction is toward combos that take several cards and some setup (Welder loops, Bomberman, Painter) and away from two-card wins that end the game on turn two. Time Vault plus Key is the latter. We would not expect it back.

## Was Hullbreacher handled well?

Hullbreacher was added in December 2020 and removed quickly, which Overturf attributes partly to poor play patterns and partly to a client bug (same May 2023 column). It then returned in the packages era and is on the October 7, 2026 list. In his [January 31, 2024 review](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/mtgo-vintage-cube-what-worked-what-didnt-and-next-steps/) he compared it to Time Vault as a card the cube treats inconsistently, and asked for White Plume Adventurer as a counterweight.

This one is a design-consistency argument more than a power one. If Hullbreacher's play pattern against draw-sevens is acceptable, the argument goes, why is Time Vault's not? Reasonable people disagree. Our table dislikes Hullbreacher more than Time Vault, for what that is worth, because the Vault at least ends the game.

## Should the list change mid-league?

In the first Daybreak run of May 2023, the team cut seven cards after week one (Sorcerous Spyglass, Rampaging Ferocidon, Thoughtpicker Witch, Tezzeret the Seeker, Angel of Sanctions, Looter il-Kor, Cabal Therapist) and added eight, including Retrofitter Foundry and Thundermaw Hellkite. Overturf had "mixed feelings," noting that removing Academy Ruins changed how his decks played mid-run, and defended Tezzeret the Seeker as a staple.

Then in January 2024 he argued the opposite problem: a five-and-a-half-week run on a static list was too long without updates.

The 2026 answer has been to shorten leagues to about four weeks, update between them, and leave the list alone during a run. None of the 2026 posts we reviewed mention mid-league changes. That seems like the right compromise and it has quietly ended the argument.

## Are packages making the cube parasitic?

The packages method, where themed clusters of cards rotate in and out together, started in the holiday 2023 run. Overturf's January 2024 review called the result strong at its high points but "too specific and parasitic in places," with foretell cards, Prismatic Omen and Scapeshift as examples of cards that only work inside their package.

The 2026 record supports both sides. Aristocrats (January to May 2026) and madness (January to September 2026) were full packages that got drafted, got data, and got rotated. Persist lasted one update (January to March). The charm cycle and five-color piles of May were gone by July. Each one left behind a few cards that stayed (Yawgmoth, Thran Physician outlasted aristocrats; Goblin Welder became permanent) and took the parasitic pieces with it. If you believe a cube is never finished, this is what managing it looks like. If you want a stable list to learn, it is exhausting.

## Is aggro being squeezed out?

A perennial. Overturf's January 2024 piece criticized the removal of Student of Warfare and City of Traitors as a push against mono-color aggro toward midrange. In 2026 the curator trimmed white aggro in January (Ademi, Ranger-Captain, Descendant of Storms out, with notes that white aggro was "saturated") and said in October that Boros "remains the strongest archetype even after trimming its numbers."

Those two facts resolve the argument for now: the trims happened and the deck is still the best. Whoever is worried about aggro dying in the Vintage Cube can stop. The October 2026 red prowess package is the curator trying to give aggro a second shape, not fewer slots. Our general piece on [why aggro dies in cubes](/articles/aggro-in-cube/) explains why unpowered paper cubes have the opposite problem.

## Does Universes Beyond belong in the Vintage Cube?

The newest argument and the one with the least settled data. The MTGO cube added Marvel, Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles, The Hobbit, Final Fantasy, Avatar and Fallout cards through 2025 and 2026 as each set released. Player sentiment about Universes Beyond in general has been mixed: Mark Rosewater acknowledged "ongoing complaints" in his State of Design 2025 article, as [ICv2 reported in August 2025](https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/60266/magic-the-gathering-universes-beyond-sets-receive-ongoing-complaints), while noting the opposition was shrinking.

Two things are true at once. First, the cube is a promotional vehicle for each MTGO set release, and the curator's posts say so in the structure if not the words: each update is named for the set. Second, the UB cards are evaluated and cut like any other. Of the twenty July 2026 Marvel additions, three were gone by August. Bilbo, Thief in the Night was held back in August and added on trial in October. The cards are treated as cards. Whether you want Batroc the Leaper next to Black Lotus is a taste question, and the curator does not get a vote on your paper list.

## Should the client decide what cards are in the cube?

This is the one we think will matter most going forward, and it has barely been argued yet. The October 2026 post added Auriok Salvagers because MTGO's [combo automation of August 5, 2026](https://www.mtgo.com/news/combo-play-improvements) made the loop practical. The post says the difficulty of executing it efficiently was the reason it had stayed out.

That is an honest statement and a strange one. It means the most-followed cube list in the world has a selection criterion no paper cube shares: click count. Cards that are great in paper and tedious online have been quietly absent for years, and cards that become easy to click will arrive. Anyone treating the Vintage Cube as a pure card-quality reference should know this. We cover the paper side in [which Vintage Cube changes belong in your cube](/articles/vintage-cube-changes-for-paper/).

## FAQ

### Who said the Vintage Cube "is not a card museum"?

Ryan Spain, then Creative Director of Magic Online, in the August 15, 2023 update post on mtgo.com.

### Is Time Vault in the Vintage Cube?

No, as of the October 7, 2026 list. It was cut years ago and has not returned despite public arguments for it.

### Did the Vintage Cube change mid-league?

It did in May 2023. The 2026 posts show updates between leagues only.

### Is Boros too strong in Vintage Cube?

The curator's October 2, 2026 post says it remains the strongest archetype after repeated trims, and that aggressive slots are being kept limited. Whether that is "too strong" is a judgment the post does not make.
