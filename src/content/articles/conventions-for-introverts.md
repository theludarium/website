The best convention tips for introverts are not about being braver. They are about pacing. A gaming convention is a four-day marathon of noise, strangers, and decisions, and the people who enjoy it most are not the loudest ones. They are the ones who know when to step out, where the quiet rooms are, and how to let the game do the talking.

This plan was written for Gen Con, which is the hardest case, and works at any of the smaller shows too. If you have ever left a convention early and told yourself you were just tired, this is for you.

## Why are conventions hard for introverts in particular?

Three things drain introverts faster than most: unstructured social contact, sensory load, and open-ended time. A convention is all three at once.

The Exhibit Hall is a wall of sound and motion. Open gaming means approaching strangers. The schedule is a thousand options with no default. And the whole thing runs from 8 a.m. to midnight for four days.

The good news is that tabletop games are, by design, the easiest social setting an introvert will ever find. A game gives you a reason to be at the table, a script for what to say, a defined end time, and something to look at besides faces. We wrote about why in [board games and social anxiety](/articles/board-games-social-anxiety/). The convention is hard; the tables are easy. Plan around that.

## What does a sustainable day look like?

Here is a template. Adjust the times, keep the shape.

| Time | Activity | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| 7:30 | Breakfast alone or with one person. No phone scrolling of the schedule. | Charge |
| 9:00 | One ticketed event. Structured, seated, finite. | Spend |
| 11:30 | Quiet hour. Walk outside, sit in a hotel lobby, read. | Charge |
| 12:30 | Lunch away from the convention center. | Charge |
| 2:00 | Exhibit Hall, 45 minutes maximum, with a list. | Spend hard |
| 3:00 | Open gaming, one game, at a table you chose. | Spend gently |
| 5:00 | Back to the room. Shower, lie down, no guilt. | Charge |
| 7:00 | Dinner with people you met at the 3:00 table, or alone. | Optional spend |
| 9:00 | One evening game or bed. | Optional |

The structure matters more than the specifics. Alternate spending and charging. Never stack two hard spends. Build the quiet hour in before you need it, because once you need it you will be too depleted to find it.

## Where is the quiet at Gen Con?

The Indiana Convention Center and the downtown hotels hide calm places if you know to look.

- **Hotel lobbies and mezzanines.** Connected hotels have seating areas a floor above the crowds. Nobody checks badges to sit.
- **The far end of open gaming.** The open-gaming halls are big. The corners away from the entrance are slower and quieter.
- **Outside.** A five-minute walk from the convention center in any direction drops the noise by half. The [Canal Walk](https://tclf.org/indianapolis-canal-walk) runs through White River State Park and along the west edge of downtown, a few blocks from the convention center.
- **Early mornings.** The building at opening time is a different building. Do the Exhibit Hall at 10 a.m. Thursday, not 2 p.m. Saturday.
- **Your room.** A connected or walkable hotel pays for itself in retreats. If you are in the suburbs, your car in the garage is a legitimate sanctuary.
- **The Quiet Room.** Gen Con runs a designated [Quiet Room in room 211 on the second floor of the convention center](https://www.gencon.com/gen-con-indy/accessibility), open 10 to 6 Thursday through Saturday and 10 to 3 Sunday, with talking prohibited and phones on silent. Check the accessibility page for the current year's room.

Noise-cancelling headphones or earplugs change the Exhibit Hall from an assault to a walk. Wear them without apology.

## How do you meet people without small talk?

You do not need small talk at a game convention. You need a chair.

**Use ticketed events as introductions.** A four-hour RPG or a two-hour board game teach puts you at a table with the same five people for long enough that conversation happens on its own. By the end you have names and a shared story. That is more than most parties produce.

**Use the "looking for players" systems.** Open-gaming halls use flags, signs, or boards. Putting a flag up is less exposing than walking up to a group: it makes them come to you. Sitting down at a table with a flag up is less exposing than asking "can I join?" The system was built for exactly this.

**Bring a game you can teach in three minutes.** Teaching gives you a role. Something like The Crew <!-- AFFILIATE -->, a cooperative trick-taking game, is good because the players are on the same side and the talking is about the cards. Wavelength <!-- AFFILIATE --> and Just One work the same way for bigger groups.

**Let the game carry the conversation.** "Nice move" and "oh no" are complete sentences at a game table. Nobody expects more.

**Leave when the game ends.** You do not owe anyone a second game. "That was great, I'm going to go find some water" is a complete exit.

**Follow up with one person, not five.** If you met someone you liked, ask whether they are playing anything tomorrow. One real connection a convention is a good convention.

## What about going alone?

Going to a convention alone is fine, and for many introverts it is better. You set the pace. You leave when you want. You are not managing anyone else's energy.

The trick is to schedule a few anchors so the day has shape: a morning event, an afternoon open-gaming hour, a dinner reservation for one at a place with a counter. Between anchors you drift, and drifting alone through a game convention is one of the pleasant experiences this hobby offers.

If you want company without commitment, the smaller regional conventions are easier than Gen Con. The halls are calmer and the regulars are used to welcoming new faces. We list several in [board game conventions in the Midwest](/articles/board-game-conventions-midwest/).

## What are the warning signs that you have overspent?

Learn your own, but these are common:

- You are reading your phone in a corner instead of playing
- Small decisions, like which aisle, feel impossible
- You are irritated by people who have done nothing
- You skip a meal because the line looks like too much
- You tell yourself you will "push through" to the evening event

Each of those is a signal to charge, not spend. Go back to the room. Sleep if you can. The evening event will be there next year, and the version of you who shows up rested is the one people enjoy sitting across from.

## A note on leaving early

It is allowed. A convention is not a test. If you have had two good days and the third morning feels like dread, go home, play the games you bought, and plan a better-paced trip next year. Many of the best convention-goers we know do three days of a four-day show, every year, on purpose.

The game is the reason. The people are the point. You can meet the people one table at a time, at your own speed, and that is still meeting them.

## FAQ

### Can an introvert enjoy Gen Con?

Yes. Plan structured events over unstructured wandering, schedule quiet hours before you need them, and use games as the way to meet people instead of small talk.

### Where can I find quiet at a gaming convention?

Hotel lobbies and upper floors, the far corners of open-gaming halls, outdoors, and the convention center itself in the first hour after opening.

### How do I join a game with strangers at a convention?

Use the "looking for players" flags or sign-up boards in open gaming, or book ticketed events where the table is assigned. Both remove the need to approach a group cold.

### Is it weird to go to a board game convention alone?

No. Many attendees go solo, and the hobby's structures, from ticketed events to open-gaming flags, are built to seat single players.
