The togethering meaning we use is this: people coming together, usually in person, to share life. The tabletop game is the medium that gets everyone to the table and gives the time a shape. The time together is the point. We put the word on everything The Ludarium makes, and people ask about it often enough that it deserves its own explanation, including why none of the existing words would do.

## What does togethering mean?

Togethering is a noun. It names the thing that happens when a few people sit down in the same room, on purpose, for a few hours, and come out knowing each other better than when they went in.

It is not the game. The game is the occasion. It is not the conversation, exactly, though conversation is most of it. It is the whole act: showing up, settling in, doing something together that does not matter very much, and letting the things that do matter come out sideways while you do it.

The clearest example we know is a weekly campaign table. Four people, the same night every week, playing a long game like Gloomhaven or a D&D campaign over months. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Ask them what the campaign was about and they will tell you about the fights. Ask them what it was for and they will tell you about the divorce one of them went through in month three, and how the table was the only place that week where he was not alone.

That is togethering. It is also the thing adults have the fewest places to do, which is the [third place problem](/articles/third-place-problem/) in one sentence. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## Why did the existing words not work?

We tried them. Every one carried the wrong weight.

**Socializing** sounds like a cocktail party. Standing, small talk, a drink in one hand, looking for the exit. It describes the thing most adults say they are bad at and avoid.

**Networking** is socializing with an agenda. Worse.

**Hanging out** is close, but it undersells. Hanging out is passive. It can happen in front of a television. Togethering is a verb turned into a noun on purpose: something you do, not something that happens to you.

**Community** is the right idea at the wrong scale. Community is the hundred people. Togethering is the four people at one table, which is where community is actually made.

**Quality time** has been worn smooth by greeting cards and parenting books.

**Fellowship** is beautiful and has a church in it, which is fine for some of our readers and a barrier for others.

We needed a word with no baggage, that pointed at the in-person act, that worked for a family, a group of coworkers, two old friends, or four strangers at a public game night. So we made one, or rather, we picked up one that had been lying around unused and gave it a job.

## Why does a game lounge business need a mission word at all?

Because without one, the business drifts toward the thing that is easy to measure.

A game lounge can measure table turns, covers, drinks sold, and library size. None of those is the point. We have watched venues optimize for them and become loud, fast, and forgettable. We wrote about that drift in [how a game lounge membership works](/articles/game-lounge-membership/): a cover charge pushes a room toward volume, and volume pushes out the Tuesday table.

Togethering is the thing we cannot put on a dashboard and the thing we exist to produce. Keeping the word in front of us is how we remember that when the spreadsheet says otherwise. If a decision produces more revenue and less togethering, it is the wrong decision, and we want the word on the wall to make that argument when we are tired.

## How does a game produce togethering?

Better than most things, and for specific reasons.

**It gives permission to sit.** Adults do not sit at a table with other adults for three hours without an excuse. A game is the excuse. Nobody has to say "I would like to spend time with you," which is a sentence most of us find hard to say.

**It removes the pressure to perform.** Conversation at a dinner party is the activity, so silence is failure. At a game table, the game is the activity, so silence is thinking. People who freeze at parties relax at tables. We hear this most from people who describe themselves as introverts or anxious.

**It makes the time repeatable.** "Let's get dinner sometime" dies on the calendar. "Thursday is Catan night" survives, because the structure does the scheduling. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Togethering is a habit, and games are a habit machine.

**It surfaces character.** How someone handles a bad dice roll, a betrayal in a social deduction game, or a teammate's mistake in Pandemic tells you more about them in an hour than months of small talk. <!-- AFFILIATE --> That knowledge is intimacy, and it accumulates.

## What does "for a lifetime" add?

Our full mission line is "Togethering for a lifetime." The second half matters as much as the first.

Most play is treated as a phase. Kids play. College students play. Then adults stop, and we call it growing up. We think that is a mistake with real costs. The [Harvard Study of Adult Development](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/02/work-out-daily-ok-but-how-socially-fit-are-you/), which has followed its participants since 1938, has reported over and over that satisfaction with one's relationships predicts health and happiness at 80 better than midlife cholesterol or blood pressure. The [U.S. Surgeon General's 2023 advisory](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf) on loneliness and isolation made a similar case in public-health terms.

Relationships need maintenance, and maintenance needs occasions. A standing game is an occasion that can run for fifty years. We know people whose weekly card game has outlasted jobs, houses, and marriages. That is what "for a lifetime" means: play as a practice you keep, not a thing you age out of.

This is also why The Ludarium is connected to the LEV Institute, a longevity-ethics project that treats social and mental health as part of living long and living well. Games are one of the cheapest, oldest tools for that. We just think they deserve a room.

## How do we use the word in practice?

Carefully, and not as a slogan.

- We ask of every event, "Does this produce togethering, or just attendance?" A 40-person trivia night produces attendance. A 40-person night with tables of five who talk to each other produces togethering.
- We ask of every design decision in the space, "Does this help people stay at the table?" Chairs, light, noise, and food all pass or fail that test.
- We ask of our corporate events service, "Will these coworkers know each other better at the end?" Not "Was it fun." Fun is easy.
- We use the short form, "Never stop playing," when the full line is too much.

And we try not to overuse it. A word that is on every sentence stops meaning anything. It belongs on the mission, on the wall, and in articles like this one.

## FAQ

### What is the meaning of togethering?

People coming together, usually in person, to share life. The Ludarium uses it to describe what happens at a game table when the game is the occasion and the people are the purpose.

### Is togethering a real word?

It is an English word formed the ordinary way, by turning "together" into a verb and then a noun. It has appeared occasionally in other contexts. We use it with a specific meaning and have not trademarked the concept of people liking each other.

### Why does The Ludarium use togethering instead of community?

Community describes the whole group. Togethering describes the act at a single table, which is where community gets made.

### How is togethering different from socializing?

Socializing usually means standing, small talk, and a party. Togethering means sitting down together, on purpose, with something to do, for long enough that real conversation happens on its own.
