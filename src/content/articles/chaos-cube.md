A chaos cube is a cube built to produce games nobody can predict. Where a normal cube flattens variance so that skill decides more games, a chaos cube turns variance up on purpose: coin flips, Warp World, cards that trade your deck for your opponent's, Un-set cards that ask you to do a dance, and a few honest bombs that end the game out of nowhere. It is the cube for the night when eight competitive people want to laugh instead of grind.

Building one well is harder than it looks, because "put in all the silly cards" produces a box that is funny for one draft and tedious after. This article covers what a chaos cube is and is not, the categories of card that create good chaos, the Un-card decision, and the structural slots that keep the games from falling over. For the rules-level side of draft-matters cards, see our [Conspiracy and draft-matters guide](/articles/conspiracy-cards-in-cube/).

## Chaos cube or chaos draft?

Two different things share the name. A **chaos draft** is a draft where every pack is a random retail booster from a different set: a Mirage pack next to a Kaldheim pack next to a Fallen Empires pack. The chaos is in the packs. A **chaos cube** is a curated singleton cube whose cards are chosen for variance. The chaos is in the games.

Chaos draft needs no design and costs whatever the boosters cost. A chaos cube is a design project. The rest of this article is about the second one.

## What does "good chaos" look like?

Variance is a dial, and the useful question is which kind of variance makes the table happy. Four kinds do:

1. **Symmetric upheaval.** Warp World, Scrambleverse, Thieves' Auction, Shahrazad (banned in sanctioned play, fine at your kitchen table), Possibility Storm, Grip of Chaos. Everyone's board changes at once, nobody is singled out, and the result is a new game. These are the heart of a chaos cube.
2. **Randomized decisions.** Coin flips (Mana Clash, Krark's Thumb, Fiery Gambit, Stitch in Time), die rolls (the Adventures in the Forgotten Realms d20 cards, Unstable's roll effects), and cascade or discover chains. The player chooses to take a gamble and the table watches it land.
3. **Hidden information games.** Fact or Fiction is normal Magic; Goblin Game, Illusionary Mask, and Unhinged's "name a card" effects are chaos. Small bluffing minigames inside the match.
4. **Swingy bombs at low density.** A few cards that win alone (Emrakul, the Aeons Torn off a Sneak Attack, Omniscience, Grinning Ignus storm). In a normal cube these are policed; here they are the lottery ticket that keeps a losing player in the game.

The kind of variance that does not work: cards that single out one player for a long time. Mindslaver, Stasis, and Winter Orb produce one person's chaos and three people's wait. Leave them out even here.

## Should a chaos cube run Un-cards?

Most do, and it needs a decision because the Un-sets are not one thing.

- **Unglued (1998) and Unhinged (2004)** are silver-bordered and mostly physical or out-of-game effects: Chaos Confetti (tear the card up), Mine, Mine, Mine!, Blacker Lotus, Richard Garfield, Ph.D. These are chaos in its purest form and some of them destroy the cards, so proxy anything you would tear.
- **Unstable (2017)** has contraptions, host and augment, and die rolls. The contraption deck is a real subsystem that works in cube: give each player access to the shared Contraption pile or draft contraptions as a separate mini-pack.
- **Unsanctioned (2020)** is a reprint box with a few new cards.
- **[Unfinity (2022)](https://mtg.wiki/page/Unfinity)** is black-bordered throughout and uses a holofoil stamp instead: acorn-stamped cards are casual-only, while the rest (a little over half the set) are legal in Legacy, Vintage, and Commander. The attraction and sticker subsystems are chaos-cube-ready and come with their own card types.

A workable house rule: **allow anything, but write down how each physical or out-of-game effect resolves at your table before the draft.** The Un-sets are designed with the assumption that the table decides, and the chaos cube primer is where those decisions live. We keep a one-page sheet: how "outside the game" works (bring a sideboard of ten random cards), what happens when someone has to sing, and whether Chaos Confetti is a proxy.

If you want the chaos without the rules arguments, build from black-border only. There is more than enough: the Planar Chaos and Future Sight weirdness, Conspiracy's draft-matters cards, the Commander-set chaos (Grip of Chaos, Scrambleverse), the Ravnica Clue and Baldur's Gate dice, and the Portal and Starter one-offs.

## How should the sections split?

A chaos cube still needs to be a Magic cube underneath, or the games do not reach the fun part. A house guideline for a 360:

| Slot | Count | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| Normal playables (creatures, removal, fixing) | ~200 | A peasant-level backbone; cheap removal and two-drops so decks function |
| Symmetric upheaval | 15–20 | Warp World, Possibility Storm, Scrambleverse, Thieves' Auction, Timesifter |
| Randomizers | 30–40 | Coin flips, die rolls, cascade and discover, Chaos Warp, Mana Clash |
| Hidden-information and minigame cards | 10–15 | Goblin Game, Illusionary Mask, Unhinged naming effects |
| Draft-matters and conspiracies | 15–20 | Cogwork Librarian, Lore Seeker, conspiracies, Paliano cards |
| Honest bombs | 10–15 | One-card wins, spread across colors, none below six mana |
| Un-cards and physical effects | 20–30 | Your choice of eras; proxies for destructive ones |
| Lands | 40 | Normal fixing plus a few strange lands (Maze of Ith, Thawing Glaciers, Mirrorpool) |

The important number is the first one. Over half the cube should be ordinary good cards at a modest power level, because chaos needs a stable surface to happen on. If every card is a gag, no one can attack, and a chaos game that does not end is the worst kind.

Keep the normal backbone's power at about peasant level, as described in our [peasant cube guide](/articles/peasant-cube/). That makes the chaos cards relatively bigger: Warp World in a Vintage cube is a bad card; Warp World in a cube of bears and Thriving lands is the story of the night.

## Which chaos cards earn a slot?

A short list of cards that have made our table laugh more than once, organized by what they do: <!-- AFFILIATE -->

- **Rewrite the board:** Warp World, Scrambleverse, Thieves' Auction, Switcheroo, Cultural Exchange, Shifting Loyalties.
- **Rewrite the rules for a while:** Possibility Storm, Grip of Chaos, Eye of the Storm, Hive Mind, Knowledge Pool, Timesifter.
- **Gamble:** Mana Clash, Fiery Gambit, Stitch in Time, Krark's Thumb, Goblin Bomb, Chaos Warp, Mogg Assassin.
- **Dice:** Treasure Chest, Wild Magic Surge, Delina, Wild Mage, Pixie Guide, the Baldur's Gate d20 cards, and the Unstable roll cards.
- **Draft-matters:** Cogwork Librarian, Lore Seeker, Agent of Acquisitions, Leovold's Operative, Animus of Predation, Paliano, the High City.
- **Bombs with a story:** Emrakul, the Aeons Torn; Omniscience; Enter the Infinite; Door to Nothingness; Laboratory Maniac for the player who dares.

Two per color of each category is a sensible target, and the balance across colors matters less than in a normal cube because the games are not about color lanes.

## What should stay out, even here?

- **Single-player locks.** Stasis, Winter Orb, Smokestack, Mindslaver loops.
- **Real combos that end the game before chaos happens.** Flash plus Protean Hulk, Splinter Twin pairs, Doomsday. The bombs should be big and slow.
- **Physical effects that damage cards you did not proxy.**
- **Anything whose table ruling you cannot write in one sentence.**

The test for every card: does it make the game stranger, or does it make the game shorter? Stranger stays.

## FAQ

### What is a chaos cube?

A chaos cube is a curated singleton cube built for high variance: symmetric board-shuffling effects, coin flips and dice, Un-set cards, draft-matters cards, and a few honest bombs over a backbone of ordinary playables.

### What is the difference between a chaos cube and a chaos draft?

A chaos draft uses random retail boosters from different sets; a chaos cube is a designed card pool chosen for unpredictable games.

### Can you use Un-cards in a chaos cube?

Yes, and most do. Decide in advance how physical and out-of-game effects resolve at your table, proxy any card that destroys itself, and write the rulings in the primer.

### How many "normal" cards should a chaos cube have?

More than half. A 360 needs about 200 ordinary creatures, removal, and lands at a modest power level so decks function and the chaos has something to act on.
