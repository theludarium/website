Magic: The Gathering | The Hobbit <!-- AFFILIATE --> released on August 14, 2026 as a Standard-legal Universes Beyond set, and for The Hobbit cube question the short answer is: a handful of strong legendary creatures, an equipment package that is better than most, and a lot of Limited-only tribal glue. If your cube has an opinion about Universes Beyond cards, that opinion matters more here than any single rating; we cover the policy question in [supplemental sets and cube](/articles/supplemental-sets-for-cube/). If you run them, read on for the slot calls.

Set facts from the [Wizards collecting article](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-the-hobbit): tabletop release August 14, 2026, code HOB, 183 cards in the main set plus five common dual lands, legal in all formats including Standard. Card text below was confirmed against Card Kingdom and mtg.wtf listings and is paraphrased.

## What does the set do, in one paragraph?

Dwarves and Equipment in white and red, Treasure in red and black, Adventures returning on several legends and artifacts, and a "recruit" action that makes small Dwarf or Soldier tokens. Smaug makes Treasure; Gollum punishes spells by mana value; the Hobbits cheat on costs. For cube, the themes that travel are Equipment and Treasure. The Dwarf tribal payoffs mostly stay in Limited.

## Which creatures are the real cube cards?

- **Smaug the Magnificent** ({2}{R}{R}, 4/3 flying haste; when he attacks, deals damage equal to your Treasures to any target; makes a Treasure at each of your upkeeps). **Slot:** red / 4 / finisher. **Competes with:** Hellrider, Glorybringer, Rekindling Phoenix. **Call: keep** at Legacy and Modern power, **test** at Vintage. A four-mana hasty flier that ramps you every turn and grows its own burn is exactly what red four-drops are supposed to be. Glorybringer is still better on an empty board, but Smaug is close.
- **Bilbo, Thief in the Night** ({1}{U}, 2/2; spells you cast from outside your hand cost {1} less; when he attacks, you may cast an artifact, instant or sorcery from your graveyard, and spells cast this way are exiled). **Slot:** blue / 2 / value creature. **Competes with:** Snapcaster Mage (adjacent), Ledger Shredder, Jace, Vryn's Prodigy. **Call: test.** A two-mana blue body that recasts Counterspell or Preordain from the graveyard every attack is strong if it survives. It usually does not, which is the test.
- **Gandalf, Goblins' Bane // Flameshape** ({2}{R}, 2/3; whenever you cast a noncreature spell, +1/+1 until end of turn and 1 damage to each opponent; Adventure {1}{R}: exile the top two, play them while you control a Wizard). **Slot:** red / 3 / spells payoff. **Call: test.** It is a Guttersnipe that also attacks, with a built-in two-card impulse draw. Red spells decks will take it early.
- **Radagast of Rhosgobel** ({2}{G}{G}, 2/5; your first creature spell each turn costs {2} less and has flash). **Slot:** green / 4 / ramp enabler. **Call: test** in green creature-heavy cubes, **pass** in spells-heavy ones. The 2/5 body blocks well and the discount is real from the turn after.
- **Gollum, Riddle Master** ({1}{B}, 3/1; choose odd or even as he enters; when an opponent casts a spell of that parity, choose a counter, a 2-point drain, or a card). **Slot:** black / 2 / aggressive value. **Call: test.** Three power for two is on rate, and every opponent's deck has spells of both parities. Dies to everything.
- **Elrond, Moon-Reader** ({2}{U}, 3/3; draw a card once each turn when you activate a creature's ability; {5}{U}{U} blinks two of your nonland permanents). **Call: pass** in most cubes. A three-mana 3/3 is fine, but the draw trigger needs activated abilities your blue decks rarely have.
- **Beorn the Fierce** ({3}{G}{G}, 6/6 trample; Bears get +2/+2; makes a Bear each combat; draws two with three Bears). **Call: pass.** A five-mana 6/6 trample is below the green five-drop bar, and the Bear text is Limited.
- **Thorin, Mountain-king** ({3}{R}, 3/4 trample; on entering, attach any number of your Equipment to a creature and fight). **Call: test** only if you run six or more Equipment. Otherwise pass.
- **The Sackville-Bagginses** ({1}{B}, 2/2; may sacrifice a creature or artifact on entering to draw and make a Treasure; opponents lose 1 when you sacrifice a token). **Slot:** black / 2 / sacrifice. **Call: test** in aristocrats lanes.
- **Smaug, Wicked Worm** ({3}{B}{R}, 5/5 flying; makes tapped Treasures equal to opponents' artifacts; draws when you spend Treasure mana). **Call: pass.** In a cube with few artifacts the first ability is often zero.
- **The Great Goblin** ({1}{B/R}{B/R}, 3/2; counters on Goblins ping for 2; Goblin deaths give impulse draw). **Call: pass** for non-tribal cubes.
- **Bard, King of Dale** ({4}{W}{U}, 3/5 reach vigilance; doubles your draws and token creation). **Call: pass.** Six-mana gold walls do not draft.

## Is the Equipment package worth it?

Equipment is where this set is unusually deep. Equipment in cube lives or dies by two numbers: total cost to attach and the size of the bonus. Here is how these compare.

| Card | Cost + equip | Bonus | Call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orcrist, Goblin-cleaver | {3} + {3} | +2/+2 trample, Treasures on damage | Pass |
| Glamdring, Foe-hammer // Gleam of Death | {2} + {2} | Spells cost X less (X = power); Adventure mills six for spells | Test in spells cubes |
| My Precious // Allure of Power | {3} + {2} and 2 life | Hexproof and unblockable; Adventure sacrifices to draw two | Test in black sacrifice lanes |
| Wizard's Staff | {1}{U} + {3} ({1} for Wizards) | Prowess; doubles equipped creature's triggers | Pass |
| Dwarven Shortsword | {3}{W} + {2} | Enters with a 2/2 body attached, +1/+2 | Pass |
| The Arkenstone // Seek the Heart | {5}, no equip | Anthem plus draw a card each end step | Pass |

None of these is Jitte or Sword of Fire and Ice, and none replaces Bonesplitter. Glamdring is the interesting one: a two-mana rock that makes your spells cheaper by the equipped creature's power, with a self-mill Adventure for control. My Precious is a Rogue's Passage on a stick with a Sign in Blood attached, which is more than it sounds like for a sacrifice deck.

## Which spells and enchantments get a look?

- **Settle the Wreckage** ({2}{W}{W}, instant: exile all attacking creatures target player controls; they may fetch basics). A reprint, and still a reasonable control sweeper in cubes that lean aggressive. **Call: test** if you need a fourth white sweeper.
- **The Eagles Are Coming!** ({1}{W}, instant, kicker {2}{W}{W}: return your creatures to hand; next upkeep, make a 4/4 flier for each). **Call: pass.** Cute, slow.
- **Magnificent End** ({4}{W}, instant, costs {3} less against a tapped creature: 5 damage). **Call: test** at Peasant and lower; it is a two-mana removal spell most of the time.
- **Gleaming Splendor** ({1}{W}, enchantment: Treasure when an opponent draws their second card; {2}{W} makes two players draw). **Call: pass.** A Commander card with a Standard price tag.
- **Inside Information** ({X}{B}{B}, sorcery: exile the top X of an opponent's library, play them this turn paying life). **Call: pass.**
- **Fateful Discovery** ({3}{U}{U}, enchantment: draw whenever an artifact enters under your control). **Call: pass** outside artifact cubes.
- **Dancing from Dark to Dawn** ({3}{G}{G}: counters on creature cast, 2/2 Bear on landfall). **Call: pass.**
- **Bilbo's Gambit** ({1}{W}, instant: gift a Treasure, bounce a spell; if the gift was promised, no spells this turn). **Call: pass.** Remand in white is interesting, but giving the opponent a Treasure undoes the tempo.
- **Moment of Glory** ({W}, sorcery: a +1/+1 counter, more when flashed back; flashback {4}{W}). **Call: pass.**

## What about the cheap colorless creatures?

Three colorless commons stood out for Pauper and budget cubes: **Long-Bodied Grey Dog** ({3}, 2/2 flash reach, makes a tapped Treasure), **Old Thrush** ({2}, 1/2 flier, gains 2 and may top-deck a basic), and **Troop of Ponies** ({2}, 2/1; {2}, tap, sacrifice: fetch two basics, one to play and one to hand). Troop of Ponies is real ramp for any color in a Pauper list. **Call: test** at Pauper, pass above.

## FAQ

### Is The Hobbit set good for cube?

Good, not great. Smaug the Magnificent is a real red four-drop, Bilbo and Gandalf are worth testing, and the rest is mostly Limited. It is better for cube than most Universes Beyond releases.

### Does Smaug the Magnificent replace Glorybringer?

Not for us. Glorybringer kills a creature every other turn without help; Smaug needs a few turns of Treasure before his attack trigger matters. Both can live in a 540 list.

### Should a cube run Universes Beyond cards at all?

That is a house decision. Some owners keep cubes to in-universe cards for a consistent look; others take the best card for the slot regardless. Decide once, write it in your primer, and apply it to every set.

### Which Hobbit cards work in a Pauper cube?

Troop of Ponies, Old Thrush and Long-Bodied Grey Dog are the ones we would test. The set's commons otherwise lean on Dwarf and recruit synergies that a Pauper list cannot support.
