Root factions are the reason the game feels like four games sharing one board. [Root](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F454YF3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) gives every player a different faction with its own rules, its own way to score, and its own idea of what winning looks like. The base game has four. Four expansions add nine more, for thirteen in all. This guide explains every one in plain words, shows how they fit together, and helps you pick the right one for your first game and your next.

**The quick answer:** for a first game, play the base four. Hand the Marquise de Cat to the player who likes clear goals, the Eyrie Dynasties to the planner, the Vagabond to the table talker, and the Woodland Alliance to the most patient player at the table. Add expansion factions one at a time, and check the reach total before every game.

## What are the Root factions?

Root is a war game about woodland animals, designed by Cole Wehrle and published by Leder Games in 2018. Every faction shares the same skeleton: the same map of twelve clearings, the same deck of cards, the same rules for moving, fighting, and crafting. On top of that skeleton, each faction hangs its own board. That board tells you what you do on your turn and how you earn points. The first player to 30 points wins.

So nobody plays the same game, and choosing a faction matters a lot.

If you have never played, start with our lesson on [how to play Root](/academy/root/how-to-play-root/), which covers the shared rules every faction uses.

## Root factions explained: all 13 at a glance

Here is every faction, the box it comes in, and its **reach**, a number from the Law of Root (the game's full rulebook) that measures how much of the map a faction pushes against. More on reach below.

| Faction | Box | Reach | Playstyle | How it scores |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marquise de Cat | Base game | 10 | Builder holding the whole map | Placing buildings |
| Eyrie Dynasties | Base game | 7 | Rigid program that snowballs | Roosts on the map, every Evening |
| Woodland Alliance | Base game | 3 | Slow insurgency, explosive late | Spreading sympathy tokens |
| Vagabond | Base game | 5 (second: 2) | Lone adventurer playing all sides | Aid, quests, ruins, fighting Hostile factions |
| Riverfolk Company | Riverfolk | 5 | Merchant selling services | Trade posts and its funds |
| Lizard Cult | Riverfolk | 2 | Faith that grows from the outcasts | Revealing cards that match its gardens |
| Underground Duchy | Underworld | 8 | Tunneling builders with a council | Swaying ministers |
| Corvid Conspiracy | Underworld | 3 | Hidden plots and bluffs | Flipping face-down plots |
| Lord of the Hundreds | Marauder | 9 | Warlord who burns and hoards | Ruling clearings with no enemy pieces |
| Keepers in Iron | Marauder | 8 | Knights on a relic hunt | Delivering relics, and full sets |
| Lilypad Diaspora | Homeland | 7 | Settlers who add a new suit | Peaceful enclaves |
| Twilight Council | Homeland | 4 | Peacekeepers and assemblies | Governing assemblies |
| Knaves of the Deepwood | Homeland | 4 | Three captains and a skunk crew | Acclaim and prisoners |

We left out a complexity column because we could not confirm an official rating for every faction. The guidance below is our recommendation, not Leder's.

## The four base game factions

### Marquise de Cat

The Marquise starts the game owning the Woodland, with a warrior in eleven of twelve clearings. She scores by placing sawmills, workshops, and recruiters, and each new building earns more than the last. Her turns are simple to read: take up to three actions, build when you can. The catch is that she starts as everyone's target, and every action spent defending is an action not spent building. Our [Marquise de Cat strategy lesson](/academy/root/root-marquise-de-cat-strategy/) covers wood, tempo, and surviving as the target.

**Play her if:** you like engines, clear goals, and a big army.

### Eyrie Dynasties

The Eyrie birds follow a **Decree**, a growing list of orders they must carry out every turn. Keep every order and the Eyrie snowball into a huge army that scores from its roosts every Evening. Break one order and the dynasty falls into turmoil: it loses points, throws out the Decree, and picks a new leader. The Eyrie reward players who plan two turns ahead. See the [Eyrie Dynasties strategy lesson](/academy/root/root-eyrie-dynasties-strategy/) for how to write a Decree that holds.

**Play them if:** you like programming your moves and the tension of a plan that might collapse.

### Woodland Alliance

The Woodland Alliance starts with nothing on the map, just three cards set aside as supporters. It spreads sympathy tokens across the clearings, and each token scores. When other players move into sympathetic clearings or remove sympathy, they must pay the Alliance cards through **Outrage**. When the time is right, the Alliance revolts, wiping enemy pieces out of a clearing and planting a base. It starts slowly and builds toward a dramatic late game. Our [Woodland Alliance strategy lesson](/academy/root/root-woodland-alliance-strategy/) covers sympathy, Outrage, and timing the revolt.

**Play it if:** you are patient, you like reading where the fighting will be, and you enjoy a comeback.

### Vagabond

The Root Vagabond is one pawn against whole armies. He can slip into forests that most factions never enter, explores ruins for items, completes quests, and aids other factions by giving them cards, often taking an item in return. He can befriend a faction and later turn on it, scoring for fighting factions he has made Hostile. His items are his actions, so his turn is a puzzle of which items to use and which to save. The [Vagabond strategy lesson](/academy/root/root-vagabond-strategy/) covers characters, items, and when to switch sides.

**Play him if:** you like talking, trading, and having a little bit of everyone's business.

## What is the best Root faction for beginners?

There is no single best Root faction for beginners. There is a best match between a faction and a player. Here is how we would seat a new table of four:

| Player | Faction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Likes clear goals and building | Marquise de Cat | Her board shows exactly what to build next |
| Likes plans and puzzles | Eyrie Dynasties | The Decree rewards thinking ahead |
| Social, likes making deals | Vagabond | His game is relationships with the other three |
| Patient, or has played before | Woodland Alliance | It starts slow and needs a sense of where fights will break out |

Two warnings. First, the Alliance is the base faction most likely to feel lost in a first game, because it needs the other factions to fight near it before it can grow. Give it to someone who will not mind a quiet first hour. Second, the table often gangs up on the Marquise, so give her to someone who enjoys being the one to beat.

If you are teaching, the Walking Through Root booklet in the base game is a guided walkthrough of the opening turns for exactly these four factions. Our [Root course](/academy/root/) has a full lesson on teaching it.

## What is reach, and why does it matter?

Reach is the closest thing Root has to an official balance tool. The Law of Root gives each faction a reach number and a target total for each player count. Pick factions whose reach adds up to at least the target.

| Players | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total reach | 17+ | 18+ | 21+ | 25+ | 28+ |

The Law also says adventurous groups are welcome to try any mix with a total of 17 or more.

Reach is not power. It is pressure. A high-reach faction like the Marquise (10) touches the whole map from turn one. A low-reach faction like the Lizard Cult (2) mostly reacts to what others do. A table with too little reach tends to leave one player unchecked, because nobody is in a position to stop them. Our [expansion factions lesson](/academy/root/root-expansion-factions/) walks through reach in more depth.

## Recommended Root faction setups by player count

These mixes all clear the Law's reach target.

| Players | Mix | Reach | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Marquise, Eyrie | 17 | 17 |
| 3 | Marquise, Eyrie, Woodland Alliance | 20 | 18 |
| 3 (experienced) | Marquise, Woodland Alliance, Vagabond | 18 | 18 |
| 4 | All four base factions | 25 | 21 |
| 5 | Base four, plus Riverfolk Company | 30 | 25 |
| 6 | Base four, plus Riverfolk Company and Lizard Cult | 32 | 28 |

For two players, the base game suggests Marquise against Eyrie. The Law removes all four dominance cards at two players, and the Learning to Play guide suggests two games with factions swapped and scores added together. Our [lesson on two and three players](/academy/root/root-player-counts/) explains why each of these mixes works.

### A mix that fails the check

Say a four-player table wants the Underground Duchy, Corvid Conspiracy, Woodland Alliance, and Vagabond. That adds up to 19, short of 21. Swap the Corvids for the Eyrie and the total jumps to 23. The fix is almost always the same: make sure at least one faction at the table has the reach to police the others.

## The expansion factions explained

Each expansion adds factions plus other pieces, such as new maps, Vagabond characters, or hirelings. Every expansion needs the base game. Here is what each faction asks of you, in short.

### Riverfolk Expansion

[The Riverfolk Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07GJWQP7S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) adds two factions and the pieces to play with up to six.

- **Riverfolk Company:** otters who run a trading company. They sell services, like cards from their face-up hand or their warriors as mercenaries, and get paid in other players' warriors. They score from trade posts and from their funds. Great for a player who loves deals.
- **Lizard Cult:** a faith that builds gardens where it rules and acts mostly by revealing cards. When its warriors fall defending, they become acolytes that power its conspiracies. Many players find it one of the harder factions to figure out, and it can sit at low points for a long stretch before it moves.

### Underworld Expansion

[The Underworld Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084WYZXHF?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) adds two factions and the Lake and Mountain maps.

- **Underground Duchy:** moles who tunnel up from a private Burrow, build citadels and markets, and sway ministers for points and extra actions. Losing buildings costs them ministers, so they hate being raided.
- **Corvid Conspiracy:** crows who place face-down plots, such as bombs, snares, and extortion, and score as they flip them. Rivals can guess a plot's type to remove it. A faction for bluffers.

### Marauder Expansion

The Marauder Expansion adds two factions, hirelings, and the faction setup cards used for drafting.

- **Lord of the Hundreds:** a warlord with a hoard of items. They score each Evening for clearings they rule with no enemy pieces at all, and their mobs raze enemy buildings and tokens. One of the most openly aggressive factions in the game.
- **Keepers in Iron:** knights who dig relics out of the forests and carry them to matching waystations, scoring for each relic and each full set. A route-planning faction with a steady rhythm.

### Homeland Expansion

[The Homeland Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H92CY1JJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the newest box. It adds three factions and the Marsh and Gorge maps.

- **Lilypad Diaspora:** frogs who settle enclaves and add a new frog suit to the Woodland. Peaceful enclaves score, but attacks can turn them militant.
- **Twilight Council:** bats who hold assemblies, defend the factions attacked there, and score from governing assemblies. Politics on a board.
- **Knaves of the Deepwood:** a band of three rotating captains and a crew of skunks, who score from acclaim and from the prisoners they take. They play a little like a Vagabond with a gang. The two share pieces, so the rules do not let the Knaves and the Vagabond appear in the same game.

## Which Root faction should you play next?

Once your group knows the base four, pick your next faction by what you enjoyed.

| If you loved | Try next | Box |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marquise de Cat | Underground Duchy or Keepers in Iron | Underworld, Marauder |
| Eyrie Dynasties | Lord of the Hundreds | Marauder |
| Woodland Alliance | Lizard Cult or Lilypad Diaspora | Riverfolk, Homeland |
| Vagabond | Riverfolk Company or Knaves of the Deepwood | Riverfolk, Homeland |
| Table talk of any kind | Twilight Council or Corvid Conspiracy | Homeland, Underworld |

These are our pairings by feel, not official advice. Add one new faction per game, and seat it with the player most excited to read a new board. If your group is unsure which box to buy first, our guide to [when board game expansions are worth it](/articles/expansions-worth-it/) can help. Riverfolk is the usual first step because it is the box that takes you to five and six players.

## Is there a Root faction tier list?

Not an official one. Leder Games has never published a Root faction tier list, and the community ones you will find online disagree with each other. Be skeptical of any list that ranks thirteen factions with confidence, for three reasons.

**Root is a table game, not a solo race.** A faction's strength depends on who else is playing. The Woodland Alliance is far stronger at a table full of armies than at a quiet one. The Vagabond's value depends on who he can befriend and who he can fight. A tier list ranks factions in a vacuum, and Root never happens in one.

**The table balances itself.** In Root, the leader gets attacked. A faction that looks strong on paper often draws more fire, which is part of the design. Our article on [kingmaking in board games](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) covers why that pressure matters so much in games like this.

**Reach is the official tool, and it measures something else.** Reach tells you how much a faction pushes against the map, not how often it wins. The Marquise has the highest reach and is also the faction most often targeted.

### What competitive play suggests

Competitive Root uses the **Advanced Setup** in the Law, where players draft factions instead of choosing them. The draft locks the last insurgent faction until someone picks a militant one, the kind that can police the board, and the first player in turn order picks last. That tells you something: the designers treat turn order and faction choice as linked, and they care more about every table having a police force than about any single faction being the strongest.

Tournament formats also show which factions organizers trust. At the 2026 World Boardgaming Championships, the first heat used a fixed draft pool of the Eyrie, Marquise, Underground Duchy, Woodland Alliance, and Vagabond. That is a list of well-understood factions, not a ranking.

What you will hear from experienced players, again and again, is softer than a tier list. Some factions, like the Lizard Cult and the Corvid Conspiracy, ask a lot of the player and swing hard depending on the table. Others, like the Marquise and the Lord of the Hundreds, put immediate pressure on everyone. Treat that as a sense of difficulty, not a ranking of power. We have not found published win-rate data we could verify, so we are not going to invent any. Our [Advanced Setup lesson](/academy/root/root-advanced-setup-draft/) covers how to draft well.

## FAQ

### How many Root factions are there?

Thirteen: four in the base game, two each in the Riverfolk, Underworld, and Marauder expansions, and three in the Homeland expansion. The Vagabond can also appear twice in one game with the Riverfolk Expansion, which adds a second Vagabond pawn.

### Is the Root Vagabond good for beginners?

Often, yes, for a social player. The Vagabond is one pawn and a handful of items, so his board is small. The hard part is reading the other three factions to decide whom to help and whom to fight. Give him to the person at the table who most enjoys making deals.

### Why is the Woodland Alliance so hard to play?

Because it starts with nothing on the map and depends on others to fight near its sympathy. New players often wait too long or revolt too early. Spread sympathy where armies are moving, let Outrage fill your supporters, and save your revolt for a clearing full of enemy buildings.

### Can you play Root with two players?

Yes. The suggested two-player game is Marquise de Cat against the Eyrie Dynasties, with the four dominance cards removed. Play two games, swap factions, and add the scores together.

### Which Root expansion should I buy first?

Most groups start with the Riverfolk Expansion, because it adds two factions and lets you play with five or six. If your group sticks to four players and wants more conflict, the Marauder Expansion adds the Lord of the Hundreds and the faction setup cards used for drafting.

The right Root faction is the one that fits the person holding it. Start with the base four, match each to a player, and grow the table one faction at a time.
