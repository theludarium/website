The best travel board games are small enough to fit in a jacket pocket and simple enough to play on a tray table. For two travelers, pack [Hive Pocket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0079L0EKY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Jaipur](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SRMXRZB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Star Realms](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00HRGMPIU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a family or a group in a hotel room, pack [Sushi Go!](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57VU44?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Love Letter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PHHBWM9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Skull](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R2BPLSN?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Coup](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00GDI4HX4?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a team that likes working together, pack [Hanabi](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MXTGT21?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GP7X3P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Sprawlopolis](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DP3H8S4N?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And if you want something to write on, [Qwixx](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57138C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Cartographers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDNNLGR?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) are roll-and-writes that pack flat.

**The quick answer:** if you can only bring one, bring **Sushi Go!** for groups and families (a sturdy tin, 2 to 5 players, about 15 minutes) or **Hive Pocket** for two (no board, no cards to blow away, and pieces that survive anything).

Below are 13 picks sorted by who you are traveling with, plus a comparison table, a "which one to pack" guide, and an FAQ.

## What makes a good travel board game?

A game that is great on the dining table can be miserable on a plane. Before you pack, check five things:

1. **Box size.** A tin, a pouch, or a deck box beats a square board game box every time. If it does not fit in a carry-on side pocket, it probably stays home.
2. **Table footprint.** A tray table is roughly the size of a sheet of paper. Games that use a small row of cards or a tight cluster of pieces work. Games that spread a tableau across the table do not.
3. **Few loose parts.** Tokens roll under seats. The best travel games are mostly cards, or pieces big and chunky enough to find again.
4. **Quick setup and short rounds.** Shuffle and go. Short rounds also mean you can stop when the train pulls in.
5. **Durability.** Cards get bent and sticky in a backpack. A tin, a pouch, or solid pieces last longer. Card sleeves help any card game survive the trip.

If you mostly want a short game, not a portable one, our list of [quick board games under 30 minutes](/articles/quick-board-games-under-30-minutes/) is the better guide. This list is about what packs well.

## Best travel games for two

Two travelers on a tray table is the most common travel setup, and the best served. These four are built for exactly two players.

### Hive Pocket

Designed by John Yianni and published by Gen42 Games, Hive plays 2 in about 20 minutes. The [pocket edition](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0079L0EKY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) uses smaller versions of the original solid tiles, comes in a drawstring bag, and includes the Mosquito and Ladybug expansion pieces. Each tile is a bug that moves in its own way. Surround your opponent's queen bee to win.

**Why it fits:** there is no board. The tiles are the board, so the game grows only as big as the pieces themselves, and it works on any flat surface, from a tray table to a picnic blanket. The pieces are heavy and nearly impossible to damage. Your opponent's queen is the only thing that gets hurt. If you are new to it, the rulebook suggests learning without the two expansion bugs first.

### Jaipur

Designed by Sébastien Pauchon and first published in 2009, [Jaipur](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SRMXRZB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 in about 30 minutes. You are rival merchants. You take cards from a shared market of five and sell sets of goods for tokens. Sell early for the best tokens, or wait for a bigger set and a bonus.

**Why it fits:** the market is a single row of five cards, so it fits on most small tables. It does have goods tokens, so play it where a dropped token will not vanish. It is one of the best games for couples; see our guides to [two-player games for date night](/articles/two-player-board-games-date-night/) and [board games for couples](/articles/board-games-for-couples/) for more.

### Lost Cities

Designed by Reiner Knizia and published by Kosmos in 1999, [Lost Cities](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00T0GY9CK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 in about 30 minutes. You fund expeditions in five colors by laying cards in rising order. Start an expedition and it costs you points until it pays off, so each card is a small bet.

**Why it fits:** the base game is really just a deck of cards. The small board is there to hold discards, and you can play without it. The rules take two minutes to teach. Our guide to [Reiner Knizia games](/articles/reiner-knizia-games/) covers it and several more small-box classics from the same designer.

### Star Realms

Designed by Robert Dougherty and Darwin Kastle and published by White Wizard Games in 2014, [Star Realms](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00HRGMPIU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 in about 20 minutes. You start with a weak deck, buy ships and bases from a shared trade row, and attack your opponent until their authority hits zero.

**Why it fits:** the whole game is one deck of cards in a small box. It packs more strategy and more direct conflict than anything else in this section. Authority is tracked on cards, though a phone or a scrap of paper is easier. If you like the deck-building idea, see our list of the [best deck-building games](/articles/best-deck-building-games/).

## Best travel games for groups and families

A hotel room, a cabin, or an airport gate with a few hours to kill. These pack small and stretch to bigger groups.

### Sushi Go!

Designed by Phil Walker-Harding and published by Gamewright in 2013, [Sushi Go!](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57VU44?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 5 in about 15 minutes. You pick one card from your hand, pass the rest to the left, and try to build the best set of sushi dishes over three rounds.

**Why it fits:** it comes in a sturdy tin that survives a backpack, it uses only cards, and kids and adults play it on equal terms. Each player keeps a small row of cards in front of them, so it suits a hotel bed or a kitchen counter better than a tray table. Learn it with [how to play Sushi Go](/academy/sushi-go/how-to-play-sushi-go/), or see the whole free [Sushi Go course](/academy/sushi-go/).

### Love Letter

Designed by Seiji Kanai and first published in Japan in 2012, [Love Letter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PHHBWM9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 6 in the current edition, in about 20 minutes. Each player holds one card. On your turn you draw a second and play one, using its power to guess, peek, or knock someone out of the round.

**Why it fits:** a hand is one or two cards, so it needs almost no table at all. Rounds take a few minutes, which makes it ideal for a wait at the gate. It is a fine first game for people new to modern games, and it shows up often in our guide to [adult card games](/articles/adult-card-games/).

### Skull

Designed by Hervé Marly and first published in 2011, [Skull](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R2BPLSN?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 6 in about 30 minutes. Each player has four round cards: three roses and one skull. You stack cards face down, then bid on how many roses you can flip without finding a skull.

**Why it fits:** there is nothing to lose and nothing to read. The game lives in people's faces, so it works in a hotel room, around a campfire, or on a restaurant table. The cards are thick and coaster-like, which helps them survive travel. If your group loves bluffing, our list of [social deduction games](/articles/best-social-deduction-games/) has more.

### Coup

Designed by Rikki Tahta and first published in 2012, [Coup](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00GDI4HX4?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 6 in about 15 minutes. You hold two hidden character cards and can claim to be any character to take its action. Anyone can call your bluff. Lose a challenge and you lose a card.

**Why it fits:** a small deck, some coins, and a reference card. Games are fast and loud, which is great in a rental house and less great on a quiet train. Players can be knocked out, but rounds are short enough that they are back in quickly.

## Best co-op travel games

For a team that would rather win together. Card co-ops are perfect for travel because they are quiet by design: the rules limit what you can say.

### Hanabi

Designed by Antoine Bauza and first published in 2010, [Hanabi](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MXTGT21?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2013 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 5 in about 25 minutes. You hold your cards facing out, so everyone can see your hand but you. Together you try to play cards in order to build a fireworks show, using limited clues.

**Why it fits:** it is a small deck plus a few clue tokens, and it plays in a row down the middle of the table. It is calm and quiet, which makes it one of the few group games that works on a train. Our list of the [best co-op card games](/articles/best-co-op-card-games/) compares it with others.

### The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine

Designed by Thomas Sing and published by Kosmos in 2019, [The Crew](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GP7X3P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2020 Kennerspiel des Jahres. It plays 3 to 5, with a variant for two, in about 20 minutes per mission. It is a cooperative trick-taking game: the team must win certain tricks with certain players, and you can barely talk about your cards.

**Why it fits:** the box holds a deck, a few tokens, and a logbook of 50 missions, which is a whole trip's worth of play. Missions take minutes, so you can stop at any point. Read our full guide to [The Crew card game](/articles/the-crew-card-game/), or learn it in the free [Crew course](/academy/the-crew/), starting with [how to play The Crew](/academy/the-crew/how-to-play-the-crew/).

### Sprawlopolis

Designed by Steven Aramini, Danny Devine, and Paul Kluka and published by Button Shy, [Sprawlopolis](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DP3H8S4N?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in about 15 minutes. The whole game is 18 cards in a small wallet. You work together to build one city out of overlapping cards, scoring for three goals that change each game.

**Why it fits:** it may be the smallest game on this list, and it plays solo too. The city does spread out, so it wants a bit more room than a tray table. For more games you can play alone on a long trip, see our list of the [best solo board games](/articles/best-solo-board-games/).

## Best roll-and-writes for travel

Roll-and-writes pack flat: a few dice or cards, a pad of score sheets, and a pencil. Bring your own pencils, and a small dice tray if you can.

### Qwixx

Designed by Steffen Benndorf and first published in 2012, [Qwixx](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57138C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 5 in about 15 minutes. Roll six dice. Everyone can use the white dice on every roll, and the active player can also combine white and colored dice, crossing off numbers in four colored rows.

**Why it fits:** nobody waits for a turn, the box is tiny, and anyone who has played Yahtzee already knows half the game. Dice are the one weak spot for travel, so roll into the box lid. See our guide to [roll-and-write games](/articles/roll-and-write-games/) for the rest of the family.

### Cartographers

Designed by Jordy Adan and published by Thunderworks Games in 2019, [Cartographers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDNNLGR?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 100 in 30 to 45 minutes. A card is flipped each turn showing a shape and a terrain type, and every player draws it onto their own map, chasing four scoring goals.

**Why it fits:** the player count is limited only by score sheets, so it works for a family reunion or a whole tour bus. It uses cards instead of dice, which means nothing rolls off the table. Each player needs a pencil and a clipboard or a book to write on.

## FAQ

### What is the best board game to play on a plane?

Hive Pocket for two players. It has no board and no cards, and it fits on one tray table. Love Letter and Lost Cities are good card options if you would rather keep things flat.

### What travel games work for a whole family?

Sushi Go! is the easiest pick: a tin, simple rules, and 2 to 5 players. Cartographers and Qwixx work for larger families, since everyone plays at the same time on their own sheet.

### How do I keep card games in good shape while traveling?

Sleeve the cards, keep the box in a zip bag, and pack it where it will not get crushed. A tin or a sturdy deck box beats a thin cardboard tuck box.

### Are there travel games for long-distance friends?

Yes. Many of these games have apps or online versions. Our guide to [playing board games with long-distance friends](/articles/playing-board-games-with-long-distance-friends/) covers how to keep a weekly game going when you cannot share a table.

### What should I pack for a gaming convention?

A small travel game is great for the lines and the hotel. Our [Gen Con packing list](/articles/gen-con-packing-list/) covers the rest.
