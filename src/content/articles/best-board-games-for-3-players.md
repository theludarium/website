The best board games for three players are the ones where nobody can gang up and nobody ends up as the third wheel. For an easy evening, play [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For engine builders that are at their best with three, play [Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Everdell](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09ZWQTTNR?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Lost Ruins of Arnak](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08CD3JVQB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Terraforming Mars](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GSYA4K2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). To put all three of you on the same side, play [Pandemic](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A2HD40E?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or [The Crew](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GP7X3P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And if your trio wants a real fight, [Dune: Imperium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08JHB9V72?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Root](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F454YF3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Arcs](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D856FJ1J?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) are built to handle it.

**The quick answer:** if you often play with exactly three, start with **Wingspan**. It polls best at three on BoardGameGeek, it is easy to teach, and nobody can attack anybody. For a shorter, lighter game, **Splendor** is the pick.

Below are 12 games that are genuinely good at three, sorted by the kind of evening you want, with a look at why three is a tricky number in the first place.

## Why is three players tricky?

Lots of games say "2 to 4" or "3 to 5" on the box. Far fewer are at their best with three. Our chart of [board game player counts](/articles/board-game-player-counts/) shows how often the community's favorite number differs from the box. At three, a few problems show up again and again:

1. **Two against one.** In a game where you attack each other, two players can team up on the third, on purpose or not. One person spends the evening under fire.
2. **Kingmaking.** When one player falls behind, their choices often decide which of the other two wins. With only three people, that choice is very visible. Our article on [kingmaking](/articles/kingmaking-board-games/) explains why it happens and how designers fight it.
3. **The third wheel in trading.** In trading games, two players can strike a deal that leaves the third with nothing to offer. Catan has this problem, and it also leaves too much open land at three.
4. **A map built for four.** Some boards simply feel empty with three. Ticket to Ride closes its double routes at two or three players, which helps, but not every game adjusts.

The games on this list dodge these problems in one of three ways. Some let each player build their own thing, so there is little to gang up on. Some put everyone on one team. And a few conflict games are built with balancing tools, such as shared enemies or "beat the leader" scoring, that keep three-way fights fair. Designers think about this a lot; see our piece on [player count design](/articles/player-count-design/).

## Easy games for three

These three are quick to teach and calm to play. They work as well for a family as for three friends after dinner.

### Splendor

Designed by Marc André and published by Space Cowboys in 2014, Splendor plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes. You take gem chips and spend them on development cards, and each card you own becomes a permanent discount on future buys.

**Why it fits:** [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a race, not a fight. You can reserve a card someone else wants, but you cannot take anything away from them. At three, the game uses five chips of each gem and four nobles, so the supply is tight enough to feel tense without anyone getting locked out. Learn it with [how to play Splendor](/academy/splendor/how-to-play-splendor/) and our [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/).

### Azul

Designed by Michael Kiesling and published by Plan B Games in 2017, Azul won the 2018 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. You draft colored tiles from shared factory displays and place them on your board to build a mosaic wall.

**Why it fits:** Azul does have a bite. Taking tiles can stick another player with tiles they cannot use. But with three, that spite spreads around the table instead of landing on one person. Three players use seven factories, which keeps the drafting crowded and interesting. [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is also beautiful to look at. Learn it with [how to play Azul](/academy/azul/how-to-play-azul/), then read our [Azul strategy guide](/articles/azul-strategy/).

### Cascadia

Designed by Randy Flynn and published by Flatout Games and AEG in 2021, Cascadia won the 2022 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. Each turn you take a habitat tile and a wildlife token from a shared offer and add them to your own landscape.

**Why it fits:** every player builds their own map, so nobody can attack anyone. The only interaction is taking the pair someone else wanted, which is friendly enough for any trio. Each player gets the same 20 turns, so the game feels even. [Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the calmest pick here. Learn it with [how to play Cascadia](/academy/cascadia/how-to-play-cascadia/).

## Engine builders that shine at three

Engine builders are the sweet spot for three players. You spend most of your time on your own board, so a third player adds competition for cards and spaces without adding much waiting. Our explainer on [engine-building games](/articles/engine-building-games/) covers why this style is so satisfying.

### Wingspan

Designed by Elizabeth Hargrave and published by Stonemaier Games in 2019, Wingspan won the 2019 Kennerspiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 5 in 40 to 70 minutes. You attract birds to your wildlife preserve, and each bird adds a power to its habitat row, so your turns get stronger as the game goes on.

**Why it fits:** [Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) polls best at three on BoardGameGeek. At two it can feel a little quiet; at five, the wait between turns gets long. Three is just right. There is almost no way to hurt another player, so it is a good pick for a trio that includes someone new to games. Learn it with [how to play Wingspan](/academy/wingspan/how-to-play-wingspan/).

### Everdell

Designed by James A. Wilson and published by Starling Games in 2018, Everdell plays 1 to 4 in 40 to 80 minutes. You send woodland critters out to gather resources and build a city of 15 cards, playing constructions and creatures that combo with each other.

**Why it fits:** some of Everdell's worker spots hold only one worker. At four, those spots get crowded fast. At three, there is still a race for the best spots, but nobody gets shut out of the game. [Everdell](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09ZWQTTNR?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is also one of the most charming boxes on any shelf. We compared it with Wingspan in [Wingspan vs Everdell](/articles/wingspan-vs-everdell/). Learn it with [how to play Everdell](/academy/everdell/how-to-play-everdell/).

### Lost Ruins of Arnak

Designed by Michaela "Mín" Štachová and Michal "Elwen" Štach and published by Czech Games Edition in 2020, Lost Ruins of Arnak plays 1 to 4 in 30 to 120 minutes. You explore an island, building a deck of cards and sending archaeologists to dig sites, fight guardians, and climb a research track.

**Why it fits:** [Lost Ruins of Arnak](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08CD3JVQB?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) mixes deck-building with worker placement, and it polls best at three. With three explorers, the board fills up enough to matter without anyone being blocked for a whole round. It is a smooth next step for a trio that has outgrown Splendor. Learn it with [how to play Lost Ruins of Arnak](/academy/lost-ruins-of-arnak/how-to-play-lost-ruins-of-arnak/).

### Terraforming Mars

Designed by Jacob Fryxelius and published by FryxGames in 2016, Terraforming Mars plays 1 to 5 in about two hours. You run a corporation working to make Mars livable, playing project cards to raise the temperature, the oxygen, and the oceans.

**Why it fits:** this is a long game, and every extra player adds time. At three, it stays a long evening rather than a very long one. There is some interaction, such as placing tiles next to each other and a few attack cards, but it is spread across three players, so nobody gets singled out. Save [Terraforming Mars](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GSYA4K2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) for a trio that likes big strategy games. Learn it with [how to play Terraforming Mars](/academy/terraforming-mars/how-to-play-terraforming-mars/).

## Everyone on the same side

The simplest cure for two against one is to make it three against the game. Our list of the [best cooperative board games](/articles/best-cooperative-board-games/) has more.

### Pandemic

Designed by Matt Leacock and published by Z-Man Games in 2008, Pandemic plays 2 to 4 in about 45 minutes. You are a team of specialists racing to cure four diseases before outbreaks spread out of control.

**Why it fits:** at three, each player has a distinct role, and there is enough time on each turn to talk through a plan together. Three is also small enough that one confident player rarely takes over. If that does happen, our guide to [the quarterback problem](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/) has fixes. [Pandemic](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A2HD40E?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a great first co-op. Learn it with the free [Pandemic course](/academy/pandemic/).

### The Crew

Designed by Thomas Sing and published by Kosmos in 2019, The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine won the 2020 Kennerspiel des Jahres. It plays 3 to 5 in about 20 minutes, with a two-player variant. It is a cooperative trick-taking game, like hearts or spades, played over a campaign of 50 missions that get harder as you go.

**Why it fits:** three is the base count for [The Crew](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GP7X3P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). Each player holds 13 or 14 cards, which gives you lots of control, and the rulebook includes a harder "Challenge for Three" once it starts to feel easy. Our lesson on [The Crew's player counts](/academy/the-crew/the-crew-player-counts/) covers it, and our [guide to The Crew](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) explains why it is so loved.

## Conflict games that handle three

These are for trios who want to push each other around. Each one has tools that keep the third player from getting crushed.

### Dune: Imperium

Designed by Paul Dennen and published by Dire Wolf in 2020, Dune: Imperium plays 1 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. You build a deck of cards and use it to send agents to board spaces, then fight over a conflict each round for rewards.

**Why it fits:** [Dune: Imperium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08JHB9V72?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) polls best at three, edging out four. The fighting happens in a shared conflict each round, not in attacks aimed at one player, so two people cannot easily team up on the third. Every round is a fresh scramble. Learn it with [how to play Dune: Imperium](/academy/dune-imperium/how-to-play-dune-imperium/).

### Root

Designed by Cole Wehrle and published by Leder Games in 2018, Root plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 90 minutes. Each player controls a different woodland faction with its own rules: the industrious Marquise de Cat, the proud Eyrie Dynasties, the rebel Woodland Alliance, and the wandering Vagabond.

**Why it fits:** because every faction plays differently, there are no natural pairs to team up. Root also rewards the table for keeping the leader in check, which softens the kingmaking problem. The usual three-player setup is the Marquise, the Eyrie, and the Alliance. Our lesson on [Root player counts](/academy/root/root-player-counts/) covers which mixes work best, and [Root table talk and kingmaking](/academy/root/root-table-talk-kingmaking/) covers the politics. [Root](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F454YF3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a good fit for a trio that likes to talk.

### Arcs

Designed by Cole Wehrle and first published by Leder Games in 2024, Arcs plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. You lead a space empire through a few chapters of play, using a hand of cards to move ships, build, and fight, and you choose which goals, called ambitions, will score each chapter.

**Why it fits:** at three, [Arcs](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D856FJ1J?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) uses a smaller deck and a map with fewer clusters open, so space is tight and every card counts. The ambition system lets a trailing player change what scores, which gives everyone a path back. One warning from our lesson holds for many three-player games: watch the quiet player who builds while the other two fight. Our lesson on [Arcs at 2, 3, and 4 players](/academy/arcs/arcs-player-counts-expansions/) explains the differences.

## FAQ

### What is the best board game for 3 players?

Wingspan is the safest pick. It polls best at three, it is easy to teach, and there is no way to gang up on anyone. For something lighter, Splendor; for a co-op, Pandemic or The Crew.

### Why are some games bad with three players?

Mostly because of two against one and kingmaking. In games with direct attacks or trading, two players can team up on the third, or a trailing player can hand the win to someone. Games with low interaction, shared goals, or a cooperative design avoid this. See our guide to [catch-up mechanics](/articles/catch-up-mechanics-board-games/) for how designers keep trailing players in the game.

### Is Catan good with three players?

It works, but it is not at its best. Three players leaves a lot of open land and makes trading lopsided, since a deal between two people can leave the third out. Most groups prefer Catan at four.

### What are good three-player games for adults?

Terraforming Mars, Dune: Imperium, Root, and Arcs are deeper strategy games that work well at three. Wingspan and Lost Ruins of Arnak are a good middle ground.

### What three-player games are good for families?

Splendor, Azul, and Cascadia are all friendly, short, and easy to teach. Pandemic is a good choice if the family would rather win or lose together. Our list of [best family board games](/articles/best-family-board-games/) has more.

### What if a fourth player shows up?

Every game on this list plays four, and most of them play it well. If the group grows past that, our guide to [games for six players](/articles/best-board-games-for-6-players/) has picks for bigger tables.
