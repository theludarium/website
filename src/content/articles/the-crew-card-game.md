The Crew card game is a cooperative trick-taking game for three to five players in which you cannot talk about your cards. Each mission gives the team specific tricks to win, with specific cards, sometimes in a specific order. You have one tightly rationed way to communicate. That is all.

We think it is the best teaching game ever made, and not just for teaching card games. It teaches how a group learns to work together, in twenty-minute lessons, with nobody lecturing. Here is why.

## How does The Crew work?

If you have played Hearts, Spades, or Euchre, you know the bones. Cards are dealt out. One player leads a card; everyone else must follow suit if they can; the highest card of the led suit wins the trick, unless a trump card was played. In The Crew, the trump suit is a small set of rocket cards (or submarine cards, in Mission Deep Sea).

The cooperative twist is the mission. Before play, task cards are dealt out, and each one says a specific player must win the trick containing a specific card. Mission one might be "someone must win the green 4." Mission twenty might be "these three cards must be won in this order by these three players, and nobody may win more tricks than the captain."

If every task is completed, the team wins and moves to the next mission. If any task fails, you reshuffle and try that mission again.

The communication rule is the genius. Once per mission, each player may place one card from their hand face up and mark it with a token showing whether it is their highest, lowest, or only card of that suit. That is your whole vocabulary. No hints, no groans, no "are you sure?"

## Why is The Crew such a good teaching game?

Because it makes the lesson impossible to skip.

In most co-ops, an experienced player can carry the table by talking. In The Crew, there is nothing to say. Each player must read the table, infer what their teammates are holding, and make a choice that only they can make. The quarterback problem, which we covered in our [article on co-ops without a quarterback](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/), does not exist here. The rules killed it.

That means every player learns, every mission. They learn to:

- **Count cards.** If the pink 9 has been played, the pink 8 is now the highest pink. Everyone figures this out by mission five.
- **Signal with actions, not words.** Leading a low card says "I cannot win this; someone else must." Leading a high card says "I have this."
- **Read intent.** When a teammate uses their one communication token on a mid-value card, they are telling you something specific. Working out what is the game.
- **Fail without blame.** Missions fail constantly, and the failure is usually a shared misread. You reshuffle and try again, thirty seconds later.

That last point matters most. The Crew gives a group dozens of tiny failures and recoveries in an hour. By the end, people are reading each other's plays the way a longtime team reads a colleague's email. Nobody taught them. The missions did.

## What does The Crew teach about communication?

The limited signal forces precision. You get one card and one token. Which card you show, and when, carries all the meaning. Players quickly learn that a signal given too early is wasted and one given too late is useless.

Then there is the discipline of not talking. The rules forbid discussing your hand, and the table enforces it. People who are used to narrating every thought find this uncomfortable for about two missions. Then they discover that listening to the cards is faster than listening to each other.

We have watched groups of coworkers play it and recognize their own meeting habits by mission ten. The person who always announces the plan has to sit on their hands. The person who never speaks up discovers they are the one holding the critical card. ## Which version of The Crew should you buy?

There are two main versions, and both are good.

| | The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine | The Crew: Mission Deep Sea |
|---|---|---|
| Released | 2019 | 2021 |
| Missions | 50 | 32, with variable difficulty |
| Task system | Each mission sets how many task cards to draw | Task cards with difficulty values; the mission sets a total |
| Theme | Space | Underwater |
| Players | 3 to 5 (2 with a variant) | 3 to 5 (2 with a variant) |
| Best for | First-timers; a gentle ramp | Replay and variety; groups who finished the first |

Mission Deep Sea is the more flexible design, because the difficulty of each mission depends on which task cards come out. The same mission number plays differently every time. The Quest for Planet Nine has a steadier ramp and a slightly simpler teach.

If you are buying one, Mission Deep Sea. If you fall in love, buy both. Mission Deep Sea carries a [suggested price of about $15](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2147934610/Crew-The---Mission-Deep-Sea) at the time of writing, roughly a nice lunch, which makes The Crew one of the best values in the hobby.

## How do you teach The Crew to new players?

In this order, and it takes about four minutes.

1. **The goal.** "We win if everyone completes their task. We all win or all lose."
2. **A trick.** Deal a practice hand and play one trick with cards face up. Follow suit, highest wins, rockets are trump.
3. **Tasks.** Show a task card and explain who has to win it.
4. **The communication token.** Demonstrate once. Then say, "That is the only talking about cards we do."
5. **Play mission one.** It is designed as a tutorial.

Do not explain the advanced task tokens, the captain rules, or anything from later missions. The game introduces them when they arrive. For more on this sequence, our piece on [how to teach a board game in five minutes](/articles/how-to-teach-a-board-game/) is built on the same structure.

## Who is The Crew not for?

Players who hate silence. People who need to narrate their thinking will find the rules frustrating, at least at first. Give them three missions. Most convert.

It also plays best at four. Three is fine and five is good, but four hands of the deck create the cleanest deductions. The two-player variant works, with a dummy hand, but it is a different and more puzzly game.

## FAQ

### Is The Crew a good game for beginners?

Yes. It teaches in under five minutes, the first missions are tutorials, and you can stop after any mission.

### How long does The Crew take to play?

A single mission takes five to fifteen minutes. Most groups play several in a sitting and treat an hour as a session.

### Can you play The Crew with two players?

Both editions include a two-player variant using a dummy hand. It works, but the game is best at three to five.

### What is the difference between The Crew: Quest for Planet Nine and Mission Deep Sea?

Planet Nine has fifty fixed missions with a steady difficulty ramp. Mission Deep Sea uses task cards with difficulty values, so the same mission plays differently each time.
