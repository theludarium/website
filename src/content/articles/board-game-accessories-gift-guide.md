Board game accessories are the gift category hobbyists quietly want and rarely buy. A gamer will spend $60 on a new box without blinking and then play it for three years with cards that are getting soft at the corners and components in sandwich bags. The upgrade costs $15 and they keep not doing it.

This guide covers the four categories that get used every week, what to buy in each, and what to skip. Prices are approximate and were checked at retailer pages at the time of writing.

## Which board game accessories actually get used?

We have watched a lot of accessories arrive at tables. The ones that stay out are the ones that make setup faster or play nicer, every single time. The ones that go in a closet are decorative.

| Category | Gets used | Approximate price | Need to know their games? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card sleeves | Every play | $3 to $13 per 100 | Yes |
| Box inserts | Every play | $15 to $30 | Yes |
| Playmat | Every play | $30 to $60 | No |
| Dice tray | Every dice game | $7 to $60 | No |
| Metal coins | Some games | $24 to $35 | Yes |
| Token trays and bowls | Setup only | $10 to $25 | No |
| Game-themed decor | Never | Any | No |

The right-hand column matters. If you do not know what games they play, buy from the rows marked "No." If you do, the rows marked "Yes" are the better gifts.

## Card sleeves: the gift that sounds boring and is not

Sleeves are clear plastic pockets that protect cards from shuffling wear and spilled drinks. For a game that gets played weekly, they are the difference between cards that last two years and cards that last twenty.

**What to buy.** **Dragon Shield Matte** <!-- AFFILIATE --> is the standard for standard-size cards (63.5 by 88 millimeters, the size of a poker card). At the time of writing a 100-count pack runs about $8 to $13 depending on retailer; [Stomping Grounds lists them at $8](https://www.stompinggroundstcg.com/products/dragon-shield-game-sleeves-matte) against a market price of around $12.49. For board games with unusual card sizes, **Sleeve Kings** <!-- AFFILIATE --> and **Mayday Games** make sleeves in dozens of dimensions; [Miniature Market lists a Sleeve Kings 110-pack at $2.49](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/sks-8840.html) at the time of writing.

**How to get the size right.** Look up the game on BoardGameGeek. Nearly every game page lists card dimensions and sleeve counts in the user-maintained files. Buy ten percent more than the count.

**What to skip.** Ultra-thin "penny" sleeves. They are cheap and they feel cheap. Spend the extra $5.

## Box inserts: the gift for a game they love

An insert replaces the loose cardboard tray in a game box with molded compartments. Setup drops from ten minutes of sorting baggies to one minute of lifting trays. For a game played often, it is the single biggest quality-of-life improvement available.

**What to buy.** **Folded Space** <!-- AFFILIATE --> makes foam-core inserts for hundreds of games, typically $15 to $30. [Their Wingspan insert at Miniature Market is $15.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/fdswingv2.html) at the time of writing. They ship flat and take about an hour to glue together, which some people find pleasant and some do not. **The Broken Token** and **Game Trayz** <!-- AFFILIATE --> make wooden and plastic alternatives at higher prices; [The Broken Token's Great Western Trail organizer is $58.99](https://thebrokentoken.com/products/game-organizer-compatible-with-great-western-trail) at the time of writing.

**What you need to know.** The exact game and, for games with expansions, which expansions they own. Inserts are built for specific box contents.

**What to skip.** Generic "universal" inserts. They fit nothing well.

## Playmats: the gift when you know nothing

A neoprene playmat is the same material as a mousepad, cut large. Cards slide and lift easily, dice stop bouncing, and the table underneath stays unscratched. It works for every game, which makes it the safe accessory gift.

**What to buy.** A plain mat in a dark neutral color, 36 by 36 inches, or 24 by 48 for a narrower table. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Expect about $30 to $60 at the time of writing depending on size and whether it has stitched edges; as reference points, [Inked Gaming's 28-by-14 custom mat is $39.99](https://inkedgaming.com/products/custom-oversize-playmat) with stitched edges a $5 add-on, and [Miniature Market lists a 3-by-3-foot neoprene-backed mat at $33.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/battle-systems-gaming-mat-grassy-fields-3x3-bstxx002.html). Stitched edges last longer. Skip printed designs unless you know their taste; a plain mat goes with every game and every room.

**For RPG players.** A **dry-erase battle mat** <!-- AFFILIATE --> with a one-inch grid is the equivalent gift. Chessex's vinyl mats have been the standard for decades and run about $20 to $30; [Miniature Market lists the 23.5-by-26-inch reversible Battlemat at $22.99](https://miniaturemarket.com/reversible-battlemat-1-inch-square-blackgrey-235-x-26-chx96480.html) at the time of writing.

## Dice trays and towers: for anyone who rolls

Dice go off tables. A tray stops that, and it quiets the roll, which people with downstairs neighbors appreciate.

**What to buy.** A folding faux-leather or felt-lined tray costs about $7 to $17 and snaps flat for travel. <!-- AFFILIATE --> [Ultra Pro's magnetic folding tray at Miniature Market is $15.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/vivid-dice-tray-magnetic-foldable-black-upr16332.html) and [a basic PU-leather folding tray at Walmart was under $10](https://www.walmart.com/ip/294564045) at the time of writing. A dice tower, where dice tumble through baffles and out the bottom, runs anywhere from about $20 for acrylic to $60 or more for resin or hardwood at the time of writing ([a clear acrylic tower at Miniature Market is $19.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/engtadt100clew.html); [a polyresin dragon tower at Noble Knight is $59.95](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2148113631/Old-School-Dice---Dragons-Fury-Dice-Tower---Green)) and is more of a showpiece. For a D&D player, a tray is better; for someone who runs games, a tower looks good at the head of the table.

**Pair it with dice.** A tray plus a set of nice polyhedral dice (about $4 for [a basic Chessex set](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/chessex-dice-set-opaque-pastel-blueblack-7-chx25466.html) to about $23 for [a metal set](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/mystery-misfit-metal-polyhedral-dice-set-7-md9201.html) at Miniature Market at the time of writing) is a complete gift under $50. Our piece on [D&D dice explained](/articles/dnd-dice-explained/) covers what the seven dice are for, if the recipient is new.

## Metal coins and upgraded components

Many games ship with cardboard coins. Publishers and third parties sell metal replacements that feel better in the hand and make a satisfying clink.

**What to buy.** Only a set made for a specific game they own and play often. Common targets include Scythe and Viticulture, whose publisher sells its own sets. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Prices run about $24 to $35 at the time of writing: Stonemaier's [80-coin Scythe set is $29](https://store.stonemaiergames.com/products/80-metal-coins-expeditions-and-scythe) and its [Viticulture lira set is $24 at Miniature Market](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/stm304.html). Brass: Birmingham's official [Iron Clays poker chips](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/rox505.html) are the deluxe version of this idea at $89.99.

**What to skip.** Games that already have good components (Splendor's poker-chip tokens are the usual example), and generic "fantasy coin" sets with no game in mind. The latter end up in a drawer.

## Small useful things under $15

- **Token bowls.** A set of small ceramic or wooden bowls for sorting pieces during setup. Any kitchen store sells them. Under $15.
- **A good mechanical pencil and a pad of score sheets.** Every gamer is still using a golf pencil from mini golf.
- **A microfiber cloth and soft brush.** For cleaning dust from components and crumbs from boxes.
- **Rubber bands and small zip bags.** Dull and used constantly. Buy the good ones.
- **A card shuffler.** Controversial. Some gamers love them; many think they damage cards. Ask first.

## What not to give

- **Anything "gamer-themed" that is not for playing.** Meeple earrings, dice-pattern socks, a mug that says "roll initiative." They have these. We have written a whole article on [gifts for the board gamer who already owns everything](/articles/gifts-for-board-game-lovers/) because of this problem.
- **Storage furniture without asking.** The [Kallax shelf from IKEA](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/p/kallax-shelf-unit-white-70301537/) is the hobby standard (the 4-by-4 unit is $249.99 at the time of writing), but people are particular about their shelves.
- **Component upgrades for a game they have not played in a year.** The upgrade will not bring them back to it.

## Why an accessory is a better gift than a game

A new game is a decision you are making for them. An accessory for a game they already love is an acknowledgment of a decision they already made. It says: I noticed what you play, and I want it to be a little nicer every time.

That is the whole trick. Make the table nicer and people sit at it longer. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What card sleeves are best for board games?

Dragon Shield Matte for standard poker-size cards, about $8 to $13 per hundred at the time of writing. For odd sizes, Sleeve Kings or Mayday Games, which make sleeves in dozens of dimensions.

### Are board game inserts worth it?

For a game played often, yes. A Folded Space insert costs $15 to $30 and cuts setup from ten minutes to one. For a game played twice a year, no.

### What size playmat should I buy for board games?

36 by 36 inches suits most square tables and most games. Go to 24 by 48 or larger only if you know the table it will live on.

### What is a good board game accessory gift under $20?

A pack of Dragon Shield sleeves, a folding dice tray, or a set of token bowls. All are under $20 and used every game night.
