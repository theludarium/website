A list of the best board games of 2026 should answer one question: which new releases are worth a place on a table that already has Codenames, Wingspan, and Ticket to Ride on it? The bar is high. Hundreds of games come out every year, and most of them are fine. A handful change what a game night looks like.

This is a running list. We update it as the year goes on and as our own plays pile up. Before the picks, here is how we judge, so you can argue with us.

## How do we pick the best board games of a year?

We do not score on novelty, art, or buzz. We score on what happens at a table of real people, most of whom did not read the rulebook. Five tests:

1. The teach. Can one person explain it in under ten minutes to people who have never seen it? Under five is better.
2. The first play. Did a new player make at least one decision that felt clever, without being told what to do?
3. The second play. Did anyone ask for it? A game that nobody requests again is not a great game, however good the design.
4. The table. Did people talk, laugh, or lean in? Or did four people silently solve four puzzles?
5. The shelf test. Three months later, does it still come out? Most releases fail here.

A game has to pass all five to make this list. Several very good 2026 releases passed four.

## What counts as a 2026 release?

For this list, a game counts if it reached general retail in 2026, even if crowdfunding backers received copies late in 2025. Reprints and new editions of old games do not count. Expansions count only if they change the base game enough that we would recommend them to a first-time buyer.

We also do not include games we have played fewer than three times. That rules out some well-reviewed titles we simply have not gotten to the table enough. They may appear in a later update.

## What is the bar? Recent games that set it

To make the standard concrete, here are games from the last few years that passed all five tests and that we would still put in front of a new group tonight. New releases are measured against these.

| Game | Players | Time | Why it set the bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cascadia | 1–4 | 30–45 min | Three-minute teach, deep puzzle, no conflict |
| The Crew | 2–5 | 20 min | Cooperative trick-taking that teaches itself |
| Sky Team | 2 | 15–20 min | Two players land a plane, barely speaking |
| Wavelength | 2–12 | 30 min | A party game adults argue about for an hour |
| Heat: Pedal to the Metal | 1–6 | 45–60 min | Racing with hand management, loud and close |
| Ark Nova | 1–4 | 90–150 min | A heavy game that still earns the second play |

## The best board games of 2026 so far

| Game | Players | Time | Why it made the list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dino Racer | 2–6 | 10–20 min | A ten-minute race that gets a whole table shouting |
| JinxO | 3–7 | 30 min | Word-matching party game, Spiel des Jahres 2026 |
| Cozy Stickerville | 1–6 | 30–60 min | A legacy game that teaches itself with stickers |
| Flip 7: With a Vengeance | 3+ | 20 min | Push-your-luck with a mean streak |
| Wingspan Pocket | 1–5 | 25–35 min | Wingspan in a small box and half an hour |
| The Two Towers Trick-Taking Game | 1–4 | 20 min | Cooperative trick-taking with a story |
| Duel of Meloch | 1–2 | 45 min | A tight two-player duel in the Scythe world |

### Dino Racer

[Dino Racer](https://dicetower.com/board-game/462945) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is Marceline Leiman's card-driven racing game from Underdog Games: bet on dinosaurs, push them around a short track, shout. A race takes ten minutes, the teach under three, and upsets are constant. Wargamer gave it a [perfect score](https://www.wargamer.com/dino-racer/board-game-review). The easiest opener of the year.

### JinxO

[JinxO](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/en-GB/boardgame/price/y5ByU6gzru/jinxo) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is Martin Ang's word-association party game: fill a three-by-three grid with answers to a category, score when they match. Published in Indonesia in 2024, it reached Europe as Dito! and won the [2026 Spiel des Jahres](https://boardgamewire.com/index.php/2026/07/13/jinxo-wins-2026-spiel-des-jahres-rebirth-secures-historic-triple-for-reiner-knizia/). It passes the second-play test almost every time, because the fun is learning how your friends think.

### Cozy Stickerville

[Cozy Stickerville](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3671496/review-cozy-stickerville) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is Corey Konieczka's cooperative legacy game from Unexpected Games, released in February: build a village with more than 800 stickers over a short campaign. A Spiel des Jahres nominee. The teach happens as you play, the clearest pass of test one this year. Best for families and couples.

### Flip 7: With a Vengeance

[Flip 7: With a Vengeance](https://theop.games/products/flip-7-with-a-vengeance) <!-- AFFILIATE --> from The Op is a standalone follow-up to the push-your-luck hit, adding action cards that steal, force draws, and cut scores. It trades innocence for interaction, which is what groups who found Flip 7 too polite wanted. The teach is still under five minutes.

### Wingspan Pocket

[Wingspan Pocket](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/wingspan/wingspan-pocket/) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is Elizabeth Hargrave's compact take on her own engine builder, a twenty-dollar box released by Stonemaier in July. It is not Wingspan and does not try to be; it is the game you bring when half the table is new and you have half an hour. It passed the shelf test fastest of anything here.

### The Two Towers Trick-Taking Game

[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers Trick-Taking Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/452808/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-trick-taking) <!-- AFFILIATE --> continues the cooperative trick-taking series that began with the Fellowship game, released in January and playable standalone. Chapter scenarios run about twenty minutes each. If your group liked The Crew, this is the next box.

### Duel of Meloch

[Duel of Meloch](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/duel-of-meloch/) <!-- AFFILIATE --> is Jamey Stegmaier's standalone two-player game in the Scythe world, released in August: five-minute setup, a small map, a race to four stars. The heaviest game here and the one that most clearly earned the second play, tense and short enough to run back. For couples who have worn out Sky Team.

## Which 2026 trends are we seeing at the table?

A few patterns show up in what publishers are releasing and what groups are asking for.

- Short cooperative games keep growing. The success of The Crew and Sky Team opened a lane for 20-minute co-ops, and more designers are using it.
- Nature and landscape themes have not slowed. Wingspan, Cascadia, and Everdell proved the audience, and we expect more games about forests, oceans, and gardens. See our comparison of [Wingspan, Everdell, and Cascadia](/articles/wingspan-vs-everdell-vs-cascadia/).
- Smaller boxes. Publishers are putting serious games in boxes that fit in a bag, partly because shelf space and shipping both cost more than they used to.
- Roll-and-write and flip-and-write formats remain a reliable source of good fillers.
- Heavier games are getting better at the teach. Designers are shipping tutorial scenarios and learn-as-you-play decks, which used to be rare.

## How should you use a "best of the year" list?

Carefully. The games that are good in March are not always the games people are still playing in December. In our experience, about half of any source's "best of the year" picks are still on the table two years later. The other half were good but not durable.

A few habits help:

- Wait for the second wave of opinions. The first reviews come from people who played a prototype or a review copy once. The useful signal arrives when ordinary groups have played it five times.
- Check it against your own shelf. If you own Wingspan, do you need another engine builder? Maybe. But a new game that fills a hole on your shelf, a good two-player game or a party game that works at ten, is worth more than a better version of something you already have.
- Try before you buy where you can. A game lounge or cafe lets you play a new release for the price of a coffee. That is one reason we are building one in Indianapolis. The library model is the subject of [the library problem](/articles/board-game-cafe-library/).

## FAQ

### What is the best board game of 2026?

Dino Racer, for a mixed table: it teaches in minutes, finishes in ten, and gets requested again. JinxO is the pick if your group prefers words to dinosaurs.

### How many new board games come out each year?

BoardGameGeek's database grew from [about 80,000 entries in 2019 to more than 150,000 by 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoardGameGeek), thousands of new listings a year, though far fewer reach wide retail.

### Should I buy new board games or classics?

Classics first. A shelf with Codenames, Ticket to Ride, Pandemic, and Azul covers most groups. Add new releases once the classics are getting regular play.

### How do you judge the best board games of 2026?

Five tests: a short teach, a clever first play, a requested second play, a lively table, and whether it still comes out three months later. A game has to pass all five.
