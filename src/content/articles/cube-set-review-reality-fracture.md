Reality Fracture released on October 2, 2026 as the third main-line Standard set of the year, and this Reality Fracture cube review was the most fun to write in a while. The set is built around planeswalkers, graveyard play, and "echoed" versions of famous cards in new colors. For cube owners that means cheap walkers to evaluate, a real phoenix, a two-mana red creature that recasts your burn, and reprints of Chandra, Torch of Defiance and Tarmogoyf. Calls follow [the slot method](/articles/how-to-evaluate-new-cards-for-cube/).

Set facts from the [Wizards collecting article](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-reality-fracture): tabletop release October 2, 2026, 270 main-set cards plus ten common dual lands, set code FRA, with a ten-card Special Guests sheet (SPG) in the same boosters. Card text below was confirmed against Card Kingdom and mtg.wtf listings; rules are paraphrased.

## What does the set do, in one paragraph?

Three mechanics matter for cube. **Prepare** attaches a spell to a creature; while the creature is prepared you may cast a copy of that spell once, which unprepares it. **Empower Jace** builds a non-legendary Jace planeswalker token with a surveil and a draw ability. **Heartwood** is a red-green mana token. Prepare is the one that produces cube cards, because it puts a known spell on a body. The Jace token mechanic is parasitic. Heartwood is Limited glue.

## Which planeswalkers are worth a slot?

The set's headline is cheap walkers, so this is where the slot comparisons get crowded.

- **The Theorist, Jace Beleren** ({2}{U}{U}, loyalty 3). You draw a card at the start of each opponent's draw step. +1 makes a 1/1 Illusion, −2 bounces an artifact or creature per opponent, −6 draws three, then puts +1/+1 counters on your creatures equal to your hand size. **Slot:** blue / 4 / card advantage. **Competes with:** Jace, the Mind Sculptor; Shark Typhoon; Mulldrifter. **Call: test** at Legacy and Vintage power. The static draw is the whole card; the token protects it and the −2 resets the board. Not a Mind Sculptor, but a fine fifth or sixth blue four-drop.
- **Chandra, Chill of Compliance** ({1}{U}{U}, loyalty 3). +1 surveils and puts a noncreature, nonland hit into your hand; +1 adds {U} for noncreature spells; −X taps and stuns; −6 is a draw-per-spell emblem. **Slot:** blue / 3 / tempo walker. **Competes with:** Narset, Parter of Veils. **Call: test.** Three-mana blue walkers that defend themselves are scarce; the stun is the defense.
- **Ajani Resolute** ({1}{W}, loyalty 2). Gains loyalty when you gain life; 0 gains 1; −4 makes an Ajani's Pridemate token. **Call: pass** unless your cube has a lifegain lane; then test it as a payoff.
- **Ajani Unrelenting** ({4}{R}{R}, loyalty 5). Makes a 2/2 whenever you activate a loyalty ability; +1 gives +1/+0 and haste; −3 deals 4 to each creature except your own tokens. **Call: pass.** Six-mana red walkers compete with Inferno Titan and lose.
- **Garruk, Curse Breaker** ({3}{G}{G}, loyalty 5). Draws when a power-4-or-more creature enters; +2 untaps two lands; −3 makes a 4/4 trampler. **Slot:** green / 5 / value walker. **Competes with:** Garruk Wildspeaker, Nissa, Who Shakes the World. **Call: test.** Garruk's Uprising on a beast factory. Strong in a green ramp lane; a pass where green is mostly mana elves.
- **Garruk, Veiled Butcher** ({3}{B}{B}, loyalty 5). Exiles opponents' dying creatures; +2 gives −4/−1; −2 makes each player sacrifice a creature, and if you did, you get a 4/4 trampler; −3 is a two-card discard. **Slot:** black / 5 / removal walker. **Competes with:** Liliana, Death's Majesty; Ob Nixilis Reignited. **Call: test.** The +2 kills any one-toughness creature and takes four power off anything else until your next turn. We like this one more than the green Garruk.

## Which creatures earn a test?

The two-drop slot got the most interesting cards in the set.

- **Stingcaster Mage** ({1}{R}, 2/1 haste; on entering, an instant or sorcery in your graveyard gains flashback for its mana cost). **Slot:** red / 2 / aggressive creature. **Call: keep** at Legacy power, **test** at Vintage. Haste and two power on turn two, and later a Lightning Bolt or Chain Lightning back from the yard. Red spell-heavy aggro gets a real card.
- **Darklight Phoenix** ({3}{B}, 3/2 flying, haste; at the start of your combat, if two or more creatures died this turn, returns from your graveyard). **Slot:** black / 4 / recursive threat. **Call: test.** Black aristocrats and sacrifice decks want this. It is worse than Arclight Phoenix in isolation but black has fewer recursive fliers.
- **Enlightened Confidant** ({1}{W}, 2/1 lifelink; at your end step, if you gained life, surveil 1 and may take a cheap card). **Slot:** white / 2 / value creature. **Call: test** if your cube has a white lifegain or aggro lane; otherwise **pass**. The lifelink means it turns itself on when it connects.
- **Liliana the Faultless** ({W}, 1/1; gain 1 life when another creature or walker enters; {1}, tap, discard: another creature or walker gains hexproof). **Slot:** white / 1 / utility. **Call: test.** The [Riptide Lab cube forum](https://riptidelab.com/forum/posts/140864/) liked this more than we expected: a one-mana protection piece that feeds lifegain. Below Mother of Runes and Giver of Runes.
- **Liliana the Repentant** ({1}{B}, 2/2; mills two whenever another creature or walker enters; exhaust {5}{B} reanimates). **Slot:** black / 2 / graveyard enabler. **Call: pass** unless you run a dedicated reanimator lane; then test.
- **Seasoned Cryomancer** ({1}{U}{U}, 2/2; enters, draw two discard two, stun that many creatures; later exile from graveyard to draw two). **Slot:** blue / 3 / value. **Call: test.** A lot of text that all works.
- **Sphinx of False Conclusions** ({2}{U}{U}, 4/2 flash flying; loots on attack; leaves a copy when it dies). **Slot:** blue / 4 / tempo finisher. **Call: test.** Two bodies and a looter for four.
- **Samut, Hazoret's Champion** ({1}{R}, 2/2; your creatures have haste). **Slot:** red / 2 / aggro lord. **Call: test.** Haste for the team on a two-drop body is pure aggro support, and aggro always needs more.
- **Pompous Battlemage** ({R}, 1/1 prowess; enters prepared with Improvised Act, a one-mana rummage). **Call: pass.** Close, but Monastery Swiftspear and Soul-Scar Mage hold the slot.
- **Bloodline Recollector** ({1}{B}, 2/2; becomes prepared with Ancestral Craving, "target player draws three and loses 3 life," when three creatures died in a turn). **Call: pass.** Three deaths in a turn is rare in a 40-card deck.

## What about the spells and the big stuff?

- **Prophesied End** ({1}{W}, instant: destroy target creature; if it was not attacking, its controller draws). **Slot:** white / 2 / removal. **Call: test.** Two-mana unconditional white removal is good; the drawback is small on defense. Compare Fateful Absence.
- **Vraska's Final Mercy** ({B}{B}, sorcery: lose 2 life, destroy a creature or planeswalker). **Call: test** as the eighth or ninth black removal spell. Sorcery speed and BB keep it below Go for the Throat.
- **Overwrite the Multiverse** ({4}{B}{B}, sorcery: exile all creatures). **Call: test** at any power. A six-mana black exile sweeper is a real card for control; the Jace rider is free.
- **Frostbite Pyromental** ({U}{R}{R}, 4/4 trample haste; draw two on combat damage; sacrificed at end step). **Slot:** gold / UR / 3. **Call: test** as a Ball Lightning that draws. Gold slots are tight, so it has to beat the other Izzet card, not just be good.
- **Hexhaven Invigorator** ({G}{G}{G}{G}, 6/6 vigilance; fetch lands equal to damage dealt to it). **Call: pass.** GGGG on turn four is not a cube cost.
- **Emrakul, the Exigent Doom** ({10}, 12/12 flying trample; untaps your lands when cast; ward of three permanents). **Call: pass** unless you run Sneak Attack targets; even then, Emrakul, the Aeons Torn is better.
- **The Echoverse Fulcrum** ({2} artifact: loot on entering; {5}, tap, exile: destroy all creatures). **Call: test** in unpowered cubes as a colorless sweeper any deck can run.

## Which reprints matter?

**Chandra, Torch of Defiance** ({2}{R}{R}) is already in most Legacy and Vintage lists; the reprint lowers the price and changes nothing else. **Tarmogoyf** is the same: cheaper, and in cube it was already a fine green two-drop rather than a bomb. The five slow lands (Deserted Beach and friends) are useful budget fixing; see [cube mana fixing](/articles/cube-mana-fixing/). Splinter Twin also returns in this release, but on the Special Guests sheet rather than in the main set, per the [Reality Fracture release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/reality-fracture-release-notes). Confirm which sheet a reprint is on before counting it as a main-set card.

## FAQ

### Is Reality Fracture a good set for cube?

Yes, better than average. The cheap planeswalkers and the two-drop creatures give unpowered cubes real choices, and the reprints lower the cost of two staples.

### Which Reality Fracture card is the safest cube add?

Stingcaster Mage. A hasty two-drop that replays a burn spell fits any red aggro lane and needs no support beyond cheap spells.

### Do the empower Jace cards work in cube?

Mostly no. The Jace token needs several empower cards to add up, and a 40-card deck will not see them. Treat the empower text as blank and judge the rest of the card.

### Is Tarmogoyf good in cube now?

It is a fine green two-drop, usually a 3/4 or 4/5 by the midgame. It is not a reason to change your list, but the reprint makes it cheap to own.
