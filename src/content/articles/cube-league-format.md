A cube league is what you add when a weekly draft is healthy and you want it to have a shape longer than one night. The danger is obvious. Most league formats are tournaments stretched over weeks, and a tournament rewards the person who never misses. In a group of adults with jobs and kids, that person is the one with the fewest other commitments, and a league that crowns them every season is a league the rest of the room quietly stops caring about.

Cube Commonwealth ran weekly for about three years in Indianapolis with 12 to 16 players, and we tried a few league structures along the way. This is the one that worked, written as rules you can copy, and the reasoning behind each rule.

## What should a cube league do, and not do?

Do:

- Give regulars a reason to think about the night between nights.
- Make an individual match matter a little more without making it tense.
- Produce a story at the end of a season: who had the run, who drafted the deck everyone remembers.
- Reward showing up without punishing not showing up.

Do not:

- Create a standings board people check mid-night. We learned why in [pairings, records, and prizes](/articles/draft-pairings-and-records/).
- Make the first-timer's result count against anyone, including the first-timer.
- Require anyone to track anything during play.
- Give the best player a trophy every season. If the format does that, the format is just a ladder.

## How does the format work?

Here are the rules in full. A season is eight weeks; adjust to taste.

**1. Every night is a normal draft.** Nothing about the draft, build, or rounds changes. The league is a layer on top of results that already exist.

**2. Points per night, not per match.** You earn points for the night as a whole: 3 for a 3-0, 2 for a 2-1, 1 for a 1-2, and 1 for showing up and going 0-3. Everyone who drafts earns at least one. A 3-0 is worth three times a bad night, not infinitely more.

**3. Your season score is your best five nights out of eight.** This is the rule that does the work. Miss three weeks and you lose nothing. Miss four and you lose one night's points, which is a small gap, not a disqualification. Someone who attends all eight has more chances at a 3-0 but no guaranteed edge over someone who came five times and drafted well.

**4. One bonus point per season for a "deck of the night" win.** Voted by the room, not the host, and it goes to the deck people talked about, not the one that won. Capped at one so it stays a treat.

**5. First-timers earn points but are not ranked until their third night.** Their results count for their opponents as normal. Their own total is hidden until they are a regular. This protects a new player from seeing themselves at the bottom of a list on their first visit.

**6. Standings are posted once a week, the day after, in the results channel.** Never during the night. Never on a whiteboard.

**7. Season end: a small, silly prize for the top score and an equal prize for the most nights attended.** If the same person wins both, they pick one and the other goes to second place. Two trophies for two different virtues. <!-- EDITOR: add detail -->

That is the whole thing. It takes the host two minutes after each night to update a spreadsheet.

## Why best-five-of-eight?

Because it is the smallest change that fixes the attendance problem, and we tested the alternatives.

| Scoring approach | What it does to attendance | What it does to the mood |
|---|---|---|
| Total points, all weeks count | Rewards the person with the emptiest calendar | Missing a week feels like falling behind; some people stop coming once they are behind |
| Average points per night attended | Rewards drafting once, going 3-0, and never returning | Rewards hiding |
| Best N of M nights | Rewards showing up often enough to get your best nights in | Missing a week is a shrug; coming back is still worth it |
| No league at all | Neutral | Fine, but no season story |

Best-N-of-M sits in the right place. There is a reason to come (more nights, more chances at a good one) and no penalty for not coming. Five of eight means attending about 60 percent of the season gives you a full score. That is roughly what a committed adult with a life can manage.

## Does a league change how people draft?

A little, and mostly in the right direction, as long as the points are small.

What we saw: regulars thought about the cube more between nights, which fed the change log in [the community cube](/articles/community-owned-cube/). People tried archetypes they had been avoiding, because one bad night was cheap and a surprising 3-0 was worth three. The deck-of-the-night bonus pulled people toward interesting decks instead of safe ones.

What we watched for: anyone treating a round-three match as a must-win. If a league ever produces an argument over a rules call that would not have happened on a normal night, the points are too large. Our answer was to leave the scoring alone and make the prize smaller. The prize for a season should be something you would laugh about, not something you would drive across town for.

## How do you handle two pods?

The points are per night, so pods do not matter. A 3-0 in pod A and a 3-0 in pod B both earn three points. On a two-pod night, up to two people can take the maximum. Nobody minds. The league rides on top of whatever pairing system the night already uses.

For uneven pods, where someone draws a bye, a bye counts as a win for points. Nobody should lose a point for being the odd number.

## What about a new player who shows up mid-season?

Welcome them, seat them well, and do not mention the league until they ask. Their first three nights earn points in the background. By the time they see their name on the list, they have been to the night three times and have a feel for what the list means and does not mean.

If someone's first night is week seven of eight, their season is two nights long, and that is fine. The next season starts in two weeks, and the "best five" rule means they will be on equal footing from the first night of it.

## When should you not run a league?

When the group is under eight regulars. A league with six people ranked is a list of six people, and the bottom of a six-person list is a lonely place. Build the core first; we wrote the attendance side up in [how to get eight players to show up](/articles/how-to-get-eight-players-for-a-draft/). Add a season once the night is sturdy enough that the league is a decoration on it and not a prop holding it up.

When we relaunch Cube Commonwealth under The Ludarium, the league comes back in the second season, not the first. The first season's job is to get the weekly night back to a reliable eight. The league's job is to give that night a story. In that order.

## FAQ

### How does a casual cube league work?

Every draft night earns points based on your record, your season total is your best five nights out of eight, and standings are posted once a week after the night, never during it.

### How do you run a league where missing a week does not hurt?

Count only each player's best N nights out of M. A player who misses several weeks loses nothing as long as they attend enough nights to fill their best-N slots.

### Should a cube league have prizes?

Small and silly. One for the top score and an equal one for most nights attended, so the season rewards both drafting well and showing up.

### Do new players count in a cube league?

Their results count for their opponents from night one, but their own ranking stays hidden until their third visit.
