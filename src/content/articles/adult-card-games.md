Cards Against Humanity gets to a lot of tables because it is easy to buy, easy to explain, and reliably gets a laugh in round one. The problem is round four. The jokes are printed on the cards, so the game runs out of surprises fast. The best adult card games put the humor and the tension in the players instead, which means they stay good for years.

This list has twelve of them. Some are party games, some are bluffing games, some are quiet and clever. All of them are cards, all of them are built for adults, and none of them rely on a shock-value deck to carry the night.

## What's the actual problem with Cards Against Humanity?

It is not the crude content. Adults can handle crude. The design problem is that the player's only decision is "which of these ten prewritten jokes is funniest," and the funniest card usually wins regardless of who played it. Nobody is being clever. The deck is.

Compare that to a game like Codenames or Skull, where the funny moments come from what a specific person did at a specific table. Those moments get retold. "Remember when you clued 'Nixon' for three words" lasts a decade. A card from a box does not.

A good adult card game needs three things:

- The laughs or the tension come from players, not the cards.
- A real decision each turn.
- Replay value past the tenth game.

## The best adult card games

| Monikers | 4–16 | 30–60 min | Celebrity with a curated deck and three rounds |

### Codenames

Two spymasters try to connect their team's words with a single clue. <!-- AFFILIATE --> The humor comes from the clue-giver's mind, not a punchline. A good clue is remembered for years; a bad one is remembered longer. It handles the same crowd as Cards Against Humanity, in the same fifteen minutes.

### Skull

Each player has three flower coasters and one skull. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Stack them face down, then bid on how many flowers you can flip across the table without hitting a skull. You lose a coaster each time you fail. The game is nothing but reading faces, and it is the best bar game ever boxed.

### Coup

Two hidden role cards each, and any player can claim any role on their turn. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Call the bluff and you might be right; be wrong and you lose a card. It takes fifteen minutes and ends with everyone certain they were robbed.

### Monikers

A better Celebrity, with a deck that mixes real people, fictional characters, and internet nonsense. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Round one, describe freely. Round two, one word. Round three, charades. The same cards return, so the jokes compound. It scales to sixteen.

### The Crew

Cooperative trick-taking across a series of missions. <!-- AFFILIATE --> You can barely communicate, and the whole table has to make specific players win specific tricks. If your group grew up on Euchre or Spades, this is the next step. We covered it in depth in [The Crew: the best teaching game](/articles/the-crew-card-game/).

### Love Letter

Sixteen cards, each a character with a power. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Draw, play, deduce who holds what. A round takes three minutes, and the whole thing fits in a velvet bag.

### Decrypto

Two teams, each with four secret keywords. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Encode a three-digit sequence with clues your teammates will get and the other team will not. Every round, the opponents learn more. It rewards groups who want Codenames with higher stakes.

### Hanabi

You hold your hand facing out, so everyone sees your cards but you. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Teammates spend limited clue tokens to tell you what you hold, and together you try to play fireworks in order. It is a game about trusting that your friends are paying attention. Sometimes they are not, and that is funny too.

### Sushi Go Party!

The bigger-box version of Sushi Go!, playing up to eight. <!-- AFFILIATE --> Pass cards, collect sets, and change the menu each game so it stays fresh. It is the gentlest game on this list and still has real decisions.

### Cockroach Poker

Pass a card face down to another player and name a creature. <!-- AFFILIATE --> They either accept your claim or call you a liar. Wrong guesses pile up in front of you until someone has too many of one critter. It is a lying game with no strategy beyond knowing your friends.

### The Mind

Everyone holds numbered cards, and the table must play them in ascending order without talking or signaling. <!-- AFFILIATE --> It sounds impossible. Then a group clicks and plays a perfect level in silence, and nobody can explain how. It is twenty minutes of strange shared focus.

### Wavelength

Technically a board game, but the card deck of spectrums is the heart of it. <!-- AFFILIATE --> "Bad habit to good habit": where does coffee land? The argument is the point.

## How do you move a group off Cards Against Humanity?

Do not announce that you are replacing it. Just bring Codenames and set it up first. Nine groups out of ten will not ask for the black box again.

If the group likes the crude energy specifically, go to Skull or Coup. Both reward bluffing and get louder with drinks, and both are more fun by the third round than CAH is by the first.

If the group likes the "fill in the blank" structure, try Monikers. It keeps the shouting and adds skill.

If someone insists on CAH, play one round, then switch. One round is where it peaks anyway. For more on running a big table, see [party games for large groups](/articles/party-games-for-large-groups/).

## What about classic card games?

A standard deck still holds up. Euchre, Hearts, Spades, Oh Hell, and Cribbage are all adult card games by any definition, and they cost nothing. The downside is that they need a table that already knows them, and teaching Bridge to a new player is an evening in itself.

The games above take the ideas from the classics, trick-taking, bluffing, set collection, and package them so a new player can join in five minutes. That is the real innovation of the last twenty years of card design. Not new ideas. Faster doors.

## FAQ

### What is the best card game for adults instead of Cards Against Humanity?

Codenames for a crowd, Skull for a bar, Coup for a loud table of friends. All three teach in two minutes and stay funny for years.

### Are there adult card games that are not party games?

Yes. The Crew, Hanabi, Love Letter, and The Mind are all quiet, clever card games for two to five players.

### What card games work for a large group of adults?

Monikers handles up to sixteen. Codenames and Decrypto both handle eight or more in teams. Sushi Go Party! plays eight.

### Is Cards Against Humanity bad?

It is a fine first-round icebreaker. The complaint is that the jokes are on the cards, not in the players, so it loses its edge quickly. The games above fix that.
