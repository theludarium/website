A campaign board game missed session is the most common way a campaign dies. Not boredom, not a bad scenario, but one person who cannot make it and a group that does not know what to do. So nobody plays, and the next date slips, and the box goes on a shelf.

This article is a policy, not a pep talk. Decide these things before the first session and a missed night becomes an inconvenience instead of an ending.

## Why do campaign games die from absence?

Three reasons, and they compound.

First, campaign games carry state. A legacy game has a story in progress. [Gloomhaven](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01LZXVN4P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) has characters with personal decks and goals. Playing without someone feels like it violates something, so the default is to wait.

Second, waiting resets momentum. A group that meets every two weeks has a rhythm. Skip one and it is four weeks between sessions. Skip two and people forget the rules, the plot, and why they cared.

Third, nobody wants to be the one who says "let's play without her." It sounds disloyal. So the question goes unasked and the campaign quietly stops.

The fix for all three is the same: agree on the rules of absence before anyone is absent.

## What should your group decide before session one?

Have this conversation at the table, out loud, when the box is still sealed.

1. **The quorum.** How many players must be present to play? For a four-person group, three is a sensible minimum. For three, two.
2. **The default.** If quorum is met, do you play or wait? We recommend play. Make absence the exception that needs a reason, not the trigger for cancellation.
3. **The stand-in rule.** Who runs the missing player's character, if anyone? Options below.
4. **The decision rule.** If the story asks the group to make a permanent choice while someone is away, do you make it or defer it? Deferring is usually right for big forks; playing on is right for small ones.
5. **The catch-up method.** Who tells the absent player what happened, and how? Two minutes of recap at the next session, or a short message that night.

Write the answers on an index card and keep it in the box. It sounds fussy. It works.

## How do you handle the missing player's character?

This depends on the game. The main approaches, from most to least common.

| Approach | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | The character sits out; the game is scaled to fewer players | Gloomhaven, most co-ops with player-count scaling |
| Pilot | A present player runs the absent character as well as their own | [Pandemic Legacy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TQ5SEAI?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), lighter campaigns |
| Pause the character | Character stays put; others play a side scenario that does not advance the main plot | Games with optional or side content |
| Guest | A substitute plays a temporary character | Games with pre-built or drop-in characters |
| One-shot night | Skip the campaign; play something else | Any group |

The "rest" approach is the cleanest for Gloomhaven and its relatives, because the game's difficulty scales with player count. The absent character simply did not go on that job. They earn nothing and lose nothing, and in some groups they get a share of the loot as a courtesy.

The "pilot" approach suits games where roles are simpler and public. In Pandemic Legacy, someone can run the Medic alongside their own character without much trouble. Agree that the pilot plays conservatively; nobody wants to come back and find their character was sacrificed.

The "one-shot night" is underrated. The point of the group is the group. If the campaign cannot move, play [The Crew](https://link.amazon/B099S9RKf) or [Codenames](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B014Q1XX9S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and keep the habit alive. ## How do you catch someone up without boring everyone?

Short and specific. A good recap covers:

- What happened in the scenario, in two sentences.
- Any permanent changes to the world, board, or roster.
- Any decisions the group made and why.
- Anything the absent player's character now needs to do.

Assign the recap to one person, rotate it, and keep it under three minutes. The worst version is five people talking over each other for fifteen minutes while the absent player nods politely.

For legacy games with sealed content, be careful. Tell them what was opened, not what is inside, if they have a chance to see it themselves next time. The surprise is part of what they paid for.

## What if the same person keeps missing?

This is the harder conversation, and it is a people conversation, not a games one.

Someone who misses one in four sessions is a normal adult with a life. Someone who misses three in four is telling you something. Maybe the night does not work for them. Maybe the game does not. Maybe life changed. Ask, kindly and directly, outside the session.

Options that keep the friendship intact:

- Move the night. A different weekday may fix everything.
- Shorten the session. Two hours is easier to protect than four.
- Let them step back to a guest role, dropping in when they can with a simpler character.
- Finish the campaign without them, with their blessing, and plan the next game around their schedule.

What does not work is quietly resenting them while the campaign stalls. The campaign ends either way; the resentment lasts longer.

## How do you choose campaigns that tolerate absence?

If your group knows attendance will be uneven, pick accordingly.

Games where the party is scaled per scenario, like Gloomhaven and Jaws of the Lion, handle a missing player well. Games with short, self-contained sessions, like Pandemic Legacy, are easy to pilot through. Games with persistent, personal story arcs for each character are the hardest; a missed session there means missed story.

Our [guide to legacy games](/articles/legacy-board-games-explained/) notes which titles have shorter arcs, and the general rule is: the lighter the campaign, the more forgiving it is. A group with four busy adults might be happier with a twelve-session legacy game than a two-year epic, and that is a fine choice.

## FAQ

### Should you play a campaign board game without a missing player?

Usually yes, if you meet the quorum you agreed on in advance. Waiting kills more campaigns than playing on does.

### How do you handle a missing player in Gloomhaven?

Let their character rest. The game scales to the number of players present, so the party simply runs the scenario with fewer mercenaries.

### Can someone else play my character in Pandemic Legacy?

Yes, and it works well, because roles are public and simple. Agree that the pilot plays conservatively with your character.

### How often should a campaign group meet?

Every two weeks is a sustainable rhythm for most adults. Weekly is better for momentum but harder to keep; monthly is often too slow to remember the story.
