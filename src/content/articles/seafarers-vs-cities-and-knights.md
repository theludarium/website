Buy [Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) first if your group plays Catan a few times a year and wants more of the same game with new maps. Buy [Cities & Knights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) first if your group already plays [Catan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) often, finds the base game easy, and wants more control over the dice. Seafarers changes the board. Cities & Knights changes the game. Both need the base game, both play 3 to 4 out of the box, and the official rules let you combine them once you own both.

**The quick answer:** most groups should start with **Seafarers**. It teaches in minutes and keeps every turn familiar. Pick **Cities & Knights** first only if everyone at the table already knows Catan well and wants a longer, deeper evening.

This guide compares the two head to head. For a ranking of every Catan expansion, including the 5-6 boxes and the spin-offs, see [the best Catan expansions](/articles/best-catan-expansions/).

## What to look for when choosing

Three questions decide it for most tables:

1. **How well does your group know the base game?** Seafarers adds a few rules on top of Catan. Cities & Knights adds a new turn structure, a third die, new cards, and a threat that targets the whole table.
2. **How long is your game night?** Catan lists Seafarers at 60 to 120 minutes and Cities & Knights at 90 to 120 minutes.
3. **What frustrates your group about Catan?** If the answer is "it feels the same every time," Seafarers fixes that. If the answer is "the dice decided the game," Cities & Knights fixes that.

## What Seafarers adds

### Ships, islands, and scenarios

[Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) puts water between pieces of land. Ships cost one wood and one wool and work like roads at sea: they connect to your settlements and other ships, and they count toward Longest Route. You may also move one ship per turn if one end of it is open, which lets you redirect a route instead of rebuilding it. Gold fields produce one resource of your choice, and a pirate on the sea lets the player who rolls a 7 rob ships instead of land.

The bigger change is the scenarios. Each one sets its own map, its own special rules, and its own victory condition. In the first, Heading for New Shores, you earn bonus points for settling small islands and play to 14. Later scenarios add cloth trading, a pirate fleet, or a race to build a wonder. Our [Seafarers scenarios ranked](/articles/catan-seafarers-scenarios-ranked/) guide walks through all nine.

**Why it suits most groups:** a turn is still roll, trade, build. The new choice, "ship or road?", is easy to grasp and fun to argue about. Anyone who can play Catan can play Seafarers after a short explanation, which our lesson on [how to teach Seafarers](/academy/catan-seafarers/how-to-teach-seafarers/) shows how to do in ten minutes.

**Where it falls short:** it does not reduce luck much. Bad dice still hurt, and on a few scenarios an early lead at sea can snowball.

## What Cities & Knights adds

### Knights, barbarians, commodities, and progress cards

[Cities & Knights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) keeps the base map but rewires almost everything around it. The game runs to 13 points, and your second starting building is a city. A third die, the event die, moves a barbarian ship toward Catan and triggers card draws. When the barbarians arrive, their strength equals the number of cities on the board. If the active knights of all players together are weaker, the player or players who contributed the least lose a city. If the knights hold, the top defender earns a victory point.

Cities on forest, pasture, and mountains now produce commodities (paper, cloth, and coin) alongside a resource. You spend commodities on city improvements in three tracks: science, trade, and politics. Climbing a track improves your odds of drawing progress cards, gives a permanent ability at level 3, and a chance at a metropolis worth 2 extra points. Development cards and Largest Army are gone, and the robber stays put until the first barbarian attack.

**Why experienced groups love it:** it gives you far more ways to act on a bad roll. Progress cards, commodity trades, and the science track's level 3 ability (a free resource when you collect nothing) all soften the dice. The barbarians also force a table-wide question every few turns: who is defending, and who is getting a free ride? That makes it a deeper game of reading the table, the skill our base course covers in [threat assessment](/academy/catan/threat-assessment/).

**Where it falls short:** it is a real teach, it runs longer, and a new player can lose a city to the barbarians before they understand why. Our lesson on [what changes from base Catan](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-vs-base-catan/) is the best primer before a first game.

## Complexity and play time

Seafarers adds rules you can explain in a few minutes, but its later scenarios each bring a page of their own. The difficulty grows with the campaign, so a group can start easy and go as deep as it wants.

Cities & Knights adds everything at once. There is no gentle version. A group that is fluent in the base game will settle in within a game or two; a group that still looks up the building costs will struggle. If you want a quick test of readiness, our lesson on [the four core strategies](/academy/catan/four-core-strategies/) in the free Catan course is a good check: if your group can talk about those plans, it is ready.

Both boxes list a top end of 120 minutes. Cities & Knights lists 90 minutes at the low end, compared with 60 for Seafarers. First games of either will run longer than the box says.

## Player counts

Both expansions play 3 to 4 on their own. For five or six, you need the base [5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1LYDT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plus that expansion's own 5-6 box: the [Seafarers 5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1QR6W?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or the [Cities & Knights 5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87CXSQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). That is four boxes in total for either one. The course lessons on [Seafarers player counts](/academy/catan-seafarers/seafarers-player-counts-and-combinations/) and [Cities & Knights player counts](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/cities-and-knights-player-counts/) explain what changes at the bigger counts.

With three players, both work. Seafarers maps give three players plenty of room, and each scenario has its own 3 player setup. In Cities & Knights, each player's knights are a bigger share of the defense at three, so a player who skips defending is easier to spot.

## Seafarers and Cities & Knights together

Yes, you can play them together, and the rules come straight from the sixth edition Cities & Knights rulebook, which has a section on combining with Seafarers. Catan's own expansion pages say the same. The short version:

- **Choose the right scenario.** The rulebook recommends Heading for New Shores and Through the Desert. It warns against scenarios that reveal hidden parts of the board, such as The Fog Islands, or that spread players across many small islands, such as The Four Islands, because they make the barbarians noticeably stronger.
- **Ships follow road rules.** Any Cities & Knights rule that applies to roads also applies to ships.
- **The pirate waits.** It starts on the last space of the barbarian track and only enters play after the first barbarian attack, the same way the robber waits.
- **The barbarians hit every island at once.** All cities and all knights on the board count toward the two strengths.
- **Gold fields stay gold.** They produce resources, never commodities, and the merchant may not be placed on them.
- **Knights travel by sea.** A knight may move along your connected roads and ships, and may even stop on an intersection surrounded by sea. You may not move a ship if that would cut a knight off from your routes.

**What it is like:** you get the open map of Seafarers and the planning of Cities & Knights in one evening. It is the biggest version of Catan most groups will ever set up, and the longest. It suits a group that already enjoys both expansions separately. It is not the way to learn either one. ## What about the other expansions?

Two other big boxes come up in this decision, and neither is the usual first buy.

**[Traders & Barbarians](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF7552C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** is a collection of smaller scenarios and variants, including official rules for two players. It is the pick for couples, not for a first big change.

**[Explorers & Pirates](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF8J5C8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20)** is the most adventurous box, with a map that grows as you explore and missions to complete. Its ships move freely and carry cargo, which makes it a different game from Seafarers rather than an add-on to it.

Both are covered, with player counts and times, in [the best Catan expansions](/articles/best-catan-expansions/).

## FAQ

### Is Seafarers or Cities & Knights better?

Cities & Knights is the deeper game. Seafarers is the better first expansion for most groups, because it adds variety without a long teach. Many tables end up owning both.

### Is Cities & Knights too hard for casual players?

It can be, as a first expansion. It adds a third die, commodities, knights, barbarians, and progress cards at once. Casual players who enjoy Catan usually do better starting with Seafarers.

### Can you play Seafarers and Cities & Knights together?

Yes. The sixth edition Cities & Knights rulebook includes rules for combining them and recommends Heading for New Shores or Through the Desert as the scenario.

### Do I need the 5-6 extension for either expansion?

Only for five or six players. Then you need the base 5-6 extension plus the expansion's own 5-6 box.

### Which Catan expansion is best for learning strategy?

Cities & Knights, because it rewards long-term planning more than any other box. The free [Cities & Knights course](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/) starts with [how to play](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/how-to-play-cities-and-knights/) and goes all the way to tournament play.
