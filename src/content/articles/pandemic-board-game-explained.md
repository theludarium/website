The [Pandemic](https://link.amazon/B0eHP5GCg) board game puts two to four players on one team against four spreading diseases. You win by curing all four. You lose if outbreaks pile up, the deck runs out, or the disease cubes do. It came out in 2008, and nearly two decades later it is still the game most people mean when they say "a co-op."

This is an explainer, not a review. If you have heard of Pandemic and wondered what the fuss is about, or you own it and want to understand why it works so you can teach it better, this is for you.

## How does Pandemic actually play?

The board is a world map of cities connected by lines. Each city belongs to one of four colored regions, and each color is a disease. At setup, a handful of cities start with disease cubes on them.

On your turn you take four actions. The main ones are moving between connected cities, treating disease by removing a cube, building a research station, and curing a disease by turning in a set of matching city cards at a station. Then you draw two player cards, which might include an Epidemic card. Then you draw infection cards, which add cubes to the cities named.

That last step is the engine. When a city would get a fourth cube, it outbreaks instead, spreading one cube to every neighbor. Neighbors can outbreak too. An Epidemic card makes things worse: it infects a new city heavily, then shuffles the already-drawn infection cards back onto the top of the deck. The cities that just got sick are about to get sick again.

Each player also has a role card with a special ability. The Medic treats disease faster. The Researcher shares cards more easily. The Dispatcher moves other players. The roles are where most of the planning conversation lives.

## Why was Pandemic the first great co-op?

Cooperative games existed before 2008. Arkham Horror, Lord of the Rings, and Shadows over Camelot all predate it. But Pandemic was the first one that a general audience could learn in ten minutes, finish in forty-five, and lose in a way that felt fair.

Three design decisions did the work.

**The infection deck recycles.** Because Epidemics put the drawn cards back on top, the game is not random in the way dice are random. You know which cities are in the near future. You just do not know the order. That is enough information to plan and not enough to be safe.

**Four actions is the right number.** Three would be too few to do anything interesting. Five would make the game too easy. Four means you are always one action short of what you wanted to do, which is where the tension lives.

**Roles create real asymmetry.** Everyone shares a goal, but nobody can do everything. The Medic needs the Researcher's cards. The Scientist needs someone to bring her to the right station. Players talk because they must.

The Pandemic board game won a long list of awards and spawned a family of titles that share its core. [Forbidden Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003D7F4YY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Forbidden Desert](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B4ECHJI?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), Pandemic Legacy, [Pandemic Iberia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09XZVFQNY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and many others use the same engine with different clothes.

## What is the quarterback problem, and does Pandemic have it?

Yes. Pandemic is the game that gave the quarterback problem its name.

Because all information in Pandemic is open, an experienced player can see the whole board and tell everyone what to do. Nothing in the rules stops them. The other players nod, move their pawns, and go home having played a solitaire game that someone else solved.

We covered this at length in our [article on the quarterback problem](/articles/quarterback-problem-cooperative-games/), so here is the short version. The fix is social, not mechanical. Agree before you start that each player announces their own turn, and others can ask questions but not issue instructions. It changes the game completely.

Later co-ops fixed the problem by design, mostly by hiding information. [Hanabi](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MXTGT21?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [The Crew](https://link.amazon/B099S9RKf), and [Mysterium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F4YNPYQM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) all owe something to Pandemic's success and something to its biggest flaw. If you want games that sidestep it entirely, see our list of [co-op games without a quarterback problem](/articles/co-op-games-without-quarterback-problem/).

## How hard is Pandemic, and how do you adjust it?

Difficulty is set by the number of Epidemic cards shuffled into the player deck. Four is introductory, five is standard, six is heroic. The rulebook names them that way.

New groups should start at four, which the rules call the [introductory level](https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/Pandemic_v2.pdf), and expect to lose anyway. In our experience most first games end in defeat, usually to an outbreak chain nobody saw coming. That first loss is useful. It teaches the shape of the threat better than any explanation.

At five Epidemics, the normal setting, an experienced table in our experience wins something like half its games, which is roughly where a good co-op should sit. At six, the heroic setting, you will lose more than you win, and the wins feel earned.

## Does Pandemic still hold up in 2026?

It does, with two caveats.

First, the theme has a different weight after 2020 than it did before. Some groups find it too close to home; others find it cathartic. Know your table. Forbidden Island plays almost identically with a treasure-hunt theme if the disease angle is a problem.

Second, there are now co-ops that do specific things better. [Spirit Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MUHP51S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is deeper. The Crew is a better teaching game. [Sky Team](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CHD8RCSJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a better two-player experience. But Pandemic is still the best general-purpose co-op for four adults who have forty-five minutes and want a fair fight.

That is why it belongs on the shelf of any game lounge, including ours. It is the game you hand to a table that has never played together, because by the end they will have.

## FAQ

### How long does a game of Pandemic take?

About 45 minutes once everyone knows the rules. A first game with a rules teach runs closer to an hour.

### Can you play Pandemic with two players?

Yes, and it plays well. Two players each take one role; some groups prefer two roles each for more options.

### Is Pandemic good for kids?

The box says ages 8 and up, and that is about right for children playing alongside adults. Forbidden Island is the gentler sibling for younger kids.

### What is the difference between Pandemic and Pandemic Legacy?

Legacy uses the same core rules but adds a campaign where cards are torn, stickers are placed, and the board changes permanently over twelve months of in-game time.
