**The quick answer:** for a first campaign, buy **Jaws of the Lion** if your group likes tactical fights, or **Pandemic Legacy: Season 1** if it likes puzzles and big moments. Both are cooperative, both teach themselves as you go, and both end in a reasonable number of evenings.

## What makes a good campaign board game?

A campaign game is played over many sessions, and what happens in one carries into the next. Some are legacy games, where you change the components for good. Others save your progress in a log or a box, so the game can be reset or replayed. Our guide to [legacy board games](/articles/legacy-board-games-explained/) explains the difference in detail.

Before you buy, check four things:

1. **Length.** A twelve-session campaign is a few months of real life. A hundred-scenario campaign can take years.
2. **Player count, and who will show up.** A campaign needs the same people again and again. Pick a game that still works when someone misses a night.
3. **Co-op or competitive.** Most campaigns are cooperative. A few let you compete, which suits groups who like to win.
4. **Replay or one and done.** Legacy games are finished when the campaign ends. Card-game campaigns can be played again.

The biggest reason campaigns stall is scheduling, not the game. Decide before session one what happens [when someone misses a campaign session](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/), and you will finish far more of them.

## Start here: shorter campaigns

These four end in a few months of regular play. They are the right first campaign for nearly any group.

### Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion

Designed by Isaac Childres and published by Cephalofair Games in 2020, [Jaws of the Lion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B088QSDB7S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 30 to 120 minutes per scenario. You play mercenaries fighting through a story of 25 scenarios. There are no dice for your actions: each round you choose two cards from your hand, and managing that hand is the whole game.

**Why it fits:** it is the best way into Gloomhaven-style campaigns. The first five scenarios teach the rules one step at a time, and the box is small enough to set up on a weeknight. Our guide asks whether [the big Gloomhaven is worth it](/articles/is-gloomhaven-worth-it/), and for most busy adults the answer is to start here. The free [Jaws of the Lion course](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/) covers the rules, the [campaign](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/jaws-of-the-lion-campaign-guide/), and [what to play after it](/academy/jaws-of-the-lion/after-jaws-of-the-lion/).

### Pandemic Legacy: Season 1

Designed by Rob Daviau and Matt Leacock and published by Z-Man Games in 2015, [Pandemic Legacy: Season 1](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TQ0DXR2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in about 60 minutes per game. The campaign runs through a twelve-month year, and you play one or two games per month depending on how you do, so 12 to 24 games in all.

**Why it fits:** it is the game that made legacy campaigns famous. It starts as classic Pandemic, then surprises you with sealed boxes, new rules, and permanent changes. If you want a refresher on the base game, read [Pandemic explained](/articles/pandemic-board-game-explained/) or take the [Pandemic course](/academy/pandemic/). Finished it? [Pandemic Legacy: Season 0](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F73L5BM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a 2020 prequel by the same designers, also run over a twelve-month campaign.

### The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine

Designed by Thomas Sing and published by KOSMOS in 2019, [The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GP7X3P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 5 in about 20 minutes per session. It is a cooperative trick-taking card game with a campaign of 50 missions, each one a little harder than the last.

**Why it fits:** it is the smallest, simplest way to feel a campaign build. Sessions are short, the box fits in a pocket, and if someone misses a night you can just play a few missions without them and pick up where you left off. Our [guide to The Crew](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) and the [Crew course](/academy/the-crew/) explain the rules and [how the campaign works](/academy/the-crew/the-crew-campaign-and-distress-signal/).

### My City

Designed by Reiner Knizia and published by KOSMOS in 2020, [My City](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08626RMSL?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes. Everyone builds their own city by placing building tiles on a personal board, and the 24 episodes add new rules and permanent changes as the story goes on.

**Why it fits:** it is a light, short legacy game that works with older kids and people new to games. Each episode is quick, so a whole campaign fits into a season of family game nights.

## Card-game campaigns: play for years

These are Living Card Games: fixed boxes, no random packs. You start with a core set and add campaigns when you want more.

### Arkham Horror: The Card Game

Designed by Nate French and MJ Newman and first published by Fantasy Flight Games in 2016, [Arkham Horror: The Card Game](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GK349WJ7?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in about 1 to 2 hours per scenario. Investigators build decks and work through linked horror scenarios, and a chaos bag of tokens decides each test. Your campaign log tracks trauma, experience, and choices from game to game. In 2026, Fantasy Flight released a new core set that plays 1 to 4 from one box and includes a three-scenario campaign.

**Why it fits:** it is the deepest story campaign in a card game, and it plays very well solo or with two. The free [Arkham Horror LCG course](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/) covers [which box to buy](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-which-box-to-buy/) and how the [campaign log and trauma](/academy/arkham-horror-lcg/arkham-horror-lcg-campaign-log-trauma/) work.

### Marvel Champions

Designed by Michael Boggs, Nate French, and Caleb Grace and published by Fantasy Flight Games in 2019, [Marvel Champions](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07W1BF6D5?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 45 to 90 minutes. Each player is a hero with their own deck, and the team fights a villain together. The core set is a stand-alone game. Campaigns come in the larger expansion boxes, which link scenarios with a campaign log.

**Why it fits:** it is lighter than Arkham Horror and easier to drop in and out of, since each scenario also plays on its own. That makes it forgiving for groups that cannot meet every week. The [Marvel Champions course](/academy/marvel-champions/) explains [campaigns and expert mode](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-expert-mode-and-campaigns/) and [which expansions to buy](/academy/marvel-champions/marvel-champions-best-expansions/).

## Competitive campaigns: for groups who like to win

Most campaign games are cooperative. These four let you compete across the whole story.

### Ticket to Ride Legacy: Legends of the West

Designed by Alan R. Moon, Matt Leacock, and Rob Daviau and published by Days of Wonder in 2023, [Ticket to Ride Legacy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C6V67YZC?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 5 in 20 to 90 minutes per game, over a 12-game campaign. The map of the American West grows as you play, with new rules and surprises along the way.

**Why it fits:** everyone already knows how to play Ticket to Ride, so the teach is nearly free. It is a great first legacy game for families and casual players. Brush up with the [Ticket to Ride course](/academy/ticket-to-ride/) first.

### Clank! Legacy: Acquisitions Incorporated

Designed by Andy Clautice and Paul Dennen and published by Dire Wolf in 2019, [Clank! Legacy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07QT5PVMK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in 90 to 120 minutes. It takes the deck-building dungeon race of Clank! and adds a funny, changing story, a board you upgrade, and permanent changes across the campaign.

**Why it fits:** it suits groups who like to compete and laugh. If deck building is new to your table, read [deck-building games explained](/articles/deck-building-games-explained/) first.

### Charterstone

Designed by Jamey Stegmaier and published by Stonemaier Games in 2017, [Charterstone](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06X16ZVGF?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 6 in 45 to 75 minutes, over a 12-game campaign. Each player builds up one part of a shared village with worker placement, and the buildings you add become actions everyone can use.

**Why it fits:** it handles up to six, which is rare for a campaign. And when the campaign ends, the finished village is still a playable game. Our guide to [worker placement](/articles/worker-placement-explained/) covers the core idea.

### Arcs: The Blighted Reach

Designed by Cole Wehrle and published by Leder Games in 2024, [The Blighted Reach](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D8RH22Q8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the campaign expansion for [Arcs](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D856FJ1J?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and it needs the base game. It plays 2 to 4 over three linked games, called Acts, each taking under two hours. Every player gets a secret Fate that shapes their goals.

**Why it fits:** it is the best competitive campaign for experienced groups who like sharp, interactive games. It is only three sessions, so it is easy to finish. The [Arcs course](/academy/arcs/) has a lesson on [the Blighted Reach campaign](/academy/arcs/arcs-blighted-reach-campaign/).

## The big ones: long campaigns

These are huge, rewarding, and a real commitment. Start one only when your group has finished a shorter campaign together.

### Sleeping Gods

Designed by Ryan Laukat and published by Red Raven Games in 2021, [Sleeping Gods](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084GWHLGK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes per session. You captain a ship and crew lost in a strange world, sailing across an atlas and reading story passages from a storybook as you explore.

**Why it fits:** it is the most open of these campaigns. You choose where to sail, and you save your progress between sessions. It suits groups that love story more than tactics.

### Gloomhaven

Designed by Isaac Childres and published by Cephalofair Games in 2017, [Gloomhaven](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01LZXVN4P?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes per scenario. It has 95 scenarios, with branching paths, sealed content, and characters who retire and unlock new ones.

**Why it fits:** it is the classic big campaign game, with deep tactics and a huge amount of content. It is also a very large box and a long commitment. Play Jaws of the Lion first and see if your group wants more.

### Frosthaven

Designed by Isaac Childres and published by Cephalofair Games in 2022, [Frosthaven](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08FK7B2SC?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 90 to 180 minutes per scenario, across more than 100 scenarios. It is the follow-up to Gloomhaven, set in a frozen outpost that your group builds up between missions.

**Why it fits:** it is for groups that finished and loved Gloomhaven. Our comparison of [Gloomhaven vs Frosthaven](/articles/gloomhaven-vs-frosthaven/) covers which one to start with.

## FAQ

### What is the best campaign board game for beginners?

Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion or Pandemic Legacy: Season 1. Both teach their rules as the campaign goes, and both finish in a reasonable number of sessions. For a lighter start, The Crew is a small card game with a 50-mission campaign.

### What is the difference between a campaign game and a legacy game?

A campaign game carries results from one session to the next. A legacy game is a campaign game where you permanently change the components, like tearing up cards or adding stickers to the board. Every legacy game is a campaign game, but not every campaign game is legacy.

### What is the best campaign board game for two players?

Arkham Horror: The Card Game and Marvel Champions both play very well with two. Jaws of the Lion and Pandemic Legacy also work at two.

### Are there competitive campaign board games?

Yes. Ticket to Ride Legacy, Clank! Legacy, Charterstone, My City, and Arcs: The Blighted Reach all let players compete across the campaign.

### What happens if someone misses a session?

Decide before you start. Some groups play on with a stand-in, some pause, and some play a side scenario. Our guide to [campaign game missed sessions](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/) lays out the options, and our piece on [keeping a group together](/articles/keeping-a-group-together/) helps with the bigger picture.
