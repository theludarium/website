Crokinole rules fit on an index card. Players sit around a round wooden board and take turns flicking small discs toward the center. Every shot must hit an opponent's disc if one is on the board, and if none are, it must land near the middle. A disc that drops into the center hole scores 20. Knock your rivals off the board, stay on it yourself, and score the most points when everyone's discs are gone. That is the whole game, and it is one of the best things you can put on a table.

**The quick answer:** take turns flicking one disc from your quarter of the board. If the other side has discs in play, you must hit one; if not, your disc must end touching or inside the 15 line. Shots that break the rule are removed. After all discs are played, score 20, 15, 10 or 5 per disc by where it sits, and compare totals.

## What does a crokinole board look like?

A crokinole board is a round playing surface, typically about 26 inches across, set inside a raised rim with a gutter (players call it the ditch) that catches discs knocked off. From the outside in, the board has:

- **The 5 ring:** the wide outer band, split into four quarters by lines. Each player shoots from their own quarter.
- **The 10 ring:** the middle band.
- **The 15 ring:** the inner circle, guarded by **eight pegs** (small posts or bumpers) set around it.
- **The 20 hole:** a shallow hole dead center, just big enough for a disc.

Each region has a value. Discs score by where they rest when the round ends, and a disc touching a line scores the lower of the two regions. Discs are small and flat, about the size of a checker, and players often rub them in fine shuffleboard wax so they glide.

## Crokinole rules: how do you play a round?

Here is a round, step by step, based on the National Crokinole Association rules and the World Crokinole Championship rules. Casual tables often relax the seating and posture rules, but the shot rules are the heart of the game.

### 1. Sit and split the discs

Two players sit across from each other. Four players play as two teams, with partners sitting across the board from each other. Each side gets its own color.

### 2. Place your disc on the shooting line

On your turn, set one disc flat on the outer line of your own quarter. It must touch that line, and it has to start from your own section, though it may also touch one of the side lines that mark the edges of your quarter.

### 3. Flick it

Shoot with a flick of one or more fingers. Only your shooting hand, wrist and forearm may touch the board or table during the shot. In tournament play you must keep at least part of your seat in contact with the chair, keep all four chair legs on the floor, and never move the board. This is often called the one cheek rule.

### 4. Wait for everything to stop

Play goes clockwise. Each player shoots one disc per turn, and you wait until every disc has stopped moving before the next shot.

### 5. Pull out any 20s

If a disc drops cleanly into the center hole, it is a 20. Remove it right away and set it aside where everyone can see it. It counts for its owner at the end of the round.

### 6. Repeat until all discs are shot

The round ends when every disc has been played. Then you score.

## What is the must-hit rule in crokinole?

This is the rule that makes crokinole more than target practice.

**If any opposing disc is on the board, your shot must hit one.** You can hit it directly, or you can hit one of your own discs and knock it into an opponent's. If your shot touches no opposing disc, the shot fails.

**If no opposing discs are on the board, your shot must play to the middle.** When everything stops, your shooting disc (or one of your discs it moved) must be touching the 15 line or inside it. A shot that drops into the 20 hole counts.

When a shot fails either test, the shooting disc and any of your own discs it touched come off the board and go into the ditch. A 20 made on that failed shot does not count either.

The result is a game of constant tension. Every disc you leave on the board is a target, and every disc your opponent leaves is something you are required to go after.

## What counts as a foul in crokinole?

The official rules list a handful of fouls and what happens with each:

- **Off the board:** a disc that touches or crosses the outer line is out of play. It goes into the ditch and stays there for the rest of the round.
- **The spinning disc exception:** if a disc touches or crosses the outer line, hits nothing but the playing surface, and rolls back inside on its own, it stays in play.
- **Damage stays:** if a disc goes out, bounces off the rim or the discs in the ditch, and comes back onto the board, it is removed. But any discs it knocked around stay where they ended up, and any 20 it caused still counts.
- **Posture and contact:** lifting off your seat, leaning your chair, touching the board with anything but your shooting arm, or moving the board breaks the rules. In tournaments this can cost the shot, the round, or worse.
- **Distraction:** no touching the board or table, placing a disc, or making distracting noise or motion during the other player's turn.

At a kitchen table, most groups simply remove a fouled shot and move on.

## How does crokinole scoring work?

After the last disc stops, count each side's total before anyone moves a disc:

| Where the disc is | Points |
| --- | --- |
| In the 20 hole (set aside during play) | 20 |
| Inside the 15 line | 15 |
| Between the 15 and 10 lines | 10 |
| Between the 10 line and the outer line | 5 |
| Touching a line | The lower of the two regions |

There are two common ways to turn those totals into a winner.

### Casual scoring: race to 100

Subtract the smaller total from the larger. The side with the higher total scores the difference; the other side scores nothing that round. Keep playing rounds until one side reaches 100. A handy shortcut: pull off pairs of equal discs from both sides, since they cancel out, and count what is left.

### Tournament scoring: points per round

In National Crokinole Association and World Championship play, the winner of each round gets 2 points and a tied round gives each side 1. A game is four rounds, and the starting player alternates from round to round. Shooting last in a round is an advantage, which is one reason the start rotates.

## What is the difference between singles and doubles in crokinole?

- **Singles:** two players sit across from each other. Under NCA and World Championship rules, each player shoots 8 discs per round.
- **Doubles:** four players, two per team. Partners sit across from each other, share a color, and each shoots 6 discs per round. Turns go clockwise, so teams alternate shots. Partners may coach each other during play, which is not allowed in singles.

At home, play with whatever count your set allows. Just keep it even for both sides.

## Where did crokinole come from?

Crokinole is a Canadian folk game. The earliest known board was made in 1876 by Eckhardt Wettlaufer in Perth County, Ontario, reportedly as a birthday present for his son. Boards were also being made in the United States by the late 1800s. It is related to finger-flicking games like carrom and pichenotte, and the name likely comes from the French *croquignole*, a word tied to the flick of a finger.

Since 1999 the [World Crokinole Championship](https://worldcrokinole.com/thegame.html) has been held each June in Tavistock, Ontario, near where Wettlaufer lived. It now draws players from across Canada, the United States and overseas, with singles, doubles and cue divisions.

## Crokinole tips for new players

1. **Start soft.** Most new players flick too hard. A shot that hits and stays on the board is worth more than one that clears the table and leaves your own disc in the ditch.
2. **Hide behind the pegs.** A disc tucked just behind a peg is hard to hit. Your opponent may have to bank off something risky to reach it, and a miss costs them their disc.
3. **Hit and stick.** The ideal hit knocks the enemy disc off while your shooter stays in a good spot. Aim slightly off center to control where your disc goes after contact.
4. **Count the discs left.** Near the end of a round, the number of shots each side has remaining matters more than the current score. The last shot is a big advantage, so plan around it.
5. **Do not chase every 20.** An open 20 is great. Gambling for one when the other side has discs to hit can leave you with nothing.
6. **Play doubles to learn.** A partner means more advice, more laughs, and a gentler start for anyone new.

## FAQ

### How many discs does each player get in crokinole?

Under NCA and World Championship rules, 8 per player in singles and 6 per player in doubles. Casual groups often split whatever their set includes evenly between the two sides.

### Do you have to hit a disc in crokinole?

Yes, if your opponent has any disc on the board. If they have none, your shot must end touching or inside the 15 line instead. A shot that fails is removed along with any of your own discs it touched.

### Does a disc touching a line count in crokinole?

It counts, but for the lower value. A disc touching the 15 line scores 10, and a disc touching the 10 line scores 5. A disc touching the outer line is out of play.

### How do you win crokinole?

Casual players subtract the lower round total from the higher and play to 100 points. Tournaments award 2 points for each round won and 1 for a tie, over games of four rounds.

### How big is a crokinole board?

The playing surface is typically about 26 inches across, with a raised rim and a gutter around it. It has three scoring rings (5, 10 and 15), eight pegs around the 15 ring, and a 20 hole in the center.
