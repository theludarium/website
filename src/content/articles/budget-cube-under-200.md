A budget cube does not have to play like a budget cube. What people love about the MTGO Vintage Cube is not the price tags; it is the feel: turn-one mana, decks that do something unfair, every archetype a real plan, games that swing. Most of that feel comes from design choices, and design is free. The famous cards cost money, but almost every famous card has a cheap cousin that produces the same decision at the table.

This article is about building a 360-card cube for under $200 that drafts like a powered cube, archetype by archetype, and about the handful of places where spending a little more buys a lot. Prices move; every number here is approximate, checked in October 2026.

## What makes Vintage Cube feel like Vintage Cube?

Strip away the card names and the format has five traits.

1. **Fast mana exists.** Someone at the table can make four mana on turn two.
2. **Unfair decks are supported.** Reanimator, Storm, Sneak-and-Show, artifact combo. Not every draft, but the lanes are there.
3. **Fair decks are fast.** White and red aggro can kill by turn five if left alone.
4. **Interaction is cheap and strong.** One-mana removal, free or near-free counters.
5. **Card quality is high enough that every pick matters.** Few vanilla slots.

A budget cube can hit all five. The cost is in the exact cards, not the structure. The cube community has done this before; CubeCobra's [Balanced Budget Cube](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/balbud), now archived, once capped each mono-colored section at about $11 and still aimed for an environment more powerful than retail Limited. Our target is a little more ambitious on power and a little looser on price.

## Where should the $200 go?

A house budget, using TCGplayer prices for the cheapest printings of each card in October 2026 (check a retailer such as Card Kingdom or TCGplayer before ordering; Card Kingdom's near-mint prices run higher):

|---|---|---|---|
| Five colors | 250 | $120 | Mostly under $0.50 each; about fifteen cards at $2 to $8 |
| Gold | 50 | $20 | Signposts are mostly uncommons |
| Colorless | 25 | $20 | Mana rocks are where budget power lives |
| Lands | 35 | $15 | Pain lands and check lands are now cheap |
| Basics, sleeves, box | | $20 | Do not skip sleeves |
| **Total** | **360** | **About $195** | |

Lands and rocks are a sixth of the cards and cost even less than that share, which is the good news. Fixing and acceleration are what make a cube feel powerful, and they are the categories where cheap cards most closely imitate expensive ones.

## How do you fake fast mana for a few dollars?

You cannot afford Black Lotus or the Moxen. Sol Ring costs about a dollar, but we leave it out because it warps a budget cube badly. What you can run:

- **Signets and Talismans.** Two-mana rocks that fix. Ten of them, about a dollar or less each, give nearly every deck a turn-two accelerant.
- **Rituals.** Dark Ritual plus one red ritual such as Desperate Ritual. Rituals are no longer pennies (about $5 to $15 each at the time of writing, with Cabal Ritual and Seething Song at the top), so two is the budget number. They enable storm and reanimator, and they give black and red the "turn one, do something absurd" feel.
- **Mind Stone, Prismatic Lens, Coldsteel Heart, Fellwar Stone.** Colorless two-drops that keep the rock count high without raising the price.
- **Mana creatures.** Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Fyndhorn Elves, Arbor Elf. Birds of Paradise runs about $8, so it waits for a bigger budget. Green's version of fast mana is cheap.

The effect at the table is that four-mana plays happen on turn three in half the decks. That is most of what fast mana does in Vintage Cube, minus the turn-one Mox-into-two-drop opening.

## Which unfair archetypes work on a budget?

**Reanimator.** One of the cheaper unfair decks to support. Entomb is not budget, but Buried Alive, Faithless Looting, Careful Study, Thrill of Possibility, and Stitcher's Supplier fill the yard. Animate Dead (about $7), Exhume, Dread Return, and Unburial Rites bring it back; Reanimate itself listed around $13 and up at Card Kingdom at the time of writing, so treat it as a luxury. Targets are the place to spend: Griselbrand listed around $9 to $11 and Archon of Cruelty around $10 at Card Kingdom at the time of writing (both less on TCGplayer), and one or two of them are worth it.

**Storm.** Rituals plus cheap cantrips plus Brain Freeze, Empty the Warrens, Tendrils of Agony, and Grapeshot. Add Past in Flames and Mizzix's Mastery. Most of it costs a few dollars or less. Storm is the archetype that makes a budget cube feel like it has a ceiling, and it is also the one to watch for warping the draft; two or three storm payoffs is plenty.

**Artifact aggro and Tinker-style cheats.** Tinker is cheap enough to run; reprints listed around $2 to $4 at Card Kingdom at the time of writing. Pair it with a few expensive-to-cast, cheap-to-buy artifacts (Myr Battlesphere; Wurmcoil Engine, about $10, if the price allows) and the Signet count above, and you have a lane.

**Sneak and Show.** Sneak Attack is out of budget at about $10 to $13, but Through the Breach is under a dollar, and Elvish Piper and Quicksilver Amulet do a slower version. Keep this one small; it is the least reliable of the four.

## Which fair decks need the money?

White and red aggro are where a budget cube feels cheapest if you are not careful, because the premium one-drops are rares with real prices. The fix is depth, not stars. Fourteen one- and two-drops per aggro color, every one of them with a keyword or a bonus, beats six great ones and eight vanilla bodies. Savannah Lions, Elite Vanguard, Thraben Inspector, Kytheon's cheaper cousins; Goblin Guide and Kytheon himself now cost a dollar or two, alongside Monastery Swiftspear, Falkenrath Pit Fighter, Rakdos Cackler, Firedrinker Satyr. Equipment matters here: Bonesplitter is pennies and swings aggro mirrors.

Blue control gets Counterspell, Mana Leak, Remand, Memory Lapse, Fact or Fiction, Compulsive Research, and cheap card advantage. Blue is the easiest color to make feel premium cheaply, so do not overspend on it.

The removal suite is where budget cubes often come out ahead of expectations: Swords to Plowshares, Lightning Bolt, Doom Blade, Go for the Throat, Chain Lightning, Journey to Nowhere, and most sweepers cost little. The counts are in [how much removal a cube needs](/articles/cube-removal-suite/).

## Where does a bit more money buy a lot?

If someone hands you an extra $50, spend it in this order.

1. **Lands.** Pain lands and check lands are now cheap enough to sit in the base budget, so the upgrade is shock lands at about $6 to $11 each: four or five for your deepest pairs. Fetchable original duals are out of range, but a cube with untapped fixing feels a tier better. The [mana fixing piece](/articles/cube-mana-fixing/) covers cycles by price.
2. **Three or four marquee reanimation targets and finishers.** A few $5 cards that end the game make the unfair lanes exciting.
3. **The rituals you skipped.** Cabal Ritual, Seething Song and Pyretic Ritual add about $30 and deepen storm.

Do not spend it on planeswalkers. Cheap walkers are mostly fine and expensive ones distort a budget cube more than they improve it.

## What should a budget cube not try to copy?

Power nine, the original dual lands, Mana Drain, Force of Will, Library of Alexandria, and the Legacy staples priced like rent. Proxies are a separate decision with its own etiquette; this article is about a cube you own outright. The feel of those cards comes from the structure above. Drafters who have never seen a real Mox do not miss it when their Talisman lets them cast a four-drop on turn three.

## FAQ

### Can a budget cube really feel like Vintage Cube?

Mostly. Fast mana via rocks and rituals, supported unfair archetypes, cheap interaction, and fast aggro reproduce the decisions that make Vintage Cube fun. What you lose is the top one percent of card power, which drafters feel less than owners expect.

### What is the most important purchase in a budget cube?

Lands and mana rocks. They are now among the cheapest cards per slot, so buy them first; they make every other card more playable and they imitate expensive cards best.

### Should a budget cube include Sol Ring?

We leave it out. At this power level a turn-one Sol Ring decides games on its own, and there is no Mox-level company to keep it balanced.

### How much does a 360-card budget cube cost in 2026?

Roughly $150 to $250 including sleeves and basics at the time of writing, if you buy near-mint commons, uncommons, and cheap rares in one order; for comparison, [The Pauper Cube](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/thepaupercube) on CubeCobra, 450 commons, totals about $150 to $200 at the time of writing. Prices move, so check a retailer's cart before committing.

## Spend on structure

The feel of a powered cube is a set of decisions, not a set of price tags. Build the decisions and the drafters will forget what the cards cost. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
