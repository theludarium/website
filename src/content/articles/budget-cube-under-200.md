A budget cube does not have to play like a budget cube. What people love about the MTGO Vintage Cube is not the price tags; it is the feel: turn-one mana, decks that do something unfair, every archetype a real plan, games that swing. Most of that feel comes from design choices, and design is free. The famous cards cost money, but almost every famous card has a cheap cousin that produces the same decision at the table.

This article is about building a 360-card cube for under $200 that drafts like a powered cube, archetype by archetype, and about the handful of places where spending a little more buys a lot. Prices move; every number here is approximate and the editor will check them before publication.

## What makes Vintage Cube feel like Vintage Cube?

Strip away the card names and the format has five traits.

1. **Fast mana exists.** Someone at the table can make four mana on turn two.
2. **Unfair decks are supported.** Reanimator, Storm, Sneak-and-Show, artifact combo. Not every draft, but the lanes are there.
3. **Fair decks are fast.** White and red aggro can kill by turn five if left alone.
4. **Interaction is cheap and strong.** One-mana removal, free or near-free counters.
5. **Card quality is high enough that every pick matters.** Few vanilla slots.

A budget cube can hit all five. The cost is in the exact cards, not the structure. The cube community has done this before; CubeCobra's [Balanced Budget Cube](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/balbud), for example, caps each mono-colored section at roughly $11 by CubeCobra's prices and still aims for an environment more powerful than retail Limited. Our target is a little more ambitious on power and a little looser on price.

| **Total** | **360** | **$200** | |

Lands and rocks get a third of the money for a quarter of the cards. That is on purpose. Fixing and acceleration are what make a cube feel powerful, and they are the categories where cheap cards most closely imitate expensive ones.

## How do you fake fast mana for a few dollars?

You cannot run Black Lotus, the Moxen, or Sol Ring at this budget, and Sol Ring alone is affordable but warps a budget cube badly. What you can run:

- **Signets and Talismans.** Two-mana rocks that fix. Ten of them, under a dollar each, give nearly every deck a turn-two accelerant. <!-- AFFILIATE -->
- **Rituals.** Dark Ritual, Seething Song, Pyretic Ritual, Desperate Ritual, Cabal Ritual. They are cheap, they enable storm and reanimator, and they give black and red the "turn one, do something absurd" feel.
- **Mind Stone, Prismatic Lens, Coldsteel Heart, Fellwar Stone.** Colorless two-drops that keep the rock count high without raising the price.
- **Mana creatures.** Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Fyndhorn Elves, Arbor Elf, Birds of Paradise if the price allows. Green's version of fast mana is cheap.

The effect at the table is that four-mana plays happen on turn three in half the decks. That is most of what fast mana does in Vintage Cube, minus the turn-one Mox-into-two-drop opening.

## Which unfair archetypes work on a budget?

**Reanimator.** This is the cheapest unfair deck in Magic. Entomb is not budget, but Buried Alive, Faithless Looting, Careful Study, Thrill of Possibility, and Stitcher's Supplier fill the yard. Animate Dead, Exhume, Dread Return, and Unburial Rites bring it back; Reanimate itself listed around $13 and up at [Card Kingdom](https://www.cardkingdom.com/catalog/search?search=header&filter%5Bname%5D=reanimate) at the time of writing, so treat it as a luxury. Targets are the place to spend: Griselbrand listed around $8 to $11 and Archon of Cruelty around $13 to $17 at [Card Kingdom](https://www.cardkingdom.com/catalog/search?search=header&filter%5Bname%5D=griselbrand) at the time of writing, and one or two of them are worth it.

**Storm.** Rituals plus cheap cantrips plus Brain Freeze, Empty the Warrens, Tendrils of Agony, and Grapeshot. Add Past in Flames and Mizzix's Mastery. All of it is cheap. Storm is the archetype that makes a budget cube feel like it has a ceiling, and it is also the one to watch for warping the draft; two or three storm payoffs is plenty.

**Artifact aggro and Tinker-style cheats.** Tinker is cheap enough to run; reprints listed around $2 to $4 at [Card Kingdom](https://www.cardkingdom.com/catalog/search?search=header&filter%5Bname%5D=tinker) at the time of writing. Pair it with a few expensive-to-cast, cheap-to-buy artifacts (Myr Battlesphere, Wurmcoil Engine if the price allows) and the Signet count above, and you have a lane.

**Sneak and Show.** Sneak Attack is out of budget, but Through the Breach is sometimes within it, and Elvish Piper and Quicksilver Amulet do a slower version. Keep this one small; it is the least reliable of the four.

## Which fair decks need the money?

White and red aggro are where a budget cube feels cheapest if you are not careful, because the premium one-drops are rares with real prices. The fix is depth, not stars. Fourteen one- and two-drops per aggro color, every one of them with a keyword or a bonus, beats six great ones and eight vanilla bodies. Savannah Lions, Elite Vanguard, Thraben Inspector, Kytheon's cheaper cousins; Goblin Guide if the price allows, otherwise Monastery Swiftspear, Falkenrath Pit Fighter, Rakdos Cackler, Firedrinker Satyr. Equipment matters here: Bonesplitter is pennies and swings aggro mirrors.

Blue control gets Counterspell, Mana Leak, Remand, Memory Lapse, Fact or Fiction, Compulsive Research, and cheap card advantage. Blue is the easiest color to make feel premium cheaply, so do not overspend on it.

The removal suite is where budget cubes often come out ahead of expectations: Swords to Plowshares, Lightning Bolt, Doom Blade, Go for the Throat, Chain Lightning, Journey to Nowhere, and most sweepers cost little. The counts are in [how much removal a cube needs](/articles/cube-removal-suite/).

## Where does a bit more money buy a lot?

If someone hands you an extra $50, spend it in this order.

1. **Lands.** Pain lands and check lands over tapped duals. Fetchable duals are out of range, but a cube with untapped fixing feels a tier better. The [mana fixing piece](/articles/cube-mana-fixing/) covers cycles by price.
2. **Three or four marquee reanimation targets and finishers.** A few $5 cards that end the game make the unfair lanes exciting.
3. **The premium aggro one-drops.** Two or three rares at $3 to $8 each.

Do not spend it on planeswalkers. Cheap walkers are mostly fine and expensive ones distort a budget cube more than they improve it.

## What should a budget cube not try to copy?

Power nine, the original dual lands, Mana Drain, Force of Will, Library of Alexandria, and the Legacy staples priced like rent. Proxies are a separate decision with its own etiquette; this article is about a cube you own outright. The feel of those cards comes from the structure above. Drafters who have never seen a real Mox do not miss it when their Talisman lets them cast a four-drop on turn three.

## FAQ

### Can a budget cube really feel like Vintage Cube?

Mostly. Fast mana via rocks and rituals, supported unfair archetypes, cheap interaction, and fast aggro reproduce the decisions that make Vintage Cube fun. What you lose is the top one percent of card power, which drafters feel less than owners expect.

### What is the most important purchase in a budget cube?

Lands and mana rocks. Spend about a third of the budget on fixing and acceleration; they make every other card more playable and they imitate expensive cards best.

### Should a budget cube include Sol Ring?

We leave it out. At this power level a turn-one Sol Ring decides games on its own, and there is no Mox-level company to keep it balanced.

### How much does a 360-card budget cube cost in 2026?

Roughly $150 to $250 including sleeves and basics at the time of writing, if you buy near-mint commons, uncommons, and cheap rares in one order; for comparison, a 450-card all-commons list on [CubeCobra](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/mattspaupercube) prices near $245. Prices move, so check a retailer's cart before committing.

## Spend on structure

The feel of a powered cube is a set of decisions, not a set of price tags. Build the decisions and the drafters will forget what the cards cost. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
