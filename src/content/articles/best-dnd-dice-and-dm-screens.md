## What to look for in D&D dice

A standard RPG dice set has seven dice: a d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20, and a second d10 marked in tens (00, 10, 20 and so on) for percentile rolls. Our guide to [D&D dice explained](/articles/dnd-dice-explained/) covers what each one is for. When you shop, check five things:

1. **All seven dice.** Most sets include them. Some cheap sets drop the percentile die, and some deluxe sets add extras. Seven is the number you want.
2. **Readability.** Numbers in a strong contrasting color are easier to read across a table, especially by candlelight or for older eyes. Swirled, glittery, or heavily decorated dice look great and read slower. Check that the 6 and 9 are marked so you can tell them apart.
3. **Size.** About 16mm is the common standard. Bigger dice are easier for kids and for anyone who wants to read the result from the other end of the table.
4. **Material.** Acrylic (plastic) dice are light, cheap, and safe on any table. Resin dice are often handmade and come in wild designs, but quality varies by maker. Metal dice are heavy and satisfying, and they will dent a wooden table, so roll them in a tray.
5. **Balance claims.** Plenty of listings promise "precision" or "perfectly balanced" dice. Treat that as marketing unless the maker explains how they test it. For a home game, any set from a known maker rolls fair enough to play.

## What to look for in a DM screen

A DM screen is a folding wall that hides the game master's notes and rolls. The inside holds the rules tables they look up most. Four questions decide which one fits:

1. **Landscape or portrait.** A landscape screen is short and wide, so the DM can see and reach over it to move figures on the map. A portrait screen is taller and hides more, but it can feel like a wall between the DM and the players.
2. **Panel count.** Four panels is standard. More panels give more reference space and more table footprint.
3. **System-specific or blank.** Official screens print the rules tables for one game. Blank, customizable screens have clear pockets where you slide in your own printed sheets, so one screen works for every game you run.
4. **Who sees the art.** Most screens show art to the players. A customizable screen lets you swap that art to match the campaign.

## Dice for every player

These four are the everyday sets. They are the dice to buy in multiples.

### Chessex Opaque Black with White

The [Chessex Opaque Black with White set](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008KWIU20?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is seven acrylic dice at the standard 16mm size: d4, d6, d8, two d10s (one in tens), d12, and d20. The dice are solid black with white numbers, and the 6 and 9 are underlined.

**Why it fits:** this is the readability pick. High contrast, no swirls, no glitter. It is the set to hand a brand new player, because nobody squints at it. Chessex makes the Opaque line in many color pairs, so every player can have their own color without losing the clear numbers. If you are getting ready for a first session, our guide on [how to play D&D for beginners](/articles/how-to-play-dnd-for-beginners/) walks through what those dice will be doing.

### Chessex Speckled Space

The [Chessex Speckled Space set](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000RZLRSW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a standard seven-die set from Chessex's Speckled line, where each die is flecked with several colors.

**Why it fits:** Speckled dice are the classic look of the hobby. They have more character than a plain set and still read well, because the numbers sit on a busy but dark background. Space is one of the long-running colors in the line, and there are many others, from Golden Recon to Silver Volcano, so it is easy to match a set to a character.

### Chessex Gemini Purple-Steel with White

The [Chessex Gemini Purple-Steel with White set](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9UDG96?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a seven-die set from the Gemini line, which Chessex describes as two colors swirled together.

**Why it fits:** Gemini sets are the step up for a player who has been to a few sessions and wants dice that feel like theirs. The swirl is the draw. Pick a color pair with light numbers on darker plastic, like this one, and readability stays good. Very pale swirls with gold numbers are pretty but slower to read.

### Chessex Pound-O-Dice

The [Chessex Pound-O-Dice bag](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008C0KXYS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a bag of roughly 80 to 100 assorted dice in many shapes and colors. Chessex says the bag mixes first and second quality dice, so some will have small cosmetic flaws.

**Why it fits:** this is the bag for groups, not for one player. Teachers, library programs, family tables, and anyone running a [drop-in RPG night](/articles/drop-in-rpg-night/) for strangers can pour it into a bowl and let people build their own sets. Kids love digging through it. Our guide to [D&D for kids](/articles/dnd-for-kids/) has more on running a table for young players. You will need to sort out matching sets, and every bag is different.

## Dice that feel special

These are the sets people keep for years. They make great gifts and great "I finished my first campaign" rewards.

### Q-Workshop Dragons Black & Red

The [Q-Workshop Dragons Black & Red set](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000MFDBV8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a seven-die set with dragon art engraved into each face. Q-Workshop makes its dice in Poznań, Poland, and is known for carved, ornamented designs.

**Why it fits:** these look like treasure. The engraving wraps around the numbers, which is the tradeoff: they take a beat longer to read than a plain set. For a DM rolling behind a screen, or a player who wants their dice to be part of the character, that is a fair trade. Q-Workshop makes many other themes too, including Celtic and Elvish designs.

### Norse Foundry Draugr metal dice

The [Norse Foundry Draugr set](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08245S8K3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a seven-die set of metal dice. Norse Foundry says its RPG dice are full metal down to the core, cast in zinc alloy, with no holes or fills.

**Why it fits:** metal dice are heavy, cool to the touch, and land with a satisfying thunk. That weight is the whole appeal. It is also the warning: metal dice will dent a wooden table and chip plastic dice they hit, so pair them with a tray. They are not for very young players. Some Norse Foundry sets also run larger than the 16mm standard, so check sizes if you want them to match a plastic set.

### Ultra Pro Vivid folding dice tray

The [Ultra Pro Vivid folding dice tray](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CTKWCV26?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a faux-leather tray that snaps into shape with magnets and folds flat for travel.

**Why it fits:** a tray keeps dice from bouncing off the table, into drinks, or under the couch. It also protects the table from metal dice and settles the occasional "that one was cocked" argument. A dice tower, where dice drop through baffles and roll out the bottom, does the same job with more flair and more space. It is made to pair with the 2024 Player's Handbook, Monster Manual, and Dungeon Master's Guide.

**Why it fits:** if your table plays D&D with the 2024 books, this is the default. The tables inside match the rules your players are reading. ## FAQ

### How many dice do you need for D&D?

One seven-die set per player is enough to play. Many players like a second d20 for advantage and disadvantage, and a few extra d6s come in handy for big damage rolls. See [D&D dice explained](/articles/dnd-dice-explained/) for what each die does.

### Are metal dice bad for D&D?

No, they roll fine. They are heavy enough to dent wooden tables and chip other dice, so roll them in a tray or on a mat. Keep them away from small children.

### Do I need a DM screen?

No. Many DMs run without one and roll in the open. A screen helps if you want to keep notes and monster rolls private, and the reference tables save you flipping through books. It is a nice tool, not a requirement.

### Is a landscape or portrait DM screen better?

Landscape is better for most tables, because it is low enough that you can see the players and reach the map. Portrait hides more and suits DMs with lots of notes. The Dungeon Master's Screen Reincarnated and the Pathfinder Core GM Screen are both landscape.

### Are cheap dice sets fair?

For a home game, sets from a known maker roll fairly enough that nobody will notice a difference. Be skeptical of "perfectly balanced" claims without an explanation of how the maker checks it.

### What dice and screen should I get for a kid?

Big, plain, high-contrast plastic dice, and no metal dice for little ones. Skip the screen at first; kids like seeing the rolls. Our [D&D for kids](/articles/dnd-for-kids/) guide and [best D&D starter set gift](/articles/best-dnd-starter-set-gift/) picks are good next reads.
