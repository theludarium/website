Dungeon Crawler Carl is a series of novels by Matt Dinniman about a man, his ex-girlfriend's prize-winning cat, and a dungeon that swallowed the world. It is funny, brutal, and surprisingly kind. It is also the closest thing in fiction to sitting at a tabletop RPG night with a game master who loves you and wants you dead.

**The quick answer:** if you play D&D or any tabletop RPG, start with book one, *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, as an audiobook. The series is built from things RPG players know by heart: levels, loot, classes, a dungeon with rules, and a party that has to trust each other. The audiobook, narrated by Jeff Hays, is the version most fans recommend.

## What is Dungeon Crawler Carl?

In the first chapter, every building on Earth disappears. The people still outside are offered a choice: enter the World Dungeon or die. The dungeon is eighteen floors deep, and the whole thing is a game show broadcast across the galaxy. Carl goes in wearing a jacket, boxer shorts, and not much else, with Princess Donut, a show cat who belonged to his ex. Then the cat starts talking.

The books are LitRPG, a genre where the story runs on visible game rules. Characters get notifications, gain levels, pick classes, and open loot boxes, and the reader sees the numbers. Dungeon Crawler Carl is the series most people name when they explain what LitRPG is.

## Why tabletop RPG players love it

| What you know from the table | How the books use it |
| --- | --- |
| The game master | The dungeon's AI, who sets the rules, bends them, and plainly enjoys the chaos |
| Character sheets | Stats, skills, and achievements the reader sees as they change |
| Loot | Loot boxes with gear that is sometimes brilliant and often absurd |
| Session zero | The early floors teach the rules the way a good first session does |
| The party | Carl and Donut learning to work as a team, with different strengths and opinions |
| Time pressure | Each floor closes on a timer, so dawdling has a cost |
| A mentor NPC | A guide character who teaches the crawlers how the game really works |

If you have ever argued with a rules lawyer, watched a player find a loophole the game master did not see coming, or laughed at a terrible item roll, these books will feel familiar.

## Is it just a joke book?

No. The humor is loud, and some of it is crude, so it is a book for adults. Under the jokes, the series is about grief, power, and what people do for each other when the world ends. That mix is why it holds readers for eight books instead of one.

## Should you read it or listen to it?

## FAQ

### Is Dungeon Crawler Carl good for D&D players?

Yes. The series is built on the same parts as a D&D campaign: a dungeon, levels, classes, loot, and a party. Readers who play tabletop RPGs tend to catch the jokes about rules, dice, and game masters fastest.

### What age is Dungeon Crawler Carl for?

Adults. It has strong language, violence, and crude humor. Older teens who enjoy that kind of comedy often love it, but it is not a children's book.

### Do I need to know LitRPG to enjoy it?

No. The first book explains its rules as Carl learns them. If you have played a video game or a tabletop RPG, you will be at home within a chapter.

### How many Dungeon Crawler Carl books are there?

Eight, as of 2026. Book 8, *A Parade of Horribles*, came out in May 2026, and the series is planned to continue.

### Is the audiobook really better?

Many fans think so, mostly because of Jeff Hays's narration and the way the game's announcements are performed. The story is the same in every format, so pick the one you will actually finish.
