The best strategy board games depend on how much your table wants to think. For a light game anyone can learn in ten minutes, play [Ticket to Ride](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0975277324?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and for two players, [7 Wonders Duel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B014DMSTXK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a group that plays most weeks, the sweet spot is medium weight: [Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [The Castles of Burgundy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R5ZH6FQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Dune: Imperium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08JHB9V72?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Terraforming Mars](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GSYA4K2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Root](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F454YF3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And for a long, deep evening, reach for [Brass: Birmingham](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884047?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Ark Nova](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09L6FCP9S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Spirit Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MUHP51S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or, if you have a whole day, [Twilight Imperium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074YPSTRP?tag=cubecommonw0a-20).

**The quick answer:** if you buy one strategy game, make it **Splendor** for a new group, **Wingspan** or **Dune: Imperium** for a regular group, and **Brass: Birmingham** for a group that already loves heavy games. For two players, **7 Wonders Duel** is hard to beat.

Every game on this list has a free course in the [Ludarium Academy](/academy/), so you can learn the rules, then get better at it, without paying for anything but the box.

## What makes a great strategy board game?

A strategy game is one where good decisions beat good luck over time. Some luck is fine, and even useful. What matters is that the better plan usually wins. Look for four things:

1. **Real choices every turn.** Two or three good options, not one obvious move.
2. **A plan that pays off.** What you do early should shape what you can do late.
3. **Something to read at the table.** Watching what other players need is half the skill.
4. **The right weight for your group.** A brilliant heavy game is a bad night for a table that wanted something light.

That last point is the one people skip. Weight is how complex a game is to learn and play. Our guide to [board game weight](/articles/board-game-weight-explained/) explains the scale, and this list uses three bands: light (teach in about ten minutes), medium (teach in twenty to thirty), and heavy (plan an evening).

## Light strategy games: teach in ten minutes

These are the games to bring when someone new is at the table. The rules are short, but there is real skill underneath. Our list of [gateway board games](/articles/gateway-board-games/) has more in this band.

### Ticket to Ride

Designed by Alan R. Moon and published by Days of Wonder in 2004, [Ticket to Ride](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0975277324?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2004 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 5 in 30 to 60 minutes. You collect colored train cards and spend them to claim routes, trying to connect the cities on your secret tickets.

**Why it fits:** on your turn you do one of three things, so anyone can play. The strategy is in timing: when to grab a route before someone blocks it, and when to take a risk on more tickets. Learn it in the [Ticket to Ride course](/academy/ticket-to-ride/), then read our [Ticket to Ride strategy guide](/articles/ticket-to-ride-strategy/).

### Azul

Designed by Michael Kiesling and published by Plan B Games (now Next Move Games) in 2017, [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2018 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. You draft colored tiles from shared displays and place them on your board to build a wall pattern.

**Why it fits:** it looks like a pretty pattern game, but the real fight is over what you leave behind. Take the wrong tiles and you hand your opponent a perfect row, or stick yourself with penalties. It rewards counting tiles and reading what others need. Start with the [Azul course](/academy/azul/) and our [Azul strategy guide](/articles/azul-strategy/).

### Splendor

Designed by Marc André and published by Space Cowboys in 2014, [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes. You take gem chips and buy development cards, and each card you own becomes a permanent discount on the next.

**Why it fits:** it is the cleanest introduction to engine building there is. Every turn is one small choice, but those choices add up to a race, and good players see the finish line ten turns early. The [Splendor course](/academy/splendor/) and our [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/) show how.

### Carcassonne

Designed by Klaus-Jürgen Wrede and published by Hans im Glück in 2000, [Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2001 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 5 in about 35 to 45 minutes. You draw a tile, add it to a shared map of roads, cities, and fields, and may place a follower to claim something.

**Why it fits:** each turn is simple, but where you place a follower, and when you take it back, decides the game. It is also a sneaky game: joining someone else's city to share the points is a classic move. Learn it with the [Carcassonne course](/academy/carcassonne/), then try our [Carcassonne strategy guide](/articles/carcassonne-strategy/).

### 7 Wonders Duel

Designed by Antoine Bauza and Bruno Cathala and published by Repos Production in 2015, [7 Wonders Duel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B014DMSTXK?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays exactly 2 in about 30 minutes. You take cards from a shared layout to build a civilization, and you can win on points, by science, or by military.

**Why it fits:** three ways to win means you always have to watch what your opponent is building, not just your own plan. Every card you take may reveal one they wanted. It is the best two-player strategy game for its size. See the [7 Wonders Duel course](/academy/7-wonders-duel/), and our guide to [two-player games for date night](/articles/two-player-board-games-date-night/) for more.

## Medium strategy games: for regular game nights

These take twenty or thirty minutes to teach and reward playing again and again. This band is where most groups end up living.

### Wingspan

Designed by Elizabeth Hargrave and published by Stonemaier Games in 2019, [Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) won the 2019 Kennerspiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 5 in 40 to 70 minutes. You play bird cards into three habitats, and each row becomes an action that gets stronger as you add birds.

**Why it fits:** it is a calm, low-conflict engine builder, so it works for groups that do not enjoy direct attacks. The strategy is in combos and in timing your switch from building to scoring. The [Wingspan course](/academy/wingspan/) covers it from first game to tournament play, and our guide to [engine-building games](/articles/engine-building-games/) explains the idea.

### The Castles of Burgundy

Designed by Stefan Feld and first published by alea in 2011, [The Castles of Burgundy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R5ZH6FQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in its current edition, in about 90 to 120 minutes. You roll two dice each round and use them to take hexagon tiles and build your estate in the Loire valley.

**Why it fits:** dice decide your options, but there are always ways to bend them, and the better planner wins. It is a pure, quiet puzzle with many small decisions. The [Castles of Burgundy course](/academy/castles-of-burgundy/) starts with a first-round walkthrough.

### Dune: Imperium

Designed by Paul Dennen and published by Dire Wolf in 2020, [Dune: Imperium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08JHB9V72?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. Each round you play cards to send agents to board spaces, then reveal the rest of your hand for buying new cards and fighting over the round's conflict.

**Why it fits:** it blends deck building with worker placement, and adds open conflict on top. That makes it tense and social without being mean. It is a great pick for groups moving up from light games. Learn it in the [Dune: Imperium course](/academy/dune-imperium/), and see [worker placement explained](/articles/worker-placement-explained/) if that term is new.

### Terraforming Mars

Designed by Jacob Fryxelius and published by FryxGames in 2016 (Stronghold Games in the US), [Terraforming Mars](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GSYA4K2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 5 in about two hours. Corporations race to raise the planet's temperature and oxygen and place oceans, playing project cards that build an economy.

**Why it fits:** the card pool is huge, so no two games feel alike, and the long arc of building an engine is deeply satisfying. It sits at the heavy end of medium. Start with [how to play Terraforming Mars](/academy/terraforming-mars/how-to-play-terraforming-mars/) in the [Terraforming Mars course](/academy/terraforming-mars/).

### Root

Designed by Cole Wehrle and published by Leder Games in 2018, [Root](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F454YF3?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 90 minutes. Woodland factions fight for control of a forest, and each faction plays by different rules.

**Why it fits:** asymmetry is the strategy. You have to understand your faction and also what every other faction wants. It is the most interactive game on this list, with lots of table talk. Our guide to [Root's factions](/articles/root-factions-explained/) and the [Root course](/academy/root/) make the first game much smoother. For more games like it, see our list of [area control board games](/articles/area-control-board-games/).

## Heavy strategy games: plan an evening

These are rich and demanding. Teach them to people who already enjoy a medium game, and expect the first play to be slow. If your table tends to freeze over big decisions, read our tips on [analysis paralysis](/articles/analysis-paralysis-board-games/) first.

### Brass: Birmingham

Designed by Gavan Brown, Matt Tolman, and Martin Wallace and published by Roxley in 2018, [Brass: Birmingham](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884047?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. You build canals, then railways, and industries across the English Midlands, and your network shares coal, iron, and beer with everyone else's.

**Why it fits:** almost no luck, tight money, and a shared network where your build helps your rivals too. It is one of the most respected strategy games of its era. The [Brass: Birmingham course](/academy/brass-birmingham/) walks through both eras and the common beginner mistakes.

### Ark Nova

Designed by Mathias Wigge and published by Feuerland Spiele in 2021 (Capstone Games in English), [Ark Nova](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09L6FCP9S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 90 to 150 minutes. You build a modern zoo, house animals, and support conservation projects, using five action cards that get stronger the longer you wait to use them.

**Why it fits:** it rewards long-term planning and card evaluation, and the action-card system creates a timing puzzle every turn. It is heavy, but its zoo theme makes it easier to remember than most games this deep. See the [Ark Nova course](/academy/ark-nova/).

### Spirit Island

Designed by R. Eric Reuss and published by Greater Than Games in 2017, [Spirit Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MUHP51S?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 1 to 4 in 90 to 120 minutes. Players are spirits of an island, working together to drive off colonizing invaders.

**Why it fits:** it is cooperative, so the whole table shares one hard strategic problem instead of competing. The invaders move in a pattern you can predict, which makes it a planning game more than a luck game. It suits groups who love strategy but not conflict. The [Spirit Island course](/academy/spirit-island/) starts with the best starter spirits, and our list of [cooperative board games](/articles/best-cooperative-board-games/) has more.

### Twilight Imperium

Designed by Dane Beltrami, Corey Konieczka, and Christian T. Petersen and published by Fantasy Flight Games in 2017, [Twilight Imperium: Fourth Edition](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074YPSTRP?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 6 in about 4 to 8 hours. Galactic empires compete through trade, politics, and war to claim the throne.

**Why it fits:** this is the biggest game on the list, and an event as much as a game. Deals, votes, and alliances matter as much as fleets. It is not for every group, but for the right one it is unforgettable. Read [how long Twilight Imperium takes](/academy/twilight-imperium/how-long-is-twilight-imperium/) before you book the day, then use the [Twilight Imperium course](/academy/twilight-imperium/).

## FAQ

### What is the best strategy board game for beginners?

Splendor or Ticket to Ride. Both teach in about ten minutes and still reward good play. Azul and Carcassonne are close behind.

### What is the most strategic board game with no luck?

Of the games here, Brass: Birmingham has very little luck once the game starts. Chess and Go are the classic answers. If you want to know how luck and skill mix in modern games, see our guide to [luck vs skill in board games](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/).

### What is the best strategy board game for two players?

7 Wonders Duel was built for two. Splendor, Azul, and Carcassonne also play well at two, and Ark Nova and Brass: Birmingham work at two for heavier tables.

### Are strategy board games good for adults who are new to games?

Yes, if you start light. A game like Ticket to Ride gives new players the feeling of a clever plan without a long rulebook. Save the heavy games for later.

### How do I get better at strategy board games?

Play the same game several times in a row, and review what decided the result. Each Academy course has openings, midgame, and endgame lessons for exactly this. Our guide on [how to learn a board game](/articles/how-to-learn-a-board-game/) helps with the first play.
