Is Gloomhaven worth it? For most busy adults, the answer is: the smaller version is, and the big one usually is not. Gloomhaven is a brilliant game that asks for a hundred-plus hours and a group that shows up every time. Jaws of the Lion gives you most of the experience in a quarter of the box and a fraction of the commitment.

That is the short answer. The rest of this article explains how Gloomhaven works, what it costs in time and money, who it fits, and how to decide without buying a twenty-pound box first.

## What is Gloomhaven, in plain terms?

Gloomhaven is a cooperative campaign game for one to four players. You each play a mercenary with a unique deck of ability cards. You explore dungeons laid out from modular tiles, fight monsters controlled by the game, and make choices that change the story and the world map.

The combat system is the heart of it. Each turn you pick two cards from your hand. One card's top action and the other's bottom action happen, in an order you choose. Cards you play are eventually lost for the rest of the scenario. Your hand shrinks, so every scenario is a race against your own exhaustion.

There are no dice. Attack results come from a personal modifier deck that you improve as your character levels. Monsters act on their own card-driven logic, so nobody has to be the dungeon master.

Characters retire when they complete a personal goal, and a retirement opens a sealed box with a new class. That mechanism, more than any other, is why people describe Gloomhaven as a game that keeps surprising them.

## How much time does Gloomhaven actually take?

This is the question that matters for busy adults, so here are the numbers, hedged.

- **Scenarios:** The base campaign has [95 scenarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloomhaven), though you will not play all of them on one run.
- **Per scenario:** Two to three hours including setup and teardown, in our experience. Faster once you are practiced.
- **A full campaign:** Well over a hundred hours. The publisher's stated time is [90 to 115 minutes per scenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloomhaven), and a run through the campaign takes dozens of scenarios.
- **Setup:** Fifteen to thirty minutes per scenario for the tiles, monsters, and tokens, unless you buy or build organizers.

Put that against a real calendar. If your group meets every other week for three hours, that is a two-year campaign. Most groups do not finish. The players who love Gloomhaven most are the ones who accepted that going in.

## What does Gloomhaven cost?

The box itself is around $200 at retail; the publisher listed the [second edition at $199.99](https://cephalofair.com/collections/all) at the time of writing (October 2026), and the first edition carried a $140 suggested price. Prices move. That is the smallest cost. Add an organizer if you want setup under twenty minutes. Add a digital companion app, which most groups use to track monsters and avoid the cardboard clutter.

Measured per hour of play, Gloomhaven is one of the cheapest entertainments a group can buy. Measured per hour of your life you have to schedule, it is one of the most expensive.

## Who is Gloomhaven right for?

It fits a specific kind of group.

- **A stable foursome or trio.** The same people, every time. Gloomhaven handles a missing player less gracefully than most campaign games, because each character's deck and progress is personal.
- **People who enjoy tactical puzzles.** The combat rewards planning three turns ahead. If your group prefers talking over thinking, it will drag.
- **Players who can tolerate rules weight.** The rulebook is long and the edge cases are many. One person needs to own the rules.
- **A dedicated table.** Not literally required, but a game that takes thirty minutes to set up is much better when you can leave it out.

If that describes your group, Gloomhaven may become the best thing you play together for years. If it does not, there is no shame in that. It is a particular game for a particular table.

## How does Gloomhaven compare to Jaws of the Lion and Frosthaven?

| | Gloomhaven | Jaws of the Lion | Frosthaven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Players | 1–4 | 1–4 | 1–4 |
| Scenarios (approx.) | ~95 | 25 | ~140 |
| Campaign length | 100+ hrs | 30–50 hrs | Longer than Gloomhaven |
| Teach | Steep | Tutorial-driven; first scenarios teach the rules | Steep, assumes experience |
| Setup | Modular tiles, long | Scenario book is the map; fast | Modular, plus town building |
| Price tier | High | Low-mid | Highest |
| Best for | Committed groups | Busy adults, first-timers | Veterans who finished Gloomhaven |

Figures are approximate and vary by edition; the [Gloomhaven and Frosthaven scenario counts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloomhaven) are the published ones, the hour estimates are ours.

Jaws of the Lion is the recommendation for almost everyone reading an article titled "is Gloomhaven worth it." It uses the same combat system. It teaches the rules across the first five scenarios instead of in a manual. The map is printed in a spiral-bound book, so setup takes five minutes. You can finish it in a few months of casual play.

If you finish Jaws of the Lion and want more, buy Gloomhaven with confidence. You will already know the rules and you will already know whether your group can commit.

## What are the alternatives if Gloomhaven is too much?

Plenty of games scratch the campaign itch with less weight.

- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 1** is twelve to twenty-four sessions of forty-five minutes each and tells a tighter story. We explain how it works in our [legacy games explainer](/articles/legacy-board-games-explained/).
- **Sleeping Gods** is an open-world exploration campaign with lighter combat and a strong atlas-based map.
- **Forgotten Waters** is a pirate campaign with an app that narrates, and it is funny.
- **Oathsworn** and **Too Many Bones** are heavier but shorter per campaign.

And if your group's real problem is attendance rather than weight, read our piece on [keeping a campaign going when someone misses a session](/articles/campaign-game-missed-session/). The game is rarely the issue. The calendar is.

## So, is Gloomhaven worth it?

For a committed group with a free table and a two-year horizon: yes, without reservation. It is one of the richest cooperative experiences the hobby has produced.

For a busy adult who gets three game nights a month and wants to spend them with a mix of people: buy Jaws of the Lion, play it through, and decide then. You lose nothing by starting small. You lose a lot by buying the big box and watching it gather dust.

## FAQ

### How long does a Gloomhaven campaign take?

Most estimates land between 100 and 150 hours of play for the full campaign, which is one to two years for a group meeting every other week.

### Can you play Gloomhaven solo?

Yes. Solo play with two characters is common and well supported, and the digital companion app makes it manageable.

### Should I start with Gloomhaven or Jaws of the Lion?

Jaws of the Lion. It is cheaper, teaches itself, and uses the same core system, so nothing you learn is wasted.

### Is Gloomhaven good for beginners?

Not as a first modern board game. Play a few lighter co-ops first, then Jaws of the Lion, then decide.
