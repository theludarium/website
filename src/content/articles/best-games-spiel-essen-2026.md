SPIEL Essen 2026 ran from October 22 to 25 in Essen, Germany, and drew a record crowd of about 230,000 people to the world's largest board game fair. The games people lined up for were Queen Alice, Greenwood, -60°, and Kingdom Come: Deliverance, while the Fairplay visitor vote crowned two smaller surprises: Nokosu Dice and Vroom. Below are the 11 new releases worth knowing, who each one suits, and when you can expect them in the US.

**The quick answer:** for a light game you can find soon, watch Minikin City and Vroom. For a big strategy game, the buzz belonged to Greenwood, Timespan, and -60°. For a family or two-player table, Cascadia: Alpine Lakes is the easy pick. Most of these reach US stores between late 2026 and early 2027.

## What happened at SPIEL Essen 2026?

SPIEL, often just called Essen, is where much of the hobby's new releases debut each autumn. Publishers time their big launches for it, and visitors buy, demo, and rate games all week.

A few headlines from 2026:

- **A record crowd.** About 230,000 visitors over four days, up from about 220,000 in 2025. For the first time the fair used the entire Messe Essen fairgrounds.
- **Long lines and sellouts.** Some visitors reported waits of up to three hours to try Queen Alice. Greenwood reportedly sold out every day.
- **A trading card game rush.** The first-day queue for Riftbound's Secret Garden bundle grew so long that organizers asked Riot Games to pause sales; they resumed the next day with limits.
- **Awards at the fair.** The Deutscher Spiele Preis, a German award voted by players, went to Artengarten (Sanctuary in English) by Mathias Wigge, with Boss Fighters QR second and Galactic Cruise third. Toriki took the children's award. This year's Kennerspiel winner, Rebirth, placed sixth.

## What is the Fairplay scout list, and who topped it in 2026?

Fairplay, a German games magazine, has run a visitor vote at the fair for many years. Volunteer "scouts" rate games they played at the fair, and Fairplay posts running lists that change as votes come in. It splits games into an expert list and a family list, and a game needs at least 25 ratings to qualify. The final standings were set on Sunday, October 25.

The 2026 results, as reported by several European outlets:

- **Expert list winner: Nokosu Dice**, a Japanese trick-taking game from 2016, newly published in German by Pegasus Spiele.
- **Family list winner: Vroom**, a tiny cooperative racing game from Big Moustache Games.
- **Also near the top of the expert list:** Greenwood in second, then Gibberers, Tea & Rum, Vantage, and Drillers. Reports differ slightly on ties below second place.

Two lessons from the list. First, scout ratings reward games people actually played at the fair, so a small, quick game can beat a hyped heavy one. Second, it rates more than brand-new titles: Vantage, Jamey Stegmaier's open-world cooperative game, came out in 2025, and Nokosu Dice is a decade old in Japan. Very Big Monsters sat first on the family list in one Sunday update and then vanished from the final list. When readers asked why, a scout organizer said on a fan forum that irregular votes had been found and corrected.

## What were the most anticipated games of SPIEL 2026?

BoardGameGeek's most-wanted list for the fair is a measure of hype rather than of play. Queen Alice and -60° traded places at the top depending on when you looked, with Timespan, Entropie, Drillers, Gaudi, and Prague Unseen close behind in one widely shared snapshot. Only Drillers made both that list and the Fairplay expert top ten, which tells you how differently hype and hands-on ratings can behave.

### Queen Alice

Queen Alice was the line everyone talked about. Designed by Héctor Carrión and David Heras Pino with art by Amelia Sales, it sets you in a world inspired by Through the Looking-Glass. Each turn you discard one card for a small resource and play another to move a chess piece on a small board. Where the piece lands decides which mini-game you can use and what resource you gain, while you back the Red and White Queens in their race. It plays 1 to 4, and a full four-player game can run about two hours.

The praise was for the art, the production, and how well the puzzle fits the theme; the warnings were about length and about first-time players feeling lost because they are not tied to one queen. **Who it's for:** players who enjoy chewing on a turn. **US timing:** the edition sold at the fair is multilingual; wider US distribution was not confirmed at press time.

### Greenwood

Greenwood is the big autumn game from Feuerland, the publisher of Ark Nova. Christos Giannakoulas and Manolis Zachariadis designed it: you play primordial spirits freeing creatures held by druids, and you can activate your own companions and your opponents'. It plays 1 to 4 at roughly 40 minutes per player, ages 14 and up. It sold out every day of the fair, even with fresh stock each morning.

**Who it's for:** groups who already enjoy heavy euros. **US timing:** Capstone Games is publishing it in North America, listed for the fourth quarter of 2026.

### -60° (Sixty Below)

-60° sat at or near the top of BoardGameGeek's most-wanted list. Alexander Pfister, designer of Great Western Trail and Maracaibo, made it with Peter Prinz. Research teams land on the Antarctic coast, build networks of stations, and push toward a meteorite, with worker placement and deck building at the core. It plays 1 to 4 in about two to three hours.

**Who it's for:** players who want a long, weighty expedition game. **US timing:** Nanox Games publishes it, with distribution through dlp games; we found no confirmed US release date.

### Kingdom Come: Deliverance, The Board Game

Czech Games Edition's adaptation of the video game comes from Tomáš Holek, designer of SETI, and Vlaada Chvátil, the designer of Codenames. You play a poor commoner in 15th-century Bohemia rather than a hero, with more than 500 cards in the box. It plays 1 to 4 at about 90 minutes per player. It is a premium, big-box game.

**Who it's for:** groups who want one long game night with a story. **US timing:** the English edition launched at the fair, and CGE said US preorders ship in the first quarter of 2027.

### Timespan

Timespan is a civilization game from Kenneth Minde and Kristian Amundsen Østby, the team behind Automania and Bad Company, published by Alion. It promises the arc of a civ game, from the Stone Age to space, in about 90 minutes, using tableau and engine building with simultaneous turns. It plays 1 to 5, ages 12 and up, and includes a solo mode.

**Who it's for:** players who love civilization games but not five-hour sessions. **US timing:** retailers list the English edition for late 2026, with some preorders delivering in January 2027.

### Drillers

Drillers from Czech Games Edition is by Adam Španěl and Roman Bednář. Your drilling rig pushes through a collapsing mine, and each card can either add fuel or spend fuel on its main effect, which makes every play a small trade-off. It plays 1 to 4. Reviewers found it accessible; one questioned its long-term variety.

**Who it's for:** players who like card-driven mid-weight games. **US timing:** CGE publishes it in English, and US retailers have listed it since early autumn 2026.

### Tea & Rum

Tea & Rum was the fair's sleeper. Fabien Gridel's worker placement game from Belgian publisher Captain Games plays over five rounds. Its odd twist: in a four-player game, two players win and two lose. It plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes and finished in the top five of the Fairplay expert list.

**Who it's for:** worker placement fans who want something they have not seen before. **US timing:** an English edition was up for US preorder, due around the end of October 2026.

### Minikin City

Minikin City was one of the most talked-about small games of the autumn, and a reviewer at Opinionated Gamers called it a likely standout of Essen. Simone Luciani and Matthieu Verdier designed it for Cranio Creations. Over eight rounds you draft a card and add it to your city; card values and colors set the turn order and rewards for the next round, and cards only score if you fill them with little Minikin figures. It plays 2 to 5 in about 30 minutes (often 15 to 20 in practice), ages 10 and up.

**Who it's for:** anyone who wants a quick opener with real tension. **US timing:** Capstone Games is taking US preorders.

### Nokosu Dice

The Fairplay expert winner is not new: Yusuke Matsumoto's trick-taking game came out in Japan from Engames in 2016, and Pegasus Spiele now publishes it for a wider market. You play number cards and dice into tricks, and the die you leave behind (nokosu means "to leave behind") shapes what you score. It plays 3 to 5 in about 40 to 60 minutes.

**Who it's for:** fans of [The Crew](/articles/the-crew-card-game/) and other trick-taking games who want a fresh twist. **US timing:** some US stores carry it as an import; we found no wide US release announced.

### Vroom

The Fairplay family winner is a 15-minute cooperative racing game designed by Antonin Boccara for Big Moustache Games. Each player moves a small car by a speed card the others cannot see, and the table wins together if every car finishes in its assigned place. A game is three races. It plays 3 to 6, ages 8 and up.

**Who it's for:** families and parties. **US timing:** a French edition has been out since May; no US edition had been announced at press time.

### Cascadia: Alpine Lakes

Cascadia: Alpine Lakes is a standalone follow-up to the 2022 Spiel des Jahres winner from Randy Flynn and Flatout Games. Habitat tiles are now double hexes that can stack to add elevation, and scoring looks at how well wildlife and lakes are surrounded and raised. Flatout pitches it for the whole family, and like the original it plays well at two.

**Who it's for:** families, couples, and anyone who already loves Cascadia. **US timing:** it launched on Kickstarter first; check Flatout Games for retail timing. New to the original? Start with [how to play Cascadia](/academy/cascadia/how-to-play-cascadia/).

## Should you buy games right after Essen?

Not always. Fair buzz is real but early: it comes from first plays, often taught by the publisher's own staff. A few habits help:

- **Wait for a second wave of reviews.** First impressions at a fair skew positive. Reviews written after several home plays are more useful.
- **Check the English edition.** Many fair copies are German or multilingual. Some games change slightly between editions.
- **Try before you buy.** A local game store, library, or game cafe may get demo copies first.

Our guide to [convention buying without regret](/articles/convention-buying-without-regret/) covers more. If you would rather try games in person closer to home, our list of [Midwest board game conventions](/articles/board-game-conventions-midwest/) has eleven smaller shows to consider.

## How does SPIEL connect to the year's awards?

The fair falls between award seasons. The Spiel des Jahres jury announces its winners each summer, judging games released in Germany over the prior year, so many Essen 2026 titles will be eligible for the 2027 lists. This year's winners, [JinxO](/articles/how-to-play-jinxo/) and [Rebirth](/articles/how-to-play-rebirth-board-game/), were both in demand at stands across the fair. For the full history, see our list of [every Spiel des Jahres winner](/articles/spiel-des-jahres-winners/).

## FAQ

### When was SPIEL Essen 2026?

October 22 to 25, 2026, at the Messe Essen fairgrounds in Essen, Germany. About 230,000 people attended, a new record.

### What won the Fairplay scout list at SPIEL 2026?

Nokosu Dice topped the expert list and Vroom topped the family list, according to post-fair reports. Greenwood, Gibberers, Tea & Rum, Vantage, and Drillers also placed near the top of the expert list.

### What was the most hyped game at Essen 2026?

Queen Alice drew the longest lines, with some waits reported at up to three hours, and it shared the top of BoardGameGeek's most-wanted list with -60°.

### When will Essen 2026 games be available in the US?

It varies by game. Minikin City and Greenwood have US publishers in Capstone Games, Timespan and Kingdom Come: Deliverance are listed for late 2026 or early 2027, and some smaller titles have no US edition announced yet.

### Is SPIEL Essen worth attending?

If you want to see the year's new games first and can handle crowds, yes. Tickets are sold online only and the fair has sold out in recent years, so buy early.
