Fun cards in a cube are the ones drafters remember after the night, and strong cards are the ones that win it. The two overlap less than a power-ranked list suggests. Every cube owner, at every slot, is choosing between a card that is slightly stronger and a card that produces a better game, and the choice compounds across 540 slots into the feel of the whole cube.

This article is about making that choice on purpose. It names cards that are strong and dull, cards that are fun and weak, and the smaller set that are both. Then it offers a test for the borderline picks.

## Why is the strongest card often the wrong one?

A cube is not a deck. In a deck, you want the best card for each slot because you are trying to win. In a cube, you are trying to produce eight good decks and twelve good games, and the best card for winning is often the worst card for that.

Three ways strength works against the night:

1. **Strong cards shorten decisions.** Oko, Thief of Crowns answers any permanent and makes a 3/3. There is no decision about which mode to use; the right play is nearly always obvious. Fiery Confluence (three modes, choose three, repeats allowed) is weaker and asks a real question every time.
2. **Strong cards remove the opponent from the game.** The One Ring, Necropotence and Grief are strong because the opponent has little say. Cards that are fun to play against give the opponent a choice: Fact or Fiction lets them split the piles, Gifts Ungiven lets them pick two of four.
3. **Strong cards are the same in every deck.** Jace, the Mind Sculptor is a four-mana Brainstorm engine in a control deck and in a tempo deck and in a ramp deck. Sneak Attack is a different card in every deck that draws it. The second is more fun to draft because the drafter has to imagine the deck.

None of this means cutting strong cards. It means strength is one input and the owner should know what it is costing.

## Which cards are strong but dull?

A short list, each with why it lands here. These are house opinions and reasonable people run every one.

- **Grief.** Free turn-one Thoughtseize plus a 3/2 menace body. The opponent loses their best card before their first land drop and has no decision in it.
- **Thassa's Oracle plus a library-exile spell.** The game ends with no board, no combat and nothing to respond to for most decks.
- **Stasis, Winter Orb, Armageddon.** One player plays Magic. The other watches.
- **Blood Moon.** In a cube with fetchlands and duals it either does nothing or ends the game on the spot. The variance is the problem, not the power.
- **Mind Twist.** Four mana (one black plus X): target player discards X cards at random. Turn-two Ritual into Mind Twist for three is a game that has not started yet and is already over.
- **Thieving Skydiver, Hullbreacher, Opposition Agent.** Hate cards that punish a drafter for drafting the archetype the cube told them to draft.

Every card above is strong. Several are in the current MTGO Vintage Cube. The question for your cube is whether the slot could do the same job with more of the opponent in the game.

## Which cards are fun but weak?

- **Sneak Attack.** Four mana for an enchantment that puts a creature into play with haste for one red, then sacrifices it at end of turn. Needs the right deck, dead otherwise, and produces the best stories in cube.
- **Birthing Pod.** Four mana (two of them Phyrexian green): sacrifice a creature and search for one that costs exactly one more. Builds a chain of decisions every turn and is almost never the best card in the deck.
- **Gifts Ungiven.** Four mana at instant speed: search for four cards with different names, the opponent picks two for your graveyard. A puzzle for both players.
- **Recurring Nightmare, Survival of the Fittest, Oath of Druids.** Engines that reward building around and do nothing for the deck that did not. Survival was cut from the MTGO Vintage Cube in October 2026 as part of a wider move away from creature-cheat; it remains a joy in a cube that supports it.
- **Goblin Welder, Daretti, Scrap Trawler.** Artifact-recursion cards that make a lane exist. Weak in a vacuum.
- **Cogwork Librarian.** A draft-matters card: take two picks now, put Librarian back in the pack. It changes the draft rather than the game, and drafters talk about it for weeks.
- **Chaos Warp, Warp World, Thieves' Auction.** Pure variance. A cube should have one or two of these and no more.
- **Settle the Wreckage, Comeuppance, Deflecting Palm.** Punish cards that reward an opponent's bad read. Weak unless the opponent walks into them, and memorable when they do.

The MTGO Vintage Cube designers named this category directly in their [May 2026 update](https://www.mtgo.com/news/sos-vintage-cube-update): the four- and five-color charms and value piles "might not necessarily clear the competitive bar fully," they wrote, but they are "of the fun variety." That is the Vintage Cube, the most power-focused cube in the game, saying out loud that some slots are for fun.

## Which cards are both?

The best cube cards are the ones where strength and play pattern point the same way. They tend to share three properties: they ask a question, they can be answered, and they are different in different decks.

- **Fact or Fiction.** Strong card advantage; the opponent makes the split.
- **Splinter Twin and its creatures.** A two-card combo where every piece is a playable card, the combo can be broken by any instant-speed removal, and the opponent knows to hold it up.
- **Fiery Confluence.** Six damage to a face, three artifacts destroyed, or a one-sided sweep of X/2s; the caster chooses and the opponent can plan.
- **Recruiter of the Guard, Imperial Recruiter, Stoneforge Mystic.** Tutors that fetch a creature or equipment: strong, and the choice of target is a decision the drafter made in deckbuilding.
- **Glorybringer.** A five-mana hasty flier that exerts to deal four damage to a creature. Strong, answerable, and it asks the caster whether to attack or hold back every turn.
- **Garruk Relentless, Elspeth, Knight-Errant, Chandra, Torch of Defiance.** Walkers with two or three modes that are all playable, where the right one depends on the board.
- **Tireless Tracker, Courser of Kruphix, Dark Confidant.** Card advantage on a body that dies to a Bolt.
- **Wasteland, Karakas, Rishadan Port.** Utility lands that interact without locking.

If your cube has to choose between one card from the strong-but-dull list and one from this one for the same slot, this one wins every time.

## A test for the borderline pick

When a slot comes open and two cards are close in power, ask four questions. Score each yes as one.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Does the opponent get a decision when this card resolves? | Cards that give the opponent a choice produce interactive games. |
| Can a reasonable deck answer this card after it resolves? | Removal, counters, attacking; if none works, it is a lock piece. |
| Is this card different in two different archetypes? | Cards that change by deck make the draft more interesting. |
| Would a drafter tell a story about this card? | The honest measure of fun. Sneak Attack yes; Mind Twist no. |

Three or four: the card is in. Two: it is a cut candidate the next time a stronger card comes along. Zero or one: it had better be very strong, and even then it should be alone in its category. This is a house test, not a standard, and it is the one Cube Commonwealth uses when the power argument is a tie. The broader method is in [cube card selection](/articles/cube-card-selection/).

## Where should the balance sit?

A rough guide by band. Vintage cubes can afford more strong-but-dull cards because everything is strong and the famous cards are the point; a drafter who signs up for Vintage cube signs up for Mind Twist. Legacy and Modern bands should lean hard toward the both list, because the whole reason to play unpowered is to have the interactive games. Pauper cubes get the balance for free, since almost nothing at common removes the opponent from the game.

What does not change by band is the floor. No cube is better for having three lock pieces. One is a known lane; three is a reason players stop coming. [Cube power levels explained](/articles/cube-power-levels-explained/) covers how band and feel interact.

## FAQ

### Should I cut strong cards from my cube if they are not fun?

Cut the ones that remove the opponent from the game or offer no decisions, one at a time, and watch whether the games improve. Keep the strong cards that ask questions.

### What makes a cube card fun to play against?

The opponent gets a choice, has a way to answer it afterward, and can see it coming. Fact or Fiction, Splinter Twin and Glorybringer all pass; Grief and Stasis do not.

### Are build-around cards worth cube slots?

Yes, in small numbers per lane. Sneak Attack, Birthing Pod and Recurring Nightmare each make an archetype exist. Two or three build-arounds per lane is a house guideline; more than that and they go unplayed.

### Is the MTGO Vintage Cube designed for fun or power?

Both, and its designers say so. The May 2026 update added multicolor charms it described as not fully clearing the competitive bar but "of the fun variety."
