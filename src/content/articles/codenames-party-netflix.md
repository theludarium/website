Codenames Party on Netflix is a digital version of the word game Codenames that you play on your TV or in a web browser, with each player's phone as a controller. It arrived on Netflix on September 29, 2026, it was made with Czech Games Edition, the publisher of the board game, and it is included with a Netflix subscription. Up to ten people can play in one room, and nobody needs to download a separate game or own a console.

**The quick answer:** open the Games row in the Netflix app on a supported TV (or play in Chrome or Edge on a computer), choose Codenames Party, and have everyone scan the QR code with their phone. Pick Classic for the Codenames you know, Case Studies to warm up, or the new Party mode, where everyone is a spymaster at once.

Below: what it is, how to set it up, how each mode plays, how it differs from the box, and when the physical game is still the better choice.

## What is Codenames Party on Netflix?

Codenames is Vlaada Chvátil's 2015 word game for two teams. Each team's spymaster knows which words on a 5 by 5 grid belong to their team and gives one-word clues to help teammates find them. Codenames Party brings that to the TV, plus a new mode built for the screen.

## How do you set up Codenames Party on Netflix?

Setup takes about two minutes.

1. **Open Netflix on the big screen.** Use the Netflix app on a supported smart TV or streaming device, or open Netflix in Chrome or Edge on a computer you can connect to a TV.
2. **Find the game.** Scroll to the Games row, or search for Codenames Party, and select Play Game.
3. **Scan the QR code.** Each player points their phone camera at the code on screen and taps the link. On iPhone and iPad, a Netflix controller pop-up opens. On Android, you may need to install the Netflix app first and then scan the code again.
4. **Wait for everyone to connect.** Each phone joins the game as a controller.
5. **Choose a mode and play.**

Two things hosts like to know. Guests do not need their own Netflix account; a phone that joins as a controller does not sign in to anything, according to Netflix's help center. And every player needs their own phone or tablet, because the phone is where secret information lives.

## How does Codenames Party play?

### Classic mode

This is Codenames as you know it, for two teams. The TV shows the 25-word grid to everyone. Each spymaster sees the secret key on their phone, which replaces the cardboard key card and stand. The spymaster gives a clue (one word and one number), and their team picks words. The game reveals each word's identity and keeps score, and the team that reaches the assassin loses on the spot.

If anyone at your table has never played, the rules are short. Our Academy lesson on [how to play Codenames](/academy/codenames/how-to-play-codenames/) covers every rule in a few minutes of reading, including the plus one rule most people forget.

### Case Studies mode

Here the game supplies pre-made clues and word sets, so nobody has to be a spymaster. It works solo or with a group, and it is a gentle way to learn how a good Codenames clue is built before anyone has to give one. It makes a good first ten minutes for a group of friends who don't play yet.

### Party mode

Party mode is new, and it is the reason this version exists. There are no teams. The grid shrinks to 16 words. Each player secretly gets eight target words on their phone and writes one clue that links as many of them as they can. Then everyone guesses at everyone else's clues. You score when other players find your words from your clue, and you score when you correctly find theirs.

The result plays more like a drawing-and-guessing party game than like Codenames. Everyone is a spymaster and a guesser at the same time, so nobody waits through a long turn. The tradeoff is the feeling of a team winning or losing together, which is half of what makes classic Codenames work at a party.

## Codenames Party vs the board game: what is different?

| | Codenames Party (Netflix) | Codenames (the box) |
|---|---|---|
| What you need | Netflix subscription, a supported TV or browser, one phone per player | The box and a table |
| Players | Up to 10 | 2 to 8 on the box, and larger groups work with bigger teams |
| Setup | QR code, about two minutes | Lay out 25 cards and a key card, about two minutes |
| Spymaster's key | On the phone | Cardboard key card in a stand |
| Scoring and reveals | Automatic | Spymaster places tiles by hand |
| Modes | Classic, Case Studies, Party | Classic, plus house rules |
| Play from different homes | No | No (the free official browser version, codenames.game, does this) |
| Screens at the table | Everyone is on a phone | None |
| Takes it on the road | Only where there is a TV and Wi-Fi | Anywhere |

The biggest difference is where people look. With the box, everyone leans over one table and watches the spymaster's face. With the TV version, eyes go to the screen and to phones. That changes the room more than any rule does.

## When is the Codenames box better?

The physical game still wins in most of the situations a host faces. Reach for the box when:

**You want people looking at each other.** Codenames is at its best when you can see a teammate's eyebrows go up at a clue. Phones in hand pull attention down and away.

**You are not at home.** A cabin, a picnic table, a coffee shop, a family reunion, a classroom: the box goes anywhere and needs no Wi-Fi, no TV, and no subscription.

**Someone does not have a phone handy.** Kids, grandparents, and the friend whose battery died can all play the box. The Netflix version needs one device per person.

**You are playing as a couple.** The box family includes [Codenames: Duet](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FJMNPGXM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) , a cooperative two-player version that is one of the best date-night games around. Our lesson on [Codenames Duet strategy](/academy/codenames/codenames-duet-strategy/) shows why.

**You want a different flavor.** [Codenames: Pictures](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01HT9DERU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) , the Disney and Marvel editions, and others all play on the same rules. Our guide to [Codenames variants and editions](/academy/codenames/codenames-variants-and-editions/) sorts them.

**You are running something on purpose.** For a team offsite or a classroom, the box gives you control over teams, pacing, and discussion. Our guide to [Codenames for team building](/articles/codenames-team-building/) explains how to run it.

## When is Netflix the better choice?

The TV version earns its place in a few specific moments:

- **Big groups in a living room.** Ten people can see one big screen more easily than one tabletop.
- **Holiday gatherings with Netflix already on.** No one has to find the box, and the game handles all the bookkeeping.
- **Friends who don't play board games yet.** A familiar screen and a phone in hand feel less like "a board game night," which lowers the stakes for some people. Case Studies teaches clue logic without pressure.
- **Hosts who want to play, not referee.** The game reveals cards, tracks score, and runs the turns.
- **Everyone wants to give clues.** In Party mode, the quiet friend who would never volunteer as spymaster gets to try.

Our view: the Netflix version is a great gateway and a great overflow game for big nights. The box is the one you keep playing.

## How do you give good clues in Codenames Party?

Every mode rewards the same skill: linking words by meaning in a way your table will understand. A few habits help right away:

1. **Clue for two before you clue for four.** A safe two-word clue beats a risky four-word clue that hits the assassin.
2. **Check the danger words first.** Before you say anything, look at the assassin and the other team's words, and rule out clues that touch them.
3. **Know your audience.** A clue that works for your college friends may fail with your parents. Party mode makes this obvious, because you score only when other people get it.
4. **Use the number.** The number tells your team how bold to be. Later in the game, the extra guess lets them pick up words from earlier clues.

## FAQ

### Is Codenames Party free on Netflix?

It is included with a Netflix subscription at no extra cost, and early reviews report no ads or in-app purchases. You do need an active subscription on the account that runs the game. Guests joining with their phones do not need accounts.

### How many players can play Codenames Party on Netflix?

Up to ten players in the same room. Classic mode needs at least two, Party mode needs at least three, and Case Studies can be played solo.

### Can you play Codenames on Netflix with friends in other places?

No. Codenames Party is local multiplayer only, with everyone in the same room. For remote play, Czech Games Edition runs a free official browser version at codenames.game.

### What do you need to play Codenames Party?

A Netflix account, a supported smart TV or a computer running Chrome or Edge, and one phone or tablet per player. Physical game controllers do not work.

### Is Codenames Party the same as the board game?

Classic mode follows the board game's rules, with phones replacing the key card. Case Studies and Party mode are new. Party mode drops teams, uses 16 words instead of 25, and lets everyone give clues at once.
