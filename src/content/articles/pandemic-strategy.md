Pandemic board game strategy changes completely between four epidemic cards and six. At the introductory setting, a competent team wins most games by curing diseases as fast as possible. At Heroic difficulty, with six epidemics in the player deck, that approach loses to the outbreak chain before the third cure. The game stops being about curing and starts being about controlling the infection deck.

This guide assumes you know the rules and have beaten the game at the standard setting. It is written for the group that keeps losing on Heroic and wants to know what they are doing wrong. <!-- AFFILIATE -->

Rules are described generally for the base game, second edition. The number of epidemic cards for each difficulty is printed in the [Z-Man rulebook](https://aadl.org/files/catalog_guides/Pandemic_Rules.pdf): four for Introductory, five for Standard, six for Heroic.

## Why is Heroic difficulty so much harder?

Three reasons, and they compound.

**More epidemics, closer together.** Each epidemic adds three cubes to a new city, reshuffles the infection discard pile onto the top of the infection deck, and increases the infection rate. The player deck holds [59 cards](https://aadl.org/files/catalog_guides/Pandemic_Rules.pdf) including the epidemics, and after four players take two-card opening hands, six epidemics in the remaining fifty-one cards means one every eight or nine cards, which at two cards drawn per turn is roughly one epidemic per round at four players.

**The infection rate climbs faster.** The rate track goes two, two, two, three, three, four, four. With six epidemics you reach four cards per turn before the game is half over. At four cards per turn the board degrades faster than four players can clean it.

**Fewer player cards per cure.** The player deck is the clock. With six epidemics taking up slots, you have fewer city cards to draw, and you need five of a color (four for the Scientist) for each cure. You cannot afford to discard a card you might need.

## What is the single biggest mistake Heroic teams make?

They treat cubes as the problem. Cubes are a symptom. The problem is the infection discard pile.

Every epidemic puts the discard pile back on top of the deck. The cities in that pile are the cities that will be infected next, in some order, before any new city is touched. That means after the first epidemic, you know exactly which cities are at risk, and there are not many of them.

Heroic strategy is therefore:

1. After each epidemic, read the discard pile before it is shuffled, or remember it. Those cities are your whole world until the next epidemic.
2. Keep the cities in that pile at two cubes or fewer. A city at three cubes in the known-danger set is an outbreak waiting to happen.
3. Ignore cities that are not in the pile unless they are at three cubes already. They cannot be drawn until the pile is exhausted.

Teams that lose spend actions treating a city at one cube on the far side of the board because it "looks bad." It is not bad. It is not going to be drawn. The city at two cubes that was discarded last turn is the one that will kill you.

## How should you choose and use roles in Pandemic?

Roles are dealt randomly in the standard rules, but many groups allow a choice, and in either case you should know what each one is for at this difficulty.

| Role | What it does for you on Heroic | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Medic | Removes all cubes of a color in one action; removes cured colors by presence | Keep moving through the danger set; never sit still |
| Quarantine Specialist | Prevents cube placement in her city and all neighbors | Park her on the hub of the danger cluster and leave her there |
| Scientist | Cures with four cards instead of five | Everyone funnels their cards to her at a research station |
| Researcher | Gives any city card to a player in the same city | The card engine that makes the Scientist work |
| Dispatcher | Moves other pawns as an action | Moves the Medic into position so the Medic can treat |
| Operations Expert | Builds research stations anywhere; moves from a station to anywhere by discarding | Builds stations near the danger set, not spread evenly |
| Contingency Planner | Reuses a discarded event card | Hold back a second Resilient Population or One Quiet Night |

The team you want on Heroic is usually some combination of Quarantine Specialist, Medic, and a card-sharing engine (Scientist plus Researcher, or Dispatcher to collect people). The Quarantine Specialist is the best role in the game at six epidemics, because she turns a dangerous cluster into a dead zone without spending any actions.

If you are dealt roles without a card engine, slow down. You will cure diseases later than you would like, and your actions will go mostly to cube control. That is fine. The game is won by curing four diseases, not by curing them fast.

## How do you use the event cards on Heroic?

The five event cards are the most powerful cards in the deck, and new teams waste them.

- **Resilient Population** removes one card from the infection discard pile from the game. Use it right after an epidemic, before the pile is reshuffled, on the city you can least afford to see again. This card alone prevents more outbreaks than any role.
- **One Quiet Night** skips the next infection step. Use it when the next infection step would draw from a freshly reshuffled pile and the board is already hot. Do not use it on a quiet turn just because you have it.
- **Forecast** lets you look at the top six infection cards and rearrange them. Use it to put the cities you have under control on top and the dangerous ones at the bottom, buying time.
- **Airlift** moves any pawn to any city. Best spent to put the Medic or Quarantine Specialist where they need to be the turn you need them.
- **Government Grant** builds a research station without the city card. Use it to put a station in the middle of the danger set so curing does not require a cross-map trip.

Event cards can be played at any time, including during another player's turn, and they do not cost an action. Hold them until the moment they prevent an outbreak, and no later.

## When should you cure in Pandemic, and when should you wait?

A cure removes the long-term threat of a color, and if the Medic is in play, it makes that color trivial to clean. But a cure costs five cards, and the trip to a research station costs actions. Both are scarce.

Cure as soon as a player has the cards and a station is within two or three moves. Do not wait to eradicate (remove every cube of a cured color); eradication is nice, not necessary.

Do not plan to cure in a specific order. Cure whatever color the cards give you. Teams that decide "blue first" and discard yellow cards to hold blue ones end up with no cure at all when the deck runs short.

Keep an eye on the player deck count. On Heroic, running out of player cards is a real way to lose, and it sneaks up on teams who are focused on the board. If the deck is under fifteen cards and you have two cures, the remaining two must come from cards already in hands. Count them.

## How do you handle the endgame?

Most Heroic losses come in one of two shapes.

**The chain.** A city at three cubes outbreaks into a neighbor at three cubes, which outbreaks again. Two or three outbreaks in one infection step can take the outbreak marker from a safe four to a losing eight. The prevention is upstream: no city in the danger set at three cubes, ever, and the Quarantine Specialist parked on the busiest neighbor.

**The deck.** The team has three cures and is two cards short of the fourth when the player deck runs out. The prevention is earlier: give cards to the player who is closest to a cure every time you share a city, even when it feels premature.

If you are losing on Heroic more often than you win, that is normal. The setting is designed to beat good teams most of the time. The point is the conversation around the table, the plan that almost worked, and the group that wants to try again. Heroic difficulty makes that worse. Agree before you start that each player announces their own plan.

The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### How many epidemic cards is Heroic in Pandemic?

Six in the [second edition rulebook](https://aadl.org/files/catalog_guides/Pandemic_Rules.pdf), with Introductory at four and Standard at five. Check your printing, because some editions label the tiers differently.

### What is the best role in Pandemic on hard difficulty?

The Quarantine Specialist, because she prevents cube placement in a whole cluster without spending actions. The Medic and the Scientist are close behind.

### Should you eradicate a disease in Pandemic?

Only if it is cheap. Eradication means that color's cards do nothing when drawn, which helps, but it is rarely worth the actions on Heroic compared with controlling the discard pile.

### Why do we keep losing to outbreaks?

Because a city in the infection discard pile reached three cubes before the next epidemic reshuffled it to the top. Treat the known-danger cities first, always.
