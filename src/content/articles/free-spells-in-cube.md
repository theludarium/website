Free spells in a cube break the one rule the rest of the game is built on: you get one land a turn, so you get one turn's worth of action a turn. A spell that costs zero mana, or that lets you exile a card from hand instead of paying, gives its caster two actions in a turn where everyone else gets one. In a singleton draft format, the players who end up with those cards are playing with a different clock.

This article sorts the free spells into families, says what each family does to a table, and gives a line that keeps the best of them without letting them run the format.

## What makes a free spell different from a cheap one?

A one-mana spell is cheap. It still costs the turn-one land drop. A zero-mana spell costs nothing on the turn it is cast, and more importantly it can be cast on the opponent's turn while your mana is tapped. That second property is the problem.

Consider a control deck that taps out for a four-mana planeswalker on turn four. Against a cube with no free spells, the opponent's answer must wait, and the walker gets one activation. Against a cube with Force of Will, the walker can be countered for zero mana on the turn it is cast by a player who is also tapped out. Force of Will does not just counter a spell. It removes the cost of holding up mana, which removes the tempo trade that makes control decks beatable.

The same logic applies to the aggro side. Gitaxian Probe (two life instead of one blue mana: look at an opponent's hand, draw a card) and Mutagenic Growth (two life instead of one green: plus two plus two) let a one-land hand do two things on turn one. Both are in the current MTGO Vintage Cube, and the designers said in August 2025 that they added Mutagenic Growth to give Vivi Ornitier "more cheap/free spells" to convert into mana ([source](https://www.mtgo.com/news/eoe-vintage-cube-update)). It left the cube in January 2026 and came back in October 2026 as a prowess enabler. That is free spells as a supported engine, which is a legitimate choice in a Vintage cube and a dangerous one anywhere else.

## Family one: the pitch counterspells

- **Force of Will.** Five mana, or exile a blue card from hand and pay one life: counter target spell. The defining free spell. It exists so that blue decks can stop a turn-one combo, and it also stops a turn-four planeswalker for free.
- **Force of Negation.** Three mana, or exile a blue card if it is not your turn: counter target noncreature spell, exile it. Narrower, and only free on the opponent's turn.
- **Daze.** Two mana, or return an Island you control to hand: counter target spell unless its controller pays one. Free on turn one or two, nearly dead on turn six.
- **Misdirection, Foil, Commandeer.** Older pitch spells, rarely worth a slot now.

All three of the first group are in the MTGO Vintage Cube. In an unpowered cube, Force of Will's job shrinks. There is no turn-one Tinker to stop, so it ends up countering a Signet or a three-drop at the cost of two cards. Many Legacy-band owners keep it anyway because drafters expect it; it is a reasonable cut if you want blue to pay for its answers.

## Family two: the evoke elementals

The Modern Horizons 2 cycle changed the free-spell conversation more than any set since Alliances. Each is a creature that can be cast for its mana cost or evoked for free by exiling a card of its color from hand.

- **Solitude** (white): exile another target creature; its controller gains life equal to its power. Free Swords to Plowshares, and a 3/2 lifelink if you pay five.
- **Fury** (red): deal four damage divided among any number of creatures and planeswalkers. Free one-sided sweeper against aggro.
- **Grief** (black): target opponent reveals hand, you choose a nonland card, they discard it. Free Thoughtseize on turn one.
- **Endurance** (green): target player puts their graveyard on the bottom of their library. Flash 3/4 reach. Free graveyard hate.
- **Subtlety** (blue): put target creature or planeswalker spell on top or bottom of its owner's library. The weakest of the five.

All five are in the current MTGO list. Each is individually a good card and together they do something specific to a cube: they make the color's best removal free, which means a tapped-out opponent is never safe. Fury in particular ended several aggro decks' viability in high-powered cubes, because the two-drop deck that was ahead on turn three loses its whole board to a spell that cost zero. If [aggro in cube](/articles/aggro-in-cube/) is a lane you care about, Fury is the first free spell to test cutting.

## Family three: the zero-mana cantrips and pump

- **Gitaxian Probe**: Phyrexian mana, look at a hand and draw.
- **Mutagenic Growth**: Phyrexian mana, plus two plus two.
- **Gush**: five mana, or return two Islands to hand: draw two. Free card advantage that also refuels landfall.
- **Manamorphose**: two mana, add two mana, draw a card. Nets zero mana and a card.
- **Snuff Out**: four mana, or pay four life if you control a Swamp: destroy target nonblack creature. Free removal for black aggro.
- **Fireblast**: six mana, or sacrifice two Mountains: four damage to any target. The red aggro finisher.
- **Dismember**: one plus two Phyrexian: minus five minus five. Usually cast for one mana and four life.

Snuff Out and Fireblast are both in the current MTGO list and both are good for aggro, which is the reverse of the elementals. Free spells are not uniformly a problem for any one archetype; they are a problem for whichever archetype does not have them. If you cut the evoke elementals for aggro's sake, consider keeping Snuff Out and Fireblast for the same reason.

## Family four: the free permanents

- **Mox Diamond, Chrome Mox, Lotus Petal, Mana Crypt**: fast mana rather than free spells, but they share the property and belong in the same count.
- **Ornithopter, Memnite, Phyrexian Walker**: zero-mana creatures. Only matter with affinity or Mox Opal synergies.
- **Mishra's Bauble, Urza's Bauble**: zero-mana artifacts that draw a card next turn. Harmless alone, strong with delirium and Lurrus.
- **Engineered Explosives**: the October 2026 update added it partly as a zero-mana spell that enables The Fantasticar. Zero-mana artifacts are the quiet edge of this category.

## What does each family cost the format?

| Family | Example | Who it punishes | Keep in a Legacy-band cube? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch counters | Force of Will, Daze | Anyone who taps out | Force yes, Daze optional |
| Evoke elementals | Solitude, Fury, Grief | Aggro, especially red and white | Solitude and Endurance yes; test Fury and Grief |
| Zero-mana cantrips | Probe, Growth, Gush | Nobody directly; fuel storm | Cut Probe and Growth; Gush only with landfall |
| Free removal | Snuff Out, Fireblast, Dismember | Midrange creatures | Yes, they help aggro |
| Free permanents | Baubles, Explosives | Nobody; fuel synergies | Optional |

That last column is a house guideline. Vintage-band cubes keep all of it; Pauper cubes have almost none of it beyond Gitaxian Probe, Mutagenic Growth, Snuff Out and Fireblast, each of which has a common printing.

## Where should an unpowered cube draw the line?

Our recommendation, in order of what to cut first if free spells are warping your table:

1. **Gitaxian Probe and Mutagenic Growth.** Free information and free pump have no archetype that needs them outside storm. Cut both.
2. **Fury and Grief.** The two elementals that most reshape games. Fury blanks aggro; Grief on turn one followed by a two-drop is a different game than the opponent signed up for.
3. **Daze.** Strong on turn two, dead on turn eight, and the variance is not fun for either player.
4. **Force of Will.** Keep it unless you have also cut the fast combos. Once Sneak and Show and Reanimator are gone, Force is protecting against nothing and costing blue two cards.

Keep Solitude, Endurance, Snuff Out and Fireblast. They are free spells that make games more interactive, not less, and three of the four help the archetype most hurt by the others. ## FAQ

### Is Force of Will good in an unpowered cube?

It is playable but it is a worse card than its reputation. With no turn-one combo to stop, it mostly trades two cards for one on a fair spell. Many unpowered owners keep it for drafters' expectations.

### Are the evoke elementals too strong for cube?

Solitude and Fury are the two that change games most. Both are fine in a powered cube and worth testing in anything lower, especially if aggro is underperforming.

### Which free spells can a Pauper cube run?

Gitaxian Probe, Mutagenic Growth, Snuff Out, Fireblast, Gut Shot and Manamorphose all have common printings. Most Pauper lists run two or three of them and skip Probe.

### Does Phyrexian mana count as free?

Yes. Paying two life is not a mana cost, so the spell can be cast with no lands and on any turn. Treat Phyrexian-mana spells as zero-mana spells when you count.

## Count the zeros

Sort your list by mana value and look at everything that can be cast for zero. If it is more than a dozen in a Legacy-band cube, you have more than you think.
