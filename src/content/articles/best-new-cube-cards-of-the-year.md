The best cube cards this year came from the two-drop slot. That is the headline after six Standard-legal sets between January and October 2026 and the six set reviews in this series. Erode and Bitterbloom Bearer are the two cards we would tell any cube owner to find. Stingcaster Mage, Emeritus of Conflict, Smaug the Magnificent and Bloom Tender round out the cards we would keep outright. Below is the full accounting: what we would keep, what we would pass, what we would still test, and what the year says about where new cards are coming from.

Each set has its own review in this series; this article is the summary. Set release dates are from Wizards' collecting articles for each set, for example [the Reality Fracture one](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-reality-fracture).

## Which cards earned a permanent slot?

Our standard for "keep" is that the card beats a named incumbent on the slot comparison and we would expect it to survive three drafts without anyone at the table asking for the old card back. Six cards meet it.

| Card | Set | Slot | Would replace | Why we would keep it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Erode ({W}, instant) | Secrets of Strixhaven | W / 1 / removal | A conditional two-mana white removal spell | One mana, hits walkers, destroys rather than exiles. White needed a third one-mana answer. |
| Bitterbloom Bearer ({B}{B}, 1/1 flash flyer) | Lorwyn Eclipsed | B / 2 / engine | A three-mana black value creature | A second Bitterblossom. Blocks, carries Equipment, is a Faerie. |
| Bloom Tender ({1}{G}, dork) | Lorwyn Eclipsed (reprint) | G / 2 / mana | A colorless two-mana rock | Taps for two in any two-color deck from turn three. |
| Emeritus of Conflict ({1}{R}, 2/2 first strike) | Secrets of Strixhaven | R / 2 / aggro | A vanilla-adjacent red two-drop | Playable body; a free Lightning Bolt on the turns red spells decks go off. |
| Stingcaster Mage ({1}{R}, 2/1 haste) | Reality Fracture | R / 2 / aggro | A three-mana red spells payoff | Haste on turn two, Bolt from the graveyard on turn four. |
| Smaug the Magnificent ({2}{R}{R}, 4/3 flying haste) | The Hobbit | R / 4 / finisher | A five-mana red dragon | Hasty flier that ramps and burns. Closes games the turn after it lands. |

Four of the six are two-drops. Three of the six are red. Both facts are telling. The two-drop slot is where Wizards is pushing power in Standard, because it is where Standard decks live, and red is the color whose cube incumbents are oldest and most vulnerable to a well-costed creature. If your red two-drops have not changed in five years, this was the year to look at them. Our earlier article on keeping aggro alive in cube makes the longer argument.

## Which tests failed, and why?

Passing is the normal result. A sample of the cards we would pass, with the reason:

- **The Sentry, Golden Guardian** (Marvel Super Heroes). A 5/5 indestructible flier for four that gives the opponent one too. Two 5/5 indestructible fliers staring at each other is a stalled game, and stalled games are the least fun thing a cube card can produce.
- **Emeritus of Abundance** (Secrets of Strixhaven). A 3/4 vigilance for three with a Regrowth that will rarely re-trigger. Fine, and a late pick.
- **Loki, God of Mischief** (Marvel Super Heroes). The draw trigger needs abilities that target, and blue in most cubes does not target with abilities.
- **Hexhaven Invigorator** (Reality Fracture). GGGG on turn four is not a cost a two-color draft deck pays.
- **Leonardo, Cutting Edge** (TMNT). A 1/1 lifelink that needs lifegain support most cubes do not have.
- **Krang, Utrom Warlord** (TMNT). A reanimation target that wins the game it comes back in and sits in the sideboard otherwise. Nine mana is nine mana.

The pattern is the same as every year: cards that need a specific supporting lane fail in a cube that does not have the lane, no matter how strong the card looks in Standard or Commander. The slot method is in [how to evaluate new cards for cube](/articles/how-to-evaluate-new-cards-for-cube/) and it flags four of these six on paper.

## Which cards would we still test?

Some cards we would neither keep nor pass on paper as this is written in October. These are the ones we would run through three drafts before deciding.

- **The Theorist, Jace Beleren** ({2}{U}{U}, Reality Fracture). Draws every opponent's draw step. Strong on paper and probably not oppressive. Leaning keep.
- **Super Shredder** ({1}{B}, TMNT). A menace one-drop body that grows whenever anything leaves. Good in sacrifice decks, a bear elsewhere. Leaning keep as the sacrifice lane's two-drop.
- **Deceit** ({4}{U/B}{U/B}, Lorwyn Eclipsed). The one Incarnation that matters. Expect it to be evoked on turn two far more often than cast. Leaning keep.
- **The Mind Stone** ({1}{W}, Marvel Super Heroes). White control players will love it; nobody else will draft it. That is fine for a rock. Leaning keep.
- **Casey Jones, Vigilante** ({1}{R}{R}, TMNT). A 4/3 that draws three for three mana. The random discard will cost games. Undecided.
- **Darklight Phoenix** ({3}{B}, Reality Fracture). Too new to call; it depends on how many creatures die per turn in your black decks.
- **Garruk, Veiled Butcher** ({3}{B}{B}, Reality Fracture). Also new. The +2 should kill a two-drop most games it is played.

## What did the year teach us about reading sets?

Three lessons we are writing into the primer.

**Universes Beyond sets are now Standard sets.** Three of the six releases this year carried a licensed property, and Wizards' collecting articles describe each of [Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles), Marvel Super Heroes and The Hobbit as legal in all formats. If your cube's policy is "no Universes Beyond," you skipped half the year's new cards, including Smaug and the Mind Stone. That is a defensible choice, and it should be a deliberate one. We discuss the policy question in [supplemental sets and cube](/articles/supplemental-sets-for-cube/).

**Prepare and sneak are the mechanics that travelled.** Both put a known effect on a creature body, so the slot comparison was easy. Mechanics that need density, like empower Jace, choose-a-creature-type, and Hero tribal, produced zero keeps across all six sets. When a new mechanic appears, ask whether a single copy of the card does something. If not, skip the whole mechanic.

**Reprints are good news and not new cards.** Chandra, Torch of Defiance and Tarmogoyf in Reality Fracture, Bloom Tender and five shock lands in Lorwyn Eclipsed, and the five Innistrad slow lands in Secrets of Strixhaven all came back this year at Standard prices; [MTGGoldfish's set indexes](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/index/FRA-main_set) list each of them in the main set. None of that changes a list that already runs them, and all of it makes the budget version of a cube cheaper to build. If you are building a first cube now, the year's reprints are a real gift.

## FAQ

### What is the single best new cube card of 2026?

Erode. A one-mana white instant that destroys a creature or planeswalker belongs in every cube at every power level, and it is the only card this year we would call an automatic include.

### How many new cards should a cube add per year?

For a 540-card list at Legacy or Vintage power, five to ten is normal in our experience. Six keeps across six sets is right on that line. More than fifteen usually means the list had soft slots.

### Are Universes Beyond cards worth considering for cube?

Yes, if your cube allows them. Smaug the Magnificent and The Mind Stone were two of the better cards of the year. If your cube excludes them for aesthetic reasons, that is a legitimate choice to make once and apply consistently.

### Which color got the most new cube cards this year?

Red, with three keeps, all aggressive creatures. Black and white got one new keep each plus the Bloom Tender reprint for green. Blue's best new card, The Theorist, is a test rather than a keep.
