Every Gen Con survival guide tells you to drink water. This one tells you why Saturday afternoon is when people fall apart, and how to arrange Thursday and Friday so you do not. Gen Con is four days, [more than 74,000 people](https://boardgamewire.com/index.php/2026/08/05/gen-con-tops-74000-attendees-as-record-breaking-show-preps-for-much-needed-venue-hotel-expansion/), two connected buildings the size of several city blocks, and a downtown that runs on convention time. Your schedule is a plan. Your body is the constraint. This guide is about the constraint.

The logistics below reflect the 2026 show (July 30 to August 2) at the Indiana Convention Center and Lucas Oil Stadium; the next show is [August 5 to 8, 2027](https://www.gencon.com/). Hours and policies can change year to year.

## How much walking is Gen Con, really?

More than a theme park. The Indiana Convention Center is long, Lucas Oil Stadium is connected by an enclosed walkway at the far end, and events are spread across both plus the downtown hotels. A normal day is many miles on foot for people who move between halls, and there are long stretches of hard floor.

Three rules handle most of it.

1. **Shoes you have already walked in.** Not new ones. Bring two pairs and alternate days; the change in pressure points matters more than the brand.
2. **Group your events by building.** When you build your wish list, put morning events in one building and afternoon events in the other, so you cross the connector once, not six times.
3. **Sit down on purpose.** Open gaming halls, hotel lobbies, and the steps outside are free. Twenty minutes off your feet every two hours is the difference between Saturday night and Saturday nap.

## What should you eat, and when?

The convention center's food is fine and expensive, and the lines at noon are a full event ticket's worth of time. Eat off-peak and eat outside.

- **Breakfast before you arrive.** The Exhibit Hall opens at 10 a.m.; eat at 8.
- **Lunch at 11 or 2.** Never at 12:30.
- **Snacks in the bag.** Nuts, jerky, a few bars, fruit that survives a backpack. One real meal plus steady snacks beats three failed attempts at a food court.
- **Dinner downtown.** The Georgia Street corridor, Mass Ave, and the restaurants around Monument Circle are within a fifteen-minute walk. Reservations help on Friday and Saturday.
- **Water you carry.** A refillable bottle and the knowledge that fountains exist. Soda and coffee do not count as water, whatever Saturday tells you.

Food trucks park around the convention center during the show, and the free [Block Party on South Street](https://www.visitindy.com/blog/post/things-to-do-in-indy-during-gen-con-2026-without-a-badge/) outside the Indiana Convention Center had trucks, vendors, and entertainment in 2026. It is a good lunch move when the halls are packed.

## How do you sleep enough at a convention built to keep you up?

Open gaming runs past midnight, and the best pickup games start late. You will not sleep eight hours. Aim for six and protect them.

| Habit | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Stay downtown if you can | Walking back at 1 a.m. beats a 30-minute drive and a parking garage |
| Book nothing before 10 a.m. on Friday and Saturday | The late nights are where the convention is; give them room |
| Shower at night | Morning becomes faster and you sleep cleaner after a day on concrete |
| One early night | Thursday or Friday, in bed by eleven, buys Saturday back |
| Earplugs | Hotel walls are thin and conventions are loud |

Hotels in the downtown block sell out quickly when housing opens, usually about two weeks after badges go on sale. We cover the options in [the Gen Con hotel guide](/articles/gen-con-hotel-guide/). The short version: close and small beats far and large.

## How do you work the Exhibit Hall without losing a day?

The Exhibit Hall is the largest room in tabletop, with hundreds of publishers, demos at nearly every booth, and convention-only releases. It runs [10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Thursday through Saturday and 10 to 4 on Sunday](https://www.gencon.com/attend/policies). Treat it as three passes, not one.

**Pass one, Thursday morning.** Walk the whole floor once without buying anything. Note booths you want to return to. This is the least crowded the hall will be all weekend, and it is the only time you can see the layout clearly.

**Pass two, Friday or Saturday.** Demos. Go back to the five booths that caught you, sit down, and play. A fifteen-minute demo tells you more than any review. This is where you decide what to buy.

**Pass three, Sunday.** Buy. Some vendors discount on the last day to avoid shipping stock home, and you only carry your purchases for a few hours instead of three days. The exception is a convention exclusive with a limited run; buy that when you see it.

Two more things. Bring a folding bag or a sturdy tote for the trip out on Sunday (carts and rolling luggage are not allowed in the Exhibit Hall), and set a dollar limit before you walk in. Writing it on your hand sounds silly and works.

## What goes in the bag?

Smaller than you think. A full packing list is in [the Gen Con packing list](/articles/gen-con-packing-list/); the daily carry is this.

- Badge on a lanyard (it carries your e-tickets), plus any paper tickets for the day
- Water bottle, snacks, a small first-aid kit with blister pads and painkillers
- Phone battery pack; signal in the halls is poor and the app drains it
- Hand sanitizer, because you will touch ten thousand components
- Generic tickets, a pen, dice, and a pencil
- A light layer; the halls are cold and the street is hot

Leave the giant backpack at the hotel. It bangs into tables and you will carry it for ten miles.

## Why does the Saturday crash happen?

Because three days of poor sleep, poor food, concrete floors, and sensory load land at once. The hall is loudest, hottest, and fullest on Saturday afternoon. People who are fine at noon are short with their friends by three and miserable by five.

The fix is upstream. Eat breakfast on Thursday. Take the early night on Friday. Keep Saturday afternoon unscheduled and spend it somewhere quiet: open gaming, a hotel lobby, the Canal Walk ten minutes away. The costume parade and the big evening events are better when you arrive rested. Introverts in particular should read our [convention pacing plan](/articles/conventions-for-introverts/).

## FAQ

### What should I wear to Gen Con?

Broken-in walking shoes, layers for cold halls and hot streets, and anything with pockets. Costumes are welcome; plan for the walking either way.

### Can I bring food into Gen Con?

Snacks in a bag are normal and widely done. Eat your main meals off-peak or at downtown restaurants to avoid noon lines.

### When is the best time to visit the Gen Con Exhibit Hall?

Thursday morning at opening for the lightest crowds, and Sunday for buying, when some vendors discount and you carry purchases home the same day.

### How many events should I schedule per day at Gen Con?

One or two. Leave at least half of each day open for the Exhibit Hall, demos, and open gaming.

## Take care of the player

The convention will be there all four days. Make sure you are too. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
