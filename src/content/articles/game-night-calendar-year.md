A game night calendar does for a group what a syllabus does for a class: it tells people what is coming, so they can decide to come. Most game groups program one week at a time, which means every week is a fresh negotiation about what to play and whether to bother. A calendar set once, in January, removes that friction for twelve months.

This is the calendar we use. One featured night per month, matched to the season, with regular nights in between. Each entry has the theme, the games, and the reason it lands in that month and not another. Use it as written or swap months to fit your group.

## Why match game nights to the season?

Because the season is already doing half the work. People's energy, schedule, and appetite for company change across the year in predictable ways. January is full of resolutions and empty evenings. July is hot and people are traveling. November is tense with family plans. A calendar that ignores this fights the current; one that uses it coasts.

It also gives each night a reason beyond "it's Tuesday." A cooperative night in February is a game night. A cooperative night in February called "Everyone Loses Together" the week after Valentine's Day is an event people text each other about.

If you want the longer list of themes to draw from, our [themed game night ideas](/articles/themed-game-night-ideas/) has twelve with setup notes. This calendar picks and sequences them.

### January: Gateway Night

The year starts with the door open. Every regular brings one person who has never come, and the games are only the ones that teach in five minutes: Ticket to Ride <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Azul <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Codenames <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Sushi Go! <!-- AFFILIATE -->. January is when people are looking for something new to do, and a game night that is built for newcomers catches them. Some of these guests become regulars by March.

### February: Co-op Night

The shortest month, the coldest weather, and the fewest reasons to leave the house. Everyone on one side: Pandemic <!-- AFFILIATE -->, The Crew <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Forbidden Island <!-- AFFILIATE --> for the newer players. Run it the week after Valentine's Day with the rule that at least one table has to lose. The groups who were a little tense in January are not by the end of this one.

### March: Bracket Night

March is when the rest of the country is filling out brackets, so borrow the energy. One fast game, single or double elimination, a small trophy. Love Letter <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Hive <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Jaipur <!-- AFFILIATE -->. Our guide to [running a board game tournament](/articles/how-to-run-a-board-game-tournament/) covers formats; for most groups of twelve or more, Swiss is the right choice and the bracket is for the final four.

### April: Shelf of Shame

Spring cleaning, applied to games. Everyone brings one game they own and have never played, and the owner is not allowed to read the rules in advance. This is a low-key month after the tournament, and it is where the group discovers the game that becomes the summer favorite.

### May: Outdoor and Dexterity

The first evenings warm enough to open the door. Move the tables to a patio, a yard, or a park shelter. Dexterity games that do not mind wind: Crokinole if anyone owns a board, Klask <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Jenga, Rhino Hero <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Molkky <!-- AFFILIATE --> or cornhole for the lawn. Card games stay inside this month.

### June: Two-Player Night

Wedding season, graduation season, and the month when attendance gets unpredictable. Two-player tables handle an odd count better than anything else: set up five of them, rotate opponents every game, and nobody plays the same person twice. Patchwork <!-- AFFILIATE -->, 7 Wonders Duel <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Hive, Jaipur.

### July: Big-Box Party Night

Holiday weekends mean half the group is away and the other half has visiting relatives. Program for a crowd of unknown size and composition: Wavelength <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Just One <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Telestrations <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Codenames. Potluck, loose start time, no game with more than one page of rules. It is the easiest month to host and the one most likely to bring a partner who "doesn't play games."

### August: Gen Con Debrief

Gen Con runs in Indianapolis around the turn of July and August each year ([August 5–8 in 2027](https://www.gencon.com/)), and whether your group goes or not, August is when the new releases land and the people who went come back with stories. Everyone who attended brings one new game and teaches it. Everyone who did not gets to play things that are a month old. If your group is anywhere near Indiana, this is the month to plan a trip around.

### September: League Kickoff

School starts, schedules settle, and people want a routine. Start a ten-week league that ends before Thanksgiving. Placement points each week, best eight of ten count, standings on a shared sheet. The [board game league format](/articles/board-game-league-format/) we use handles absences without drama. The featured night is week one; the league runs through the regular nights of September, October, and early November.

### October: Social Deduction

Bluffing, hidden roles, and lying to your friends have a month, and it is this one. One big table: The Resistance: Avalon <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Blood on the Clocktower <!-- AFFILIATE --> if someone can run it, Werewolf if the group is big. Phones in a bowl. Run it the weekend before Halloween with the lights lower than usual.

### November: Family Table

Thanksgiving week is not the time for a heavy strategy game or an elimination bracket. It is the time for a table that a grandparent, a teenager, and a brother-in-law can all sit at. Multigenerational games that forgive everything: Ticket to Ride, Kingdomino <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Telestrations, Sushi Go!. The league final lands the week before, so the standings are settled and the room is relaxed.

### December: One Game, All Night

End the year the way a group ends a good dinner: warm, familiar, unhurried. One game the group loves, played three or four times in a row, with a few minutes of talk between games. Catan <!-- AFFILIATE -->, Dominion <!-- AFFILIATE -->, or whatever the year's Shelf of Shame discovery turned out to be. Schedule it early in the month, before the calendar fills.

## How do regular nights fit around the featured ones?

The featured night is one night a month. The other weeks are regular game nights, and they should feel regular: people bring what they want, tables form on their own, nothing is announced. The contrast is the point. A featured night is memorable because the weeks around it are comfortable.

Two exceptions. During the September-to-November league, regular nights are league nights; that is the whole idea of a league. And in months with a fifth week, the extra night is a good time for the thing that does not fit anywhere else: a puzzle night, a learn-to-play night for a bigger game, or a drop-in RPG table.

## How do you set the calendar with a group?

In December or early January, at a regular night, with everyone there.

1. Put the twelve months on a sheet with the suggested theme for each.
2. Ask what to swap. Someone will want Social Deduction in a different month; let them argue for it.
3. Pick dates. One featured night per month, on a fixed week (the second Tuesday, the last Friday). Put all twelve dates on the sheet.
4. Assign a host per month. The host for a featured night owns the games, the announcement, and the rule that keeps the theme intact.
5. Share it. A shared calendar, a pinned message, a printed sheet on the fridge.

That is the entire planning meeting. Twenty minutes, once a year.

## FAQ

### How often should a game group have a themed night?

Once a month. Regular nights in between give people their familiar games; the monthly theme gives them a reason to come on the nights they might skip.

### What is a good game night theme for winter?

Co-op Night in February and Gateway Night in January both suit long, cold evenings: low conflict, easy to join, and good for bringing new people in.

### What is a good game night theme for summer?

Outdoor dexterity games in May, two-player rotations in June when attendance is uneven, and party games with a potluck in July when the group is half visitors.

### How far ahead should you plan game nights?

Set the featured night for every month once a year, with dates. Regular nights need no planning beyond a fixed weekday.
