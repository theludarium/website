People who read this series ask how to support a local game cafe that does not have a door yet. It is a kind question with a practical answer, and most of the answer is free. This article ranks the ways you can help The Ludarium open in Indianapolis, from the thing that costs nothing and matters most, to the founding membership we will eventually offer. We have tried to be plain about which ones move the needle.

## What helps most: subscribe and tell us who you are

The newsletter list is the business right now. Not the brand, not the articles, not the plan. The list.

Here is why. Every decision ahead of us, where to put the first space, how big to make it, what price a membership should be, which city comes second, depends on knowing how many people want this and where they live. We laid that dependency out in [the membership math](/articles/game-lounge-membership-model/): a room needs a few hundred members, and the only way to know whether they exist before signing a lease is to count the people who said yes beforehand.

So the single most useful thing you can do is subscribe, and when we send the short survey asking where you live and how often you would come, answer it. Five minutes, no money, and it is the data we will take to a landlord.

## What helps almost as much: bring one person

A game lounge is a network business. Its value to each member comes from the other members. That means every person you bring is worth more than yourself, because they make the room better for you and for everyone else.

You do not have to pitch it. Forward one article to the friend who used to host game night and stopped. Mention it to the coworker who said they miss playing. Send the togethering piece to someone who will understand it. We would rather have one hundred people who came because a friend told them than a thousand who saw an ad.

The person we are building for is not the hobbyist, though they are welcome. It is the adult who misses the table and does not know where to find one. You probably know three of them.

## What helps and pays us: book a corporate session

If you manage a team or know someone in HR, the corporate tabletop events service is the one thing you can do today that puts money toward the room. A facilitated session for a team in Indianapolis, run by us, in your space or an offsite venue.

We explained why events come before the lounge in [why corporate game nights come before the space](/articles/corporate-game-nights-before-a-space/). The short version: events earn revenue without rent, teach us to host, and introduce us to people who would never walk into a game cafe. Every session we run is a session closer to a lease we can afford.

If a full session is more than your team needs, a referral helps nearly as much. Tell us who to talk to.

## What helps and costs nothing: come to a public event

Before there is a room, we will run public game nights in borrowed and rented spaces around Indianapolis. <!-- EDITOR: add detail when a schedule exists --> Showing up to those does three things.

1. It lets us practice hosting on real strangers, which is the skill the whole business rests on.
2. It lets you find out whether you actually like this before paying for it. We would rather you discover that at a free event than at month two of a membership.
3. It builds the group of people who will be the first regulars. The Tuesday table in the eventual lounge will be made of people who met at a church hall or a brewery back room a year earlier.

Bring a game if you have one. Bring a friend if you have one. Bring neither and we will have both.

## What helps later: the founding tier

At some point before we sign a lease, we will offer a founding membership. It will be priced higher than the eventual full tier and it will include things that only founders get: a name on the wall, early access, a reserved night, a voice in the library list. We described the general shape of tiers in [how a game lounge membership works](/articles/game-lounge-membership/).

The founding tier is not a fundraiser. It is a test. If a few hundred people in Indianapolis will pay for a lounge that does not exist yet, we have a business and we sign. If they will not, we have learned something important for far less than the cost of a lease.

We will tell subscribers first, and we will cap it. We are not announcing a price or a date now, because we do not have either. <!-- EDITOR: add detail when founding tier is set -->

## What we would rather you did not do

A few well-meant forms of help that do not help, or help less than they seem to.

| Offer | Why we usually decline |
|---|---|
| Donating a box of old games | The library is a curated menu, and most garage games are not on it. Ask first. |
| Volunteering to run things | Hosting is the job we need to pay for and train for. We will ask when we need hands. |
| Telling us about the perfect building | We appreciate it, but we have a checklist and we will tour when we are ready. |
| Pre-paying for a membership before the founding tier | We do not want your money until we can tell you what it buys. |

We are not being ungrateful. We are being careful with other people's generosity, which is a resource that runs out if you spend it badly.

## What you get back

An invitation first, when there is a door. A say in the library and the programming. A room in Indianapolis built around the thing we keep writing about: people coming together, in person, with a game as the excuse. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

And, if you are the kind of person who likes watching something get built, a front-row seat to every decision, including the ones we get wrong. We have already written up a batch of those, and there will be more.

## FAQ

### How can I support a local game cafe that hasn't opened yet?

Subscribe to its newsletter and answer its surveys, bring friends, attend its public events, and if you run a team, book its events service. Founding memberships, when offered, are the biggest single step.

### Will The Ludarium have a founding membership?

Yes, before signing a lease, capped and offered to subscribers first. Price and timing are not set.

### Can I donate games to The Ludarium?

Ask first. We take games that fit the opening library list, in good condition with all pieces, and decline the rest with thanks.

### Can I invest in The Ludarium?

We are not raising from the public and have nothing to offer in that direction right now. If that changes, subscribers will hear about it, and this article will be updated. <!-- EDITOR: confirm wording with counsel before publishing -->
