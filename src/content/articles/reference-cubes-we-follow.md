A reference cube is a public list you check against your own, not one you copy. We follow four kinds: the MTGO Vintage Cube for tested card evaluations at the top of the power band, The Pauper Cube for the same thing at the bottom, Lucky Paper's writing and tools for design thinking, and a short rotation of public CubeCobra lists for ideas at our own power level. Each one answers a different question, and none of them answers "what should my cube be?"

This article says what we follow, where it lives, how often it changes, and what we actually do with it. Dates are as of October 2026. Where we could not confirm a detail, we say so.

## Why keep reference cubes at all?

Because a cube owner's sample size is tiny. Our list sees a few drafts a month. <!-- EDITOR: confirm Commonwealth draft frequency --> The MTGO Vintage Cube sees thousands in a four-week league. The Pauper Cube has had fifteen years of community play. When one of them cuts a card we run, that is evidence we could not produce ourselves. When they add a card we never considered, that is a lead.

The trap is treating any of them as a template. The Vintage Cube is built for a client with click limits and a mandate to feature each new set. The Pauper Cube is built for commons. Lucky Paper's cubes are built for Lucky Paper's tables. The filter we apply to the first, copy only what has survived two updates and applies to your table, is the one we apply to the rest.

## The MTGO Vintage Cube

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | Daybreak's 540-card powered cube on Magic Online, curated by Chris Wolf |
| Where | [mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist](https://www.mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist), plus an update article per set |
| Cadence in 2026 | Six updates between January 23 and October 2, 19 to 48 swaps each |
| Latest | Reality Fracture update, effective October 7, 2026 |
| What we use it for | Verdicts on famous cards; finished archetype packages; the curator's reasoning vocabulary |
| What we ignore | Client-driven additions; Universes Beyond test cards; anything that has not survived two updates |

This is the one we track most closely. The specific value is the reasoning column. "Tarmogoyf has lost power" (March 2026) and "Time Warp is past its prime" (October 2026) are one-line summaries of thousands of games. The specific hazard is the churn: Leovold has left three times since March 2026.

## The Pauper Cube

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | The community commons cube started by Adam Styborski in 2008 |
| Where | [thepaupercube.com](https://thepaupercube.com/), with the list on CubeCobra |
| Cadence | About twice a year, roughly April and October, plus "Quick Hits" for new sets |
| Latest we found | Spring 2026 Update, May 16, 2026; Fall 2025 Update, October 30, 2025 |
| What we use it for | Card evaluation where power is flat; removal and two-drop benchmarks; the model of a slow, documented, community-run update |
| What we ignore | Nothing in particular; it is the most directly copyable list here if you run commons |

The Pauper Cube is the opposite of the Vintage Cube in every way that matters for a reference. Updates are rare, posted on a blog with full reasoning, and discussed on a Discord before they land. The site credits Eric Klug, Alex Ullman, Usman Jamil and Craig Sybert as contributors alongside Styborski. For an owner trying to decide how formal to make their own process, this is the mature end of the spectrum. Our piece on [the Pauper Cube](/articles/the-pauper-cube/) goes deeper.

## Lucky Paper

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | A cube design site with articles, data tools and set reviews |
| Where | [luckypaper.co](https://luckypaper.co/) |
| Cadence | Roughly weekly articles in 2026; the most recent we saw was October 5, 2026 |
| What we use it for | Design thinking rather than card lists; the Cube Map; the List Differ and Spellchecker; community set reviews |
| What we ignore | Any individual cube list as a template |

Lucky Paper is a reference for how to think, not what to run. Parker LaMascus's 2026 pieces on scarcity in drafting (September 21) and on cube conceits (September 28) are the kind of thing that changes a swap you were about to make. The Retro Reviews series, which re-evaluates old sets for cube design, is a useful corrective to new-set hype. The tools page hosts the List Differ we use to compare versions of any list, and the Cube Map that shows where a cube sits relative to hundreds of others. We covered the site's influence in [what Lucky Paper taught cube design](/articles/lucky-paper-cube-design/).

One note on currency: a January 2026 article says LaMascus is selling off the legacy cube he had run for nearly ten years, so do not treat any older Lucky Paper list as a maintained reference. The writing is the reference.

## Public CubeCobra lists at our power level

The fourth category is the one that changes most. We keep a short rotation of three to five public CubeCobra cubes that share our constraints: unpowered, 540, no Universes Beyond, aggro supported. <!-- EDITOR: confirm current Commonwealth constraints --> We do not name them here because the list turns over and because a name in an article becomes a recommendation. The selection rule is more useful than the names:

1. **Active in the last three months.** CubeCobra's history tab shows it.
2. **Has a primer or blog.** A list without reasoning is just a list.
3. **Different from ours in one deliberate way.** A cube that supports storm and we do not, or runs 450, or has a strict no-planeswalker rule. The value is in the contrast.
4. **Not famous.** The famous cubes are already covered above and have their own gravity.

We check these quarterly, diff them against the previous check, and read the changelog. Twenty minutes each. Twenty minutes each, with a text differ.

## What we do not follow

**Arena cubes.** The Arena Powered Cube, introduced in October 2025 per the [MTG Wiki](https://mtg.wiki/page/Vintage_Cube), uses a different card pool constrained by what Arena has. Interesting as a product, not useful as a reference for paper.

**Vendor cubes.** Pre-built cubes sold by stores are built to a price. Useful for a new owner's shopping list; not a design reference.

**Our own old lists.** Every owner's temptation. The history tab is for answering questions, not for arguing that the 2023 version was better.

## How often do we check each one?

| Reference | Check | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| MTGO Vintage Cube | Every update | The curator's article posts (about five days before each league) |
| The Pauper Cube | Twice a year plus Quick Hits | Blog post |
| Lucky Paper | Weekly skim | New article |
| CubeCobra rotation | Quarterly | Calendar |

The total is maybe two hours a month. For an owner, that is the cheapest testing available anywhere.

## FAQ

### What is the best cube list to follow?

For a powered cube, the MTGO Vintage Cube at mtgo.com. For commons, The Pauper Cube. For design thinking at any power level, Lucky Paper. There is no single best list because they answer different questions.

### How often does the MTGO Vintage Cube change?

In 2026, with every major set release: six updates between January 23 and October 2, each with 19 to 48 swaps.

### How often does The Pauper Cube change?

About twice a year, around April and October, with occasional Quick Hits updates for new sets, per thepaupercube.com. The most recent full update we found was May 16, 2026.

### Should I copy a reference cube?

No. Diff it against yours, read its reasoning, and test the changes that apply to your table. Copying imports the other owner's constraints along with their cards.
