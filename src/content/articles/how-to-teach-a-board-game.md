The person who teaches the game has more power over the night than the person who brought it. A great teach turns a confusing box into a fun evening. A bad one turns it into homework.

You do not need to be a rules expert. You need a plan.

## Know it before they arrive

Read the rules once, then set up the game and play a few turns by yourself. You will spot the confusing parts long before your friends do. Watching a short how-to-play video helps too.

## Teach in this order

1. **The theme in one sentence.** "We are rival bakers racing to fill orders before the shop closes." A story makes rules easier to remember.
2. **How you win.** Every rule makes more sense once people know the goal.
3. **A turn, step by step.** Walk through one turn using real pieces on the table.
4. **The big gotcha.** Most games have one rule that surprises people. Say it out loud now.
5. **Start playing.** Save the rare rules for when they actually happen.

## Things to skip

- **Reading the rulebook aloud.** Paraphrase instead. Keep the book nearby for questions.
- **Every exception up front.** Nobody remembers them until they matter.
- **Strategy tips nobody asked for.** Let people discover the clever moves. That is half the fun.

## Play the first round open

Let everyone play with their cards face up for the first round, or call it a practice round. Players can ask "what should I do here?" without feeling silly, and the whole table learns together.

The goal of a teach is not perfect understanding. It is getting everyone playing, curious, and comfortable asking questions. The rest comes with the second game.
