The best Catan expansion for most groups is [Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). It adds ships, islands, and a set of scenarios without changing how a turn feels. For a group that has played [Catan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1ZH2D?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) for years and wants a real challenge, [Cities & Knights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the deepest box in the line. If your problem is simply too many friends, the [5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1LYDT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the one to buy first. After that come [Traders & Barbarians](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF7552C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), a grab bag of scenarios with a proper two-player variant, and [Explorers & Pirates](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF8J5C8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), the most adventurous of the big boxes.

**The quick answer:** buy **Seafarers** first. Buy **Cities & Knights** second, once everyone at the table knows the base game cold. Buy the **5-6 Player Expansion** only if you regularly have five or six players, and know that every big expansion then needs its own 5-6 box too.

Below is the full ranking, a chart of which boxes you need for five or six players, a plain note on the 2025 sixth edition, and an FAQ.

## What to look for in a Catan expansion

Catan, designed by Klaus Teuber and first published by Kosmos in Germany in 1995, plays 3 to 4 in about 60 to 120 minutes. Every expansion below needs that base game. Before buying, ask three questions:

1. **What does your group want more of?** More map and exploration (Seafarers, Explorers & Pirates), more decisions and less luck (Cities & Knights), or more variety (Traders & Barbarians).
2. **How many people play?** The base game stops at four. Five and six need the 5-6 extension, and each big expansion has its own 5-6 box on top of that.
3. **How long is your evening?** Most expansions add time. Cities & Knights and Explorers & Pirates both list 90 to 120 minutes.

If you are still deciding whether to expand at all, our guide to [when board game expansions are worth it](/articles/expansions-worth-it/) has a simple test.

## 1. Seafarers: the best first expansion

### Catan: Seafarers

First published in 1997, [Seafarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK3S5MJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes. It adds sea hexes, frame pieces for maps of different sizes, ships, gold fields, and a pirate. Ships cost one wood and one wool and work like roads on water, so you can sail out to new islands. In the first scenario, every player earns bonus victory points for their first settlement on each new island. The box has a campaign of eight scenarios, from small archipelagos to open seas, plus a New World variant for building your own maps. Our free [Seafarers strategy course](/academy/catan-seafarers/) walks through every scenario, and [the scenarios ranked](/articles/catan-seafarers-scenarios-ranked/) helps you pick where to start.

**Why it ranks first:** it changes the map, not the rules you already know. A turn still means rolling, trading, and building, so anyone who can play Catan can play Seafarers after a two-minute explanation. The new choice, "do I build a road or a ship?", is easy to grasp and fun to argue about. And because there are many scenarios, the box keeps paying off. For five or six players, add the [Seafarers 5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1QR6W?tag=cubecommonw0a-20).

## 2. Cities & Knights: the best for experienced groups

### Catan: Cities & Knights

[Cities & Knights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87W8SS?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 4 in 90 to 120 minutes, for ages 12 and up. A barbarian fleet sails toward Catan, and its strength grows with the number of cities on the board. You build and activate knights to defend the island. Cities next to forest, pasture, and mountains also produce commodities (paper, cloth, and coin), which you spend on city improvements. Those improvements unlock progress cards and, eventually, metropolises worth two extra points. A third die drives events, and the game runs to 13 points instead of 10. To learn it properly, our free [Cities & Knights course](/academy/catan-cities-and-knights/) goes from your first game to tournament play, and [every progress card, explained](/articles/cities-and-knights-progress-cards/) is a handy reference at the table. Torn between the two? See [Seafarers or Cities & Knights first](/articles/seafarers-vs-cities-and-knights/).

**Why it ranks second:** it is the best expansion, but not the best first one. It gives you far more control than the base game. Commodities and progress cards soften bad dice, so skill matters more. The cost is a real rules teach and a longer evening. Save it for a table that already plays well; our free lesson on [the four core strategies](/academy/catan/four-core-strategies/) is a good check that your group is ready. For five or six players, you need the [Cities & Knights 5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DY87CXSQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) too.

## 3. The 5-6 Player Expansion: the best for bigger groups

### Catan 5-6 Player Expansion

The [5-6 Player Expansion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYK1LYDT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) first came out in 1996. It adds frame pieces and terrain for a bigger island, pieces in two more colors, and more resource and development cards.

**Why it ranks third:** it does one job and does it well, but it does not change the game. More players means more waiting. Older printings handled this with a special building phase between turns. Newer printings use a paired player rule instead: two players are active on each turn, so the board builds out faster. Catan has offered this rule as a free download for owners of older extensions. If you usually play with five, it is a must. If you play with five twice a year, look at our list of [the best board games for 6 players](/articles/best-board-games-for-6-players/) instead.

## 4. Traders & Barbarians: the best for variety and two players

### Catan: Traders & Barbarians

[Traders & Barbarians](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF7552C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in 60 to 120 minutes, for ages 10 and up. It is a collection rather than one big change. The box holds five scenarios: Fishermen of Catan, The Rivers of Catan, Merchant Train, Barbarian Attack, and Traders & Barbarians, where you haul goods across the island while barbarians get in the way. It also has variants, including event cards that can replace the dice and an official Catan for Two variant that works with the base game and every scenario.

**Why it ranks fourth:** it is the most flexible box, but no single scenario is as strong as Seafarers or Cities & Knights. Fishermen and Rivers are small tweaks you can add to almost any base game. The two-player rules are a real reason to buy if you play Catan as a couple. Up to six players need the Traders & Barbarians 5-6 Player Expansion along with the base 5-6 box.

## 5. Explorers & Pirates: the most adventurous

### Catan: Explorers & Pirates

[Explorers & Pirates](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF8J5C8?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 2 to 4 in about 90 to 120 minutes, for ages 12 and up. The map starts small and grows as you explore. Ships move freely across the sea, carrying settlers, crews, fish, and spices. You build outposts on new islands and complete missions: fighting pirate lairs, bringing fish home, and delivering spices to villages that teach your ships upgrades.

**How it differs:** it feels the least like classic Catan and the most like an adventure game. That is a plus for groups who want something new and a minus for groups who love the base game as it is. One important note: Catan says Explorers & Pirates and Seafarers cannot be combined, because their ship rules are too different. It can be combined with Cities & Knights or Traders & Barbarians. For five or six, add the Explorers & Pirates 5-6 Player Expansion and the base 5-6 box.

## 6. Legend of the Sea Robbers: for Seafarers owners

### Catan: Legend of the Sea Robbers

[Legend of the Sea Robbers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B075VCC9YJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) plays 3 to 4 in about 95 minutes, with a separate download for two players. It is a campaign of four linked scenarios for Seafarers, so you need both the base game and Seafarers to play it.

**Why it fits:** if your group has played through the Seafarers scenarios and wants more, this is the natural next box. It is not a starting point. Think of it as a second helping, not a new meal.

## The spin-offs

### Catan: Starfarers

[Starfarers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YFFZ9DT?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is a revised reissue of a 1999 game, set in space. It plays 3 to 4 in about 120 minutes, for ages 14 and up, and has its own 5-6 extension. It is **standalone**: you do not need the base game. Ships, spaceports, alien trading posts, and space pirates replace roads and robbers.

**How it differs:** buy it as a new game for Catan fans, not as an expansion. The same goes for Catan: New Energies, a standalone game from 2024 built around power plants and the environment. If you want a fresh game that keeps the trading, our list of [games like Catan](/articles/games-like-catan/) has more options outside the Catan family.

## How many boxes do you need for 5 or 6 players?

This is the most common buying mistake. Each big expansion has its own 5-6 box, and that box only works alongside the base 5-6 extension. To play any expansion with five or six, you need four boxes:

| To play this with 5 to 6 | You need |
|---|---|
| Base game | Catan + 5-6 Player Expansion (2 boxes) |
| Seafarers | Catan + 5-6 Player Expansion + Seafarers + Seafarers 5-6 (4 boxes) |
| Cities & Knights | Catan + 5-6 Player Expansion + Cities & Knights + Cities & Knights 5-6 (4 boxes) |
| Traders & Barbarians | Catan + 5-6 Player Expansion + Traders & Barbarians + Traders & Barbarians 5-6 (4 boxes) |
| Explorers & Pirates | Catan + 5-6 Player Expansion + Explorers & Pirates + Explorers & Pirates 5-6 (4 boxes) |
| Legend of the Sea Robbers | Listed for 3 to 4 players only |

## Do Catan expansions work across editions?

Catan's sixth edition came out in 2025, thirty years after the original. The rules are the same. The art, the rulebooks, and many components were redesigned. Seafarers and Cities & Knights arrived first, followed by Traders & Barbarians and Explorers & Pirates, with 5-6 boxes for each.

Catan says older and newer editions can be used together, with a few catches:

- **Card backs changed.** That only matters when cards from two editions get shuffled into one deck. The two cases to watch are the base game's resource cards with the 5-6 extension's, and the Cities & Knights commodity cards, which shuffle in with the resource cards. Sleeves or replacement cards fix it.
- **Some pieces changed.** Wooden roads are slightly larger, and the 5-6 extension's extra colors changed, so mixed sets can look odd.
- **Some parts changed form.** In Cities & Knights, for example, flip charts became boards, and in Traders & Barbarians, wagon cards became tiles.

The simplest rule: buy expansions from the same edition as your base game. If you are buying everything new, buy the sixth edition throughout. The Amazon links above point to sixth edition boxes.

## FAQ

### What is the best Catan expansion?

For most groups, Seafarers. It adds the most variety for the smallest teach. Cities & Knights is the deeper game and the favorite of many experienced players, but it is a harder first step.

### Which Catan expansion should I buy first, Seafarers or Cities & Knights?

Seafarers, unless everyone at your table has played Catan many times. Cities & Knights adds commodities, knights, and progress cards, and it runs longer. It shines once the base game feels easy.

### Do Catan expansions need the 5-6 player extension?

Only if you want to play them with five or six. Every big expansion plays 3 to 4 (Traders & Barbarians and Explorers & Pirates also play 2) on its own. For five or six, you need the base 5-6 extension plus that expansion's own 5-6 box.

### Can you combine Seafarers and Cities & Knights?

Yes. Catan publishes rules for combining them. Explorers & Pirates is the one to watch: Catan says it cannot be combined with Seafarers.

### Is there a two-player Catan?

Traders & Barbarians includes an official two-player variant for the base game and its scenarios. Explorers & Pirates also lists 2 to 4 players.

### Will older Catan expansions work with the 2025 edition?

Mostly, yes. The rules match, but card backs and some pieces changed. If you plan to shuffle cards from two editions together, use sleeves or match editions.
