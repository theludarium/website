Board games for teenagers fail for a specific reason: the gift was chosen by an adult imagining a teenager, not by anyone who has watched six of them in a basement at 10 p.m. What that room wants is fast, funny, a little mean, and playable by however many people showed up. It does not want a two-hour strategy game, and it does not want anything with "family" on the box.

The eleven games below have survived that room. Prices are approximate and were checked at publisher or major retailer pages at the time of writing.

## What do teenagers actually want from a game?

Watch a group of teens pick a game off a shelf and you will see them apply four filters in about ten seconds.

1. **Can we start in two minutes?** Long rules lose them before the first turn.
2. **Does it work with everyone here?** Friend groups are five to eight people, not four. A game that caps at four leaves someone on their phone.
3. **Is there a reason to laugh or talk trash?** Bluffing, accusations, bad clues, and betrayal all qualify. Silent optimization does not.
4. **Will I look stupid in a way I can control?** Teens will happily be ridiculous on purpose. They will not risk being bad at something in front of friends. Games where skill shows clearly, like trivia and chess, are risky.

Games that pass all four get played until 2 a.m. Games that fail one go back on the shelf.

## The 11 best board games for teenagers

### Social deduction and bluffing

**Coup** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $13; [$12.99 at Miniature Market](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/ibcgp-b0813.html) at the time of writing). Two to six players hold hidden roles and lie about them. Call a bluff correctly and they lose a card; call it wrong and you do. Five minutes a round. It is the game most likely to produce a grudge that lasts the whole weekend, in the good way.

**One Night Ultimate Werewolf** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20 to $30; [Miniature Market lists it at $19.99](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/bezonuw.html) and [Bezier Games at $29.95](https://beziergames.com/products/one-night-ultimate-werewolf) at the time of writing). Three to ten players, one round of ten minutes, a free app that narrates. Everyone gets a secret role, some roles swap in the night, then the table argues about who the werewolf is. There is no elimination, so nobody sits out. We wrote about [why social deduction games work](/articles/why-social-deduction-games-work/) and teens are the proof.

**The Resistance: Avalon** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20; [$19.99 at Miniature Market](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/ibcava1.html) at the time of writing). Five to ten players. A longer, more structured version of the same idea, with secret teams voting on missions. Better for a group that has played Werewolf and wants the serious version.

### Party games with a mean streak

**Poetry for Neanderthals** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $10 to $20; Wargamer tracked it at [$9.99 on Black Friday 2025](https://www.wargamer.com/board-games/love-letter-black-friday-2025-deal)). Describe a word using only single-syllable words. Slip up and get hit with an inflatable club. The club is the reason teens pick it.

**Hues and Cues** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $26; [$25.95 at The Op](https://theop.games/products/hues-and-cues) at the time of writing). Guess a color from a one-word clue on a board of 480 shades. Up to ten players. The arguments about whether a clue was fair are the game.

**Herd Mentality** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20; [$19.97 at Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/Herd-Mentality/16980300226) at the time of writing). Everyone answers the same question; you score if your answer matches the majority. The one person who is different gets the pink cow and cannot win until they pass it. It rewards knowing your friends, which teens are very good at.

### Competitive games that are fast

**Codenames** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20 to $25; Target lists it at $24.99 per [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/9tB3SAHmND/codenames)). Two teams, word grid, one-word clues. It works with eight and it works with four. The teen version of this game involves clues that reference inside jokes nobody else at the table could possibly get, and that is fine.

**Sushi Go Party!** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $25 to $38; Walmart listed it at [$37.95](https://www.walmart.com/ip/54017366) at the time of writing, though it is often cheaper). The bigger version of Sushi Go!, seating up to eight. Pass cards, collect sets, done in twenty minutes. The base Sushi Go! is cheaper but caps at five.

**Skull** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $21; [$20.99 at the Asmodee store](https://store.asmodee.com/products/skull) at the time of writing). Each player has four coasters: three flowers and one skull. Stack them face down, bid on how many flowers you can flip without hitting a skull. Three to six players, ten minutes, and the purest bluffing game ever printed on cardboard.

### For the teen who wants something deeper

**7 Wonders Duel** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $28 to $30 at most US retailers, per [Board Game Oracle](https://boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/PJgyOvkAJx/7-wonders-duel)). A two-player civilization game in thirty minutes. For the sixteen-year-old who has outgrown party games and wants to beat one specific friend repeatedly.

**Dungeons & Dragons Starter Set: Heroes of the Borderlands** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $45 to $50; [Noble Knight listed it at $44.95](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2148328562/Starter-Set---Heroes-of-the-Borderlands) at the time of writing). For a group, not a person. The 2025 starter set has maps, tokens, and short scenarios built so that a group of teens can run it without an experienced adult. Our [D&D starter set gift guide](/articles/best-dnd-starter-set-gift/) covers what to add to the box.

## What should you avoid giving a teenager?

- **Anything with "family" or "junior" on the box.** It will be read as an insult, even when the game is good.
- **Long strategy games.** Catan, Ticket to Ride, Wingspan. They are fine games. They are also games teens associate with parents. Let them come to those later.
- **Trivia.** It reveals who studies. Nobody wants that measured at a sleepover.
- **Adult party games.** The ones with the black boxes. Parents will have views, and the games are worse than the ones above anyway.
- **Games that need exactly four players.** Teens do not travel in fours.

## Do teenagers really still play board games?

More than their parents assume. Phones are everywhere, and that is exactly why a table with no phones on it has become a novelty rather than a chore. The game gives the room a reason to put the phones down that does not feel like a rule. We have written more about this in our piece on [board games with teenagers](/articles/board-games-with-teenagers/), including how to host a teen game night without hovering.

The trick, if you are the parent, is to buy the game and then leave. The gift is the game and the basement. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What board games do teenagers actually like?

Fast games with bluffing, arguing, or laughing built in: Coup, One Night Ultimate Werewolf, Codenames, Poetry for Neanderthals, and Skull are the ones that get played at the sleepovers we have seen.

### What is a good board game for a 13-year-old?

Sushi Go Party! or Codenames. Both are quick to learn, seat a crowd, and do not read as kids' games.

### What is a good board game gift for a 16-year-old?

Coup or Skull for a competitive teen, 7 Wonders Duel for one with a best friend, or the D&D Starter Set for a group that watches actual-play shows.

### How many players should a teen board game support?

At least six. Friend groups run five to eight, and a game that caps at four leaves someone out.
