Board games for teenagers fail for a specific reason: the gift was chosen by an adult imagining a teenager, not by anyone thinking about six of them in a basement at 10 p.m. What that room wants is fast, funny, a little mean, and playable by however many people showed up. It does not want a two-hour strategy game, and it does not want anything with "family" on the box.

**The quick answer:** For a teen with a big friend group, give One Night Ultimate Werewolf or Codenames. For a sharp, competitive teen, give Coup or Skull. For a group that watches actual-play shows, give the D&D Starter Set: Heroes of the Borderlands.

The eleven games below are built for that room. Prices are approximate and were checked at publisher or major retailer pages at the time of writing.

## What do teenagers actually want from a game?

Teens choosing a game tend to apply four filters in about ten seconds.

1. **Can we start in two minutes?** Long rules lose them before the first turn.
2. **Does it work with everyone here?** Friend groups are often five to eight people, not four. A game that caps at four leaves someone on their phone.
3. **Is there a reason to laugh or talk trash?** Bluffing, accusations, bad clues, and betrayal all qualify. Silent optimization does not.
4. **Will I look stupid in a way I can control?** Teens will happily be ridiculous on purpose. They will not risk being bad at something in front of friends. Games where skill shows clearly, like trivia and chess, are risky.

Games that pass all four get played until 2 a.m. Games that fail one go back on the shelf.

## What are the best board games for teenagers?

### Social deduction and bluffing

**Coup** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $13 to $17; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/jMT-_Uoie5/coup) showed it in stock from $12.44, with Walmart and Amazon at $16.99, at the time of writing). Two to six players hold hidden roles and lie about them. Call a bluff correctly and they lose a card; call it wrong and you do. Five minutes a round. It is the game most likely to produce a grudge that lasts the whole weekend, in the good way.

**One Night Ultimate Werewolf** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $22 to $25; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/TooyG41PWz/one-night-ultimate-werewolf) showed it in stock at $21.99 at Miniature Market and $24.95 at Walmart and Amazon at the time of writing). Three to ten players, one round of about ten minutes, a free app that narrates. Everyone gets a secret role, some roles swap in the night, then the table argues about who the werewolf is. There is no elimination, so nobody sits out. We wrote about [why social deduction games work](/articles/why-social-deduction-games-work/), and teens are the proof.

**The Resistance: Avalon** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $16 to $20; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/ItF8JsIfpj/the-resistance-avalon) showed it in stock from $16.16, with Miniature Market at $19.99, at the time of writing). Five to ten players. A longer, more structured version of the same idea, with secret teams voting on missions. Better for a group that has played Werewolf and wants the serious version.

### Party games with a mean streak

**Poetry for Neanderthals** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $19 to $20; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/LskejQAtEb/poetry-for-neanderthals) showed it at $19.99 at Target and Walmart at the time of writing). Describe a word using only single-syllable words. Slip up and get hit with an inflatable club. The club is the reason teens pick it.

**Hues and Cues** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $25; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/h-SqjrUA6j/hues-and-cues) showed it in stock at $24.49 to $24.99 at Miniature Market, Target, and others at the time of writing). Guess a color from a one-word clue on a board of 480 shades. Three to ten players. The arguments about whether a clue was fair are the game.

**Herd Mentality** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $19 to $20; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/Sy1tees3kI/herd-mentality) showed it at $19.97 at Walmart and $19.99 at Target at the time of writing). Everyone answers the same question; you score if your answer matches the majority. The one person who is different gets the pink cow and cannot win until they pass it. It rewards knowing your friends, which teens are very good at.

### Competitive games that are fast

**Codenames** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20 to $25; Target lists it at $24.99 per [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/9tB3SAHmND/codenames) at the time of writing). Two teams, word grid, one-word clues. It works with eight and it works with four. Teen clues tend to reference inside jokes nobody else at the table could possibly get, and that is fine. Our free [Codenames course](/academy/codenames/) covers better clue-giving.

**Sushi Go Party!** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $20 to $28; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/ES4wI7y9g7/sushi-go-party) showed it in stock from $19.99, with Amazon at $21.99, at the time of writing). The bigger version of Sushi Go!, seating up to eight. Pass cards, collect sets, done in twenty minutes. The base Sushi Go! is cheaper but caps at five.

**Skull** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $17 to $21; [Board Game Oracle](https://www.boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/1vuFDqERsY/skull) showed it in stock at $16.79 at Walmart and Amazon, with a $20.99 list at the Asmodee store, at the time of writing). Each player has four coasters: three flowers and one skull. Stack them face down, bid on how many flowers you can flip without hitting a skull. Three to six players, quick rounds, and the purest bluffing game ever printed on cardboard.

### For the teen who wants something deeper

**7 Wonders Duel** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $30 to $37; in stock from $29.59 at several US retailers per [Board Game Oracle](https://boardgameoracle.com/boardgame/price/PJgyOvkAJx/7-wonders-duel) at the time of writing). A two-player civilization game in thirty minutes. For the sixteen-year-old who has outgrown party games and wants to beat one specific friend repeatedly.

**Dungeons & Dragons Starter Set: Heroes of the Borderlands** <!-- AFFILIATE --> (about $45 to $55; [Noble Knight listed it at $44.95 against a $54.99 list price](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2148328562/Starter-Set---Heroes-of-the-Borderlands) at the time of writing). For a group, not a person. The 2025 starter set comes with maps, tokens, and short scenarios aimed at new players and a first-time Dungeon Master. ## What should you avoid giving a teenager?

- **Anything with "family" or "junior" on the box.** It will be read as an insult, even when the game is good.
- **Long strategy games.** Catan, Ticket to Ride, Wingspan. They are fine games. They are also games teens associate with parents. Let them come to those later.
- **Trivia.** It reveals who studies. Nobody wants that measured at a sleepover.
- **Adult party games.** The ones with the black boxes. Parents will have views, and the games are worse than the ones above anyway.
- **Games that need exactly four players.** Teens do not travel in fours.

## Do teenagers really still play board games?

More than their parents assume. Phones are everywhere, and that is exactly why a table with no phones on it has become a novelty rather than a chore. The game gives the room a reason to put the phones down that does not feel like a rule. We have written more about this in our piece on [board games with teenagers](/articles/board-games-with-teenagers/), including how to host a teen game night without hovering.

If the teen is hosting over winter break, our list of [New Year's Eve party games](/articles/new-years-eve-party-games/) has more picks that hold a crowd until midnight.

The trick, if you are the parent, is to buy the game and then leave. The gift is the game and the basement. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### What board games do teenagers actually like?

Fast games with bluffing, arguing, or laughing built in: Coup, One Night Ultimate Werewolf, Codenames, Poetry for Neanderthals, and Skull are our top picks.

### What is a good board game for a 13-year-old?

Sushi Go Party! or Codenames. Both are quick to learn, seat a crowd, and do not read as kids' games.

### What is a good board game gift for a 16-year-old?

Coup or Skull for a competitive teen, 7 Wonders Duel for one with a best friend, or the D&D Starter Set for a group that watches actual-play shows.

### How many players should a teen board game support?

At least six. Friend groups often run five to eight, and a game that caps at four leaves someone out.
