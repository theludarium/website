Board game advent calendars are worth it when the person will enjoy the daily ritual as much as what is behind the doors. They cost more per item than buying the same things in a box, and most of the contents are small. What you pay for is 24 mornings of a little surprise, and for the right household, that is a fine gift.

**The quick answer:** for a family or a couple who like puzzles, the EXIT advent calendar is the best game calendar for 2026, a 24-day escape room story in one box. For an RPG player, the Chessex holiday dice calendar gives the most dice for the money. If neither fits, make your own countdown of game nights at home for close to nothing.

Prices below were checked at retailer pages at the time of writing. Stock on these is thin and sells out early, so if one appeals, buy it before December.

## What is a board game advent calendar?

An advent calendar has 24 small doors, one for each day from December 1 to December 24. Game versions come in three main kinds:

- **Puzzle story calendars.** Each door holds a riddle or a small puzzle, and solving them in order tells one story. EXIT is the best-known line.
- **Dice calendars.** Each door holds a die or a small accessory. These are for tabletop RPG players and dice collectors.
- **Collectible calendars.** Trading card game and toy brands sell calendars full of booster packs, cards, and small extras.

A few calendars build a whole board game across the month, with a piece or two behind each door. Those come and go from year to year, so check the year on the box.

## How do you decide if an advent calendar is worth it?

Run the calendar through four questions. If you answer yes to three, buy it.

1. **Will they enjoy the countdown itself?** Some people love a small daily ritual. Others will open all 24 doors on December 2. If it is the second kind, buy a regular gift.
2. **Is the same person home every day in December?** Calendars work for a household that wakes up in the same place. They work badly for someone traveling for half the month.
3. **Will they use what is inside after December 24?** Dice get rolled for years. A finished EXIT calendar gets recycled. Both can be worth it, but know which you are buying.
4. **Would you pay this much for the contents alone?** A $50 calendar works out to about $2 a day. If the daily moment is worth that to them, fine. If not, $50 buys a very good board game. Our [board game gifts under $50](/articles/board-game-gifts-under-50/) has picks.

One more test for families: count who will want to open the doors. One calendar for three kids can start a fight every morning. EXIT calendars avoid this, since the whole family solves the riddle together.

## Can you make your own game advent calendar?

Yes, and it may be the best version. A homemade calendar costs little, uses games you already own, and turns the month into time together instead of a stack of small objects.

### A countdown of game nights

Get 24 small envelopes or a cheap reusable calendar with pockets. In each one, put a card that names a game from your shelf and a tiny twist. Some ideas:

- "Tonight: Uno. Winner picks dessert."
- "Tonight: any game, but the youngest player teaches it."
- "Tonight: a game nobody has played this year."
- "Tonight: a cooperative game. Everyone wins or nobody does."
- "Tonight: the group votes." Use [Game Night Madness](/game-night-madness/), our free bracket app, so everyone votes from their phone until one game is left.

Not every night has to be a full game night. On busy evenings, the card can say "one round of a ten-minute card game before bed." Our [Christmas games for the whole family](/articles/christmas-games-for-family/) has more ideas.

### Twenty-four small games

The other version puts a small game behind every door. Twenty-four boxed games would cost far more than any calendar, so mix it up:

- A few small boxed games from our [stocking stuffer games](/articles/board-game-stocking-stuffers/) list on key days, like the first, the last, and each weekend
- Print-and-play games, printed and cut out at home; our guide to [print and play board games](/articles/print-and-play-board-games/) explains where to find them
- A deck of cards with a different classic card game written on each day's slip
- Pencil-and-paper games like dots and boxes or hangman, written out ready to play

The point is not the stuff. It is that every day in December has a small reason to sit down together for a few minutes.

## Who should not get an advent calendar?

- **Someone who wants one big gift.** Twenty-four small things can feel like less than one thing they really want.
- **Collectors who care about mint condition.** Sealed calendars are opened door by door, and some come with flimsy packaging.
- **Anyone you will not see in December.** It needs to arrive by November 30 to work as intended.

## A month of small reasons to sit down

The best thing about any advent calendar is the habit it builds: a few minutes a day, at the same table, with the same people, doing something small together. You can buy that habit in a box, or make it from envelopes and the games already on your shelf. Either way, the doors are just the excuse. The game is the reason. The people are the point.

## FAQ

### Are board game advent calendars worth the money?

For people who love the daily ritual, yes. If they only care about what is inside, a regular gift of the same price gives them more.

### What is the best board game advent calendar for adults?

The EXIT advent calendar for puzzle lovers, and the Chessex or FanRoll dice calendar for RPG players. All three are made with grown-ups in mind.

### Can an EXIT advent calendar be played more than once?

No. You mark, fold, and tear the pieces as you solve them, so each calendar plays once.

### When should you buy a game advent calendar?

By mid-November. Popular calendars sell out, and the gift has to be in hand by December 1.
