The best board games for 7 and 8 year olds give kids real choices and let them win on merit. At seven, start with kid-sized versions of famous games, like [Ticket to Ride: First Journey](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MQ4IKQJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) and [Catan Junior](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B007N0KZ64?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), plus quick family hits like [Qwirkle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1933054395?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Ice Cool](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GDHGYHG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Rat-a-Tat Cat](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00000GBQJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Zombie Kidz Evolution](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07M5BFVP2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). By eight, most kids can play the real grown-up games: [Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Kingdomino](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01N3A4070?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Sushi Go!](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57VU44?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Camel Up](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09KWZMXLX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Ghost Blitz](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0047N0Y52?tag=cubecommonw0a-20).

**The quick answer:** for a seven-year-old, get **Ticket to Ride: First Journey**. For an eight-year-old, get **Kingdomino**. Both are short, both are games adults enjoy, and both lead straight to bigger games later.

Below are 12 picks sorted by age, a look at what changes between seven and eight, a comparison table, and a "which one first" guide.

## What can 7- and 8-year-olds handle in a board game?

Seven and eight is the age when board games really open up. Kids can now do most of what a casual adult player does, just more slowly. Children vary a lot, so use this as a rough guide.

1. **Reading: some.** Most seven-year-olds can read a short word or two on a card. Most eight-year-olds can read a simple card on their own. Games with a little text are fine; games with paragraphs are not, yet.
2. **Math: adding small numbers.** Adding up a score at the end works, especially with a grown-up checking. Long scoring with multiplication is still a stretch at seven.
3. **Turns: a real decision.** This is the big change. Kids can now weigh two or three options and pick one for a reason. "If I take this tile, you can't have it" is a thought they can have.
4. **Attention: 20 to 45 minutes.** A 30-minute game is the sweet spot. Many eight-year-olds will happily play longer if the game moves quickly.
5. **Losing: better, not solved.** Most kids this age can lose a game without a meltdown, especially if it was close. Some still struggle, and that is normal. Our guide on [teaching kids to lose at games](/articles/teaching-kids-to-lose-at-games/) covers what to say in the moment.
6. **Fairness: a big deal.** Seven- and eight-year-olds care a lot about rules being fair and the same for everyone. They want to win for real. Games where an adult has to hold back feel less fun to them now.

The practical difference: a seven-year-old is ready for a kids' version of a real game. An eight-year-old is often ready for the real game itself, as long as it is short.

## For 7-year-olds: kid versions and quick family games

These six are rated 6 or 7 and up. They give a seven-year-old real choices without asking for much reading.

### Ticket to Ride: First Journey

[Ticket to Ride: First Journey](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MQ4IKQJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the kids' version of Alan R. Moon's Ticket to Ride from Days of Wonder. It plays 2 to 4, rated 6 and up, in 15 to 30 minutes. Collect colored train cards, spend them to claim short routes between cities, and complete your tickets. The first player to finish six tickets wins.

**Why it fits:** it keeps the heart of Ticket to Ride, collecting cards and claiming routes, and drops the scoring math. A seven-year-old makes real decisions every turn, like whether to grab a route now or wait for one more card. It is also the cleanest path to the full game. When your child starts winning easily, move up to Ticket to Ride with our [Coast to Coast course](/academy/ticket-to-ride/).

### Catan Junior

[Catan Junior](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B007N0KZ64?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Klaus Teuber as the family version of Catan. It plays 2 to 4, rated 6 and up, in 30 to 40 minutes. Players are pirates building lairs on islands and ships to reach new ones. Islands produce resources like wood, wool, and molasses when the die rolls their number. The Ghost Captain blocks an island, but unlike Catan's robber, it never steals.

**Why it fits:** it teaches trading, which is a wonderful skill for kids. "I'll give you wool for a cutlass" is real negotiation. Resources sit face up in front of each player instead of hiding in hands, so kids can see what everyone has. It is the step before the full game; when your child is ready, our [games like Catan](/articles/games-like-catan/) guide and the free [Catan course](/academy/catan/) are waiting.

### Qwirkle

[Qwirkle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1933054395?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Susan McKinley Ross, published by MindWare in 2006, and won the 2011 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4, rated 6 and up, in 30 to 45 minutes. Place wooden tiles in lines where every tile shares a color or a shape, with no repeats. Score a point for each tile in every line you add to.

**Why it fits:** there is no reading at all, so a seven-year-old plays on equal footing with grandparents. The scoring is simple addition, which is good practice. Our [Qwirkle rules](/articles/qwirkle-rules/) guide walks through a full game.

### Ice Cool

[Ice Cool](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GDHGYHG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Brian Gomez (the shared name of a Latvian design team) and published by Brain Games in 2016. It plays 2 to 4, rated 6 and up, in about 20 minutes, and won the 2017 Kinderspiel des Jahres. The box becomes a school of nested rooms. Flick your penguin through the doorways to grab fish, while the hall monitor tries to flick into you and catch you.

**Why it fits:** it is pure physical skill, and the penguins curve and even jump. Kids often get better at it faster than adults, which seven-year-olds love. It is the loudest game on this list.

### Rat-a-Tat Cat

[Rat-a-Tat Cat](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00000GBQJ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Monty and Ann Stambler and published by Gamewright. It plays 2 to 6, rated 6 and up, in about 10 to 15 minutes. Each player has four face-down cards and gets to peek at two. Draw and swap to get your total as low as possible. Cats are low; rats are high. Call "Rat-a-tat cat!" when you think you are lowest.

**Why it fits:** it sneaks in memory and adding, and the game is over fast enough to play several rounds. Power cards that let you peek or swap add small, satisfying decisions. It fits in a backpack.

### Zombie Kidz Evolution

[Zombie Kidz Evolution](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07M5BFVP2?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Annick Lobet and published by Le Scorpion Masqué in 2018. It plays 2 to 4, rated 7 and up, in about 15 minutes. Kids work together to lock the school gates before zombies overrun it. After each game, they open sealed envelopes, earn stickers, and unlock new rules.

**Why it fits:** it is a "legacy" game, so it changes as you play, and that turns it into a story kids want to keep going. It is cooperative, so siblings are on the same side. Our [cooperative games for kids](/articles/cooperative-games-for-kids/) list has more for families where competition causes fights.

## For 8-year-olds: real games, short and sharp

These six are rated 7 or 8 and up. They are games many adults buy for themselves, which is the best compliment a kids' pick can get.

### Carcassonne

[Carcassonne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00NX627HW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Klaus-Jürgen Wrede, first published by Hans im Glück in 2000, and won the 2001 Spiel des Jahres. Z-Man Games' edition plays 2 to 5, rated 7 and up, in about 30 to 45 minutes. Each turn, draw a tile and add it to the growing map of roads, cities, and fields. Then you may place one of your little wooden followers to claim what you built.

**Why it fits:** one tile and one choice per turn is the perfect size for this age, and the map you build together looks great. Kids can skip farmers in their first few games and still have a full game. Learn it with [how to play Carcassonne](/academy/carcassonne/how-to-play-carcassonne/), and grown-ups can teach it well with [how to teach Carcassonne](/academy/carcassonne/how-to-teach-carcassonne/).

### Kingdomino

[Kingdomino](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01N3A4070?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Bruno Cathala, published by Blue Orange Games in 2016, and won the 2017 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4, rated 8 and up, in about 15 to 20 minutes. Pick a domino tile, add it to your own little kingdom so the terrain matches, and grow big areas with crowns in them. The better the tile you pick, the later you pick next round.

**Why it fits:** it is the best first strategy game for an eight-year-old. Every turn has a real tradeoff, and the game is over in 20 minutes. Scoring is multiplication, which is good practice with a grown-up checking. Our free course is [Every Crown Counts](/academy/kingdomino/).

### Azul

[Azul](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077MZ2MPW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Michael Kiesling, published in 2017, and won the 2018 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 2 to 4, rated 8 and up, in 30 to 45 minutes. Take all the tiles of one color from a display, line them up on your board, and move them to your wall to score. Tiles you cannot use fall to the floor and cost you points.

**Why it fits:** the tiles feel like candy, and the puzzle is simple to see but hard to master. Eight-year-olds quickly learn to leave a bad pile for someone else, which is real strategy. Learn it with [how to play Azul](/academy/azul/how-to-play-azul/), then try our [Azul strategy guide](/articles/azul-strategy/).

### Sushi Go!

[Sushi Go!](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57VU44?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Phil Walker-Harding and published by Gamewright in 2013. It plays 2 to 5, rated 8 and up, in about 15 minutes. Pick one card from your hand, pass the rest to the left, and collect sets of sushi dishes that score in different ways. Play three rounds and add it up.

**Why it fits:** everyone picks at the same time, so there is no waiting. Kids learn to watch what others are collecting, which is a big step in thinking about other players. Our free course is [Pick and Pass](/academy/sushi-go/).

### Camel Up

[Camel Up](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09KWZMXLX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Steffen Bogen and won the 2014 Spiel des Jahres. The second edition from 2018 plays 3 to 8, rated 8 and up, in 30 to 45 minutes. Camels race around a track, and they stack on top of each other, so a camel at the back can ride to the front. Players do not own camels; they bet on which ones will win each leg and the whole race.

**Why it fits:** it is silly, loud, and great with a crowd, and the luck means an eight-year-old can beat anyone. Betting teaches a gentle sense of odds: "the blue camel is on top, so it moves with whoever is under it."

### Ghost Blitz

[Ghost Blitz](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0047N0Y52?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Jacques Zeimet and published by Zoch in 2010. It plays 2 to 8, rated 8 and up, in about 20 minutes. Five wooden objects sit in the middle of the table: a ghost, a chair, a bottle, a book, and a mouse. Flip a card and grab the right object as fast as you can. Sometimes the right object is on the card. Sometimes it is the one that is not.

**Why it fits:** it rewards quick thinking, not experience, so eight-year-olds regularly beat adults. Rounds take seconds, and a whole game fits before dinner.

## FAQ

### What is the best board game for a 7-year-old?

Ticket to Ride: First Journey. It is rated 6 and up, plays in under 30 minutes, and gives a seven-year-old real decisions without reading or scoring math.

### What is the best board game for an 8-year-old?

Kingdomino. It is rated 8 and up, plays in about 20 minutes, and has real strategy that adults enjoy too. Carcassonne and Azul are close behind.

### Can a 7-year-old play adult board games?

Many can play lighter adult games rated 7 or 8 and up, like Carcassonne, with a grown-up helping in the first few games. Heavier games are usually a year or two away. Our list of the [easiest board games to learn](/articles/easiest-board-games-to-learn/) is a good place to look for more.

### Should I let my 7-year-old win?

Not on purpose. Kids this age can usually tell, and they want to win for real. Pick games with some luck, like Camel Up, or games where kids have an edge, like Ice Cool and Ghost Blitz. If losing is still a big struggle, read our guide to [playing with a kid who hates losing](/articles/kid-who-hates-losing/).
