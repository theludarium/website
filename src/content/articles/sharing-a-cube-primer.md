You wrote the primer. Now the question is how to share a cube primer so the people who need it find it, in the thirty seconds they are willing to spend looking. The answer is almost always three places, each holding a different amount: the full document on CubeCobra, a short version pinned in Discord, and one printed sheet in the box.

This article covers what belongs in each place, how to keep three copies from drifting apart, and the mistakes that leave a good primer unread. It closes the how-to part of a series on writing one; the next and last piece is the primer for our own cube, published the way this article recommends.

## Why three places and not one?

Because the three readers from the first article in this series are standing in three different places when they want it.

The stranger browsing cube lists is on CubeCobra. The regular checking what changed is in Discord on their phone. The first-timer at the table has neither open and may have no signal. One location serves one of them and loses the other two. Three locations, each with the right amount, serve all three.

The cost is maintenance, and the next section is about keeping that cost low. The rule that makes it manageable: one copy is the source and the other two are summaries that link to it.

## What goes on CubeCobra?

The full primer, as the cube's overview. CubeCobra gives every cube a description field on its main page that accepts formatted text, and that field is where the complete document lives: the one-line description at the top, then every section in order, down to the FAQ and links. <!-- EDITOR: confirm the overview field's formatting support against the live CubeCobra site -->

This is the source copy, for three reasons. It sits next to the list, so a reader can move between the primer and the cards without leaving the page. It is public and searchable, so the stranger finds it. And the changelog is already there as the cube's blog, so the primer's "last three changes" section can simply link to it. The setup of the page itself is in the [CubeCobra guide](/articles/cubecobra-guide/).

Two habits: put the date of last update in the first line, and put the Discord invite and the visual spoiler link in both the first paragraph and the footer. People do not scroll to footers.

## What goes in Discord?

A pinned post in the cube's channel with the short version: the one-line description, how a night works, the archetype list with one line each, the house rules, and a link to the full primer on CubeCobra. About four hundred words, which is the most a pinned Discord post can hold and still be read on a phone.

The pinned post is a summary, not a copy. If the full primer and the pin both carry the full house rules and one gets edited, the two disagree within a month. The pin carries the rules because drafters check them on the night; it says at the top "full primer and current list: [link]" so anyone who finds a gap knows where the answer is.

Two more things belong in Discord and nowhere else. The weekly post, with the venue, time, and seat count, which changes and should not be in a permanent document. And a thread for primer feedback, so that questions and corrections arrive in one place instead of in direct messages to the host. The channel structure that makes this work is in [Discord for a game group](/articles/discord-setup-for-a-game-group/).

## What goes in print?

Two sheets, in the box, laminated or in sleeves.

The first is the one-page first-timer guide: how a draft works, two colors, seventeen lands, the three easy lanes, the five mistakes. It is handed to anyone drafting for the first time and it goes back in the box at the end of the night. This is the most-read page you will produce, because it is read at the moment of need by the reader who needs it most.

The second is the house rules, as a list, with the one-line description at the top and a QR code to the CubeCobra page at the bottom. It sits on the table during the draft. When a rules question comes up, the host points at it instead of answering, which is faster and ends the question for good.

A printed visual spoiler is optional; it earns its paper for new-player nights and for venues without phone signal, and the fifth article in this series covers when and how.

Print is the copy most likely to go stale, because it takes a trip to a printer to fix. Keep the printed sheets to content that rarely changes: the first-timer guide almost never does, and the house rules change a few times a year at most.

## How do you keep three copies from drifting?

One rule and one habit.

The rule: CubeCobra is the source. Any edit happens there first. The Discord pin and the printed sheets are summaries that are rebuilt from the source when the relevant part changes, never edited independently.

The habit: when you post a changelog entry, ask whether the change touches anything in the pin or the print. Card swaps almost never do. House rule changes, archetype additions, and size changes do. For those, update the pin the same day and reprint the sheet before the next draft. It is a two-minute check and it is the entire maintenance system.

| Location | Holds | Length | Changes when |
|---|---|---|---|
| CubeCobra overview | Full primer, source copy | 1,200 to 1,800 words | Any edit |
| Discord pin | Summary: description, night, lanes, rules, link | About 400 words | Rules, lanes, size, format change |
| Printed first-timer sheet | One-page draft guide | About 200 words | Almost never |
| Printed rules sheet | House rules, description, QR code | Half a page | Rules change |

## What about a website, a blog, or a Google Doc?

A Google Doc is fine as a drafting space and a poor place to publish: it is not near the list, it is not searchable by strangers, and sharing permissions trip people up. If the cube is not on CubeCobra, a doc is a workable source copy, but the reasons to move the list to CubeCobra apply to the primer as well.

A blog or a personal site is a good home for the philosophy section and for longer essays about the cube, with the primer on CubeCobra linking to them. It is a poor home for the primer itself, because the list is not there. The exception is a group with its own site that also hosts its calendar and its other communities, in which case the primer can live there and CubeCobra can link to it; that is the model our own group is moving toward as a branch of The Ludarium.

## What mistakes leave a primer unread?

- **Posting the full primer in Discord.** A two-thousand-word message is scrolled past. Pin the summary and link out.
- **No link from the list to the primer.** If the CubeCobra description is empty and the primer is somewhere else, the stranger never finds it.
- **No date.** An undated primer is assumed to be old.
- **Three independent copies.** They disagree within a month and the drafters stop trusting all three.
- **No feedback path.** If there is nowhere to say "this section is wrong," the corrections arrive during round two, out loud.

## FAQ

### Where should a cube primer be posted?

The full version on the cube's CubeCobra overview, a four-hundred-word summary pinned in the group's Discord channel, and a one-page first-timer guide printed and kept in the box.

### Should the whole primer be pinned in Discord?

No. Pin a summary with the description, how a night works, the archetype list, and the house rules, and link to the full primer. Long pinned posts are scrolled past.

### How do you keep the primer copies in sync?

Treat the CubeCobra copy as the source. When a change touches the rules, lanes, or size, rebuild the Discord pin the same day and reprint the sheet before the next draft.

### Is a Google Doc a good place for a cube primer?

For drafting it, yes. For publishing, no: it is not next to the list and strangers cannot find it. Put the finished primer on CubeCobra.

## Put it where they are standing

A primer is only as good as the moment it is read, and the moment is different for each reader. Put the right amount in each of the three places, keep one as the source, and the document does its job without anyone being told to go read it. The game is the reason. The people are the point.
