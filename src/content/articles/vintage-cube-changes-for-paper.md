The MTGO Vintage Cube is the best-tested cube list in the world, and most of its changes should not go into your paper cube. Both things are true. Taking a Vintage Cube paper list from mtgo.com and copying it card for card gets you a cube built for a different client, a different player pool, and a four-week shelf life. Reading the changelists with a filter gets you the actual value: tested card evaluations, finished archetype packages, and an early warning on which old staples have stopped earning their slot.

This article is that filter, applied to the six 2026 updates posted between January 23 and October 2, 2026.

## Why can't you just copy the Vintage Cube?

Three structural differences, before any card is named.

**The client.** The October 2026 update added Auriok Salvagers and Pyrite Spellbomb because MTGO shipped combo automation in August 2026 that made the loop take six clicks instead of dozens. The curator says so in [the post](https://www.mtgo.com/news/fra-vintage-cube-update). In paper, Bomberman has always been three sentences and a handshake. The reason the card arrived has nothing to do with your table.

**The player pool.** A Vintage Cube league draws thousands of drafts in a month from players who have read the list. Your pod of eight might include two people who have never seen Bazaar of Baghdad. A package that is "under-drafted but wins when drafted," as the September 2025 post described pump spells, works on MTGO because enough drafters eventually find it. In paper, an under-drafted lane is just dead cards.

**The clock.** The MTGO list turns over every six to eight weeks and brings cards back freely. Paper owners pay for every swap in money and sleeving time, so a change you make should still be right in a year.

## What translates well from the Vintage Cube to paper?

**Single-card verdicts on old staples.** When the curator benches a card everyone owns, that is free testing. In 2026 the posts cut Tarmogoyf (March, "has lost power"), Grave Titan (January, "has declined"), Wrath of God (October, resting behind Damn and Sunfall), Time Warp (October, "past its prime"), Eternal Witness (October, superseded by Emeritus of Abundance), and Craterhoof Behemoth (August 2025, benched while ramp is out). If one of those is in your cube on reputation, you now have a reason to look at it.

**Cheap interaction that is new.** Sear (January 2026) and Erode (May 2026) are the kinds of additions that are good in every cube with a reasonable curve. Bloodchief's Thirst has gone in and out three times since August 2025, which tells you it is always close to the line and fine either way.

**Complete archetype packages.** The Vintage Cube does not add one landfall card; it adds eight and a reason. The October 2026 landfall package (Quirion Ranger, Carnivorous Cultivator, Elvish Reclaimer, Marwyn, Escape to the Wilds, Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes, Absorbing Man, with Mole Man already in) is a finished lane with enablers, payoffs, and a tutor. If you want landfall, that is a shopping list. Our guide to [archetype density](/articles/archetype-density/) covers how many such lanes a 540 can hold.

**The vocabulary.** "Benched," "trimmed," "on trial," "rested for now." Borrow it for your own changelog. It tells drafters whether a card is gone or just away.

## What does not translate?

| Category | Example from 2026 | Why it stays on MTGO |
|---|---|---|
| Combo that depends on the client | Auriok Salvagers, Pyrite Spellbomb (Oct) | Added because clicks got cheaper, not because the combo got better |
| Universes Beyond test cards | Batroc the Leaper, Okoye, Krang (Jul); Bilbo (Oct) | Many paper groups exclude UB; several already rotated back out |
| Four-week experiments | Madness package (Jan to Sep); Aristocrats (Jan to May); Persist (Jan to Mar) | Each one was removed inside the year; paper cannot churn that fast |
| Cards bought for their restricted status | The Fantasticar (Aug), added partly because it is restricted in Vintage | Flavor of the format, not a design need |
| Mid-run reversals | Leovold (out Mar, in May, out Oct); Paradoxical Outcome (out Jan, in Jul) | The team is calibrating; wait until a card stays put for two updates |

The Universes Beyond row deserves a word. The MTGO cube has every card at no marginal cost and a mandate to feature new sets, so Marvel, TMNT, and Hobbit cards go in the week they release. That is a promotional constraint. Of the twenty Marvel Super Heroes additions in July 2026, Together as One, Krang, and Shadowspear were already out by August. A paper owner who bought in would have been left holding them.

## A simple rule for reading each changelist

For each row in a Vintage Cube changelist, ask four questions in order:

1. **Is the card out something I own and play on reputation?** If yes, test the cut.
2. **Is the card in part of a package?** If yes, either take the whole package or none of it. Half a landfall lane is worse than no landfall lane.
3. **Has the card in survived at least one further update?** If no, wait. Erode (May) survived July, August, and October. Brightglass Gearhulk has been in, out, and in again. One of those is settled.
4. **Does the reason given apply to my table?** "Boros remains the strongest archetype" is an MTGO finding from an MTGO population. Check your own results before trimming your white two-drops.

Rule three is the one most owners skip. The Vintage Cube's whole method is to try things in public and revert quickly. Copying an addition the week it posts means volunteering for the experiment without the sample size.

## FAQ

### Should I copy the MTGO Vintage Cube list exactly?

Only if you want a powered 540 for a group that will draft it many times a month and if you accept that the list will be stale within two months. For most groups, use it as a reference and a test lab, not a template.

### How do I know which Vintage Cube additions are permanent?

Watch for a card surviving two consecutive updates. The Hobbit (August 2026) and Reality Fracture (October 2026) posts are the current pair to check against.

### Are Universes Beyond cards required to keep up with the Vintage Cube?

No. The MTGO cube adds them because it promotes each set release. Several 2026 UB additions were cut within one or two updates.

### Where do I read the changelists?

Each update article on mtgo.com has a changelist table and a link to the full list at [mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist](https://www.mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist). Our [guide to how the updates work](/articles/how-mtgo-vintage-cube-updates-work/) explains the format.
