The best dice games range from a cup of dice you can teach in one minute to full board games where every roll is a puzzle. For a quick, small-box game, play [Zombie Dice](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003IKMR0U?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Qwixx](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57138C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or the classic [Yahtzee](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TLEMRKM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For push-your-luck tension with family or friends, play [Can't Stop](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005DBRDPG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Las Vegas](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FNNC1R99?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [King of Tokyo](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01F46RPS4?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And when you want dice that do more than add up, the bigger boxes are [Sagrada](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GL8V2VFD?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Dice Forge](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XVW4N17?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Space Base](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0793MT3Y6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Roll Player](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01E9539HM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Dice Throne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884756?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [The Castles of Burgundy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R5ZH6FQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20).

**The quick answer:** for a family or a mixed group, start with **King of Tokyo**. It is loud, quick, and plays up to six. For a small, inexpensive game that fits in a pocket, **Qwixx** is hard to beat. For a strategy group, **The Castles of Burgundy** shows how much thinking two dice can hold.

Below are 12 dice games sorted from the smallest box to the biggest, with a comparison table and a guide to which one to buy first.

## What makes a great dice game?

Everyone has played a dice game that was all luck and no fun: roll, move, repeat. The great ones give you a real choice about what to do with the roll. There are four main ways they do it:

1. **Push your luck.** You keep rolling as long as you dare, and one bad roll wipes out your turn. Zombie Dice and Can't Stop are pure versions of this.
2. **Choose which dice to use.** You roll a handful and pick the ones that help you most. Yahtzee started here; King of Tokyo and Las Vegas build on it.
3. **Draft from a shared pool.** Someone rolls, then players take turns picking dice. Sagrada and Roll Player work this way, and it means the dice you leave behind matter too.
4. **Change the dice themselves.** In Dice Forge, you rebuild your dice face by face. In Space Base, you build a board that pays out on every number.

A little luck is a feature, not a bug. It keeps newer players in the game and makes the big moments feel big. Our article on [luck vs skill in board games](/articles/luck-vs-skill-board-games/) explains why the best games mix the two.

## Small-box dice games

These three are the ones to keep in a drawer, a backpack, or a glove box. Each teaches in a couple of minutes.

### Zombie Dice

Designed by Steve Jackson and published by Steve Jackson Games, Zombie Dice plays 2 or more in 10 to 20 minutes. You are a zombie. You pull three dice from a cup and roll them: brains score, feet let you reroll, and shotgun blasts hurt. Keep going for more brains, but three shotguns and you lose everything from that turn.

**Why it fits:** [Zombie Dice](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003IKMR0U?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the purest push-your-luck game there is. Every turn ends with the whole table cheering or groaning. It is small, inexpensive, and works for any number of people who can pass a cup. It is a great warm-up while you wait for the last guest.

### Yahtzee

Yahtzee was first sold by Edwin S. Lowe in 1956 and is now published by Hasbro. It plays any number of players in about half an hour. You roll five dice up to three times a turn, keeping the ones you want, then score the result in one of 13 boxes on your sheet.

**Why it fits:** [Yahtzee](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TLEMRKM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the game most people already know, and it is the root of a lot of modern design. The "roll up to three times and keep what you like" rule lives on in King of Tokyo and many others. It is still a fine pick for grandparents and grandkids together; see our guide to [games to play with grandparents](/articles/games-to-play-with-grandparents/).

### Qwixx

Designed by Steffen Benndorf and first published in 2012, Qwixx plays 2 to 5 in about 15 minutes. One player rolls six dice, and everyone can use the white dice to cross off a number on their own score sheet. The active player can also use a colored die. Numbers have to be crossed off in order, left to right, so every skip is a small gamble.

**Why it fits:** everyone uses every roll, so nobody waits for a turn. [Qwixx](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00J57138C?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) feels like Yahtzee with more choices, and it is a classic of the roll-and-write style. Our article on [roll and write games](/articles/roll-and-write-games/) explains why the format is so hard to design well.

## Push-your-luck and family favorites

These three are a step up. They still teach fast, but they add a board, a city, or a fight to the dice.

### Can't Stop

Designed by Sid Sackson and first published in 1980, Can't Stop plays 2 to 4 in about 30 minutes. You roll four dice and split them into two pairs. Each pair's total moves a marker up that numbered column on the board. Keep rolling to climb higher, but if no pair lets you move, you lose everything from that turn. Claim three columns to win.

**Why it fits:** [Can't Stop](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005DBRDPG?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) teaches a little probability without anyone noticing. The middle columns, like 7, come up often but are long; the edges, like 2 and 12, are short but rare. Deciding when to stop is the whole game, and it is just as fun for kids as for adults.

### Las Vegas

Designed by Rüdiger Dorn and first published by alea and Ravensburger in 2012, Las Vegas plays 2 to 5 in about 30 minutes. There are six casinos, numbered 1 to 6, each with cash on the table. On your turn you roll all your dice and must place every die showing one number on the matching casino.

**Why it fits:** the twist is that ties cancel out. If two players have the same number of dice at a casino, neither of them wins anything there. That makes [Las Vegas](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FNNC1R99?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) a game of friendly sabotage, full of groans and laughter. It plays well at five, which few dice games do.

### King of Tokyo

Designed by Richard Garfield and published by IELLO in 2011, King of Tokyo plays 2 to 6 in about 30 minutes. You are a giant monster. You roll six dice up to three times, Yahtzee style, to score points, heal, gain energy, or smash the other monsters. Whoever sits in Tokyo hits everyone else, and everyone else hits them.

**Why it fits:** [King of Tokyo](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01F46RPS4?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the best dice game for a mixed table. The rules are simple, the monsters are fun, and the power cards add just enough strategy. It plays up to six, and at five or six a second spot, Tokyo Bay, opens up. Learn it with the free [King of Tokyo course](/academy/king-of-tokyo/), and see the [dice odds lesson](/academy/king-of-tokyo/king-of-tokyo-dice-odds/) for when to reroll.

## Bigger dice-driven board games

In these, dice are the engine of a full board game. You still roll, but the decisions about what to do with the roll take center stage.

### Sagrada

Designed by Adrian Adamescu and Daryl Andrews and published by Floodgate Games in 2017, Sagrada plays 1 to 4 in 30 to 45 minutes. Each player builds a stained-glass window out of colored dice. Each round, dice are drawn and rolled, and players take turns drafting them into their window, following color and number rules on each square.

**Why it fits:** [Sagrada](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GL8V2VFD?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) looks stunning on the table and plays like a puzzle. It is the pick for people who like Sudoku or Azul. We compared the two in [Azul vs Sagrada](/articles/azul-vs-sagrada/).

### Dice Forge

Designed by Régis Bonnessée and published by Libellud in 2017, Dice Forge plays 2 to 4 in about 45 minutes. Each player has two dice with faces that pop off. You spend gold to buy better faces and snap them onto your dice, so your rolls get stronger over the game. Everyone rolls on every turn, not just the active player.

**Why it fits:** [Dice Forge](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XVW4N17?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) turns "building an engine" into something you can hold in your hand. It is a great bridge between family games and strategy games. Our guide to [engine-building games](/articles/engine-building-games/) covers the idea behind it.

### Space Base

Designed by John D. Clair and published by AEG in 2018, Space Base plays 2 to 5 in about 60 minutes. On your turn you roll two dice and take a reward either from each die or from their sum. Then you can buy a new ship card for one of your twelve numbered sectors.

**Why it fits:** the clever part is that every roll pays everyone. Cards you have deployed earn you rewards on other players' turns too, so you are always watching the dice. [Space Base](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0793MT3Y6?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) suits groups who liked the dice in Catan and want more control. The first player to 40 points triggers the end.

### Roll Player

Designed by Keith Matejka and published by Thunderworks Games in 2016, Roll Player plays 1 to 4 in 60 to 90 minutes. You build a fantasy hero by drafting dice into six attribute rows, such as strength and wisdom, and buying gear and traits at a market.

**Why it fits:** [Roll Player](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01E9539HM?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the dice game for people who love making characters in tabletop RPGs but want a game that ends in an hour. Every die is a small puzzle: right number, right color, right row. If you mean polyhedral dice for role-playing, our guide to [D&D dice](/articles/dnd-dice-explained/) explains what each one does.

### Dice Throne

Designed by Nate Chatellier and Manny Trembley and published by Roxley, Dice Throne plays 2 to 6 in about 20 to 40 minutes, and it is at its best as a duel. Each hero has their own five dice, player board, and card deck. You roll up to three times to land combos, then attack, defend, and play cards to change the dice.

**Why it fits:** [Dice Throne](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1988884756?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is King of Tokyo grown up into a fighting game. Every hero plays differently, so learning a new one feels like learning a new game. The Season One Rerolled Battle Chest gives you a set of heroes in one box. It suits pairs who want a quick, punchy head-to-head battle.

### The Castles of Burgundy

Designed by Stefan Feld and first published by alea and Ravensburger in 2011, The Castles of Burgundy plays 1 to 4 in the 2019 edition. Plan for about 90 minutes. Each turn you roll two dice, and the numbers decide which hex tiles you can take from the board and where on your estate they can go.

**Why it fits:** [The Castles of Burgundy](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R5ZH6FQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) proves that dice do not have to mean luck. Workers let you adjust a die up or down, and every tile type does something different, so a bad roll is rarely a wasted turn. It is the deepest game on this list. Learn it with the free [Castles of Burgundy course](/academy/castles-of-burgundy/) and the lesson on [dice odds and workers](/academy/castles-of-burgundy/castles-of-burgundy-dice-odds-workers/).

## FAQ

### What is the best dice game?

For most tables, King of Tokyo. It is easy to teach, plays up to six, and works for families and adults alike. For a deeper game, The Castles of Burgundy; for a pocket game, Qwixx or Zombie Dice.

### What are good dice games for adults?

Las Vegas and Can't Stop are great with friends and a snack. For more strategy, try Space Base, Dice Forge, or The Castles of Burgundy. Our list of [quick games under 30 minutes](/articles/quick-board-games-under-30-minutes/) has more short options.

### What dice games can you play with just regular dice?

Yahtzee needs only five dice and a score sheet. Farkle and Liar's Dice also use standard dice. Most modern dice games come with custom dice, which is part of their charm.

### Are there cooperative dice games?

Yes. Sky Team is a two-player co-op where you land a plane by placing dice in secret. Learn it with the free [Sky Team course](/academy/sky-team/), or see our list of [best cooperative board games](/articles/best-cooperative-board-games/).

### Are dice games good for kids?

Many are. Can't Stop, King of Tokyo, and Qwixx are all good family picks, and they teach a little counting and probability along the way. Our list of [best family board games](/articles/best-family-board-games/) has more ideas for mixed ages.

### What is a dice drafting game?

A game where dice are rolled into a shared pool and players take turns picking them. Sagrada and Roll Player are good examples. The idea is the same as card drafting; our guide to [card drafting](/articles/card-drafting-explained/) explains it.
