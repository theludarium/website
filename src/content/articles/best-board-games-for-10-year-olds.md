The best board games for 10 year olds are the same games adults play, because at ten most kids are ready for them. For a first big strategy game, get [Catan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00U26V4VQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Takenoko](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0049H9NVW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For a kid ready to go deeper, try [Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [7 Wonders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F65MX4L?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), or [Heat: Pedal to the Metal](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BGXPZG4B?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). For teamwork, play [Forbidden Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003D7F4YY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) or [Pandemic](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A2HD40E?tag=cubecommonw0a-20). And for quick games with big laughs, keep [Love Letter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PHHBWM9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), [Codenames: Pictures](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01HT9DERU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20), and [Hive](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000EN5ZLW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) close by.

**The quick answer:** for most ten-year-olds, start with **Splendor** (30 minutes, easy rules, real strategy) or **Catan** if the family likes to talk and trade. For a kid who already loves games and wants a challenge, **Wingspan** is the step up.

Below are 12 picks, what changes at age ten, a comparison table, and a "which one first" guide. Every game is rated 10 and up on the box or younger.

## What can a 10-year-old handle in a board game?

Ten is the age when the "kids' game" label stops mattering. Most ten-year-olds can learn the same rules an adult learns, and many will out-plan the adults within a few games. Children still vary, so take this as a typical picture.

1. **Reading: fluent.** Cards with a sentence or two of text are fine. That opens up games like Wingspan and 7 Wonders, where each card does something different.
2. **Math: comfortable.** Adding a long score sheet, counting resources, and simple multiplication are no problem.
3. **Planning: several turns ahead.** This is the big change from eight. A ten-year-old can hold a plan for a whole game, like "I'm collecting blue cards so later ones are cheaper," and change it when someone gets in the way.
4. **Reading the table: starting.** Ten-year-olds start to notice what other players need and block them on purpose. That is when games get really interesting.
5. **Attention: 30 to 90 minutes.** A focused ten-year-old can play an hour-long game. Most still prefer games under 45 minutes on a school night.
6. **Losing: mostly fine, if it was fair.** Ten-year-olds care a lot about fairness. A loss in a close, fair game is usually fine. A loss to a parent who "obviously" knew a trick is not. Teach the strategy openly, and they will thank you by beating you.

The practical upshot: buy games adults rate highly, keep them under an hour, and teach them as equals.

## First real strategy games

These four have short rules and deep play. A ten-year-old can learn any of them in ten minutes and still be finding new ideas a year later.

### Catan

[Catan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00U26V4VQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Klaus Teuber, first published in Germany in 1995, and won the 1995 Spiel des Jahres. The current edition plays 3 to 4, rated 10 and up, in about 60 to 90 minutes. Dice hand out wood, brick, sheep, wheat, and ore, and players trade and spend them to build roads, settlements, and cities. First to 10 points wins.

**Why it fits:** ten is right when Catan clicks. Kids can now track what they need, make deals, and plan where to build. The trading builds real negotiation skills; our lesson on [the art of the trade](/academy/catan/art-of-the-trade/) explains why it matters. The free [Catan course](/academy/catan/) goes from first settlement to strategy, and our guide to [how to win at Catan](/articles/how-to-win-at-catan/) is a good next step. If your child played Catan Junior at seven or eight, this is the natural graduation.

### Splendor

[Splendor](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00IZEUFIA?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Marc André and published by Space Cowboys in 2014. It plays 2 to 4, rated 10 and up, in about 30 minutes. Take gem chips, spend them on development cards, and let each card you own act as a permanent discount. Race to 15 points.

**Why it fits:** the rules are three sentences, but the strategy grows with the player. It teaches a big idea: invest now to get stronger later. It also plays well with two, which makes it great for a parent and child. Learn it with [how to play Splendor](/academy/splendor/how-to-play-splendor/), then try our [Splendor strategy guide](/articles/splendor-strategy/).

### Cascadia

[Cascadia](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B093H8RGXX?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Randy Flynn, published by Flatout Games and AEG in 2021, and won the 2022 Spiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 4, rated 10 and up, in 30 to 45 minutes. Each turn, take a hex habitat tile and a wildlife token, add them to your own landscape, and score for animal patterns and big connected habitats.

**Why it fits:** there is almost no conflict, so it is calm and kind, and it has a solo mode for a kid who likes to puzzle alone. Different scoring cards each game keep it fresh. Learn it with [how to play Cascadia](/academy/cascadia/how-to-play-cascadia/).

### Takenoko

[Takenoko](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0049H9NVW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Antoine Bauza and published by Bombyx and Matagot in 2011. It plays 2 to 4, rated 8 and up, in about 45 minutes. Players tend a bamboo garden for the emperor's hungry panda. Each turn, roll a weather die, then take two actions, like adding a garden plot, growing bamboo with the gardener, or moving the panda to eat it. Complete secret goal cards to score.

**Why it fits:** the panda and gardener are charming, and the secret goals add a little mystery. It sits right between the eight-year-old games and the heavier ones, so it is a good first "hour-long" game for a ten-year-old.

## Ready for more: deeper games

These three have more going on. They suit a ten-year-old who already loves games and wants to play what the adults play on their own.

### Wingspan

[Wingspan](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YQ641NQ?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Elizabeth Hargrave, published by Stonemaier Games in 2019, and won the 2019 Kennerspiel des Jahres. It plays 1 to 5, rated 10 and up, in 40 to 70 minutes. Players attract birds to their wildlife preserve. Each bird card has a power, and the birds you play make your future turns stronger.

**Why it fits:** every card is a real bird with a real fact on it, so it is a nature book and a strategy game at once. The reading on each card is short. A ten-year-old who plays it a few times will start building clever combos. Learn it with [how to play Wingspan](/academy/wingspan/how-to-play-wingspan/) and see our [Wingspan strategy guide](/articles/wingspan-strategy/).

### 7 Wonders

[7 Wonders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F65MX4L?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Antoine Bauza, published by Repos Production in 2010, and won the first Kennerspiel des Jahres in 2011. The current edition plays 3 to 7, rated 10 and up, in about 30 minutes. Everyone picks one card from a hand at the same time, plays it, and passes the rest. Over three ages, you build an ancient city through science, military, trade, and wonders.

**Why it fits:** it plays seven in half an hour with almost no waiting, which is rare. That makes it the best pick for a big family or a group of cousins. If your child already loves Sushi Go!, this is the grown-up version. Learn it with [how to play 7 Wonders](/academy/7-wonders/how-to-play-7-wonders/).

### Heat: Pedal to the Metal

[Heat: Pedal to the Metal](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BGXPZG4B?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Asger Harding Granerud and Daniel Skjold Pedersen and published by Days of Wonder in 2022. It plays 1 to 6, rated 10 and up, in about 60 minutes. Play cards to set your car's speed, and push hard into corners at the risk of overheating your engine.

**Why it fits:** racing is a theme ten-year-olds get instantly, and the choices are easy to see: go fast now, or save your engine for later. It also has solo and robot-car rules for when the table is short. Learn it with [how to play Heat](/academy/heat/how-to-play-heat/).

## Team games: play together, win together

Cooperative games put the whole table on one side. At ten, kids can carry their share of the planning, which makes these feel very different from the co-ops they played at five. For the full comparison, see [Pandemic vs Forbidden Island](/articles/pandemic-vs-forbidden-island/).

### Forbidden Island

[Forbidden Island](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003D7F4YY?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Matt Leacock and published by Gamewright in 2010. It plays 2 to 4, rated 10 and up, in about 30 minutes. The team explores a sinking island, collects four treasures, and escapes by helicopter before the water takes everything. Each player has a special role.

**Why it fits:** the goal is concrete and the board is small, so it is the easier of the two co-ops here despite the higher box age. It is an excellent first "real" cooperative game.

### Pandemic

[Pandemic](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A2HD40E?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Matt Leacock and published by Z-Man Games in 2008. It plays 2 to 4, rated 8 and up, in about 45 minutes. Players are disease specialists flying around the world to treat outbreaks and find four cures before time runs out.

**Why it fits:** it is a harder puzzle than Forbidden Island, and ten-year-olds love a game that is genuinely difficult to beat. Losing to the game together is a great way to practice losing well. Learn it with [how to play Pandemic](/academy/pandemic/how-to-play-pandemic/), or read [Pandemic explained](/articles/pandemic-board-game-explained/) first.

## Quick games for any night

These three are short, small, and great for a school night or a sleepover.

### Love Letter

[Love Letter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PHHBWM9?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by Seiji Kanai and first published in 2012. The 2019 Z-Man Games edition plays 2 to 6, rated 10 and up, in about 20 minutes. Each player holds one card. On your turn, draw a second and play one, using its power to guess, peek, compare, or knock someone out of the round.

**Why it fits:** it teaches deduction and bluffing with very few cards. A round takes a couple of minutes, so a knockout never stings for long. It fits in a jacket pocket.

### Codenames: Pictures

[Codenames: Pictures](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01HT9DERU?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) is the picture version of Vlaada Chvátil's Codenames, published by Czech Games Edition in 2016. It is rated 10 and up and plays best with four or more, in about 15 minutes. Two teams each have a spymaster who gives one-word clues to point their team at the right pictures on a grid.

**Why it fits:** the pictures are odd and funny, so clues get creative fast, and kids can play on a team with adults. It is the best party pick here for sleepovers. Our [Codenames course](/academy/codenames/) covers the original word version too.

### Hive

[Hive](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000EN5ZLW?tag=cubecommonw0a-20) was designed by John Yianni and published by Gen42 Games in 2001. It plays 2, rated 9 and up, in about 20 minutes. There is no board. Players place and move insect tiles, each with its own move, trying to surround the other player's queen bee.

**Why it fits:** it is a chess-like game that teaches in five minutes. For a ten-year-old who likes to beat a parent head to head, it is perfect, and the chunky tiles survive a backpack.

## FAQ

### What is the best board game for a 10-year-old?

Splendor is the best first pick for most ten-year-olds. It is rated 10 and up, takes about 30 minutes, and has strategy that keeps growing as the player does. Catan is the pick for a chatty family.

### Can a 10-year-old play Catan?

Yes. Catan is rated 10 and up, and most ten-year-olds can play the full game with a grown-up explaining trades in the first game or two.

### What board games can a 10-year-old play with adults?

Every game on this list. That is the point of this age: kids and adults can sit down as equals. Wingspan, 7 Wonders, and Heat are games adults buy for themselves.

### What are good board games for 10 to 12 year olds?

## Equals at the table

Ten is the year a kid can sit down at a game night and simply be another player. That shift is a gift for the whole family. Teach the strategy openly, play to win, and enjoy being beaten fairly.
