The player count on a board game box tells you how many people the game allows. It does not tell you how many people the game is best with. That second number is the one that decides whether your game night sings or drags, and it is often smaller than the box suggests. This guide gives the box range and the best board game player count for 40 popular games, based on the BoardGameGeek community poll, plus what changes at each count.

**The quick answer:** most modern strategy games play best with 3 or 4, even when the box says 1 to 5. Wingspan is best at 3, Catan and Ticket to Ride at 4, Carcassonne and Azul at 2, and Codenames at 6. If your group size is fixed, pick the game to fit the group, not the other way around.

## Why is the "best" player count different from the box?

A box range is a promise that the rules work. A best count is a judgment about when the game feels its best. Publishers have good reasons to print a wide range: a game that says 1 to 5 fits more tables than one that says 3. But adding or removing a player changes almost everything about a game:

- **Waiting.** At two players you watch one other turn. At five, you watch four, and a 60-minute game can stretch past two hours.
- **Competition for space.** A board that feels tight at four can feel empty at two, so nobody bumps into anyone.
- **Control.** With fewer players, the market or the card row changes less between your turns, so your plans survive.
- **Politics.** At three or more, you can gang up on the leader. At two, every hit lands on your only rival.

That is why a game can be "1 to 5" on the box and best at 3. The rules hold at every count. The feeling peaks at one. Our piece on [player count design](/articles/player-count-design/) explains the math behind this from the designer's side.

## How do you read BoardGameGeek's player count poll?

[BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/) (BGG) is the largest board game database online, and every game page has a community poll. For each player count, users vote **Best**, **Recommended**, or **Not Recommended**. BGG then sums it up near the top of the page, in a line like "Community: 1 to 4, Best: 3."

Three tips for reading it:

1. **Look at the whole spread, not just the winner.** A game "best at 4" might have nearly as many votes for 3. Click the poll to see the bars. A close race means the game is flexible.
2. **Watch for lopsided counts.** If a count gets a big pile of Not Recommended votes, take it seriously, even if the box allows it.
3. **Remember who votes.** BGG voters are mostly hobbyists who play a game many times. They tend to favor tight, low-downtime games, so polls lean toward smaller counts for heavy games. If your group is casual and chatty, an extra player may bother you less.

## What are the best board games for 2 players?

Eleven games on our list poll best at two. Four of them only play two: **7 Wonders Duel, Sky Team, Patchwork, and Jaipur.** These are built for the duel and feel sharp because of it.

The other seven are bigger games that happen to shine at two:

- **Azul and Carcassonne:** both turn into a tactical, almost chess-like contest where you can plan around one opponent.
- **Cascadia and Kingdomino:** calm puzzles where the draft becomes a real choice, not a lottery.
- **The Castles of Burgundy and Ark Nova:** heavy games that stay a sane length at two.
- **Spirit Island:** a co-op where two players can talk through every move.

## What are the best board games for 3 players?

Three is the awkward count for a lot of games, which makes the eight that poll best at three worth knowing: **Wingspan, Dune: Imperium, Terraforming Mars, Everdell, Splendor, Lost Ruins of Arnak, Dominion, and Gloomhaven.**

Most of these are engine builders or deck builders. You spend most of your time on your own board, so a third player adds competition for cards and spaces without adding much waiting. Three also avoids the "two against one" problem that plagues direct-conflict games, since there is little direct conflict to begin with.

If you often have exactly three, Wingspan and Splendor are the friendliest place to start. Terraforming Mars and Dune: Imperium suit a group ready for a longer evening.

## What are the best board games for 4 players?

Four is the classic game night, and sixteen of our forty games poll best there: **7 Wonders, Catan, Ticket to Ride, Dune: Imperium Uprising, Root, Brass: Birmingham, Pandemic, The Crew, Sushi Go!, King of Tokyo, Power Grid, Scythe, Cartographers, Quacks of Quedlinburg, Agricola, and Hanabi.**

These games need pressure from several directions. Catan needs enough players to fill the island and keep trading alive. Ticket to Ride needs enough trains to make routes scarce. Root and Brass: Birmingham need rivals on every side of the map. With fewer players, these games still work, but they lose the squeeze that makes them memorable.

## What are the best games for 5, 6, or more players?

Only five games on our list poll best above four, and they share one trait: everyone plays at once, or in teams.

- **Flip 7** (best at 5): a quick push-your-luck card game that plays well into a crowd.
- **Heat** (best at 6): more cars on the track means more slipstreaming and more blocked corners.
- **Codenames, Wavelength, and Just One** (all best at 6): team and group games where more people means better debate.

7 Wonders deserves a mention too. It polls best at 4, but because everyone picks a card at the same time, a seven-player game takes about as long as a three-player one.

## How do you choose a game by player count?

Use this order of questions, and the right game usually falls out.

1. **How many people are coming, for sure?** Plan for the count you will have, not the count you hope for.
2. **Is that count the game's best, or just allowed?** Check the chart above, or the BGG poll for anything not listed.
3. **If it is only allowed, can you split?** Two tables of three often beat one table of six playing a game best at three.
4. **Will the extra player add waiting?** Games with simultaneous turns (7 Wonders, Quacks, Cartographers) stretch well. Games with long, separate turns (Terraforming Mars, Ark Nova) do not.
5. **Is anyone new?** A new player slows every turn. Treat a table with two newcomers as if it had one extra person.

The [Card Catalogue](/card-catalogue/) can do some of this work for you. It sorts games by how they feel and lets you filter for the size of your table.

## What are the Wingspan, Codenames, 7 Wonders, and Carcassonne player counts?

These four get searched the most, so here is a little more detail.

### Wingspan player count

Wingspan plays 1 to 5 and polls best at 3. Two players is a calm, close race. Four is still good. Five works but adds noticeable waiting, since each turn means reading a card and triggering powers. The Asia expansion adds a Flock mode that stretches the base game to 6 or 7, with two players taking turns at once. The [Wingspan course](/academy/wingspan/) covers the game from first flight to tournament play.

### Codenames player count

Codenames lists 2 to 8 and up. The poll picks 6: two teams of three, so each spymaster has two guessers to argue. Four works with one guesser per team. Above eight, it still runs, but quieter players get drowned out. For two players, use Codenames: Duet, the cooperative version.

### 7 Wonders player count

The current edition of 7 Wonders (2020) plays 3 to 7 and polls best at 4. The 2010 first edition included a two-player variant with a shared dummy city, which is why BoardGameGeek and some stores still list 2 to 7. The second edition dropped that variant. For two, 7 Wonders Duel is the better game.

### Carcassonne player count

Carcassonne plays 2 to 5 and polls best at 2. At two, you can track the tiles and block your rival. At five, it becomes looser and luckier, which suits a family table just fine. Expansions add a sixth player.

## FAQ

### What does "best player count" mean on BoardGameGeek?

It is the player count that got the most "Best" votes in the game's community poll. Voters choose Best, Recommended, or Not Recommended for each count. The result is an opinion, not a rule, but for popular games it reflects thousands of votes.

### What is the most common best player count for board games?

Four. In our list of 40 popular games, 16 poll best at four, 11 at two, 8 at three, and 5 at five or more.

### Is it bad to play a game outside its best count?

No. A game is designed to work across its whole box range. The best count is just where it feels most balanced or moves fastest. Many groups happily play Catan at three or Wingspan at five.

### What games play best with 3 players?

Wingspan, Splendor, Everdell, Terraforming Mars, Dune: Imperium, Lost Ruins of Arnak, Dominion, and Gloomhaven all poll best at three.

### How can I find the best player count for a game not on this list?

Open the game's BoardGameGeek page and look at the player count line near the top, then click it to see the full poll. Check how close the votes are before you decide.
